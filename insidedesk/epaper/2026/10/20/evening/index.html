European shares fell for a second day on oil and AI nerves. Wiesbaden's quieter release said more: German producer prices rose 6.7% in a year, the most since March 2023, and almost all of the new heat came from energy.

Tuesday had two kinds of market news, and only one of them made a noise.

The noise. European shares fell for a second day. Reuters reported the STOXX 600 down 0.7% in the afternoon after a fall of close to 1% on Monday, with chipmakers including ASML and ASMI down almost 3% and French stocks off almost 1%4. By the close the index stood at 634.78, 0.75% lower on the day5. The explanations were the familiar ones: Brent at a five-month high above $111, nervousness about whether money poured into artificial intelligence will earn its cost, and French bond yields after a weekend of riots4. All of it is true. None of it is a number you will pay directly.

The receipt. At 08:00 the Federal Statistical Office in Wiesbaden published something you will pay, eventually. Producer prices for industrial goods sold in Germany were 6.7% higher in September than a year earlier, and 1.9% higher than in August1. It was the steepest annual rise since March 2023, when the rate was 7.5%1. Economists polled beforehand had expected 6.2%3. That is half a point of surprise in a single month, at the factory gate, before anyone has printed a price tag.

Where the heat is. Read the release for one paragraph more and the picture becomes narrower, and calmer. Energy was 14.9% dearer than a year ago and 5.8% dearer than in August1. Mineral-oil products were up 51.3% on the year, light heating oil 91.8%, motor fuels 48.6%1. Strip energy out and producer prices rose 3.2% on the year and just 0.1% on the month1. Consumer goods, the things that end up in a shopping basket, were 1.8% cheaper than in September 20251.

So the shock is real and it is specific. It sits in barrels, tonnes of gasoil and the invoices of anyone who heats with oil or drives for a living. It has not yet spread through everything else.

The sequence, from the table. The statistics office’s own series shows how the year turned. In February producer prices were 3.3% lower than a year earlier2. They crossed zero in April at +1.7%, reached 3.0% in July, 4.6% in August and 6.7% in September2. Energy went from −12.5% in February to +14.9% in September2. That is not a gentle drift. It is the war in Iran and the Gulf arriving in German factory accounts, month by month, which is exactly what Destatis says it is1.

Why the dull number beats the loud one. A falling share price tells you that someone, somewhere, changed their mind about the future. It may change back by Friday; on Tuesday Nasdaq futures slipped 0.7%, a day after finding some support4. A producer price is different. It is what a buyer paid on the 15th of the month for a real delivery, recorded on a fixed survey day under published methods1. It does not reverse because sentiment improves. It feeds through, with a lag, into the consumer-price index, which already showed fuels up 37.1% and heating oil up 74.9% on the year in September6.

There is a constructive reading here, and it is not wishful. The consumer-goods line is still negative and the ex-energy monthly change is a tenth of a point1. Inflation that stays inside the energy bill is painful but bounded. The thing worth watching is not the headline 6.7%; it is whether that 0.1% becomes 0.3%, 0.5%, then a habit1.

What this desk is not saying. It is not saying the market is wrong to worry, or that prices will fall, or that you should do anything with your savings. It makes no forecast. It is saying that on a day when the screens were red, the most useful number in Europe came from a statistics office’s press release, published on time, with a methods note, a base year and a survey date at the bottom1. That is the dull machinery working. It is worth reading before the commentary.

Nothing here is investment advice.

What to watch

  • The October producer-price release: September's survey day was the 15th, so it does not yet show the tank discount that started on 1 October.
  • Whether prices excluding energy, up 0.1% on the month in September, start to move faster. That is where a fuel shock becomes a general one.
  • Intermediate goods, up 6.4% on the year. They are what one factory sells to the next.
Desk comment

A share price is an opinion about next year; a producer price is a receipt from last month.

After months of petitions, court rulings and foreign-influence warnings, 75.8% of Alberta's voters chose to remain in Canada. The ballot box did what the speeches could not: it settled the question for now.

On Monday Albertans answered a question built as a choice between two options: remain a province of Canada, or have the provincial government begin the legal process for a binding referendum on separation1. With about 73% of the count reported, Remain had 1,315,426 votes (75.8%) and the referendum option 420,038 (24.2%)1.

The machinery before the vote. The path to the ballot ran through institutions at every step. A separatist petition was struck down by the Court of King’s Bench in May over the duty to consult Indigenous peoples; a pro-Canada petition was verified with more than 400,000 signatures1. The final polls had Remain between about 66% and 74%1. The result landed at the top of that range.

The noise around it. A Globe and Mail investigation reported that operatives tied to the US Republican Party had helped the secession campaign, and University of Regina researchers attributed most suspected foreign influence activity to Russian sources, with a rising US share1. None of that moved the count far from where the pollsters had it.

What the market did. The Canadian dollar rose 0.4% to C$1.405 per US dollar on Tuesday, which Reuters linked to the result2.

The constructive point is simple. A contested constitutional question was taken out of rallies and put into a count, run by an elections office, with numbers anyone can check3. It does not end the argument. It ends this round of it.

What to watch

  • Elections Alberta's official count; the figures here are the reported count with about 73% reporting.
  • Results of the other nine referendum questions, still being tallied.
  • Canada's foreign-influence commissioner, whose office contacted a separatist group before the vote.

A referendum is a very large, very boring receipt. That is its virtue.

At the first questions session since the 17 October violence, Sébastien Lecornu conceded that Saturday's police set-up was too static and not mobile enough. A minister admitting a fault to elected members is the system doing its job.

The National Assembly held its first questions-to-the-government session on Tuesday since the violence around the 17 October protests1.

The admission. Answering the MoDem group leader Marc Fesneau, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said Saturday’s arrangement had been “trop statique et pas assez mobile”, too static and not mobile enough1. He promised a “massif” security deployment for the protests called for Saturday 24 October and said the interior minister had already instructed prefectures1. The doctrine, he said, would be revised in volume, forces committed, reactivity and mobility1.

The exchange. Manuel Bompard of La France insoumise called the government’s handling a “véritable fiasco”; Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez replied: “J’ai ma part de responsabilités, vous avez la vôtre” — I have my share of responsibility, you have yours1. The RN deputy Laure Lavalette asked whether the minister could maintain the state’s legal authority1.

The calendar. Lecornu said on Tuesday that new curfews would be imposed where needed, after a Monday night that police said passed without major incidents2. He meets union officials on Wednesday and political parties on Thursday, and France’s main unions have not backed Saturday’s protest call2.

Nothing in the session settled who is to blame for Saturday. That is not its job. Its job is to put the government’s account, and its admissions, on the record where they can be checked against what happens on the 24th.

What to watch

  • Wednesday's meeting between the prime minister and union officials, and Thursday's talks with political parties.
  • Whether the revised policing doctrine is published or only described.

No cabinet meeting this week. The government spokesman says the work goes on and the calendar to December is full.

The federal government has suspended its usually weekly cabinet meetings for the autumn school holidays, government spokesman Stefan Kornelius told the dts news agency on Monday1. He said cabinet planning to the end of the year was “vollgepackt”, packed, and that work on cabinet business and legislation continued without interruption1.

The pause itself is not new, Kornelius said1. The report notes that the Merz government held no such pause last year, and that in autumn 2024 the Scholz government held no cabinet meeting for two weeks running, shortly before that coalition broke up; the agency adds that it draws no connection between the two1.

The constructive reading is the dull one. A cabinet meeting is the moment decisions are formally taken, not the moment work is done. The test of the pause is simple and checkable: how many drafts reach the cabinet table in the first sessions after the holidays.

In a 462-person experiment, an AI that affirmed and personalised its replies won more advice-taking. It won it when its advice was right and when it was wrong.

Three researchers in Turkey ran a randomised experiment with 462 working adults who had used generative AI in the previous three months1. Each made six judgements about which of two countries had the larger population, then saw an AI recommend the other one1.

Half saw a neutral message. The other half saw a package that acknowledged their view, called their first answer understandable, personalised the wording and was somewhat longer1.

Result. When participants had been right and the AI was therefore wrong, 24.2% switched in the neutral condition and 32.3% with the warm package1. When they had been wrong and the AI was right, switching rose from 26.3% to 39.4%1. The warm framing raised the odds of following bad advice by about half (odds ratio 1.507) and good advice by about four-fifths (1.800); the difference between those two effects was not statistically reliable1.

The honest limits. The authors stress that the AI’s correctness depended on each person’s first answer rather than being randomised, so the study cannot say whether warmth makes people better or worse at telling good advice from bad1. The warm package also bundled several features at once1. People with higher self-reported AI literacy reported checking more and suspending judgement less; that is an association, not a cure1.

The plain reading: a friendly tone made the machine more persuasive in both directions. Persuasive is not the same as reliable, and the only thing that separates them is checking.

What to watch

  • A follow-up design in which the AI's accuracy is varied independently, which the authors themselves say is needed.
  • Your own habit: whether you check an answer more when it agrees with you, or less.
Desk comment

Being understood is pleasant. It is not evidence.

Psychology & Behaviour · Short item · ePaper extra, added 17:30 CEST

A ten-country survey of 2,683 people found widespread exposure to deepfakes and scams, and a confidence that the authors treat with caution.

Researchers in Serbia surveyed 2,683 people in ten countries, including Germany, France and the United States, through the Prolific platform between January and June 20261. 60.8% said they had encountered deepfake content and 25.0% were unsure; 36.7% said they had been targeted by an online scam1. About half, 49.9%, rated their own ability to identify manipulated content as high or very high1. The authors note that previous research suggests subjective confidence often exceeds actual detection accuracy1.

In their model, people who reported more exposure to AI manipulation reported more fear of AI fraud, and fear went with more privacy-protective behaviour such as sharing less and checking sites before giving data1. Exposure itself was not directly linked to trust in AI governance1.

The data are cross-sectional and the sample is highly educated and online, so none of this shows cause and effect1. The usable point is modest. Worry that ends in a habit, such as checking a sender, is worth more than worry that ends in a feeling.

Tehran restated that it is ready to open Hormuz, and Washington said crude exports are back near four-fifths of pre-war. The gasoil price shows a product shortage that no statement has fixed.

Iran's first vice-president Mohammad Reza Aref; US Energy Secretary Chris Wright (on Fox News), as reported by HeizOel24, 19–20 October 2026
Print≈ two-thirds

Diesel in Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Antwerp cost about two-thirds more than crude on Friday, and gasoil for November delivery traded more than 3% above December.

Gasoil and diesel market prices: ICE gasoil, ARA diesel, as reported by HeizOel24, 16–20 October 2026

The podium. On Monday Iran formally restated that it is ready to open the Strait of Hormuz, while its first vice-president, Mohammad Reza Aref, said Tehran had “keine andere Wahl”, no other choice, than to enforce control over it, in HeizOel24’s German rendering1. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said on Fox News that oil exports through the strait had recovered to about 80% of pre-war levels, while shipments of refined products remained impaired1.

The print. The market’s own figures tell a narrower story. HeizOel24 reports that diesel in the Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp hub cost about two-thirds more than crude on Friday1. Gasoil, the exchange basis for both diesel and heating oil, closed Monday about 2.5% above Friday while Brent closed slightly lower, and gasoil for November delivery cost more than 3% more than December1. A drone strike forced Russia’s Ryazan refinery to shut its main distillation unit; the plant makes about 3.8 million tonnes of diesel a year, according to Reuters as cited by HeizOel241.

The gap. Both statements can be accurate. Crude can flow at four-fifths of normal while the products made from it stay short, because refineries and tankers are a separate bottleneck. Germany’s producer prices already show it: light heating oil was 91.8% dearer in September than a year earlier, motor fuels 48.6%3.

Verdict. Read the strait statements as news about crude. Read the gasoil curve as news about your tank. Until near-dated gasoil stops costing more than later gasoil, the shortage is current1.

What to watch

  • Wednesday's official US inventory report from the Energy Department; the API estimate comes late on Tuesday.
  • The November–December gasoil gap. While near-dated fuel costs more than later fuel, the shortage is still now, not later.
  • Whether Saudi Arabia confirms or denies the Houthi claims on Yanbu and Shaybah.
Desk comment

Crude moves on headlines. Diesel moves on tanks.

Finland's central-bank governor told parliamentarians that rising long-term rates are slowing growth and inflation. His test for the next move is narrow and checkable: whether the energy shock spreads.

Olli Rehn, who governs the Bank of Finland and sits on the ECB’s Governing Council, spoke to a Finnish parliamentary committee on Tuesday. His main sentence was plain: “The recent rise in long-term interest rates is dampening growth and slowing inflation”1.

He put the rise in borrowing costs down to inflation expectations, uncertainty and competition for capital, naming governments with large deficits and companies investing in artificial intelligence. “The increased demand for capital has driven up its price,” he said1. Higher market rates are already feeding into bank lending rates for households and firms, he added1.

The test. Rehn said future decisions will depend “on whether we see broader effects on inflation”, and that so far they have not appeared1. He pointed to moderate, slowing wage growth and a labour market that is no longer tight, both unlike 20221. On the same morning, Germany’s producer prices excluding energy rose just 0.1% on the month3, which fits his description rather than contradicting it.

The caveat he chose. He also said a return to the “old normal” in energy markets “seems increasingly unlikely”1. That is not a forecast of rates. It is a governor warning that the energy bill may stay high even if inflation outside it stays calm.

Elsewhere, the euro held at about $1.12 and the dollar index slipped 0.1%2. Meeting by meeting, Rehn said, is “particularly justified” now1. It is also the only honest way to run a committee when the oil price is set by missiles.

What to watch

  • Euro-area core inflation in the next flash estimate: Rehn's own condition is 'broader effects', which is where they would show.
  • Bank lending rates for households, which Rehn said are already being pushed up by market rates.
  • German producer prices excluding energy, up 0.1% on the month in September.
Desk comment

A central banker who names his test has done you a favour: you can mark it yourself.

The factory-gate index says +91.8%, the consumer index +74.9%, and Tuesday's portal average €1.73 a litre against about 90 cents a year ago. They measure different moments of the same delivery.

On Tuesday the same litre of heating oil appeared in three official or semi-official places, with three different numbers. None is wrong.

At the factory gate. Destatis said light heating oil was 91.8% dearer in September than a year earlier, measured at the producer level on the 15th of the month, excluding VAT1.

In the shop. The consumer-price index for September put heating oil 74.9% above a year earlier, and 18.5% above August2. Consumer prices include taxes and delivery margins that move more slowly than the commodity, which is why the percentage is smaller even though the euros are larger.

At your door, today. HeizOel24 put Tuesday’s national average at €1.73 a litre for a 3,000-litre order, about 2.5 cents up on Monday, and said that a year ago the same litre cost around 90 cents3. It rose in every Land, by between almost two and just over four cents3. Hamburg was cheapest at €1.68, North Rhine-Westphalia dearest at €1.783.

Why it jumped today. Gasoil, the exchange basis for heating oil and diesel, rose faster than crude because diesel is short worldwide3. And the Rhine gauge at Kaub read 21 cm on Tuesday morning, which keeps barge deliveries to the west and south difficult3.

Nothing here is advice to order, wait or switch. The useful habit is to know which of the three numbers you are looking at. Only the written offer for your address is a price.

What to watch

  • The Rhine at Kaub: 21 cm on Tuesday morning, with little improvement expected before Saturday. Low water makes barge deliveries dearer in the west and south.
  • Whether the portal average gives back Tuesday's 2.5 cents.
Desk comment

One litre, three thermometers. Use the one with your postcode on it.

Elice, a Korean AI data-centre company, priced at the top of its range and fell about 30% on its first morning. Order books measure enthusiasm; the first trade measures price.

Elice was founded in 2015 as an AI education platform and now also runs cloud services and data centres built on graphics processors1. Its book-building drew 2,367 institutions and was 388.84 times subscribed; the retail tranche was 408.26 times subscribed and attracted more than 10 trillion won in deposits1. It priced at 90,500 won, the top of its range1.

At 9:30 on Tuesday morning in Seoul the stock traded at 63,000 won, 30.39% below the offering price, and touched 61,000 won1. The market value implied at pricing, about 1 trillion won, fell to around 700 billion1.

The wider tape. The same morning, Samsung Electronics closed 3% lower and SK Hynix 4.2% lower, and the Kospi fell 3.7% to its lowest close since 7 April2. In Europe, Siltronic fell 6%, Soitec 5.7%, Aixtron 5.2%, and ASML 3%2. A State Street strategist put the investor question in one line: whether “every dollar of AI capex earns more than it costs to finance”3.

The company’s own numbers. Elice reported revenue of 39.5 billion won and operating profit of 5.2 billion won last year, and 34.4 billion won of revenue with 10.3 billion won of operating profit in the first half of 20261. Those are real figures for a real business. They were simply worth less on Tuesday than the subscription queue had suggested.

The lesson is not that AI is a bubble or that it is not. It is that an oversubscribed order book is a count of people who wanted an allocation at a fixed price. The first trade is the first time anyone has to agree on a price with someone who is selling.

What to watch

  • Quarterly results from Netflix and SpaceX after the US close on Tuesday, and Meta and Tesla later in the week.
  • Elice's first reported quarter as a listed company, against first-half 2026 revenue of 34.4 billion won.

A warm machine got followed when it was wrong, too

In a 462-person experiment, an AI that affirmed and personalised its replies won more advice-taking. It won it when its advice was right and when it was wrong.

Three researchers in Turkey ran a randomised experiment with 462 working adults who had used generative AI in the previous three months1. Each made six judgements about which of two countries had the larger population, then saw an AI recommend the other one1.

Half saw a neutral message. The other half saw a package that acknowledged their view, called their first answer understandable, personalised the wording and was somewhat longer1.

Result. When participants had been right and the AI was therefore wrong, 24.2% switched in the neutral condition and 32.3% with the warm package1. When they had been wrong and the AI was right, switching rose from 26.3% to 39.4%1. The warm framing raised the odds of following bad advice by about half (odds ratio 1.507) and good advice by about four-fifths (1.800); the difference between those two effects was not statistically reliable1.

The honest limits. The authors stress that the AI’s correctness depended on each person’s first answer rather than being randomised, so the study cannot say whether warmth makes people better or worse at telling good advice from bad1. The warm package also bundled several features at once1. People with higher self-reported AI literacy reported checking more and suspending judgement less; that is an association, not a cure1.

The plain reading: a friendly tone made the machine more persuasive in both directions. Persuasive is not the same as reliable, and the only thing that separates them is checking.
