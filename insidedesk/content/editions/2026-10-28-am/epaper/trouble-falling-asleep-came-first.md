Researchers from Jining Medical University and partners followed 7,054 middle-aged and older residents of three cities in Shandong, China, surveying them twice, 1 to 1.5 years apart, between April 2021 and July 2024 [1]. Of ten links between sleep and mood that held up, seven ran from sleep-related symptoms to later depressive symptoms and three the other way [1]. The strongest: difficulty falling asleep and difficulty staying asleep predicted later sleep problems on the depression questionnaire, and daytime lethargy predicted later low energy [1]. The authors are careful: these are exploratory associations, "not causal or within-person temporal effects" [1]. Still, if your nights have gone wrong for weeks, the study is one more reason to mention it to a doctor rather than wait for the mood to follow.
