Brent crude rose $1.48, or 1.3%, to $115.36 a barrel at 02:40 CET on Wednesday, CNBC reports [1]. US crude, WTI, rose $1.22 to $101.12 [1]. The trigger was a claim: Yemen's Houthis said on Tuesday they had hit Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery, a 550,000-barrel-a-day plant, with a missile. Saudi authorities had not immediately confirmed it [1]. Bloomberg adds a second driver: a Wall Street Journal report that the US told several Gulf countries to be prepared for a short, intense military campaign after the midterm elections [2].

Hold the two halves of the week together. On Tuesday oil fell nearly $2 as talks in the region eased supply worries; on Wednesday it took most of that back [1]. A market that swings on claims and reports is a market pricing fear, not barrels.

The barrels themselves tell a calmer story. Analysts polled by Reuters expect US crude stockpiles to have risen by about 15,000 barrels in the week to 23 October [1]. The OPEC+ group is expected to keep its December output targets unchanged on Sunday, three people with knowledge of the matter told Reuters, and most of its seven core members are producing below their targets [1]. Russia's crude exports from its Baltic and Black Sea ports could fall next month as its refiners make more diesel, traders said [1].

For you, the useful number arrives at the pump, not on the screen. Today's headline price is a reaction to a statement nobody has yet verified. Wait for the Saudi side, and for Wednesday's US inventory figures. Nothing here is investment advice.
