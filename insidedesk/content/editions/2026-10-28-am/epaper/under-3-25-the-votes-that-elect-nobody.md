Israel's 3.25% threshold is a quiet rule with loud effects. On the partial official count, at about 37% of votes counted, the Reservists-Economic Party of Yoaz Hendel and Yaron Zelekha had 2.72% and Ofer Winter's Amcha Yisrael 1.77%; both are out unless the remaining ballots change things [1]. Ra'am, at 3.81%, is in with four seats [1].

Ynet reports that record turnout "left little room for smaller parties" [2]. The Haredi Public Party of Moti Leitner, which had momentum on social media, also fell short in the exit polls [2].

The blame has started. Winter had said he would recommend Netanyahu for prime minister, and Netanyahu campaigned hard to push him out; Ynet writes that staying in "appears to have cost Netanyahu's bloc tens of thousands of votes" [2]. Winter's camp argues the reverse, that without him Hendel and Zelekha would have crossed the line and given the anti-Netanyahu side four more seats [2]. Both claims are about votes that did not happen, so neither can be checked.

What can be checked is still to come. Hendel says he is counting on the double-envelope ballots, which include soldiers' votes: "By the end of the week, we'll know the results from IDF polling stations" [2]. A list that crosses 3.25% does not just win its own seats; it changes how all the others are shared out. That is why the last envelopes matter more than the first speeches.
