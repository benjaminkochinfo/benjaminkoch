The Trump administration is considering lifting sanctions on the Russian Direct Investment Fund (RDIF), sources told The New York Times [1]. Russia wants the relief so the fund can take part in supplying Russian diesel to the United States [1]. No decision has been reported.

The fund's head, Kirill Dmitriev, is a central figure in the US-Russia talks and leads the Kremlin's push for joint business ventures [1]. The Biden administration sanctioned RDIF on 28 February 2022, four days after the full-scale invasion. Treasury said then that, while officially a sovereign wealth fund, it was "widely considered a slush fund" for Vladimir Putin [1].

The diesel deal itself has already cost something. The Guardian notes that Donald Trump "stunned allies" by agreeing to buy Russian diesel, pushing US-Ukraine ties to their lowest point in 18 months [2].

Here the dull machinery is worth watching closely. One source told the Times that Treasury is handling RDIF's application under its standard procedures, and that the fund must show the reasons it was sanctioned no longer apply [1]. That is the right test, and it leaves a record. If relief comes, the reasoning should be published, so you can judge whether the facts changed or only the customer did.
