The New Zealand dollar slumped almost 2% against the Australian dollar on Wednesday, its biggest fall since 2017, after the Reserve Bank of New Zealand surprisingly left interest rates unchanged, Bloomberg reports [1]. A central bank that holds when markets expected it to act is still making a decision, and the currency priced it within minutes.

In Japan, the 10-year government bond yield rose three basis points to 2.825% [1]. Australia's 10-year rose five points to 5.21%, and the US 10-year two points to 5.02% [1]. The yen was little changed at 157.98 per dollar, and the euro at $1.1123 [1]. A Bloomberg gauge of the dollar edged higher for a fourth straight day [1].

The Bank of Japan and the European Central Bank decide later this week, after the Federal Reserve tonight [1]. Three central banks, all expected to hold, and yet the long yields are rising across the Pacific. Desk arithmetic: the Australian 10-year now yields about 0.19 points more than the American one.

For you, the lesson of Wellington is modest and useful. "Expected" is a word that markets use about central banks, not one central banks use about themselves. Gold slipped 0.4% to $4,044.59 an ounce, and bitcoin rose 0.5% to $81,102.84 [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
