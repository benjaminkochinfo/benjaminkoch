Ceconomy, which owns MediaMarkt and Saturn, published preliminary figures for its 2025/26 financial year on Tuesday: revenue of about €24 billion, up 5.3% after currency and portfolio adjustments, and adjusted operating profit (EBIT) of about €515 million, above its own target of €500 million and up from €406 million a year earlier, Retail-News reports [1]. Between July and September, adjusted sales grew 6.5%, and 6.9% on comparable floor space [1]. Household appliances and IT and mobile devices drove the final quarter [1].

**The ledger line.** Desk arithmetic: €515 million of operating profit on €24 billion of sales is a margin of about 2.1%. Of every €100 you hand over at the till for a fridge or a phone, a little over two euros is left as operating profit; the rest pays for the goods, the staff, the rent and the logistics. That is what electronics retailing looks like when it is going well.

**The shop count.** Across Europe the group ran 1,068 stores at year-end, one more than a year before [1]. In Germany the number fell from 403 to 390: one opening, 14 closures [1]. Italy went from 145 to 153, Spain from 111 to 113, and Turkey from 102 to 106 [1]. About 50,000 people work for the group [1].

The company wants services, its online marketplace and retail advertising to bring in more than half of gross profit by 2028/29, and targets adjusted EBIT of about €800 million by then [1]. Those are its own targets. The audited figures follow on 14 December [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
