On Monday Microsoft's chief people officer, Amy Coleman, told staff that the company is introducing a "Voluntary Exit Offer" for eligible employees in participating teams in Canada, India, the United Kingdom and the United States, at level 70 and below [1]. It is time-limited, it comes "with financial support and career planning resources", and some organisations are offering it while others are not [1]. Around 60,000 people are eligible, according to a person familiar with the matter cited by CNBC, about 27% of a workforce of 223,000 [2].

**The payroll line.** This is the second such programme this year. In April, Microsoft offered its first voluntary retirement programme, for which about 7% of US employees were eligible; more than 30% of those eligible took it, the company said in July [2]. In July it also announced 4,800 layoffs [2]. The new offer reaches higher up the ladder, to vice-president level, and Microsoft has no target in mind this time, CNBC's source said [2]. Some marketing, sales and engineering teams are not taking part [2]. Desk arithmetic: 60,000 eligible is not 60,000 leaving. The honest unit here is the offer, not the departure.

**The capital line.** The same CNBC report puts the offer beside the spending. Microsoft said in July that capital expenditure for the September quarter should exceed $50 billion; a year earlier, capital spending and finance leases came to $34.9 billion [2]. Free cash flow in the previous quarter was $19.64 billion, down 23% on a year earlier, while Alphabet and Amazon reported negative free cash flow [2]. Desk arithmetic: if the quarter comes in at $50 billion, that is about 43% more than a year ago.

You will hear this described as "AI replacing workers". The documents say something duller and more useful. The memo talks about giving participating organisations "another way to adapt their workforce to changing needs" [1]; the money is going into data centres [2]. A company can choose how it changes shape. A voluntary offer, with a deadline and a cheque, is the more decent of the available ways, and the one that leaves the decision with the person whose job it is.

Wednesday's results will show the building bill. The take-up of the offer will show, in December, how many people decided the door was worth walking through. Nothing here is investment advice.
