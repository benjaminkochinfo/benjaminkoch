The Kospi, Seoul's benchmark index, jumped 6.6% on Wednesday, Bloomberg reports, led by a 10% rise in SK Hynix after the memory-chip maker said it would return about $45 billion more to shareholders as its profit rose sevenfold [1]. Samsung Electronics gained 9.5% and Kioxia 16% [1]. Al Jazeera puts SK Hynix's third-quarter operating profit at 83.33 trillion won, about $62.8 billion, up about 630% on the year [2]. The broader MSCI Asia Pacific index added 1.4% [1].

**A different kind of AI news.** Much of this week's enthusiasm rests on plans. Anthropic filed to list on the Nasdaq, reporting a net loss of $80.7 billion in the first half of 2026 on $16.3 billion of revenue [2]. Meta's upsized $25 billion share sale drew demand for about five times the shares on offer, people familiar with the offering told Bloomberg, as the company seeks $98 billion for its AI spending [1]. SK Hynix is the other kind: a supplier that has already been paid and is handing cash back. Desk arithmetic: Anthropic's half-year loss is about five times its revenue; SK Hynix's quarter is a profit.

**Wall Street set the tone.** On Tuesday the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.18%, the S&P 500 0.6% and the Dow 0.03% [2]. AMD gained 5.45%, Broadcom 4% and Intel 3.61% [2]. Nasdaq 100 futures added 0.4% in Asian hours [1].

**The test comes tonight.** Microsoft and Alphabet report on Wednesday, Amazon on Thursday and Apple on Monday [2]. Investors want to see whether the hundreds of billions poured into AI infrastructure pay off [2]. Gartner forecasts global AI investment at $2.7 trillion this year and $3.6 trillion in 2027 [2]. A forecast of spending is not a forecast of returns. The invoices that SK Hynix has already collected are the closest thing to a return the boom has printed so far.

Not everything rose: Euro Stoxx 50 futures were down 0.2% [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
