Late last night Channel 13's exit poll had the two blocs tied on 51 seats each and Channel 12 had Netanyahu's bloc ahead, 52 to 49 [2]. This morning's numbers say something else again, and neither set is the result. With about 37.4% of votes counted at 04:49 CET, the Central Elections Committee's partial tally gave Benjamin Netanyahu's governing bloc 57 of the Knesset's 120 seats, the "change bloc" 47 and the Arab-led parties 16 [1]. An hour earlier, at 31.2% counted, the split was 59, 46 and 15 [1]. The committee stresses the figures are preliminary and may change [1].

By party, Likud leads on 28, Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar has 21 and the Joint List 12 [1]. Together has 11, United Torah Judaism and Yisrael Beytenu eight each, and the Democrats, Otzma Yehudit, Shas and the Religious Zionist-Zehut list seven each [1]. Ra'am is in with 3.81% of the vote, four seats; the threshold is 3.25% [1].

Desk arithmetic: 47 plus 16 is 63, so on these partial numbers the parties outside Netanyahu's bloc add up to more than 61. But a sum is not a coalition. Whether those parties can agree to govern together is what the coming talks are for, and Netanyahu's 57 is four short of 61 [1][2].

Both camps spoke as if the night were over. "The old era is over," Eisenkot told supporters; Netanyahu said "the right bloc has a majority" [2]. Turnout was 77.7%, the highest since 1999 and 7.1 points above 2022, and the committee says Arab turnout reached 80% [1]. Two new lists, the Reservists-Economic Party (2.72%) and Amcha Yisrael (1.77%), are below the threshold so far [1], and Hendel is counting on the double-envelope ballots that include soldiers' votes [3].

What happens next is procedure, not rhetoric. Once the count is final, President Isaac Herzog consults party leaders and nominates the candidate he thinks most likely to form a government, who then has up to 42 days [2]. Until a coalition is sworn in, Netanyahu's cabinet stays on as caretaker [2]. You can ignore every victory speech this week. Watch the committee's tallies instead.
