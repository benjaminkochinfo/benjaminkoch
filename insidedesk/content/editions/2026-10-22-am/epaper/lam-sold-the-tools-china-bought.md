Lam Research, which makes the equipment chip factories use to deposit and etch the layers of a semiconductor, reported revenue of $8.22 billion for the quarter to 27 September, up 22% on the previous quarter [1]. Chief executive Tim Archer called it a record for revenue, profit and earnings per share [1]. Desk arithmetic: against $5.32 billion a year earlier, revenue rose about 54%, and sales of new systems rose from $3.55 billion to $5.73 billion [1].

The geography is the line to read. China accounted for 35% of revenue, Korea 20%, Taiwan 18%, Japan 9%, the United States 8% and Europe 4% [1]. Desk arithmetic: 35% is about $2.9 billion of equipment in one quarter. Lam's own risk list names export controls, entity listings and tariffs as things that could limit its sales [1].

For the quarter to December, Lam guided to revenue of $8.60 billion, plus or minus $400 million, slightly above the $8.53 billion analysts expected, according to CNBC [1][2]. The shares still fell more than 5% after hours [2]. A record quarter is a fact; a share price is an opinion about the next one. Nothing here is advice.
