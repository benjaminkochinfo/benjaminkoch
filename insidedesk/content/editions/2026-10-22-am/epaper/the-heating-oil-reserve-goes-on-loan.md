The US Department of Energy on Wednesday invited bids for an exchange of up to 315,000 barrels of ultra-low sulphur fuel oil from the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve, held in South Portland, Maine, Boston and New Haven [1]. Reuters puts that at nearly a third of the one-million-barrel reserve [2].

The terms are the interesting part. Companies that borrow must return the same volume plus a premium, so the reserve is refilled before the 2027–28 heating season [1]. Offers run from 25,000 to 30,000 barrels, no site gives up more than 150,000, bids close at 11:00 Central Time on Tuesday 27 October, and deliveries are due by 13 November [1]. Energy Secretary Chris Wright called it "a temporary measure to solve a temporary supply disruption" [1].

The same day's inventory count explains the hurry only partly. US distillate stocks, which include diesel and heating oil, rose 1.68 million barrels to 104.3 million in the week to 16 October, against expectations of a fall [2]. A loan of barrels moves oil to where the tanks are short; it does not change how much oil there is. That is the honest size of the measure, and also its virtue: it can be repaid.
