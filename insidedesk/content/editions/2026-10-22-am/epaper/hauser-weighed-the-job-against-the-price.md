Central bankers rarely say "I don't know" in public. Andrew Hauser, deputy governor of the Reserve Bank of Australia, came close on Wednesday. Speaking at a Citi conference in Sydney, he said the inflation outlook looked "materially worse", partly because of oil, and that he was unsure whether rates would need to rise again next month [1].

Both sides of the ledger were on the table. "The monetary policy is working, labour market is easing, consumption growth is quite tepid," he said, "but there are still pockets of growth and the inflation outlook has deteriorated" [1]. Unemployment unexpectedly reached a five-year high of 4.7% in September [1].

The board meets on 2–3 November and could lift its 4.60% cash rate for a fifth time this year [1]. Markets priced about a 40% chance of a rise to 4.85%, up from 30% at the start of the week [1].

Hauser said policy cannot fix an oil shock directly, but must stop it from feeding into inflation expectations [1]. "We're in a world of hard choices," he said [1]. A committee that names its trade-off is easier to follow than one that pretends it has none. Nothing here is advice.
