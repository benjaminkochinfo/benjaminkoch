EU negotiators agreed on Tuesday to raise the threshold in the carbon market's stability reserve from 400 million to 700 million allowances; above that line, allowances are cancelled for good [2]. The deal was announced on Wednesday [1].

It is a compromise. The Commission had proposed on 1 April to stop cancelling allowances altogether; Parliament wanted 650 million and the Council 800 million [2][3]. Both rejected the indefinite halt, and agreed in principle to discuss a gradual lowering of the threshold in the wider ETS review [2]. More than three billion allowances have been cancelled since the mechanism began in 2023, according to the Council's figures as reported by 2eu.brussels [3].

Ireland's climate minister Darragh O'Brien, for the presidency, called it "a balanced outcome" that gives industry "immediate flexibility" while "protecting the integrity of the EU carbon market" [1].

Read it plainly: the cap on emissions is untouched, nothing is released to the market automatically, and the ETS2 system for heating and road fuel is not covered [3]. A bigger reserve is a bigger shock absorber. The agreement still needs formal adoption by both institutions before it changes any law [2][3].
