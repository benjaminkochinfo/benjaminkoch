An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam on Wednesday left eight people injured, Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said [1][2]. The death toll differs by report. Al Jazeera and Reuters, citing the authority, give one dead [2]. Arab News, citing it early on Thursday, gives two, though its own headline says three dead in total [1]. We report both until the authority's own figure settles it.

Earlier the same day, Saudi air defences intercepted a drone aimed at a processing facility at Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield. Falling debris killed two people, a Filipino and a Nepali, and injured two others, the energy ministry said [1]. The fire was in a contractor's vehicle outside the facility's perimeter, and "operations were unaffected", the ministry added [1]. The Saudi-led coalition said it shot down seven drones on Wednesday and four more early on Thursday [1].

It is the latest in a series of strikes on Saudi airports; an attack on Riyadh's airport on 10 October killed 12 people [2]. The same Wednesday, the US State Department ordered diplomats and family members to depart from its missions in Saudi Arabia [2].

The useful line for energy markets is the dull one: operations unaffected. The useful line for everyone else is the list of names the ministry did not give, only nationalities: a Filipino and a Nepali dead, a Pakistani and an Indian injured [1]. The people who keep the machinery running are the first to carry its risk.
