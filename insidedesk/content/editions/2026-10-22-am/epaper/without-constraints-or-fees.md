President Emmanuel Macron's tour of the Gulf, which took in Kuwait, the UAE, Bahrain and Saudi Arabia, closed in Doha with a joint statement with Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani [1]. The two leaders stressed the importance of restoring freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz "without constraints or fees" [1]. They also "condemned Iranian military attacks against countries in the region", calling them a serious breach of international law [1].

The last two words of that first phrase do the work. A strait that reopens with a toll, or with a permit system run by one coastal state, is not the strait the shipping and insurance contracts were written for. Writing "fees" into a statement signed by a mediator, Qatar, as well as a European power tells you which version both are ready to defend in negotiations.

The rest of the text reads like a to-do list: Israeli withdrawal from Gaza "in accordance with the agreed arrangements", aid "without conditions or restrictions", opposition to any annexation of the West Bank, and backing for Lebanon's framework to disarm Hezbollah [1]. Qatar and France also agreed to expand defence, security and economic cooperation [1].

A joint statement is not a reopening. It is the clause both sides will point to when one is drafted.
