Nigeria's aviation minister, Festus Keyamo, has directed the civil aviation authority to place 46 people on a no-fly list for at least 21 days, Punch reports [1]. They are the 44 people who had contact with the country's first Bundibugyo Ebola patient at the Abuja hospital where she was treated after falling ill, plus two of her domestic staff [1]. The 21 days match the period within which symptoms normally appear [1]. Airlines are to be notified, and airports are installing temperature-screening cameras [1].

The patient's condition has worsened, a laboratory director at the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control told an emergency meeting on Wednesday, according to Punch [1]. The incident manager said the contacts were "majorly healthcare workers and support staff of the hospital" [1].

The calmer numbers came from the states. Ogun said no case had been recorded as of 21 October and that 111 persons of interest had been monitored and discharged after completing surveillance [1]. Anambra said 33 of its 40 had completed monitoring and been cleared, with seven still under follow-up [1]. Health minister Muhammad Pate asked Nigerians not to "fall into a trap of hysteria or panic" [1].

A no-fly list sounds dramatic. In practice it is a spreadsheet with an end date.
