Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu receives France's parties at Matignon early on Thursday afternoon. The interior and justice ministers, Laurent Nuñez and Gérald Darmanin, will be there, along with the heads of the intelligence services and the police [1]. In his letter of invitation he promised "transparency" about how 17 October unfolded, including the "difficulties encountered", so that "everyone can have the same information" [1].

The opposition calls it a "convocation" [1]. All invited parties are expected to attend, but not all their leaders: neither Jordan Bardella nor Marine Le Pen will be there for the RN. Horizons sends Christophe Béchu, the MoDem Marc Fesneau, and LFI Manuel Bompard [1]. Bompard said he will bring the unions' demands, from fuel-price caps to a higher minimum wage [1]. The Greens' Cyrielle Chatelain put it bluntly: "We are not going there to take a lesson" [1].

Mr Lecornu will also meet the mayors of Paris, Lyon and Marseille ahead of Saturday's mobilisation [1]. And the markets are watching: France's ten-year borrowing rate closed above 5% on Wednesday, for the first time since 2002, AFP reports [1].

A meeting where every side hears the same briefing is not reconciliation. It is a shared set of facts, and that comes first. Nothing here is advice.
