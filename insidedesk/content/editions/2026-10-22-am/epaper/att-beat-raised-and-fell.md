On paper, Wednesday was a good day for AT&T. Revenue rose 5.3% to $32.3 billion, adjusted earnings per share were $0.65 against $0.54 a year earlier, the company added 379,000 postpaid phone customers and a record 769,000 internet customers, and it lifted its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to about $2.40 [1]. The shares closed 6.5% lower anyway [2]. T-Mobile US fell 2.5% and Verizon 2.2% [2].

The reason sat in another company's results. SpaceX confirmed it plans to turn Starlink into a major US mobile carrier and is buying low-band spectrum to do it [3]; its results and the spectrum purchase "fanned fears" of a new telecoms competitor, Huntington Bank's Marc Dizard told Reuters [2].

AT&T's chief executive, John Stankey, answered with arithmetic. He told analysts not to "over-rotate on what I would call one carrier of low-band spectrum", noting that AT&T runs six to eight such carriers in a typical city and that 40–45% of Chicago's mobile traffic starts indoors, where satellite signals struggle [3].

Both can be true: a threat can be years away and still priced tonight. What the quarter shows is that the existing network is earning more, not less. Nothing here is advice.
