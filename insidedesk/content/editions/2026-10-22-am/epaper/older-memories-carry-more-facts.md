In a University of East Anglia study of 37 older and 37 younger adults, published in Psychology and Aging, both groups recalled one-off events equally accurately, 81% against 80% [1]. But only 66% of the older group's details were specific to the episode, against 79% for the young; general facts filled the gap [1]. Because that pattern held under every instruction, the authors read it as a meaningful narrative style, not compensation for failing memory [1]. The older group also rated their memories as more vivid [1]. The sample was small and not preregistered [1]. Fewer details, it seems, need not mean less memory.
