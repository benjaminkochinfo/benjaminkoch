Deutsche Post plans to charge €1.15 for a standard domestic letter from 1 January 2027, up from 95 cents, subject to the Bundesnetzagentur's final approval [1]. A postcard also goes to €1.15, the Kompaktbrief from €1.10 to €1.25, the Großbrief from €1.80 to €2.35 and the Maxibrief from €2.90 to €5.00 [1]. Registered-mail surcharges and private PO box rents stay unchanged [1]. The new prices are meant to last two years, within a price-increase margin of 20.35% for letters that the regulator has signalled [1].

Desk arithmetic: the standard letter rises 21%; the Maxibrief 72%, though it may then weigh up to two kilograms [1]. A household that posts ten standard letters a month pays €2 more.

The company's own estimate is milder: households spend a little over €4 a month on letters and parcels, per the Federal Statistical Office, and it puts the extra at about 75 cents a month [1]. Its reasons are the dull ones: falling letter volumes and a nationwide network of post boxes and branches whose cost is spread over fewer letters [1]. One small gain: from January a Päckchen comes with tracking and liability up to €20, though its weight limit drops to one kilogram [1]. Nothing here is advice.
