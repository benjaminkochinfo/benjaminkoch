Germany's day-ahead power auction cleared Thursday's delivery day at an average of €148.61 per megawatt hour, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD data [1]. Wednesday's delivery day had cleared at €211.94 [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is a fall of about 30% in a single day, and the cheapest day since 11 October (€122.31) [1].

**What changed.** Not gas, not a summit, not a speech. The forecasts that the auction runs on changed. SMARD's day-ahead forecast for Thursday puts onshore wind at about 634 gigawatt hours, against about 237 for Wednesday; wind and solar together come to about 920 GWh, against about 421 [2]. Desk arithmetic: forecast wind and solar cover roughly 70% of Thursday's forecast consumption of about 1,319 GWh, against roughly 31% on Wednesday [2]. When more hours are met by wind, fewer are set by a gas plant, and the average falls.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-22oct]]

**What did not change.** One windy day does not make a cheap month. Over 1 to 22 October the daily price has averaged €168.96, against €95.54 for the same days of 2025 [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 77% higher. September averaged €144.82 [1]. The chart's shape is the one this desk showed yesterday: a calm spring and early summer, then an autumn that costs more [1].

**Why it matters to you.** Most households do not buy at the day-ahead price; they buy through a supplier that hedges months ahead. What the auction shows is the cost of the marginal hour, and how much of that the weather can still take off. The case for grids and interconnectors rests on exactly this: a windy Thursday in the north is worth more if the power can travel. Nothing here is advice.
