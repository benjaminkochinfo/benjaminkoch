The Paris police prefecture has issued an order banning gatherings and demonstrations in parts of the capital on Thursday from 08:00 to 20:00 [1]. The map is long and specific. It covers the Elysée, Matignon, the National Assembly and the Senate. It also covers the Champs-Elysées, the Louvre, the department-store district, the Place d'Italie, Bastille and République, the Châtelet–Halles shopping area, the Ile de la Cité and the Paris railway stations [1]. In Lyon, the Rhône prefect banned a march planned for Thursday afternoon by the student union Unef and the NPA [1]. Its route would have ended at Place Bellecour, where violence broke out on Saturday and a tourist office branch was set on fire [1].

**The review.** At 10:00 President Macron chairs a security council with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu and the intelligence services [2]. The Elysée says it will cover intelligence, anticipation and security plans for the coming weeks, "while going back over the dysfunctions" that led to last weekend's events [2]. That is an unusual sentence for a presidency to publish. It admits that something in the machinery failed, and it commits to looking at it. Speaking in Qatar on Wednesday, Mr Macron called the meeting a chance to draw up a "compte rendu", an account, of the weekend [2].

**Saturday.** The next test is 24 October. The government has promised a large police presence, armoured vehicles and the elite GIGN, BRI and Raid units, and more bans on demonstrations are expected [3]. Shopkeepers are worried. Between 2,000 and 3,000 shops were looted or damaged last weekend, and many plan to keep their shutters down [3]. "Concern is at its highest," said Pierre Bosche of the Confédération des commerçants de France [3]. Justice minister Gérald Darmanin put the number of people jailed at 174 [3].

**For you.** If you are in Paris on Thursday, the order is about gatherings and demonstrations in named places, for twelve hours, and the main railway stations are on the list [1]. Several countries, including the United States, China and Germany, have advised their citizens in France to be careful [3]. Check the prefecture's order for the exact streets, not a national headline.

The useful document today is not the ban. It is whatever the security council admits went wrong, and whether it is written down.
