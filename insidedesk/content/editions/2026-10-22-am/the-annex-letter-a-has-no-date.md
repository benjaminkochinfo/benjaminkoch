Today's annex is the document itself: the law amending Germany's tobacco tax act, dated 19 October 2026 and published in the Federal Law Gazette, issue 2026 I No. 309, on 20 October [1]. It runs to three pages and three articles. It is signed by President Steinmeier, Chancellor Merz and Finance Minister Klingbeil [1].

**Article 3.** The clause in the headlines is one sentence: "Dieses Gesetz tritt am Tag nach der Verkündung in Kraft" ("This law enters into force on the day after its promulgation") [1]. There is no second paragraph and no separate date for the new rates [1]. So everything in the law took effect on 21 October [2].

**Why that matters: letter a.** Article 1 replaces the tariff in §2(1). For cigarettes, letters b to e each begin with a period: 1 January to 31 December 2027, then 2028, 2029, and 1 January 2030 to 14 February 2031 [1]. Letter a has no period. It sets 18.57 cents per cigarette plus 21.05% of the retail price "vorbehaltlich der Buchstaben b bis e", meaning "subject to letters b to e" [1]. Read literally, letter a covers every day that b to e do not, and that includes the rest of 2026. The 2027 rate under letter b is 14.34 cents [1]. Desk arithmetic: the text as printed would tax a cigarette this autumn 4.23 cents more than next year. Fine-cut tobacco and cigars follow the same pattern [1].

**What the text meant.** The rest of the law points one way. Article 1 also allows new tax stamps to be bought five weeks before a change in the tariff, and Article 2 sets stamp-ordering rules that run "bis zum 31. Dezember 2026" [1]. Customs gives that as the reason for early entry into force: to allow time to make and order new stamps [2]. Its notice says the old rates apply unchanged until 31 December 2026 [2].

**The repair route.** The finance ministry wants to use §61 of the federal ministries' joint rules of procedure, which covers "printing errors and obvious inaccuracies". After a law has been passed, that needs the consent of the presidents of the Bundestag and the Bundesrat [3]. The ministry says the error was already in its own draft [3]. That is why the Cologne constitutional lawyer Markus Ogorek, quoted by Die Welt, doubts the route: "In any case, this is not a mere printing error in the Federal Law Gazette," he said, because the gap was already in the government draft and parliament did not correct it; and a parliamentary amending law would be the "legally certain way" [3].

**The receipt so far.** No correction had been published in issues up to No. 313 on 21 October [3], and by 05:13 on Thursday the Gazette's site had no issue 314 [4].

**The reading.** Strip out the embarrassment, and this is a three-page law with one missing date in letter a and one clause, Article 3, too short to rescue it. The fix will also be a document. Read it when it arrives, and check which of the two routes it took.
