On Tuesday the same litre of heating oil appeared in three official or semi-official places, with three different numbers. None is wrong.

**At the factory gate.** Destatis said light heating oil was 91.8% dearer in September than a year earlier, measured at the producer level on the 15th of the month, excluding VAT [1].

**In the shop.** The consumer-price index for September put heating oil 74.9% above a year earlier, and 18.5% above August [2]. Consumer prices include taxes and delivery margins that move more slowly than the commodity, which is why the percentage is smaller even though the euros are larger.

**At your door, today.** HeizOel24 put Tuesday's national average at €1.73 a litre for a 3,000-litre order, about 2.5 cents up on Monday, and said that a year ago the same litre cost around 90 cents [3]. It rose in every Land, by between almost two and just over four cents [3]. Hamburg was cheapest at €1.68, North Rhine-Westphalia dearest at €1.78 [3].

**Why it jumped today.** Gasoil, the exchange basis for heating oil and diesel, rose faster than crude because diesel is short worldwide [3]. And the Rhine gauge at Kaub read 21 cm on Tuesday morning, which keeps barge deliveries to the west and south difficult [3].

Nothing here is advice to order, wait or switch. The useful habit is to know which of the three numbers you are looking at. Only the written offer for your address is a price.
