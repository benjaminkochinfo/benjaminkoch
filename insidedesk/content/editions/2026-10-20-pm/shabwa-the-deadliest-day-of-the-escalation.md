On Monday the Houthis fired three ballistic missiles at al-Alam camp near Ataq, in Yemen's Shabwa province, according to the Saudi-backed Yemeni armed forces [1]. The camp was a training centre for recruits to the internal security forces and police [1].

Shabwa's media office first reported 53 dead and 200 injured. On Tuesday it said an investigative committee had told the governor the toll was 73, with 14 people missing [1]. Hospitals in the province were overwhelmed and moved the most seriously wounded to Hadramout and Aden [1]. The BBC called it the deadliest attack reported since the escalation began [1].

**Both sides' words.** The head of the Presidential Leadership Council, Rashad al-Alimi, called it a "criminal attack" [1]. The Houthis' military spokesman said the missiles targeted "Saudi enemy" forces [1]. Houthi-run media reported a Saudi air strike in Saada on Sunday that killed at least 10 people [1].

**The ledger.** The government's health ministry said last week that at least 1,282 people had been killed across Yemen since 6 August; at least 19 people have been killed in Houthi attacks on Saudi airports since the start of October [1]. Separately, the Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi energy sites, which Reuters could not verify [2].
