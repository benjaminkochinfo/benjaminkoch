The morning edition said the night in Gard and Hérault came in under the warning, and argued that a closed bridge is cheap safety. That still stands for those two départements. It does not describe the rest of the day.

**What the forecaster said in advance.** On Monday Météo-France described the episode as shifting further east on Tuesday [2]. It did exactly that.

**What fell.** In its 11:30 bulletin, Météo-France moved Haute-Corse to red for rain and flooding, writing that 350 mm had already fallen in six hours and 189 mm in one hour [1]. Near Solenzara it reported almost 400 mm since the episode began, including 191 mm in an hour at the airport [1]. In the Bouches-du-Rhône it recorded 100–150 mm in 24 hours from Vitrolles to Arles, and 235 mm at Saint-Chamas [1]. Gard stayed orange because of the Vidourle and Vistre rivers [1].

**The cost.** franceinfo reported that a person was found dead in the Bouches-du-Rhône after the night's rain [1]. That is the number that matters most, and it should be read before any other.

**Why the system worked anyway.** Red is reserved for exceptional intensity. Météo-France raised it when the gauges, not the models, justified it, and said so in figures [1]. A warning service that lowers its tone in one place and raises it in another on the same day is not inconsistent. It is reading its instruments.
