Researchers in Serbia surveyed 2,683 people in ten countries, including Germany, France and the United States, through the Prolific platform between January and June 2026 [1]. 60.8% said they had encountered deepfake content and 25.0% were unsure; 36.7% said they had been targeted by an online scam [1]. About half, 49.9%, rated their own ability to identify manipulated content as high or very high [1]. The authors note that previous research suggests subjective confidence often exceeds actual detection accuracy [1].

In their model, people who reported more exposure to AI manipulation reported more fear of AI fraud, and fear went with more privacy-protective behaviour such as sharing less and checking sites before giving data [1]. Exposure itself was not directly linked to trust in AI governance [1].

The data are cross-sectional and the sample is highly educated and online, so none of this shows cause and effect [1]. The usable point is modest. Worry that ends in a habit, such as checking a sender, is worth more than worry that ends in a feeling.
