The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423.39 points, or 0.85%, to 50,018.70 on Tuesday. The S&P 500 fell 0.50% to 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq Composite 1.48% to 23,733.54 [1]. Eight of the S&P's 11 sectors closed higher; health care rose 2.88% and consumer staples 1.73%, while technology fell 2.77% [3].

The selling was precise. Analog Devices fell 17.37% and Hewlett Packard Enterprise 16.31% [2]; an index of semiconductors dropped 7.9%, and Nvidia gave back 5.9% of Monday's record gain [1]. Retailers that raised their forecasts went the other way: Abercrombie & Fitch jumped 19.7% and Dollar Tree 11.2% [1].

[[chart:us-tuesday-falls-25nov]]

The breadth was thinner than the Dow suggests. The New York Stock Exchange recorded 58 new highs and 309 new lows; the Nasdaq 49 highs and 323 lows [1]. Volume was 18.89 billion shares, a little below the 20-day average [1].

The day's lesson is not "AI is over". It is that investors read the second page of an earnings release, the one with the margin guidance, more closely the morning after a party. That is a healthy habit, and you can borrow it.
