The Bundestag holds its general debate on the 2027 budget this morning, on the chancellery's section of the plan; as usual, Chancellor Friedrich Merz speaks as the second speaker [2]. The debate is traditionally opened by the leader of the largest opposition party, this time the AfD [1].

The numbers are already on paper. The core budget allows about €560 billion of spending in 2027, a record and roughly €36 billion more than in 2026 [1]. Defence spending is planned to rise to almost €140 billion [1]. "Every third euro is financed by credit," said Lisa Paus, the Green chair of the budget committee [1]. Including the special funds, new borrowing comes to about €210 billion, ZDFheute calculates [1].

One figure was still moving this week. After a late coalition committee, the electricity tax is to fall to the European minimum for all citizens and companies next year, costing the federal government about €5.4 billion in revenue [1]. A companion law is being discussed in special committee sessions on Wednesday, so that it can be worked into a budget plan of more than 3,500 pages and passed on Friday [1].
