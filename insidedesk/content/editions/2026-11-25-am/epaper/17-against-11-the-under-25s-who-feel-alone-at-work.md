Workers under 25 are 55% more likely than older colleagues to report feeling lonely or isolated: 17% did so in the previous month, against 11% of workers aged 25 and over, according to research from the insurer YuLife reported by Workplace Journal [1]. Among under-25s, 21% reported feeling down or depressed, against 15% of older workers, and 54% said they would be unprepared if their income stopped [1]. Their average burnout scores were healthier [1].

The more useful finding is about access. Only 36% of under-25 respondents could see an employee assistance programme in the app, against 44% of older workers; among those with access, 12% of younger workers opened it, against 18% [1]. Younger workers who used wellbeing check-ins in their first six months were less likely to leave their employer's scheme within a year: 23% against 31% of those who did not [1].

Support that exists but cannot be found is a filing problem, not a culture problem. Filing problems can be fixed by Friday.
