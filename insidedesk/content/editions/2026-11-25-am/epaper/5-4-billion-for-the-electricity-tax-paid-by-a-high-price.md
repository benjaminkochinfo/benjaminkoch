The coalition has agreed that the electricity tax will fall to the European minimum next year for all citizens and companies; the federal government loses about €5.4 billion in revenue, ZDFheute reports [1].

The financing is the interesting part. The cut is to be paid for with money for renewable-energy support that is not expected to be drawn down [1]. Solar and wind producers receive a guaranteed payment from the state; the higher the market price of power, the less the state has to top up [1]. The government expects power prices to stay high because of the war in the Middle East, so it does not expect to need the whole subsidy budget [1].

Read that as a hedge with one side. If wholesale prices fall, households gain on the market price, and the subsidy bill rises again. If they stay high, the tax cut softens the blow. Either way, the companion law is being discussed in special committee sessions on Wednesday [1]. The detail worth reading is in that law, not in the coalition's press conference.
