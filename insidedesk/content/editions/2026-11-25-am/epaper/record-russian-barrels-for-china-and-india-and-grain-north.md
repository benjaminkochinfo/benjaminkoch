China and India are on track for record Russian oil imports this year, plugging a supply gap caused by the Iran war, according to tanker data and industry sources cited by Reuters [1].

Grain is moving differently. The Russian Grain Union says 1.1 million tonnes of grain left Russian ports from 1 to 20 November, 3.6 times less than a year earlier; Azov and Black Sea volumes dropped to 179,600 tonnes from 3.65 million, while Baltic loadings rose 3.4-fold, led by Ust-Luga, Lachstock Consulting's wire reports [2]. That puts more Russian cargo through the narrow Gulf of Finland, where a Russian cargo ship was struck at the weekend [2].

Sanctions and wars change routes before they change volumes. Watch the ports, not only the press releases.
