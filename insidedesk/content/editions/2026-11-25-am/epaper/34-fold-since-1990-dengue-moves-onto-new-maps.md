Reported dengue incidence rose 34-fold worldwide from 1990 to 2024, with growth fastest in recently emerging regions in Africa, the Middle East and South Asia, according to research by a team led from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, presented at the annual meeting of the American Society of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene in Maryland, CIDRAP reports [1]. The team estimated 74 million cases over the period, including 10.7 million infections not recorded in World Health Organization databases [1]. "Outbreaks have doubled in cyclical strength and become increasingly synchronised globally," the authors wrote [1].

Florida's outbreak this year is a local example: 1,071 cases, one death [1]. There is no treatment beyond supportive care, but deaths are rare [1].

The constructive part is method. The researchers filled gaps in data from 143 countries with statistical methods and turned annual totals into monthly counts [1]. Better counts come before better prevention: you cannot spray, warn or vaccinate a map you have not drawn.
