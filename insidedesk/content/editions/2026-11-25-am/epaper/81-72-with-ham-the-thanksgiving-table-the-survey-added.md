The American Farm Bureau Federation's classic Thanksgiving list has not changed since 1986, so that prices can be compared over the years [1]. But the survey also prices an expanded menu that adds boneless ham, russet potatoes and frozen green beans. That raises the total by $23.09, to $81.72 for ten [1].

Regional differences are wider on the bigger table. The expanded meal costs $76.91 in the South, $79.68 in the Midwest, $81.75 in the West and $86.62 in the Northeast [1]. Volunteer shoppers checked prices in person and online in all 50 states and Puerto Rico in the first week of November, looking for the best prices without special coupons [1].

The method is the lesson. An unchanged list tells you about prices; an added list tells you about people. Good statistics keep both, and say which is which. Supermarkets often cut whole-turkey prices in the days before the holiday, the Farm Bureau notes, so the last week can be cheaper than the survey [1].
