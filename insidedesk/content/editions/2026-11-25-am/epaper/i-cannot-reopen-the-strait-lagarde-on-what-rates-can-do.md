"I can be as good a swimmer as I am, I cannot reopen the Strait of Hormuz. That's a fact," Christine Lagarde told the New York Times DealBook Summit in a conversation recorded on 18 November and published as a podcast on 24 November [1].

The sentence is often quoted as modesty. The next one is the policy. The question, she said, is whether central banks must act to make sure the shock does not travel from commodity prices to transport, "and in particular to salaries, because that's when central banks really have to intervene" [1]. They have to act, she added, before the second-round effects are seen [1].

That is the reading for December. Dutch central bank governor Olaf Sleijpen says "the direction is clear" for the December meeting and the path after that is uncertain [2]. A rate rise does not lower the price of a barrel. It tries to stop the barrel writing next year's wage settlement.
