**What happened.** The Society for Healthcare Epidemiology of America (SHEA) and the Association for Professionals in Infection Control and Epidemiology (APIC) are calling for coordinated action on a "significant shortage" of 2% chlorhexidine gluconate cloths, CIDRAP reports [1]. Hospitals use them to remove pathogens from patients' skin; they are a key tool in preventing surgical-site infections [1].

**Why it matters.** "This shortage is a reminder that infection prevention interventions depend on a resilient supply chain," said SHEA president Lisa Maragakis [1]. She asked manufacturers and federal partners for transparency about the reasons, the alternatives and the timelines to restore production [1].

**The pattern.** Shortages are rarely dramatic at the moment they start. In South Africa, the health department told parliament that national medicine availability stood at 83.4% on 16 November, with hospitals at 79.9% and depots at 64.8%; the number of high-risk products facing disruption doubled to 184 [2]. About 95% of medicines there are procured overseas [2].

**What to take from it.** The fix for a shortage is boring: several suppliers, a published inventory, a known substitute and someone whose job is to check. None of it makes news. All of it prevents news.
