The US Trade Representative is expected to publish, imminently, the findings of its investigation into alleged excess industrial capacity in 16 trading partners, three people with knowledge of the matter told Reuters; new duties on a wide range of goods are expected [1]. AP, citing its own sources, reports plans to raise tariffs on China, the European Union and 14 other economies [2]. As of early Wednesday, no rates had been published.

The list: China, India, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Switzerland, Norway, Singapore, Bangladesh, Cambodia and the EU [1]. The duties would partly replace the tariffs the US Supreme Court struck down in February [1].

The dull detail matters most. One person told Reuters the White House is expected to honour the caps written into finalised trade deals: 15% for goods from the EU, Japan and South Korea, 19% for Malaysia, and 20% for China's second-term tariffs as long as the truce due to expire on 10 January is extended [1]. Goods from Mexico and Norway are unlikely to see immediate levies [1].

For a European exporter, then, the first question is not "how high" but "above or under the cap". The answer sits in a signed text, not in a press conference.
