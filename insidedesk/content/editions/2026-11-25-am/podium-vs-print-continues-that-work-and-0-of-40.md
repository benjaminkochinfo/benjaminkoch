**The podium.** A Pentagon spokesperson said there had been no change in US policy on supporting Ukraine, and declined to comment on the weapons in question [1]. "The Department of War has pursued multiple parallel lines of effort to accelerate transatlantic industrial co-operation on the Patriot air and missile defence system with European partners and Ukraine, and continues that work to this day," the spokesperson said [1].

**The print.** PAC-3 interceptors were scheduled for Ukraine between September and December: eight in September, 10 in October, 10 in November and 12 in December. None of these batches has yet received approval for transfer, the Financial Times reported, as cited by Ukrainska Pravda [1]. About 20 older PAC-2 interceptors have also been allocated and await approval [1]. Some of the missiles sit in European arsenals and were paid for by Kyiv's Western allies, but because they are US-made, Washington must approve their export and re-export [1].

**The gap.** Policy, in the spokesperson's words, is unchanged. The schedule shows forty missiles in four monthly batches, and not one signature. Volodymyr Zelensky has said Ukraine receives an average of only 12 Patriot missiles a month [1].

**The dull part.** This is not a debate about strategy. It is an approval form. Allies paid; the stock exists; the signature is missing. Of all the problems in a war, this is the one most easily solved by an office with a pen.
