A classic Thanksgiving dinner for ten costs $58.63 this year, about $5.86 a person, according to the American Farm Bureau Federation's 41st annual survey [1]. That is more than 6% above 2025, the first overall increase since the record of $64.05 in 2022, and still $5.42 below it [1].

**The ledger.**

- 16-pound frozen turkey: $23.68, or $1.48 a pound, up 10.1% [1].
- 12-ounce bag of cranberries: $2.48, up 8.8% [1].
- Gallon of whole milk: $4.05, up 8.6% [1].
- Dozen dinner rolls: $3.81, up 7% [1].
- Veggie tray: $1.31, down 3.7%; two frozen pie crusts $3.28, down 2.7%; a pound of frozen peas $1.99, down 2% [1].

A third of the items got cheaper; low wheat prices helped some flour-based goods [1]. "High diesel prices and a shortage of farm labor have taken a toll," said AFBF deputy chief economist Faith Parum [1]. The meal is cheapest in the South at $54.33 and dearest in the Northeast at $61.57 [1].

**The line that matters.** Less than 6 cents of every food dollar goes back to the farm, USDA estimates [1]. Most of the price is diesel, trucks, packaging and shelves: the dull machinery between the field and the table.

Desk arithmetic: at $5.86 a person, the year's rise of about 6% is roughly 33 cents a plate. The turkey alone, up about $2.17 on a 16-pound bird, is the largest part. On Tuesday, Dollar Tree shares rose 11.2% after it raised its profit outlook [2]. Discount shelves are doing well when the receipt grows.
