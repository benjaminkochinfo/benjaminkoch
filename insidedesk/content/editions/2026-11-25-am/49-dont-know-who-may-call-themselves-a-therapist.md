Almost half of British adults aged 18 to 29, 49%, do not know that anyone can call themselves a therapist, according to a survey of 1,000 young adults for the British Association for Counselling and Psychotherapy (BACP), carried out by 3Gem from 7 to 10 November [1].

The same survey found that 37% scroll social media for emotional support and 23% have used chatbots for the same reason; just 25% would consider seeking support from a therapist [1]. Six in ten, 61%, said hearing musicians they follow talk about their mental health makes them feel less alone [1]. The BACP is using that finding in a campaign with two musicians, and stresses choosing a therapist on a register accredited by the Professional Standards Authority [1].

Across the Atlantic, parents are sceptical of the machines. In a national survey of 572 US parents published in JAMA Pediatrics, 76% said they were "not comfortable at all" or "not too comfortable" with AI-led therapy for their children; diagnostic accuracy was their main concern, the Harvard Gazette reports [2].

The constructive reading: young people are not refusing help. They are looking for it in the nearest place. The cheapest improvement is a dull one, a searchable register, made visible.
