Hewlett Packard Enterprise raised its fiscal 2027 revenue growth forecast to 27% to 35%, from 13% to 17%, and its fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.43 a share beat the $1.29 analysts expected, Stock Analysis reports, citing LSEG data [1]. Its shares closed 16.31% lower on Tuesday at $40.19 [2].

The reason was one line. The operating margin of the Cloud and AI segment, which holds HPE's servers, was 18.4% in the fourth quarter; HPE expects about 14% for the fiscal year that began on 1 November [1]. Finance chief Marie Myers expects "some pressure in pricing" as this year's steep price rises ease, and lower-margin AI systems will make up more of the segment's sales [1].

Chief executive Antonio Neri called 14% "a natural progression," still well above historical margins [1]. He also warned: "We expect supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained to at least the second half of calendar 2028" [1].

Read that sentence twice. It is the AI boom described from the warehouse: the orders are there, the parts are scarce, and whoever owns the scarce part keeps the margin. Dell fell 11.83% the same day [2].
