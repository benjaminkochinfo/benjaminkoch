Moldova switched on the 400 kV Vulcănești–Chișinău power line on Sunday, with President Maia Sandu and EU Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos present [1]. The 158-kilometre line and two modernised substations tie the country directly to the Romanian market and the European grid [1]. It cost €61 million in World Bank financing, under a loan approved in 2019 after talks that began in 2013 [1]. Desk arithmetic: roughly €386,000 per kilometre.

"Moldova buys zero Russian gas," Sandu wrote [2]. Two more links to Romania are being prepared, including the Bălți–Suceava line, worth about €77 million [1]. Thirteen years from first talk to live current is slow. It is also how dependence ends: one substation at a time.
