Washington's lame-duck session opened on Monday with the map nearly drawn. Democrats control at least 54 Senate seats, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled, according to Punchbowl News [1]. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom leads Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by 2,574 votes, with just under 2,900 ballots outstanding and 75 of 82 counties certified unofficially; counties have until Friday [1]. In Alaska, Mary Peltola leads Senator Dan Sullivan by 637 votes, and a ranked-choice count could leave the result weeks away [1].

In the House, 19 races remain uncalled; Democrats hold at least 254 seats for the next Congress and lead in six of the outstanding races [1]. Several seats in Florida and North Carolina could go to recounts [1].

The diary now matters more than the map. House and Senate Republicans hold leadership elections on Tuesday [1]. Representative Thomas Massie was expected to notice a privileged resolution to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, forcing a floor vote by Wednesday [1]. And government funding runs out on 11 December, with the odds of a shutdown up sharply since the election, Punchbowl reports [1].

For you, the lesson is the patient one. Certification deadlines, recount rules and funding dates are not glamorous. They are where an election becomes a government.
