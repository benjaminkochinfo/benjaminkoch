Two energy markets read the same weekend differently on Monday. Reuters had Brent down around 3% at $95.08 a barrel, after President Donald Trump called new attacks on Iran very unlikely as long as talks progressed [3]. European gas went the other way. The Dutch TTF front-month contract rose 1.4% to €86.70 per megawatt hour in morning trade, and Britain's NBP climbed 2% to 217 pence a therm, Investing.com reported [1].

The reason was physical, not rhetorical. Both US and Iranian authorities reported commercial shipping struck in or near the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, Investing.com wrote, and utility buyers stepped back in to secure prompt supplies [1]. A crude trader can price a sentence. A gas buyer heading into winter has to price a ship.

Keep the scale in view. A delayed afternoon reading of the December TTF contract showed €87.46, up from €85.59 on Friday, but still €7.41 below the €94.86 of Monday 2 November and well under the €103.94 of 30 October [2]. Last week's fall in Brent, 16%, was the steepest weekly drop since early 2020, and it dragged European gas down with it [1].

For your power bill, gas is the number to watch, not the barrel: gas plants often set the German wholesale price when wind and sun are short. Tomorrow's German day-ahead average cleared at a record €351.48 per megawatt hour on a day when oil fell [4]. Nothing here is investment advice.
