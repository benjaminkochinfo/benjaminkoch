Qatar's prime minister was heading to the White House on Monday with an Iranian proposal for an interim deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, OilPrice.com reported [1]. According to two diplomatic sources quoted by Israeli media, Tehran would reopen the strait and stop attacking Gulf tankers; Washington would lift its naval and economic blockade and release $8 billion of Iranian funds frozen in Qatar [1]. Iran would drop its demand to control the strait and charge transit fees, but still refuses to put its nuclear programme on the table [1].

Neither government has confirmed the terms [1]. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran's demands "outrageous and ridiculous" and said the sides were not close to a breakthrough; Iran's foreign minister said the offer to reopen Hormuz within two days stands if the war ends on all fronts [1]. A similar memorandum reached in Switzerland in May collapsed after an Iranian attack on tankers [1].

The market priced the hope anyway. Brent was down 4.2% at $94.23 by 14:17 CET, after a 16% fall last week that Reuters called its steepest weekly drop since the start of the pandemic [2]. JP Morgan estimated a global surplus of 1.4 million barrels a day in October [2].

A reported deal is a draft. Until it carries signatures and a first ordinary day of transits, treat the price as a mood, not a settlement. Nothing here is investment advice.
