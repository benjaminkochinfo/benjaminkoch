Oil fell again on Monday. Reuters reported North Sea Brent and US WTI each down around 3%, at $95.08 and $88.11 a barrel, after Brent had already lost 16% last week [1]. The trigger was diplomacy: US President Donald Trump called new attacks on Iran very unlikely as long as talks to end the conflict were progressing [1].

The caution came in the same report. "The underlying geopolitical risks remain high, and the possibility of renewed military escalation cannot be ruled out," said Sugandha Sachdeva of SS WealthStreet (desk translation from the German Reuters copy) [1]. A barrel priced on hope is a barrel that can be repriced on a headline.

The currency market read the day as risk-on. The dollar index lost 0.5% to 101.83 points after its recent rally [1]. Oil is priced in dollars, so a softer dollar and a cheaper barrel point the same way for anyone paying in euros.

You will meet that at the pump only slowly. The ADAC's national average for E10 on Sunday was already below €2, at €1.996 a litre, and the first Tankerkönig figures on Monday morning pointed slightly lower [2]. A week of falling oil is a fact; a lasting peace is still a negotiation. Nothing here is investment advice.
