Anthropic has struck a deal worth about $3.4 billion to secure gas-fired generators for data centres, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, as relayed by Reuters [1]. In late September the company bought commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty generators from OnePWR Solutions, an equipment supplier in San Antonio, Texas [1]. It did not acquire OnePWR, and none of its employees moved across, but the deal included the engineering and construction capability needed to install the machines [1]. Reuters could not immediately verify the report, and neither company responded to requests for comment [1].

Desk arithmetic: $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts is about $2 million per megawatt of generating capacity, before fuel.

The interesting word is "orders". What changed hands was a place in a manufacturing queue, not a power station. The Neuron's summary puts it plainly: companies can face years-long waits for the equipment that produces electricity, and taking over existing orders gets it sooner [2]. Reuters reported in September that Anthropic expects to spend more than $500 billion over ten years on AI infrastructure [1].

For you, the lesson is about scarcity. When the scarce thing is a turbine slot rather than a clever idea, the advantage goes to whoever signs the purchase order early and can pay for installation crews. That is industrial work: permits, gas connections, maintenance schedules. It also means emissions and local air permits become part of the AI story, whether the brochures mention them or not [2].
