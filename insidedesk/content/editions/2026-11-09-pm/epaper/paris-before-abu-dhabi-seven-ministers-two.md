Foreign ministers of Britain, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and the United States meet in Paris on Monday and Tuesday, DW reports, the day before and the day of the Abu Dhabi talks on Ukraine [1]. It is among the last big events of France's G7 presidency; the US takes over in 2027 [1].

Two worries fill the room. On Ukraine, European governments fear Kyiv could be pushed towards a settlement that favours Russia, after President Trump told CBS the country could "end up with a little bit different shape" [1]. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said Europeans would be represented in Abu Dhabi and that no deal affecting Europe's security architecture could be done without them [1][2]. On Iran, European governments want a diplomatic outcome rather than renewed strikes, with attention on restoring normal shipping through Hormuz [1].

A small detail counts. Secretary of State Marco Rubio is attending both days, unlike the previous G7 meeting, where he came for one [1]. Presence is not agreement. But in alliances, as in offices, decisions tend to be made by whoever stays for the second day.
