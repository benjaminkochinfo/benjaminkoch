The price comparison portal Verivox puts new-customer electricity at around 35 cents per kilowatt hour gross on 9 November, for a household using 4,000 kWh a year, with a 12-month price guarantee and bonuses included [1]. That figure is the weighted average of the ten cheapest offers per postcode, so it is a best-case shelf price, not what most people pay [1].

The gap that matters is between that shelf and the default. Verivox's worked example, as of 6 November: in Schmalkalden in Thuringia, the cheapest tariff costs €1,261.43 in the first year, the local basic-supply tariff €2,169.41 [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €907.98 a year, or about €75.67 a month, for one household that has never switched.

Regional differences are real but smaller. Verivox's cheapest recommended tariffs run from 30.50 cents per kWh in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania to 36.50 cents in Hamburg [1].

Its energy expert Thorsten Storck expects the Middle East crises to raise household power prices "with a delay", and advises anyone switching to choose a price guarantee of at least twelve months [1]. That is his view, not this desk's forecast. Since June 2025 the switch itself may only take 24 hours once the new supplier has your correct data, though notice periods still apply [1]. A contract is the most boring document in your drawer. It is also the one most likely to be costing you money.
