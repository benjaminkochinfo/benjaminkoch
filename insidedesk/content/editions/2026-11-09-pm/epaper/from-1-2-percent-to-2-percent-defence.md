Monday's Eurogroup spent part of its session with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, and Valdis Dombrovskis summed it up in three sentences: "We need to spend more. We need to spend better. And we need to spend together." [1]

The numbers behind the first sentence are already moving. EU defence spending is rising "steadily", from around 1.2% of GDP in 2021 to an expected 2% by 2027, the commissioner said [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is roughly a two-thirds increase as a share of the economy in six years.

The second and third sentences are about the machinery. Dombrovskis wants more money for defence research and development, which he said can lift productivity in the wider economy where technologies have civilian uses too [1]. And he called European procurement "too fragmented", with member states still largely buying nationally; joint buying would be "key" to strengthening production capacity [1].

Then the invoice. Fiscal space is limited, he said, and member states will need to "gradually reprioritise their national budgets" [1]. The national escape clause gives flexibility until 2028, "so planning for that reprioritisation cannot wait" [1]. In plain words: the extra room is temporary, and the choices about what else gives must be made now.

For you, this is not abstract. Reprioritising a budget means something else is funded less. Watch the 24 November Semester package, where Brussels assesses each country's plans against the rules [1].
