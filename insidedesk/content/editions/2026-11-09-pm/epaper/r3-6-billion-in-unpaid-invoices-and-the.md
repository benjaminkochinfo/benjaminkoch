The health department of Gauteng province in South Africa owes its suppliers R3.6 billion in outstanding invoices, Newsday reports, and the arrears are affecting medicine supply to clinics and hospitals [1]. Audits found it paid only 43% of its invoices within the 30-day window in the first quarter of its 2026/2027 financial year [1]. The paper says the department has struggled to pay suppliers since 2017, and cites MedicalBrief reporting annual shortfalls of R8 billion, so each year starts with debt already on the books [1].

The department acknowledged supply problems on 7 October, citing changed medicine contracts, supplier delays and broader supply-chain pressure, and said patients may receive the same medicine from different approved makers or in different packaging [1]. Health MEC Faith Mazibuko said alternatives are provided [1]. Patients quoted by the paper describe otherwise. Michele Clarke of the opposition Democratic Alliance said "up to 50 critical medicines are out of stock"; a clinic reported going three months without ADHD, anxiety and autism medicines; a pensioner on a R2,400 grant said she could not find R2,000 for a blood thinner for two months [1]. These are the party's and patients' accounts as reported, not an audit.

For you, the general point is the boring one. Many medicine shortages are not about molecules or factories. They are about whether the buyer pays on time, so that suppliers keep shipping. A 30-day payment record is a health statistic.
