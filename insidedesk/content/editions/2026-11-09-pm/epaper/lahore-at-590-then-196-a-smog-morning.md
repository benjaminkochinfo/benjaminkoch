Lahore was the world's most polluted major city on Monday morning. IQAir's live ranking for 09:00–10:00 local time put its air quality index at 590, more than three times second-placed Ulaanbaatar at 177, with Delhi at 174 [1]. An hourly reading on the same platform showed 647, and one station in Allama Iqbal Town recorded 1,033 [1]. Raiwind, Multan and Faisalabad were also in the hazardous band [1].

The Punjab government had warned a day earlier that smoke from Diwali fireworks in Indian Punjab, crop-residue burning and easterly winds could push pollution up on 9 and 10 November, with some improvement expected from 11 November [1]. By 19:00 local time IQAir showed Lahore at 196, still "unhealthy" [1].

The useful detail is in the fire counts. An analysis by Hamari Hawa of NASA satellite data recorded 13,241 fire detections in Pakistani Punjab between 1 and 29 October, against 3,158 in Indian Punjab, and found 92% of the Pakistani detections on farmland [1]. Dawn notes such detections are not individually verified [1]. The provincial environment department is due to publish a source comparison on 10 November [1].

Smog is weather plus choices. The weather changes by the hour. The choices, on farms on both sides of a border, change only when someone counts them, which is why a measurement report on a Tuesday matters more than a minister's tweet.
