Mental Health America released its annual state rankings on Monday, built from 17 measures of mostly federal data collected through 2024 [1]. Maryland, Massachusetts and Vermont ranked best; Nevada, Alabama and Wyoming lowest, with Nevada last for a third year [1]. Nationally, 23.40% of adults had any mental illness in 2024, and the prevalence of mental health concerns did not change from 2021 to 2024 [1]. The access figures moved the wrong way: 27.65% of adults with 14 or more mentally unhealthy days a month could not see a doctor because of cost, a share rising since 2021, and 65.20% of those who went without needed treatment cited cost [1]. Alabama has 640 people per mental health provider, Massachusetts 130 [1]. For you: a steady prevalence with a rising cost barrier is a problem of invoices and staffing, which is to say a solvable one.
