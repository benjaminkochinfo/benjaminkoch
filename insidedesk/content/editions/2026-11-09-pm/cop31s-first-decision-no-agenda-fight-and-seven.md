The first thing a climate summit must agree is what it will talk about, and that can take days. In Antalya it took one late-night meeting of delegation heads and a short delay on the plenary dais. On Monday morning COP31 president Murat Kurum announced that an "agenda fight" had been averted, Carbon Brief reported from the hall [1].

**What was parked.** Tripling adaptation finance, proposed by the African group, was not added for lack of consensus, "without prejudice" to it coming up elsewhere [1]. Climate-related trade measures were also left off, with submissions invited for the next dialogue in Bonn [1]. Funding for the Green Climate Fund will be handled under the existing items on the fund's report and guidance; two proposed items on mountains go to presidency consultations and Bonn research dialogues in 2027 and 2028; a seventh review of the financial mechanism was deferred to COP32 [1]. A Comoros proposal on sustainable diets was withdrawn, and long-standing amendment proposals from Russia and Papua New Guinea were "held in abeyance" [1].

**Who was pleased, who was not.** The African group thanked the president for his approach [1]. The UK, speaking for the Umbrella Group, told developing countries that adaptation finance "is not an orphan" [1]. The EU merely "took note" and said it was "deeply concerned" that a new climate finance work programme had been added as an agenda item, against what it understood from the COP30 deal; India said COP30 had agreed exactly that and regretted the adaptation item's absence [1]. Switzerland, for the Environmental Integrity Group, asked that the final cover decision not be shaped by agenda proposals [1].

This is procedure, and procedure is not nothing. A quick agenda means negotiators start on texts rather than on the table of contents. For you, the sensible frame is to ignore the opening speeches and watch the parked items: adaptation money and finance rules are where the fortnight will be won or lost, line by line.
