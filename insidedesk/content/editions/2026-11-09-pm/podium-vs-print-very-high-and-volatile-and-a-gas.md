**The podium.** After Monday's Eurogroup, Valdis Dombrovskis said growth data this year had been "more positive than previously expected", but that the outlook "remains clouded in uncertainty", especially when it comes to energy prices, "which remain very high and volatile" [1]. He asked for "very prudent fiscal policies", noting that deficits remain high and interest-rate spreads have widened [1].

**The print.** Take Europe's benchmark gas contract. The December TTF future stood at €87.46 per megawatt hour in a delayed Monday reading, up 2.2% from Friday's €85.59 [2]. In the fortnight before, it moved by more than 5% in a single session three times, from a fall of 8.7% on 2 November to a rise of 6.2% on 30 October [2]. Brent lost 16% last week and about 3% more on Monday, to $95.08 [4]. That is "volatile", printed.

**The gap.** "Very high" is harder to find in the same table. Agroprices ranks Monday's gas price as mid-range: above 43% of the prices of the last twelve months, within a twelve-month range it shows as €78.08 to €103.94 [2]. Desk arithmetic: the price is about €16.48 below that twelve-month high. Where "very high" does show up is further down the chain. Tomorrow's German day-ahead power price averages €351.48 per megawatt hour, a record for the year on SMARD's series [3]. The commissioner's adjective fits the kilowatt hour better than the molecule.

**For you.** A minister's word is a mood; a ranked price is a fact you can plan around. A household tariff is not the futures price, but suppliers buy on these markets, and that cost reaches contracts over time. If you are renewing a contract, look at the price guarantee, not the press conference. Nothing here is investment advice.
