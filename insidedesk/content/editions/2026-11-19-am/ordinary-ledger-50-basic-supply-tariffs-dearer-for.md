If your electricity or gas comes from your local default supplier, the Grundversorgung, a letter may already be on your doormat. Default suppliers can change prices at the start of a month but must announce it publicly at least six weeks ahead; for 1 January 2027, the last day is 20 November 2026, this Friday [1]. A comparison site that compiles suppliers' published price sheets counts 50 of the 116 tariffs and price areas it tracks with an announcement so far: all 50 dearer, none cheaper [1].

The spread is wide. On its standard household of 3,500 kWh of electricity or 12,000 kWh of gas a year, Stadtwerke Hamm's gas rises 23.4%, or €351.35 a year [1]. Bremen's swb raises electricity from 30.46 to 36.51 cents per kWh, 18.2% or €218.11 a year [1]. At the bottom of the list, Frankfurt's Mainova raises electricity by 2.4%, or €34.94 a year [1].

EWE's own letter shows the arithmetic. Its default electricity rate goes from 29.63 to 34.58 cents per kWh gross on 1 January, and the annual standing charge from €224.80 to €229.55; for 2,500 kWh a year that is about €128.50 more [2]. Gas rises from 11.01 to 12.90 cents, about €300 more a year at 15,000 kWh [2]. Desk arithmetic: 4.95 cents more for every electricity kWh. EWE blames procurement first: forward gas for 2027 rose about 150% to almost €83 per MWh between October 2025 and October 2026, and power about 80% to roughly €163 [2].

Now the part that is yours. When the default price changes, you may cancel without notice for the day of the change, and if you show a new contract within a month of cancelling you do not pay the new price; outside that, default supply can always be ended with two weeks' notice [1]. The site lists 'open' entries such as Bonn, Augsburg, Erfurt and Kassel, last checked between 16 and 18 November [1].

So the job for this weekend is small and dull: find the letter, note the cents per kWh and the standing charge, read last year's meter total off your bill, and multiply. Ten minutes with a calculator is the cheapest energy advice there is.
