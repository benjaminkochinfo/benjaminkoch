Weapons makers rarely tell you which operation their product was used in. Destinus, a defence company with its headquarters in the Netherlands and a factory in Hengelo, did exactly that this week [1][2]. Its president, Oleksandr Danyliuk, said Ukraine fired nine of the company's Ruta Block 1 cruise missiles at the Kerch bridge on Tuesday, and seven hit [1]. Its Da Vinci deep-strike drones were also used, and he said Destinus systems were the only foreign-made weapons in the attack [1]. The company said it spoke only after President Zelensky had already named the missiles [1].

Russia's embassy in The Hague answered on X: "Moscow has been duly informed of the NL involvement. The confession has been well noted. Is The Hague openly joining the conflict?" [2][3] The Dutch foreign ministry had not commented when DutchNews reported the post [2]. The background is not new. In April Russia's defence ministry listed two Destinus sites in Hengelo among European firms it accused of helping Ukraine, and Dmitry Medvedev called such facilities potential targets [1].

The war did not wait for the diplomacy. Overnight into Thursday the bridge was attacked again, this time its railway section, which had survived the earlier strikes; Sergei Aksyonov, Crimea's Moscow-appointed head, said it was closed to traffic [4]. Meduza counts it as the third attack in a week [4]. It came hours before Putin, Trump and Xi were due to meet in Shenzhen [3][4].

The constructive reading is a dull one. Zelensky described a weeks-long operation in seven stages, coordinated by Ukraine's security service and general staff and using 11 weapons systems [1]. That is planning and procurement, not a single wonder weapon. The Netherlands has funded it in plain budget lines: €248 million for Dutch and Ukrainian drones in April, and a €500 million package in June [1].

The risk is also a dull one. A missile analyst told NL Times that the publicity could bring sabotage and cyber attacks [1]. Destinus says it takes its staff's safety very seriously [1]. If you work near a defence plant, the useful question this week is not about Yalta. It is whether the site's fences, logins and night shifts are as good as its marketing.
