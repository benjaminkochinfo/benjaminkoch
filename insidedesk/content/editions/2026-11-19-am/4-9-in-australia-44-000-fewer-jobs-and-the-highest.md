Australia's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate rose to 4.9% in October, the highest since October 2021, the Australian Bureau of Statistics reported at 11:30 Canberra time, 01:30 CET [1]. "In October, there were 44,000 less employed people and 18,000 more unemployed people," said Sean Crick, the ABS head of labour statistics [1].

The detail is duller and more telling than the headline. Crick said a higher share of people remained unemployed than usual for an October in the post-Covid era, as in September [1]. Youth unemployment, for 15- to 24-year-olds, rose to 11.3% from 11.1%, also a five-year high, against a post-Covid average of 9.2% [1]. The employment-to-population ratio fell 0.3 points to 63.3%, participation fell 0.2 points to 66.6%, and hours worked fell 0.2% [1].

The trend series, which smooths the monthly noise, rose to 4.8% [1]. Trend employment was steady while the number of unemployed rose 13.3% over twelve months [1]. Desk arithmetic: in the ABS trend index (May 2022 = 100), employment stands at 109.4 and unemployment at 140.7; jobs have grown 9.4% since then, the jobless count 40.7%.

Markets took the hint: Sydney's ASX 200 was down 0.9% by Newsquawk's morning wrap, with the five-year high in unemployment cited alongside weaker commodity prices [2]. Note, too, what did not fall: underemployment eased 0.1 points to 6.2% [1]. A cooling market is not a collapsing one, and the institution that counts it did so on schedule.

For you, the lesson travels: when you read a jobless rate anywhere, look for the length of spells and the youth line. They move before the average does. Nothing here is investment advice.
