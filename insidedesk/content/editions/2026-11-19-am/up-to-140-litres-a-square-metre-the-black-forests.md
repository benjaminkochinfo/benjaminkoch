The German Weather Service (DWD) opened Thursday with a warning bulletin that is less about drama than about duration. Atlantic frontal systems are crossing Central Europe one after another, bringing moist, mild sea air and "a very wet and windy spell", the 05:00 CET report says [1]. Until Saturday, the hill country of the west, centre and south can expect 50 to 80 litres of rain per square metre in 48 to 72 hours; in the Black Forest, 90 to 140 litres, which meets the threshold for a severe-weather warning [1].

The rain did not start this week by surprise. Persistent rain has been soaking the Black Forest's soils since Tuesday, and the DWD's red warnings cover much of the northern, central and southern Black Forest, from Rastatt, Calw and Freudenstadt through the Ortenau and the Schwarzwald-Baar district to Breisgau-Hochschwarzwald, Lörrach and Waldshut, the Schwarzwälder Bote reported on Wednesday [2]. Some of those warnings run until 09:00 on Saturday 21 November [2]. The named risks are specific: flooded roads and underpasses, buildings close to watercourses, and landslides on steep slopes where the ground is already saturated [2]. The paper also notes what the warning does not say: it is not a forecast of widespread flooding everywhere, and local outcomes depend on how much actually falls [2].

Wind comes with it. On Thursday the DWD expects gusts of up to 60 km/h widely in the south and centre, 80 to 100 km/h on the mountains of the southern half, and up to 110 km/h on the Feldberg and higher Alpine peaks; isolated thunderstorms with hail pellets are possible in the west and south [1]. The north, by contrast, gets light frost, with lows down to minus 3 degrees and local black ice expected on the night into Friday [1].

The change of air is in the DWD's outlook rather than its warnings for now: on Saturday the rain and snow retreat towards the Alps, with showers falling as snow at higher and middle elevations from the North Sea to the Ore Mountains [3].

For you, the useful habit is boring. Read the regional warning, not the national headline; avoid flooded underpasses, which the DWD names first; and if you live by a small stream in the south-west, the time to move the car and clear the cellar drain is before Saturday, not during it.
