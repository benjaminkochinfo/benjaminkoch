France's medicines regulator, the ANSM, said on Wednesday that the severe supply strain on furosemide 20 mg and 40 mg tablets continues and should last "several more months in 2027" [1]. Furosemide is the old, inexpensive diuretic that keeps fluid off the lungs and ankles of many people with heart failure. The ANSM names the cause plainly: the supply difficulties of the laboratory Viatris persist [1].

The response is a lesson in dull machinery. Other manufacturers have, at the agency's request, brought forward deliveries and temporarily raised production; imports of equivalent medicines are under way, some found through the European solidarity mechanism [1]. Because some packs arrive in boxes of 90 or 100 tablets, pharmacists will be allowed in the coming days to dispense by the unit, up to one month of treatment [1].

The sharpest measure concerns distribution. The ANSM has temporarily banned laboratories from selling furosemide directly to community pharmacies in mainland France, so that stock flows through the wholesalers, who must supply every pharmacy [1]. The aim, it says, is to stop some pharmacies holding stock while others run dry, "even within the same territory" [1]. The ban runs for three months and can be lifted early or renewed [1].

The most old-fashioned fix is the most telling. Pharmacists may now replace unavailable tablets with capsules of 20 or 40 mg prepared in the pharmacy, a so-called magistral preparation, without a new prescription [1]. Doctors are asked to keep limiting new starts and to reconsider, at each renewal, whether the patient still needs it [1]. A price order of 17 November sets what the health insurance pays for those capsules until the tablets return [1].

If you take furosemide in France, the practical advice is the agency's own: if your usual pack is missing, your pharmacist can give you capsules on the same terms, with an instruction sheet you should read before the first dose [1]. Elsewhere, the point is wider. A system that depends on one large supplier of a generic pill is fragile; the repair kit is wholesalers, pharmacists with mortars, and a regulator that writes rules about box sizes.
