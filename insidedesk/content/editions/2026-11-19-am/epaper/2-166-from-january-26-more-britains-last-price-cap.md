The typical household in Great Britain will pay £2,166 a year for gas and electricity from January if the forecaster Cornwall Insight is right, The Guardian reported on Wednesday [1]. That is a 26% rise in the price cap, or £443 on the average dual-fuel bill, one of the biggest quarterly increases on record [1]. It is the last forecast before the regulator Ofgem announces the January-to-March cap next Wednesday [1].

The estimate has moved fast. In late September Cornwall expected 16% and £1,999 [1]. Gas prices rose through October on violence in the Middle East and worries over Europe's gas storage [1]. "The cause lies thousands of miles away," said Cornwall's Craig Lowrey [1].

Some help is already booked. The budget promised a one-off £100 payment in January to 20 million households in council tax bands A to D, on top of the £150 warm home discount for about six million means-tested households [1]. Cuts to electricity levies over the past year have shaved about £150 a year off costs, according to the paper [1]. Desk arithmetic: the £100 payment covers less than a quarter of the forecast £443.

Nothing here is investment advice. For you, the useful step comes before next Wednesday's headline: read your meter, so the first bill under the new cap is based on real numbers rather than an estimate. A forecast is a warning; a meter reading is a fact.
