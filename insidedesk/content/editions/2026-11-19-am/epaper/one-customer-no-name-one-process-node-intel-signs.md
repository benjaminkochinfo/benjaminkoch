Intel has signed its first external customer for its 18A chipmaking process, chief executive Lip-Bu Tan said on Wednesday at a summit in Austin, Texas [1]. "I'm involved in production and we just signed the first customer," he said, without naming it [1]. Intel's technology chief, Pushkar Ranade, clarified that the customer signed up for the 18A-P variant, which went into risk production in June [1].

It matters because Intel's foundry has so far mainly made chips for Intel [1]. The company has spent five years trying to compete with TSMC as a contract manufacturer, through missteps and low yields [1]. An analyst at Creative Strategies called it "a huge win for foundry" and suggested Nvidia, Apple or the hyperscalers that design their own processors as possible clients [1]. Those are guesses, and CNBC labels them as such.

Tan also named Microsoft as the first publicly named customer for EMIB, Intel's most advanced packaging technology [1]. Packaging, the step that connects chips into a system, is less glamorous than lithography and increasingly where the capacity is short.

The backdrop is sober. Intel's shares have lost more than half their value since the end of September, most of it after the company said quarterly revenue would fall at least $1 billion short of estimates [1]. A first customer is a contract, not a turnaround. The number to watch is the second one, and whether either is willing to see its name in print.
