Europe's benchmark gas price closed Wednesday at €81.11 per megawatt hour on the Dutch TTF, down 5.03% on the day and 7.36% over a month [1]. A week ago the story ran the other way: TTF climbed 7% to €92.70 on 12 November after colder forecasts and outages at Norway's Troll field cut pipeline flows [2].

What changed is unglamorous. Deliveries of feed gas to US export terminals reached an all-time high as new projects ramped up, and forecasts for late November turned milder [2]. Austria's CEGH hub, a reference for much of Central and South-East Europe, fell €6 to €87.61 on Tuesday, still a few euros above TTF [2].

The cushion is in the ground. EU storage sites are roughly 70% full, which, Investing.com notes, lets buyers absorb geopolitical shocks without bidding up the winter curve [3]. That is the reason Iranian strikes on Kuwait and Bahrain reported on Wednesday moved prices so little [3].

None of this makes gas cheap. The conflict in the Gulf has disrupted close to a fifth of global LNG exports, Qatari supply is absent from Europe's mix, and prices remain more than double pre-crisis norms [2]. Desk arithmetic: Wednesday's close is still €54.51 above the 52-week low of €26.60 [1].

For you, the lesson is to watch the dull variables: export terminals, storage levels, temperature forecasts. They move the price more than speeches do. Nothing here is investment advice.
