Ukrainian drone attacks have cut the combined crude processing of Russia's oil refineries by 45% since late October, Reuters reported on Wednesday, citing industry sources [1].

The number lands in an oil market more preoccupied with surplus than shortage. Brent was at $92.12 a barrel at 03:37 CET on Thursday, after a 1.3% fall on Wednesday, and US crude inventories rose by 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week to 13 November, against an expected 58,000 [1].

A refinery that cannot run does not make the world's crude disappear; it changes who refines it and where the diesel comes from. Traders are now watching the US–China–Russia summit for any sign of a negotiated end to the wars that have disrupted oil markets [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
