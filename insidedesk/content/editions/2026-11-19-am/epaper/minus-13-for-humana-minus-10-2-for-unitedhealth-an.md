The Dow's 1.11% fall on Wednesday had a paperwork cause. UnitedHealth closed 10.21% lower at $349.91, the worst performer in the index, and Humana fell 12.98% to $385.78, the worst in the S&P 500 [2]. Elevance lost 7.6%, CVS Health 5.2% and Cigna 3.0% [1].

The trigger is a proposal from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, called CRUSH, published on Monday [1]. Medicare Advantage plans are paid more for members with more recorded diagnoses; CMS audits a sample of records. Under the proposal it would extrapolate audit findings to a plan's wider payments, starting with 2018 audits, which it estimates would recover about $9bn per payment year [1]. The agency puts savings across its programmes at nearly $90bn over ten years [1].

Oppenheimer analysts, quoted via Yahoo Finance, expect a court fight, noting a comparable framework was thrown out in 2025, and expect insurers to pass costs into future bids if the rule stands [1]. UnitedHealth weighs about 4.3% of the price-weighted Dow [1].

The dull lesson: a comment period is not a verdict. CMS takes public comments until 19 January 2027 and can change the text before adopting it [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
