The cabinet on Wednesday adopted a reform of custody and contact law that justice minister Stefanie Hubig called "the biggest in decades" (translated) [1]. For the first time the law would say explicitly that family courts can exclude a violent parent's contact with the child where that is essential to protect the other parent's life, body, freedom or sexual self-determination, as long as it does not run against the child's welfare [1]. Domestic violence would get a legal definition and a list of criteria courts must weigh [1].

Children aged 14 and over would have to consent to certain custody and contact agreements, and would gain their own right to contact with grandparents and siblings [1]. Separated parents could decide everyday matters alone during their own care time [1]. Rules written down beat good intentions argued case by case.
