The Rosario Grain Exchange has raised its forecast for Argentina's 2026/27 wheat harvest to 24.3 million tonnes, 500,000 tonnes above its October estimate and, if confirmed, the second-largest crop on record after last season's 28.5 million [1]. Early-season forecasts had been 18 to 19 million [1].

The reason is weather that sounds unhelpful. October and the first ten days of November brought low temperatures, wide day-night swings and below-average rain; technicians consulted by the GEA described the cold as extremely unusual [1]. That reduced disease pressure, notably fusarium head blight, and prolonged grain fill; the national average yield of 36 quintals per hectare is the second-highest on record [1]. Frosts in southern Buenos Aires province are being watched, but widespread damage is not expected [1]. With 13% harvested, La Pampa is already above its previous yield record [1].

For you: climate news is not only losses. Sometimes the same thermometer that worries one farmer fills another's silo.
