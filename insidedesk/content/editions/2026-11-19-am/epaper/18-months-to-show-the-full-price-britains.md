Britain's Competition and Markets Authority is investigating travel firms and leisure businesses over drip pricing, the practice of showing a low headline price and adding fees later, the consumer group Which? said on Wednesday in its response [1]. "Sneaky fees and add-ons" can change the final price of gym memberships and holidays, said Rocio Concha, its director of policy and advocacy, and in the worst cases the gap between the advertised and final price "can reach hundreds of pounds" [1].

Which? says it keeps finding brands ignoring the new rules, and that firms "have had 18 months to get their house in order" [1]. The rule is simple: show the full price people will pay while they are still comparing deals [1]. Until enforcement bites, your defence is simpler still: compare totals at the last screen, never the first.
