A study in BMC Medicine pooled 94,122 adults over 50 from 18 countries to ask how social isolation (few contacts) and loneliness (feeling alone) relate to health, 2 Minute Medicine reported on Wednesday [1]. Both were associated with a higher probability of physical disease and of mental disorders [1].

The sizes differ. For physical disease the pooled average marginal effects were small: 0.007 for isolation and 0.011 for loneliness [1]. For mental disorders they were 0.026 for isolation and 0.243 for loneliness [1]. The design is observational, so it shows association, not cause [1].

The distinction is the useful part. Being alone and feeling alone are not the same thing, and the second carried the larger signal for the mind. A diary full of appointments does not settle it; a conversation that actually lands might.
