A federal grand jury has indicted Igor D'iu, a Russian national, on four counts: conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, acting as one without notifying the Attorney General, interstate stalking and identity-document fraud, the US Justice Department said on Wednesday [1]. The target was a "large, publicly traded" technology and defence company with an office in Palo Alto; the indictment does not name it [1][2].

According to the affidavit, D'iu was recruited on Telegram, flew from Istanbul to Mexico City in June and was helped across the border [1]. His handlers allegedly told him the work was for "the Motherland" and that the company supplied weapons used against Russia in Ukraine [1]. He is accused of photographing employees' laptop screens in a coffee shop, profiling staff and, on 3 September, following a woman for nearly 30 miles until she called 911 and drove to a police station [1].

The case rests on ordinary police work. Investigators intercepted two parcels holding an altered US passport and a falsified California ID [1]. An October search found about $18,000 in cash, two Russian passports and a notebook with 10 handwritten addresses [1]. D'iu has been in custody since 2 November and is due in court on 24 November [1]. These are allegations, not findings; he has not been convicted.

The lesson for any company that supplies Ukraine is unglamorous: staff who report being followed, and a local police force that writes it down.
