Private flu vaccination in Ireland has hit a second supply wall this winter, The Irish Times reported on Wednesday [1]. Private appointments over the next two weeks are being cancelled [1]. A pharmacy in north Dublin told patients that "due to manufacturing delays, no private batches of the flu vaccine are available at the moment", deleted the bookings and refunded them [1]. The workplace provider Medmark also cancelled clinics, citing "an unexpected disruption to the national influenza vaccine supply" [1].

It is a repeat. At the end of September companies cancelled staff clinics after what providers called "an exceptional industry-wide shortage" following "a major manufacturing failure"; many later found supply [1]. A government source told the paper that manufacturers have, for the first time in years, had to change the vaccine's formulation to target specific strains better [1].

The order of priority held. The HSE's free scheme for older people and the immunocompromised is not affected, and the pharmacy said HSE flu and Covid appointments remain available on schedule [1]. Flu activity has risen but stayed at "baseline-low levels": in the week to 8 November there were 181 influenza cases and 57 hospitalisations notified, with nine intensive-care admissions and three deaths for the season so far [1].

For you, the lesson is about queues, not panic. When supply is short, a public list that serves the most vulnerable first is doing its job. If you are in a risk group, the free slot is the one to book.
