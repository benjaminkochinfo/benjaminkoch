South Africa's health minister, Aaron Motsoaledi, has acknowledged medicine shortages across the public sector, the Cape Times reports [1]. He gave several causes at once: about 30 medicine shipments awaiting processing at the Port of Durban, ingredient imports from India and China disrupted by tension around the Strait of Hormuz, and regulator inspections that restricted manufacturers failing Good Manufacturing Practice [1]. The Eastern Cape, Northern Cape and North West are struggling to pay suppliers within 30 days [1].

The South African Society of Psychiatrists wants an investigation into stock-outs affecting patients with schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and major depression; "Patients who have been stable for years can relapse," said Dr Mahlatse Thosago [1]. Hospitals may now buy outside existing contracts [1]. Paying invoices on time, the minister conceded, "won't really help us solve this problem" alone [1]. It is still where the repair starts.
