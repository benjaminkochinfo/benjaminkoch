When a separated parent does not pay child support, the state often pays an advance, the Unterhaltsvorschuss, instead. On Wednesday the cabinet adopted a reform meant to save money through two levers [1].

The first is an income limit. From 2029, single parents with more than €40,000 of taxable income a year would lose the claim; the limit rises by €10,000 for each further child [1]. The ministry argued that with a tight budget it is "essential" to spend the money where it is most needed (translated) [1].

The second lever points at the parent who owes the money, usually the father, tagesschau notes [1]. Debtors would have to disclose their finances to the authority at any time on request; breaching that duty could bring a higher fine and a driving ban, and more of items such as a Christmas bonus could be seized [1].

The numbers explain the second lever. In 2024 the federal government and the states paid about €3.2 billion in advances, and the authorities recovered only about 17% from the parents who owed it [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is roughly €540 million back and about €2.7 billion not.

An income cap cuts the bill by paying fewer families. Better collection cuts it by making the right person pay. Only the second fixes the invoice rather than moving it. The draft now goes to parliament, and changes are still possible [1].
