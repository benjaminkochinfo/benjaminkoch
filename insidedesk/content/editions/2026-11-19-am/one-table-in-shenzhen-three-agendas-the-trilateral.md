Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are due to hold their first three-way meeting on Thursday on the sidelines of the Apec summit in Shenzhen [1]. Per a White House schedule cited by the Guardian, Xi meets Trump in the early afternoon local time, with Putin joining just under two hours later [2]. The White House says Trump wants to discuss the Iran war and a possible three-way nuclear arms agreement; Ukraine is not expected on the formal agenda [1].

Read the ledger before the pageantry. The US-China tariff truce agreed in Busan in October 2025 has been extended to 10 January 2027 [1]. Yet China's rare-earth exports to the US remain about 75% below normal levels, according to the Center for Strategic and International Studies, cited by Al Jazeera [1]. Deborah Elms of the Hinrich Foundation told Al Jazeera that a handshake without incident would be seen as a win [1]. "It is a far cry from being a new Yalta," said Vuk Vuksanovic of King's College London [1].

The economic threads are concrete. New START lapsed in February [1]. Russian oil exports to China rose 35% in the first quarter of 2026, according to a Kremlin aide [1]. Putin wants to keep a paused US sanctions bill, which would hit buyers of Russian goods including China, from taking effect, according to Scott Lucas of University College Dublin [1].

In his plenary speech Xi pledged $20m for Apec's "practical cooperation" and called for reducing barriers to trade and investment [2]. Delegates had been "working around the clock" on a joint statement, an official told Reuters on Tuesday, per the Guardian [2].

For you, the test is not the communiqué's adjectives but whether a licence, a tariff line or an export quota changes afterwards. That is where a summit either becomes paperwork or stays a photograph.
