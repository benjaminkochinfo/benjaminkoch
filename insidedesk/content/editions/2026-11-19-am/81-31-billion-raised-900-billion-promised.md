Anthropic's initial public offering raised $81.31 billion on Wednesday, overtaking SpaceX's June offering as the largest stock-market debut on record, Reuters reports [1]. It priced at $83 a share, above the targeted range of $70 to $78, after the deal was upsized by about 19% to 980 million shares [1]. That values the company at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted, or about $1.1 trillion on a non-diluted basis [1]. A further $26 billion comes from a private placement at the same price [1].

The growth figures are real and large. The annualised revenue run rate reached $81 billion at 31 October, up $10 billion from the end of September, and third-quarter revenue rose 47% from the second quarter to $16.96 billion [1]. Customers paying at least $100,000 a year numbered 11,900 at the end of September, against more than 1,500 at the end of last year [1].

Now the invoice. Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on computing and infrastructure in the first half of 2026, almost four times a year earlier, and the third quarter swung to an operating loss of $567 million from an operating profit of $974 million [1]. It has committed to nearly $900 billion of computing spending with partners including Amazon and Google [1]. Desk arithmetic: that commitment is about 11 times the current annual run rate.

The sentence worth framing is in the risk factors. Some computing providers "depend on third-party financing and credit support", and if that becomes unavailable or dearer, "contracted capacity may be delayed, repriced, reduced, or not delivered" [1]. In other words, the frontier rests on someone else's loan book. That is not a scandal; it is how factories and power stations have always been built. But it means the dull numbers that matter are data-centre credit spreads and delivery dates, not benchmark scores.

The filing is also candid about its own product: the models "could develop capabilities faster" than the company can evaluate them [1]. If you hold the shares, or an index that soon will, read that page alongside the revenue chart. Nothing here is investment advice.
