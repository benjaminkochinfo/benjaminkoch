Europe's imports of liquefied natural gas are estimated at 9.11 million tonnes for October, up from 7.76 million in September but down from 10.59 million in October 2025, according to Kpler data cited in a Reuters column [1]. That extends a run of months this year with weaker imports than in the same months of 2025 [1].

The buyers are elsewhere. Asia's October arrivals are estimated at 23.31 million tonnes, the most since July, with China taking 5.47 million, even though spot LNG for north Asia reached $29.30 per million British thermal units in the week to 16 October, the highest since December 2023 and almost triple the $10.40 of late February [1]. Japan's utilities, by contrast, are buying less: they held 2.54 million tonnes in inventory on 18 October, 36% more than a year earlier [1].

The column calls Europe's modest buying "perhaps surprising" given that EU gas storage was only 74.6% full on 21 October, 11 points below a year earlier [1]. Forecasters expect a strong El Niño this winter, which usually means milder, wetter weather for Europe [1]. That is a forecast, not a contract. Japan's full tanks are the opposite approach: insurance bought before the bill arrives. Nothing here is advice.
