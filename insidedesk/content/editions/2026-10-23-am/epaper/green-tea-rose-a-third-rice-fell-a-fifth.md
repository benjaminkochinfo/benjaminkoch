Japan's statistics bureau published September's consumer prices on Friday, and the headline, 2.1% excluding fresh food, hides a busy shopping basket [1]. Green tea rose 33.8% on a year earlier, the steepest rise since comparable records began in 1971, as growers shifted production to meet demand for matcha, Nikkei reported [1]. Saury, after poor catches, rose 27.8%; toilet paper 17.8%; mobile phones 10.9% [1].

Rice went the other way. After last year's surge, rice prices fell 20.6%, the biggest drop since January 1971, Jiji Press reported [2]. Accommodation rose because of how the holidays fell, and the end of Tokyo's free basic water charge pushed the index up too [2].

The quietest line is the policy one. The abolition of the old provisional petrol tax rate and subsidies for electricity and gas took 0.82 percentage points off the inflation rate, Nikkei calculated [1]. Electricity bills fell 2.4% and petrol 2.6%; the government subsidises refiners to hold regular petrol at about ¥170 a litre [1].

Desk arithmetic: add the 0.82 points back and the rate would have been about 2.9%. That is the number a central bank has to look through, and the one a household does not see until the subsidy ends. Nothing here is advice.
