A preregistered University of Barcelona study in Psychological Medicine scanned 116 naturally cycling women aged 18 to 35 during an approach-avoid task [1]. Higher salivary progesterone went with less distinct activity patterns in the lateral frontal pole and striatum, with correlations of about −0.25 [1]. Neither hormone was linked to speed or accuracy on the task, and estradiol showed no link at all [1]. An indirect path to worse recent mood explained 7.1% of its variation [1]. "Progesterone is certainly not a master switch," said the lead author, Macià Buades-Rotger [1]. A small, careful result is worth more than a large stereotype.
