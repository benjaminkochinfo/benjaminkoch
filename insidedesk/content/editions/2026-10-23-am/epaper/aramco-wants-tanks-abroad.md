Saudi Arabia and Aramco are looking for partners to expand oil storage outside the kingdom, chief executive Amin Nasser told Al Arabiya Business on Thursday [1]. His reason: the Iran war has exposed weak points in the kingdom's energy network [1]. Aramco is also studying more export routes, because Hormuz, Bab al-Mandab and Suez have all proved vulnerable [1].

The figures behind this are large. Citing the IEA, the report says Saudi output fell below 7 million barrels a day for much of the war's first six months, from over 10 million before it, and recovered to about 8 million in September [1]. Nasser has said earlier that nearly 3 billion barrels of supply have been lost and more than 1 billion drawn from inventories. Refilling them while meeting normal demand could take up to two years [1]. Once the war ends, he expects demand to rise by about 3 million barrels a day: 2 million of extra consumption and 1 million to rebuild stocks [1].

The East-West pipeline to the Red Sea, with capacity of about 7 million barrels a day, has been the bypass. Aramco has plans that could take it to 10 million, but drones launched from Iraq struck the pipeline in September [1].
