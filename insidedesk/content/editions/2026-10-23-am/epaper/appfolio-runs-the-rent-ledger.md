AppFolio sells software to landlords and property managers. On Thursday evening it reported third-quarter revenue of $296m, up 19% on a year earlier [1]. The number of rental units its customers manage on the platform grew 8%, to 9.8 million [1].

**The gap.** Revenue grew more than twice as fast as units. The revenue table shows where: subscription fees rose to $61.5m from $53.8m, but "value added services" rose to $231.9m from $192.1m [1]. Desk arithmetic: those services, sold on top of the basic subscription, were 78% of revenue. The release does not break them down further [1]. What it does show is that the company earns most from what its customers do on the platform, not from the fact that they log in.

**The margin.** GAAP operating income rose 64% to $57m, 19.4% of revenue, from 14.1% a year earlier [1]. Research and development spending fell slightly, to $53.1m, while sales and marketing rose to $48.6m [1]. AppFolio raised its full-year revenue range to $1.122bn–$1.127bn [1].

**The reading.** This is dull machinery in its purest form: back-office software behind millions of rented homes whose tenants never see it. Its revenue grows faster than its unit count because each unit is worth more to it each year. Nothing here is advice.
