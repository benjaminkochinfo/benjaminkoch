The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in Congo has reached Nord Ubangi, an eighth province, on the border with the Central African Republic, where armed violence keeps uprooting people [1]. Last week there were reports of 15 deaths a day in Mangina, a rural commune in North Kivu, where a WHO team arrived two weeks ago after negotiating access; "There was a lot of community preoccupation and community resistance in the beginning," said Anne Ancia, the WHO's representative in Congo [1].

There is still no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for this strain [2]. Inside Congo, researchers are studying Ervebo, which is effective against the Zaire strain, to see whether it cross-protects [1]. "We still do not have preliminary data yet," said Patrick Otim of the WHO's emergency programme [1].

In Nigeria, health minister Muhammad Ali Pate said an undisclosed number of "high risk" contacts of the imported case have been "quarantined, fully, in a facility", and that there is no evidence of community spread [2]. The patient flew from Uganda to Abuja on 3 October via Addis Ababa [2]. Africa CDC's Jean Kaseya said officials are examining whether she could have had contact at the airport with Kenya's first patient, who also left Uganda that day [2].
