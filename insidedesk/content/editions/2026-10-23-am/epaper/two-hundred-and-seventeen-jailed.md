France's justice minister, Gérald Darmanin, said on Thursday that 217 people had been jailed after fast-track trials over last weekend's disorder, and that the number will rise [1]. He said 2,700 people had been arrested across France and more than 1,000 had appeared in court. Half of those detained had no prior criminal record, he said, as reported by Le Figaro [1].

The weekend rules are now set city by city. In Paris, the curfew in Place de la République and Les Halles, 23:00 to 06:00, is extended until Monday [1]. Marseille will suspend public transport on Saturday. The prefecture has extended the curfew for minors there to the whole population overnight on Saturday 24 October, 23:00 to 05:00 [1]. Lyon and Marseille have banned all protests this weekend. Elsewhere only authorised union marches will go ahead [1]. The Louvre, Orsay and Picasso museums in Paris will close on Saturday [1].

The courts are doing what the street could not: dealing with each person by name. "No prior record" is not an excuse. It is information. It says last weekend drew in people who were not usual suspects, and that is the group a calm weekend and fair trials can still bring back.

If you are in France on Saturday, check your prefecture's own order. The rules are local.
