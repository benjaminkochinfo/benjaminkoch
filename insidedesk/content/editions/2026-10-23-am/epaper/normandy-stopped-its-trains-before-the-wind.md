Storm Austen reaches Normandy and Brittany on Friday with gusts that Météo-France expected at up to 140 km/h on the coast, and 15 départements are on orange alert [1]. The railway did not wait to find out. SNCF said no line in Normandy would run on Friday, with a gradual restart planned from 14:00 on Saturday, subject to safety checks on the track [1]. In Brittany, five lines including Quimper–Brest close until noon; in Hauts-de-France, Amiens–Rouen and three other lines close all day [1].

Around Paris, the RER C, D and E lose sections from the morning, Transilien lines U and V stop from 09:00, and tram T14 does not run at all [1]. The Manche prefecture restricted access to beaches; two MSC cruise ships cancelled their Cherbourg calls; Saint-Malo closed the Route du Rhum village for the morning [1]. Enedis warned of "un risque accru de perturbations du réseau électrique" in Normandy and put its crews on standby [1]. Early gusts on Wednesday evening had already cut power to 135 homes in two villages near Dieppe, which Enedis expected to restore by late Thursday morning [2].

A cancelled train is an inconvenience you can plan around. A tree on a live line at 120 km/h is not. Closing the network in daylight, a day ahead, is the dull decision that keeps Friday's headlines about wind rather than about rescue.
