On Thursday Brent crude jumped 4% to settle at $113.17 a barrel, its highest settlement since May, after a report that the US and Israel were drawing up plans for renewed attacks on Iran [2]. On Friday morning in Asia it barely moved, at $112.96 [3]. That is the headline. The German grid wrote a different one.

Onshore wind turbines in Germany generated 673.1 gigawatt hours on Thursday 22 October, up from 242.3 GWh on Wednesday, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD data [1]. It was the third-best wind day since the start of September. Gas-fired plants, which on Wednesday had burned their way to 241.4 GWh, the highest of the autumn so far, fell to 123.3 GWh [1]. Desk arithmetic: wind up about 2.8 times, gas down by about half, in one day.

[[chart:de-wind-gas-23oct]]

The auction followed the turbines. The day-ahead price for the Germany-Luxembourg zone, which had averaged €211.94 per megawatt hour for Wednesday's delivery, cleared at €148.61 for Thursday and €137.59 for Friday [1]. Desk arithmetic: Friday's power is about 35% cheaper than Wednesday's, in a week when oil rose.

Do not over-read it. The October average is still €167.60 per megawatt hour over 1 to 23 October, against €94.97 over the same days of 2025 [1]. Gas still sets the price in many hours, and the wind already eased overnight: by 03:00 on Friday onshore output was running at about 19 GWh an hour, against a Thursday average of 28 [1]. A windy day does not end an energy shock. It just shows you where the exit is.

That exit is built from paperwork: planning permits, grid connections, auction rounds, transmission lines finished on time. None of it trends. All of it showed up in Thursday's numbers, on a day when a report of strike plans moved oil by 4% and a weather front moved German power the other way. Nothing here is advice.
