Late on Thursday, Washington time, Donald Trump said at the White House that the US had "knocked the hell out of them, and we may do it a little bit further" [1]. A few hours later the Wall Street Journal reported Tehran's answer, given in public and through private channels. If the US and Israel resume their air campaign, Iran would widen its strikes to airports, pipelines and other civilian and economic targets in neighbouring Gulf states [1]. The warnings, the paper said, are meant to deter [1].

**The ledger so far.** The warning is believable because of what has already happened. According to the WSJ report, in the war's first phase Iran hit US bases, embassies and the Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain. It struck energy and industrial sites in the UAE, a major gas plant in Qatar and airports in Dubai, Kuwait and Bahrain [1]. At sea, the IMO counts 120 confirmed incidents and 24 seafarer deaths as of 22 October [2]. October already holds the record month, with 27 attacks on commercial ships [3]. On Thursday night the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile fired at Riyadh [1].

**The binding constraint.** The most useful line in the report is not a threat. It is about stock. US and Arab militaries face declining stocks of air-defence interceptors, the Journal reported, and Israel's supplies are also under pressure. Meanwhile Iran is believed to have resumed limited ballistic-missile production from stockpiled parts [1]. Every threat in this war is ultimately backed by an inventory, and shelves run down before rhetoric does.

**What else is moving.** Not everything points up. Iraq told Riyadh it would not let its territory be used for attacks on the kingdom [1]. On Friday, Turkey hosts foreign ministers from Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Pakistan, Britain, France and Germany for security talks that include Hormuz [1]. Neither will stop a missile. Both are the kind of slow, procedural work that makes the next missile less likely.

**For you.** Read Iran's list and Trump's "a little bit further" as bargaining positions stated in public, not as timetables. The figures worth following are the IMO's confirmed count [2] and any hard number on interceptors. Speeches do not change those figures. Events do.
