The comparison portal StromAuskunft evaluates tariffs in more than 6,300 German towns every day. On Thursday its average for the cheapest new-customer electricity tariffs reached 30.64 cents a kilowatt hour, up just over ten per cent on the month and 13.6% on the year, Berliner Zeitung reported [1].

**The curve.** The year started cheap. In January the figure was 23.84 cents, in February 23.90, helped by €6.5 billion of federal subsidy for the transmission grid charges [1]. It touched 27.24 cents in April, fell back to 23.47 in June, then climbed: 27.03 in August, 27.78 in September, 30.64 now [1]. The reason given is procurement costs: the Iran war and the Hormuz blockade pushed the European gas price at times to €59 a megawatt hour, and gas plants often set the power price [1].

[[chart:de-power-new-customer-23oct]]

**The default.** Basic supply, the tariff you are on if you never chose one, averages 40.39 cents, almost ten cents more [1]. At 3,500 kilowatt hours a year that is about €1,414 against €1,072 in the cheapest tariff, a gap of roughly €341 [1]. In Thuringia, basic supply averages 51.73 cents against 30.26 for the cheapest offer, more than €751 a year apart [1]. Existing customers paid 31.4 cents on average in August, according to Verivox data cited in the report [1].

**The geography.** The cheapest town in the sample is Ringelai in Bavaria at 26.39 cents; the dearest is Lindau on Lake Constance at 40.66 cents [1]. Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, Brandenburg and Bavaria are cheapest; Hamburg, North Rhine-Westphalia and Saarland dearest, mostly because of regional grid charges and competition [1]. Green tariffs cost only 0.54 cents more, about €19 a year at 3,500 kWh [1].

**The constructive reading.** Desk arithmetic: at 3,500 kWh, January's cheapest tariff cost €834 a year and today's €1,072, about €238 more. That is the market. The €341 between basic supply and the cheapest offer is not the market; it is paperwork, and paperwork is the part you control. Note that new-customer prices move daily, and a contract signed today fixes today's figure, not January's. Nothing here is advice.
