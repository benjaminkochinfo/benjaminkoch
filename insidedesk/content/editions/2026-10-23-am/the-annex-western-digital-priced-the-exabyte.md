**The document.** Western Digital, which makes the hard drives that fill cloud and AI data centres, published its fiscal first-quarter results on Thursday evening, for the three months to 2 October [1]. It is a short release with two tables, an outlook box and a dividend line. Read slowly, it is a lesson in what scarcity does to a manufacturer's accounts.

**The headline.** Revenue was $4.38bn, up 55% on a year earlier and 17% on the quarter [1]. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $4.64, up 161% [1]. FactSet's consensus had been $4.14bn and $4.11 a share, SquawkNews reported [2]. Chief financial officer Kris Sennesael named the cause plainly: "robust exabyte growth and improved pricing" [1]. More storage sold, at better prices.

**The margin line.** GAAP gross margin rose to 57.8% from 43.5% a year earlier; operating margin to 46.7% from 28.1% [1]. Desk arithmetic on the non-GAAP table: revenue rose by $1,563m year on year, operating income by $1,297m. About 83 cents of every extra dollar of sales reached operating profit. That is not what volume alone does. It is what price does when the factories already exist.

**The line that fell.** GAAP diluted earnings per share fell 44% on the quarter, to $4.63 from $8.21 [1]. That is not a bad quarter. Last quarter's GAAP net income of $3.20bn sat $1.81bn above its non-GAAP figure of $1.38bn, Desk arithmetic on the release's own table; this quarter the two are the same, $1.78bn [1]. A one-off flattered the comparison base. The release prints both columns side by side, which is how it should be done.

**The cash.** Operating cash flow was $1.60bn and free cash flow $1.38bn [1]. The board raised the quarterly dividend 17%, to $0.175 a share, payable on 17 December [1].

**The outlook.** WD guides fiscal second-quarter revenue to $5.1bn, plus or minus $100m, which it says is 66% to 72% above a year earlier, with a non-GAAP gross margin of 60–61% [1]. Guidance is the company's, not ours.

**The constructive reading.** The AI story usually gets told through chips. This release tells it through the boring part: somewhere the data has to be kept, and the people who make the drives are, for now, being paid properly for them. Nothing here is advice; it is a reading of a company's own release.
