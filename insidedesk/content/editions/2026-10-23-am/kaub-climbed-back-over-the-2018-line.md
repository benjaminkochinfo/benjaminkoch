Yesterday this desk reported that the Rhine gauge at Kaub had spent a month below 25 cm, the lowest daily reading of its 2010–2020 reference period, set on 22 October 2018 [2]. Overnight the line was crossed. At 23:45 on Thursday the gauge read 26 cm, its first reading at or above 25 cm in the 32 days of data the federal waterways administration publishes [1]. At 05:00 on Friday it read 28 cm [1].

[[chart:kaub-back-above-2018-23oct]]

**The arithmetic.** Desk arithmetic on the 15-minute readings: Thursday's full daily mean came to 19.1 cm, still below the 2018 line, as every daily mean since at least 22 September has been [1]. Friday's mean from midnight to 05:00 is 26.3 cm [1]. That is five hours, not a day, and the readings are raw data that can still be revised [1]. Discharge at Kaub was 555 cubic metres a second at 04:45, against 509 at 16:00 on Thursday [2].

**Upstream.** The rise is not a local blip. At Mainz the gauge read 106 cm at 05:15 on Wednesday and 125 cm at 05:00 on Friday; at Maxau, near Karlsruhe, 295 cm and 306 cm [3]. Water at Kaub is water that passed those gauges first.

**What it is not.** 28 cm is still 180 cm below Kaub's long-run mean of 208 cm, and 49 cm short of the 77 cm equivalent water level at which the shipping channel is guaranteed to be 1.90 m deep [2]. Desk arithmetic. Barges still load light. A low that has stopped getting worse is not the same as a river that works.

**The constructive reading.** The turn was visible to anyone at 23:45, the moment it happened, on a free public feed [1]. Shippers do not need a press release to know when to load an extra few hundred tonnes; they need the next quarter-hour. That is what the gauge gives them, and this morning it gave them a better number than any of the last thirty days.
