Brent crude futures fell $2.27, or 2%, to $111 a barrel early on Monday, and US WTI dropped 2.32% to $102, the Economic Times reported [1]. The trigger was diplomacy: Iranian and Qatari officials held a third consecutive day of talks aimed at ending the war [1]. Brent rose 13.6% in October, Reuters noted, so Monday's fall takes back only a small part of that [2].

OPEC+ agreed over the weekend to keep its production targets unchanged through December, NDTV Profit reported [3].

**Three numbers for the range.** Goldman Sachs estimates crude could rise to $120 if attacks on ships in the Middle East escalate, or fall towards $80 if exports return to normal, the Economic Times reported [1]. Saxo Bank expects a $70 to $90 range even after the conflict ends, against $60 to $70 before the war [1]. Neither is a promise; both are stated assumptions you can check later.

**The physical side.** Saudi Arabia raised its crude exports in October from September despite Houthi attacks on its energy infrastructure, according to two of three ship-tracking companies [1]. Last Thursday an unidentified projectile hit a tanker leaving the Strait of Hormuz, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations [1].

Talks move prices in a morning. Cargoes move them for a season. Nothing here is investment advice.
