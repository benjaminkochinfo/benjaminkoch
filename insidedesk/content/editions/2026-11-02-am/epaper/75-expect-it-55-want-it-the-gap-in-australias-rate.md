Forecasting and judging are different jobs. Finder's monthly panel of 40 economists and experts shows the difference. 75% (30 of 40) expect the Reserve Bank of Australia to raise its cash rate on Tuesday, to 4.85% [1]. Only 55% (22 of 40) say it should [1].

**What it costs.** Finder estimates that a quarter-point rise adds A$55 a month, or A$658 a year, to repayments on a A$500,000 mortgage [1]. Since January the cash rate would have risen 1.25 percentage points, which Finder puts at roughly A$258 a month on the same loan [1]. In Finder's own consumer tracker, 37% of mortgage holders said they struggled to pay their home loan in October [1].

**The case for waiting.** Bendigo Bank's David Robertson tipped a hold. He said that "having already increased rates 4 times this year the RBA can pause" [1]. Shane Oliver of AMP expects a rise, saying the RBA has "little choice" after September's inflation data [1].

**The quieter finding.** Just over a third of those who answered (12 of 34) now think a recession in the next six months is likely, up from 30% in September [1].

A survey is not a decision, and the board meets with more data than any panel. But a published poll with named people and stated reasons is a useful thing: you can check them afterwards. Nothing here is investment advice.
