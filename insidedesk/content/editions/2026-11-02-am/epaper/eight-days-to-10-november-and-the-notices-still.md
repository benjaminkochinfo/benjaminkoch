In September both Washington and Beijing said their trade truce, due to lapse on 10 November, would be extended to 10 January 2027 [1]. As of 1 November, a tracker of the legal texts finds only one formal step: a US Trade Representative proposal to keep the maritime port-fee action suspended to 9 January, with comments closed on 30 October and no final notice yet [1]. China's own suspension notice still reads 10 November [1].

What hangs on the old dates: 178 US Section 301 exclusions expiring at 11:59 p.m. EDT on 9 November (04:59 CET on 10 November), and China's October 2025 export-control package on rare earths and battery materials [1]. For you, if you import, the advice is old and dull: price your November entries on the law as published, not as announced. Speeches move markets; notices move customs.
