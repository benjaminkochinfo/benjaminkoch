The headline said "mixed". The detail said "selective". Japanese chipmaker Kioxia fell more than 15% after a cautious outlook that missed forecasts, AFP reported via CNA, and is now down around 70% from its June record while still up more than 200% this year [1]. Samsung fell more than 1%; SK hynix rose more than 1%; TSMC added nearly 2% [1].

By midday Tokyo, Sydney, Singapore and Taipei were down, while Seoul, Hong Kong and Shanghai were up [1]. Earlier in the session Reuters had the Nikkei 0.5% lower and South Korea 0.8% lower, after Korean shares shed 16% in October [2]. Morning trade turns quickly; the closing prints are the ones to keep.

**The worry has a number.** Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta are on track to spend more than $700 billion on capital investment this year, according to analyst estimates cited by CNA [1]. Goldman Sachs analysts put hyperscaler capex near $800 billion this year and $1.2 trillion in 2027, Reuters reported [2]. "Earnings growth is strong, but it is no longer accelerating," wrote Goldman's Ben Snider [2].

**The quieter number.** Bloomberg counted 90% of S&P 500 software companies beating profit expectations this season but only 70% topping revenue, CNA reported [1]. Profits are an estimate; sales are invoices. When the two drift apart, the invoices are the ones worth reading.

Nothing here is investment advice.
