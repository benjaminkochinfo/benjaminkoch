On Sunday, 1 November, eight AIS-visible commercial ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz, according to the hormuz.now counter: five inbound into the Gulf, three outbound to the Gulf of Oman [1]. None of them was a tanker; all eight were bulk carriers or general cargo ships [1]. One of them, the bulk carrier Forever Happy, crossed twice, outbound at 02:17 CET and back inbound at 05:54 CET, both bound for Sharjah [1].

The counter puts its seven-day average at 9.0 crossings a day, against a pre-crisis baseline of 84.8 [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 11% of normal traffic, and Sunday's eight is a little below even that. The declared destinations are short hops (Sharjah, Sohar, Al Suwaiq, Kandla), the trade of a neighbourhood rather than an ocean [1].

Two cautions belong next to any single count. First, it measures only ships with their transponders on and above the counter's commercial threshold; it logged eight further small-craft crossings it left out [1]. Second, the slower, official cross-check, IMF PortWatch, has not published this day yet; its latest figure is for 25 October [1].

For you, the useful part is the zero. Oil prices are quoted by the minute; the tankers that would ease them are counted one by one, and on Sunday the count for them was nil. Nothing here is investment advice.
