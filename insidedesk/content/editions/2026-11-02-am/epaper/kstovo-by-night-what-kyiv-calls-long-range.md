On Sunday the governor of Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region, Gleb Nikitin, said an "industrial enterprise" in the Kstovo district had been hit by a "massive drone attack" overnight, causing "significant damage" [1]. He said more than 100 drones were destroyed and no casualties had been reported so far [1].

Ukrainian media reports said the strike hit the LukOil refinery in Kstovo and a combined heat and power plant; RFE/RL said it could not independently verify that [1]. Video shared on Sunday appears to show the refinery in flames [1]. Less than 24 hours earlier, Ukraine's military said it had targeted the Kombinat Kamensky chemical and defence enterprise in Russia's Rostov region with drones and Neptune missiles, and Zelensky said that attack caused "tens of millions of dollars" of damage [1].

Kyiv calls these strikes "long-range sanctions" [1]. The phrase is worth taking literally. A sanction works through inventories: a refinery that cannot run is fuel that is not shipped, taxed or burned at the front, and the effect shows up weeks later in export volumes and pump prices, not in the night's video.

So keep two columns. In the first, what officials on each side say, attributed. In the second, what independent records later confirm: refinery run rates, export loadings, repair notices. The second column is slower and duller. It is also the one that tells you whether the "sanction" bit.
