From Sunday, South Africa's immunisation programme pays a little less for four vaccines [1]. The Department of Health attributes the cuts, signed on 14 October, to favourable exchange-rate changes [1]. Under the contract, the rotavirus vaccine falls from R124.46 to R122.32, the HPV vaccine from R372.77 to R366.38, a combined tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis booster from R152.28 to R150.01, and BCG from R150.85 to R148.24 [1]. The reductions are about 1.5% to 1.7% [1].

These are prices per contract unit, including VAT and delivery, not charges to patients, and the notices give no quantities, so no total saving can be worked out [1]. The useful lesson is elsewhere: when a contract is written to pass currency gains back to the buyer, the buyer notices. Most vaccine news is about fear. This one is about an invoice.
