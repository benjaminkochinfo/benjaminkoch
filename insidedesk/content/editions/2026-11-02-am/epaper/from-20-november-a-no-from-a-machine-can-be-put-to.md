In 18 days Germany starts applying the EU's revised consumer credit directive, known as CCD II, to every credit contract signed from 20 November [1]. The headline part is familiar: small loans up to €200, some interest-free financing and many "buy now, pay later" offers now come under credit law, and lenders must check more carefully whether you can repay [1].

**The clause worth remembering.** Credit checks are increasingly automated. If a decision rests on automated processing of your personal data, you can, under the legal conditions, ask for an explanation and a review by a human [1]. The European Consumer Centre Germany, the EVZ, names it as a tip in its own right: if an application is refused automatically, ask for the reasons and whether a human review is due [1]. Lenders may also no longer use data on health, ethnic origin, political or religious views, or from your social media [1].

**The clause worth watching.** The so-called "eternal" right of withdrawal largely ends. Where required contract information is missing, withdrawal now lapses at the latest 12 months and 14 days after signing, unless you were never told of the right at all [1]. Overdrafts also gain a withdrawal right [1].

**For you.** Before 20 November nothing changes for contracts already signed [1]. After it, add up all open instalments, not just the monthly one, as the EVZ advises, and keep the paperwork [1]. A rule only protects people who know it exists. Nothing here is investment advice.
