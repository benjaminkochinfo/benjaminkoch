Overnight into Sunday, Russia launched 433 drones at Ukraine, including jet-powered Shaheds; Ukraine's air force said it downed 371 [1]. Drones struck 16 locations and debris fell in 14 more [1]. Regional authorities reported one person killed, in Sloviansk, and at least 47 injured across the country, among them three children in Dnipropetrovsk region and a 16-year-old girl in Kharkiv region [1]. Desk arithmetic: 62 drones got through the air defences, about one in seven.

The target was again the grid. President Volodymyr Zelensky named substations, transformers and power lines in four regions, Dnipro, Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr and Khmelnytskyi, and said "hundreds of thousands of families remain without electricity" [2]. Three workers from the energy company DTEK were injured, and DTEK introduced emergency outages in Kyiv [1]. In Sumy region, the administration said air-raid sirens sounded for 23 hours 57 minutes of the day [1].

Zelensky also gave the week's ledger: 1,940 strike drones, more than 1,580 glide bombs and 44 missiles, 36 of them ballistic [2]. "The Russians are trying to take maximum advantage of the shortage of ballistic interceptors," he said, asking allies to implement air-defence decisions "now" [1][2]. That is a supply-chain sentence as much as a military one: the war's winter will be decided partly in factories and delivery schedules far from Ukraine.

What went right is quieter. Crews are "working to restore damaged equipment, when it is safe to do so", DTEK said [1]; Zelensky said repair crews were "working everywhere" [2]. Each restored line is a small, unreported victory over a much louder weapon. For you, the useful number is not the drones launched but the hours without power, and who is paid to shorten them.
