This is a committee week. Markets are betting on rate rises from the Reserve Bank of Australia, the Bank of England and Sweden's Riksbank, and on Norway's Norges Bank holding, Reuters reported [3]. None of that is a decision yet. It is a price, and prices can be wrong.

**Tuesday, Sydney.** Of 40 economists and experts polled by Finder, 30 expect the RBA to lift its cash rate on Tuesday from 4.60% to 4.85% [2]. That would be the fifth rise this year. Headline inflation was 4.3% in September and the trimmed mean 3.6%, above the RBA's 2–3% target band, as panellist Jeffrey Sheen noted while arguing for a hold [2].

**Thursday, London.** Economists polled by Reuters expect the Bank of England to raise Bank Rate to 4.0% from 3.75%, its first rise since August 2023, probably on a 7–2 vote [1]. UK consumer prices rose 3.5% in the year to September, against the Bank's July forecast of 3.0% [1]. Markets price three to four more quarter-point rises in 2027, which Reuters says even the committee's hawks may baulk at [1]. "Their hands are increasingly being forced," said Rabobank's Stefan Koopman [1].

**The detail worth reading.** Oxford Economics' Alexander Harvey said individual members' statements would offer important clues [1]. That is the dull machinery at work: minutes, votes and forecasts, published on a timetable, with names attached. A rise you can see coming is less painful than one you can't, and a committee that says plainly how far it intends to go saves the market from guessing. In the US, futures still imply a 72% chance of a Fed rise in December, Reuters reported [3].

**For you.** If you hold a tracker mortgage or savings priced off one of these rates, Tuesday and Thursday are the dates to note. Nothing here is investment advice.
