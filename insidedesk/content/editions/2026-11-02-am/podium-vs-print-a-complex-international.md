**The podium.** On Saturday China's National Bureau of Statistics reported that its official manufacturing purchasing managers' index fell 0.8 points to 49.3 in October [1]. Huo Lihui, a chief statistician with the bureau, gave two reasons, Xinhua reported: some demand had been brought forward ahead of the National Day holiday, and "a complex international environment" [1]. The rest of the official reading leaned on the bright side: high-tech manufacturing at 51.3, equipment at 50.6, and railway, shipbuilding, aerospace and electronics above 53 on both production and new orders [1].

**The print.** The sub-index that measures the "international environment" most directly is new export orders. It fell to 48.7 from 50.0 in September [2]. The headline reading also missed the 50.0 that economists polled by Reuters had expected, and the non-manufacturing index slipped to 49.7, with construction at 48.0 [2]. Below 50 means more purchasing managers reported falling orders than rising ones.

[[chart:china-pmi-falls-02nov]]

**The gap.** The statistician and the index do not contradict each other; the index simply sizes the phrase. Desk arithmetic: export orders fell 1.3 points, more than the headline's 0.8. A holiday that pulls demand forward should show up at home as much as abroad, yet total new orders fell 1.1 points to 49.4 [1]. And the pain is not shared evenly: large firms stayed at 50.2, medium-sized firms read 48.4 and small firms 47.5 [2]. The shipbuilders, aerospace and electronics makers are still expanding; the small workshop is where "complex" lands.

**What the speech left out.** Prices. The purchasing-price index fell 4 points to 56.8 and factory-gate prices eased 2.3 points to 51.7 [1]. For you, buying from China, that is the useful line: input costs are still rising, but more slowly, and a supplier with thin export orders has more reason to talk. Read the sub-indices before the commentary. Nothing here is investment advice.
