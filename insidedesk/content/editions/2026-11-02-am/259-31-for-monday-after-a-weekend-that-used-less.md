Monday's power was priced on Sunday, and it is not cheap. The day-ahead auction set an average of €259.31 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg on Monday 2 November, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD figures [1]. That follows €251.42 on Saturday and €255.91 on Sunday [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-02nov]]

**The weekend that didn't discount.** Grid load fell, as it does when factories and offices close: 1,349.8 gigawatt hours on Friday, 1,168.2 on Saturday and 1,112.5 on Sunday [1]. Desk arithmetic: Sunday used about 17.6% less electricity than Friday, while its price was about 4.7% lower than Friday's €268.62 [1]. Less demand, almost the same bill.

**The reason is in the air.** Onshore wind turbines generated 688.8 gigawatt hours on Monday 26 October; this Sunday, 79.0 [1]. Offshore wind managed 3.5 gigawatt hours on Saturday, against 147.9 on Tuesday 27 October [1]. When the wind drops, dearer plants run to fill the gap, and they set the price.

**The shape of Monday.** The cheapest hour is 12:00 to 13:00 CET at €210.00. The dearest is 07:00 to 08:00 CET at €328.35, as the working week switches on [1]. Desk arithmetic: the morning peak costs about 56% more than lunchtime.

**The calm point.** Every one of these numbers was published before the hours they describe, free for anyone to read. If you have a dynamic tariff, the midday hours are where the dishwasher belongs today. Nothing here is investment advice.
