**Document one: the refinery release.** On Friday the economy ministry opened a dialogue with the mineral-oil industry on the future of Germany's refineries, to be turned into a strategy paper for the cabinet [1]. Line by line:

* *The inventory.* Eleven refinery sites, a processing capacity of a little over 92 million tonnes of crude a year [1]. This is the most useful sentence in the release, because it is a count.
* *The claim.* The loss of these sites "cannot be compensated by imports" and would threaten the chemical companies that depend on them [1]. A strong sentence. It is an argument, not yet a finding.
* *The bill.* The plants must convert step by step to sustainable feedstocks, which needs "investments in the billions" [1]. The release does not say whose billions.
* *The method.* A cross-ministry study on what a resilient energy system needs, and how resilience can be anchored in planning and administration [1]. Count, then study, then decide: the correct order, and a slow one.

**Document two: the reserve regulation.** The seventh regulation on releasing stocks of Germany's oil stockholding agency took effect on Thursday [2][3]. Line by line:

* *The number.* The agency may temporarily reduce its required stock from 90 days of cover to 82 [2][3]. Eight days of national cover are being lent to the market.
* *The share.* Under the International Energy Agency's key, Germany contributes 19.5 million of a 100-million-barrel release; 6 million had been released by October [2].
* *The order.* For the remaining 13.5 million barrels, the ministry will start with diesel and heating oil [2]. Those are the products that fill the tank and the boiler.
* *The length.* Two sections, with the consent of the Bundesrat [3]. Brevity is a virtue in a regulation.

**Reading them together.** The first document is about the 2030s; the second is about November. One has a study attached, the other a number of days. On a week when Brent settled at $116.72, its highest since April [4], it was the shorter text that put barrels where people buy them.

**The constructive reading.** Neither document is dramatic. Both begin with a count, sites and tonnes in one, days and barrels in the other. A state that knows what it has before it decides what to do is doing energy policy the dull, right way round. Nothing here is advice.
