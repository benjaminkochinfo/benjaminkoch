**Orléans.** Jordan Bardella, the only candidate, was re-elected president of the Rassemblement national at the party's congress in Orléans on Saturday with 98.98% of the votes cast [1][2]. That was 75,497 votes, against 779 blank ballots, on a turnout of about 68% [1][2]. Members had voted electronically from 21 September to 22 October [1][2]. Outside, 13,500 people marched against the party, and the Interior Ministry reported no incidents [3].

**What the number measures.** Not popularity in the country. A single-candidate vote tells you two things: how many members bothered to vote, and how few chose to spoil their ballot. About a third did not vote at all [1]. Desk arithmetic: of members entitled to vote, roughly two in three said yes. That is a strong showing for discipline and a weak one for drama, which may be exactly what the party wanted.

**Berlin, the same week.** On Thursday the sixteen state premiers asked the federal government to cut the electricity tax for everyone "as quickly as possible" [4]. On Friday the deputy government spokesman said the goal stands, "under a financing proviso", and named no date [4]. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil had said that anyone proposing a cut should bring proposals for how to pay for it [4].

**The common thread.** Both stories are about the difference between a mandate and a ledger. A party can give its leader 98.98% in a month of voting. A coalition can agree a goal in a single sentence. Neither has yet said where the money for its promises comes from, and that is the question voters in both countries face at the till this autumn.

**The constructive reading.** Both systems are doing the dull part properly. A party published its vote count, blank ballots included. A federal spokesman said out loud that a tax cut needs funding. Neither is exciting. Both are the kind of sentence you can hold someone to later.
