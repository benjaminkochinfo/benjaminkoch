Japan's core inflation, which excludes fresh food, rose to 2.1% in September from 1.7% in August, its highest this year and in line with a Reuters poll, CNBC reported [1]. The "core-core" measure, which also excludes energy and is the Bank of Japan's preferred gauge, was 2.4% [1]. Rice prices fell 20.6% on the year [1]. For a country that spent decades trying to get prices to rise at all, a gauge sitting just above target is the quiet success of the week.
