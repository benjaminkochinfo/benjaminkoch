Europe's imports of liquefied natural gas are estimated at 9.11 million tonnes for October, up from 7.76 million in September but down from 10.59 million in October 2025, according to Kpler data cited in a Reuters column [1]. EU gas storage was 74.6% full on 21 October, 11 points below a year earlier [1].

Asia is buying instead. Its October arrivals are estimated at 23.31 million tonnes, the most since July, even though spot LNG for north Asia reached $29.30 per million British thermal units in the week to 16 October, the highest since December 2023 [1]. Japan's utilities held 2.54 million tonnes in inventory on 18 October, 36% more than a year earlier [1].

The column calls Europe's modest buying "perhaps surprising" [1]. Forecasters expect a strong El Niño this winter, which usually means milder, wetter weather for Europe [1]. That is a forecast, not a contract. Japan bought its insurance before the bill arrived; Europe is partly betting on the weather. This desk does not forecast winters. It notes which of the two strategies leaves less to luck. Nothing here is advice.
