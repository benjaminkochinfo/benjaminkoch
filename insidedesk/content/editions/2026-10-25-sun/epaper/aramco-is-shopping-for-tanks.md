Saudi Arabia and Aramco are looking for partners to expand oil storage outside the kingdom, chief executive Amin Nasser told Al Arabiya Business on Thursday, because the Iran war has exposed weak points in the kingdom's energy network [1]. Aramco is also studying more export routes: Hormuz, Bab al-Mandab and Suez have all proved vulnerable [1].

The numbers behind the decision are large. Citing the IEA, the report says Saudi output fell below 7 million barrels a day for much of the war's first six months, from more than 10 million before it, and recovered to about 8 million in September [1]. The East-West pipeline to the Red Sea, with capacity of about 7 million barrels a day, has been the bypass; drones launched from Iraq struck it in September [1].

Mr Nasser has said that refilling the world's drawn-down inventories while meeting normal demand could take up to two years [1].
