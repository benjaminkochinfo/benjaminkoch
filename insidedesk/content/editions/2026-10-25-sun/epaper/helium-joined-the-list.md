The US Department of Energy designated helium a critical material on Friday [1]. Its notice calls the gas "non-substitutable" for chipmaking, for leak-testing gas pipelines and for quantum computing [2]. The last addition to the list was steelmaking coal, in May 2025 [2].

Europe reads the news from the buying side. Qatar's exports have been disrupted by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, Russia restricted exports in April, and China imposed a temporary export ban in July, Euronews reported; the EU has banned direct imports of Russian helium since 2024 [3].

The department's own release names the domestic bottleneck: limited refining capacity and long permitting, not a shortage of gas in the ground [1]. That is the useful sentence. Chips are made from silicon, light and a surprising list of ordinary industrial gases. A designation does not refine anything; a permit desk that clears its pile does. Watch the plants approved, not the statements.
