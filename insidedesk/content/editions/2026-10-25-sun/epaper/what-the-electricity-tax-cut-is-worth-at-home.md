The electricity tax for households is 2.05 cents per kilowatt hour; the EU minimum is 0.1 cent [1]. Cutting it to the minimum for all consumers would cost about €5.4 billion a year, by the finance ministry's estimate [1]. So far the cut applies only to manufacturing and to agriculture and forestry [1].

At home, including VAT, a full cut would save about 2.32 cents per kilowatt hour if suppliers pass it on [1]. That is roughly €58 a year for a two-person household using 2,500 kWh, €81 for three people at 3,500 kWh and €104 for a family of four at 4,500 kWh [1].

On Friday the deputy government spokesman said the goal of implementing the cut stands, but "under a financing proviso", and gave no date [2]. Desk arithmetic: for a family of four, each year without the cut is about €2 a week. Not nothing, and not a reason to wait for it before you plan your winter. The cut becomes real on the day someone publishes where the €5.4 billion comes from.
