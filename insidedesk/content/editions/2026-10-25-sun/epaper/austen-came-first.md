Storm Austen, the first named storm of the season, reached western Europe on Friday. In France it left 288,600 homes without electricity at its height, franceinfo reported, and disrupted airports and railways [1]. The maritime prefecture for the Channel and North Sea warned of winds of force 8 to 9, sometimes 10, from the Belgian border to La Hague, and told leisure sailors not to go out "under any pretext" [1]. In Britain, the Met Office recorded gusts of 76 mph at St Mary's Airport on the Isles of Scilly and 74 mph at Aberporth in Wales [2].

Then Boelo followed within a day [2]. Two storms in quick succession are harder on networks than one big one, because crews restore lines only to see them cut again.

The constructive part is the paperwork that preceded both: named storms, warnings issued a day or more ahead, sailors told to stay in harbour. A forecast that keeps a boat tied up is the cheapest safety equipment there is.
