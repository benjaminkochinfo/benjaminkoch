Initial claims for US unemployment benefits fell to a seasonally adjusted 194,000 in the week to 17 October, the Labor Department reported on Thursday [1]. Economists polled by Dow Jones had expected 200,000 [2]. The year's low, 189,000 in the week of 18 July, was the lowest since the late 1960s, and the four-week average is now 197,750 [1].

Two readings sit inside the number. The comfortable one: American employers are not laying people off, despite oil above $100 and a sell-off in technology shares. The less comfortable one, for the Federal Reserve, which meets this week: a labour market this firm leaves room for wages to follow energy prices, the second-round effect central banks fear most. Markets expect no move in October and a quarter-point rise in December [1].

Neither reading is a forecast. A weekly claims count is noisy, and one week moves little. Its virtue is that it arrives every Thursday, before the speeches, counted from actual applications. Nothing here is advice.
