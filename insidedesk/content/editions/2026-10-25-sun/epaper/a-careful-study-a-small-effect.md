A preregistered study from the University of Barcelona, published in Psychological Medicine, scanned 116 naturally cycling women aged 18 to 35 while they did an approach-avoid task [1]. Higher salivary progesterone went with less distinct activity patterns in two brain regions linked to emotional control, with correlations of about −0.25 [1]. Neither hormone was linked to speed or accuracy on the task, and estradiol showed no link at all [1]. An indirect path to worse recent mood explained 7.1% of its variation [1].

"Progesterone is certainly not a master switch," said the lead author, Macià Buades-Rotger [1].

The study is worth your Sunday for its method rather than its headline. Preregistration means the researchers said in advance what they would test, which makes it harder to find patterns after the fact. The effect is real and small, and behaviour on the task did not change. That is how most honest findings in psychology look. Be wary of anyone who turns a correlation of −0.25 into a rule about half the population.
