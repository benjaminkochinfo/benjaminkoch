The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak has reached Nord Ubangi, an eighth province of Congo, on the border with the Central African Republic, UN News reported [1]. In Mangina, a rural commune in North Kivu, a WHO team arrived after negotiating access; "There was a lot of community preoccupation and community resistance in the beginning," said Anne Ancia, the WHO's representative in Congo [1].

There is still no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for this strain [2]. Researchers in Congo are studying whether Ervebo, which works against the Zaire strain, also protects against Bundibugyo [1]. "We still do not have preliminary data yet," said Patrick Otim of the WHO's emergency programme [1].

In Nigeria, the health minister said "high risk" contacts of the country's first case had been quarantined in a facility, with no evidence of community spread [2].

A trial that reports no data yet is not failing. It is answering a hard question in the only order that produces a trustworthy answer: enrol, follow, measure, publish. Nothing here is medical advice.
