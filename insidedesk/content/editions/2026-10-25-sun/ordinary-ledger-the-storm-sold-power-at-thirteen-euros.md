The Ordinary Ledger reads the week's prices in the order a household meets them. This week two of them went in opposite directions, and only one moved fast.

**Power, wholesale.** Germany's day-ahead electricity auction cleared at a daily average of €211.94 per megawatt hour for Wednesday, then €148.61 for Thursday, €137.59 for Friday, €30.12 for Saturday and €13.12 for Sunday [1]. Desk arithmetic: Sunday's power costs about one sixteenth of Wednesday's.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-25oct]]

**Why.** The weather, not a minister. Onshore wind turbines generated 242,281 megawatt hours on Wednesday and 1,009,174 on Saturday; gas-fired plants fell from 241,432 to 42,456 [2]. Desk arithmetic: wind about four times higher, gas down to about a sixth. When the wind supplies most of the demand, the expensive gas plant no longer sets the price, and the auction clears low.

**Why you will not see it this month.** One windy weekend is not a cheap month. Over 1 to 25 October the day-ahead price averaged €155.92, against €89.11 over the same days of 2025 [1]. Desk arithmetic: about three quarters higher. Most households pay a tariff their supplier bought months ahead. The wholesale line is next year's weather report, not this month's bill.

**Fuel, at the pump.** ADAC's weekly reading on Wednesday put Super E10 at €2.110 a litre, down 4.1 cents on the week, and diesel at €2.255, up 0.8 cents [3]. ADAC said most of the tax cut now appears to be reaching petrol pumps [3]. Desk arithmetic: a 50-litre diesel tank cost about €112.75, a 50-litre E10 tank about €105.50. Brent rose 6% after that reading was taken, so the next one may not be as kind.

**Heating oil.** On Friday afternoon the German average stood at €174.25 per 100 litres, €4.96 more than a week earlier and €40.21 more than three months earlier [4]. No wind farm helps a tank in the cellar.

**The ledger, totalled.** Down sharply: wholesale power, for a weekend. Down a little: petrol. Up a little: diesel. Up steadily: heating oil. The fast-moving price was the one you mostly do not pay directly; the ones you do pay moved slowly, and mostly up.

**The constructive reading.** Every number here was free and public by Sunday morning: an auction result and a generation count from the regulator's own platform, a weekly pump average, a daily heating-oil price. You can see your winter coming in more detail than any household could a generation ago. Nothing here is advice.
