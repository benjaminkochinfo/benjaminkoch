**The count.** As of 21 October the Democratic Republic of the Congo had reported 9,704 confirmed cases of Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus, including 4,687 deaths, in data up to 20 October [1]. In the latest reporting interval there were 67 new confirmed cases and 41 deaths, in Ituri, North Kivu and Tshopo [1]. Ituri remains the centre, with 7,040 cases [1]. 2,595 people who tested positive have recovered [1]. ECDC says the outbreak is probably larger than reported, because of gaps in data and contact follow-up [1].

**Beyond Congo.** Kenya has nine confirmed cases, including one death, all linked to a first case imported from Congo; 134 of 135 contacts are in quarantine [1]. Nigeria reported its first case on 20 October, a woman who flew in from Uganda, and 44 close contacts are being monitored [1].

**What Europe was told.** ECDC rates the likelihood of infection as very low for people living in the EU and EEA [1]. It also said something that runs against instinct: screening returning travellers "would not be effective" in keeping the virus out, judging from the 2013–2016 West African outbreak [1]. What works is giving travellers clear information on symptoms and on what to do if they fall ill after arrival [1]. That is a less visible policy than a thermometer at the gate, and a better one.

**What is missing.** There is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo virus [1]. Congo began vaccinating frontline workers in August with Ervebo, made for a different Ebola species, mainly within research protocols [1].

**Closer to home.** WHO Europe reminded the region on Friday that the flu season usually starts between mid-November and mid-December [2]. It recommends annual vaccination for older people, pregnant women, young children, people with chronic conditions and health workers, because last year's protection wanes and the vaccine is updated each year [2].

**The constructive reading.** The Ebola numbers are grim, and they are numbers: published, broken down by health zone, compared week on week. Kenya found 134 of 135 contacts. That is what a working response looks like. The flu advice is the same idea at a smaller scale: a known season, a known vaccine, a date to book. Nothing here is medical advice; ask your doctor.
