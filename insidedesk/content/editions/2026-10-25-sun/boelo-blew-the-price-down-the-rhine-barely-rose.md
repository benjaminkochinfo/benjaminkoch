**The storm.** Boelo, the second named storm of the season, reached Britain on Saturday after Austen on Friday [1][2]. The BBC reported gusts of 85 mph overnight at Machrihanish in western Scotland and at The Needles off the Isle of Wight [1]. Amber wind warnings covered Scotland, the far north of England and parts of Northern Ireland into Sunday [1]. Trains and ferries were cancelled in Scotland, and dozens of flood warnings and alerts were in place [1]. As of 06:30 on Sunday, National Grid said 10,860 properties still had no electricity; on Saturday it said more than 124,000 had been reconnected since the storms began [1].

**The power price.** The same weather system blew across the North Sea. Germany's onshore wind turbines generated 1,009,174 megawatt hours on Saturday, roughly four times Wednesday's 242,281 [4]. The day-ahead electricity price fell to €30.12 for Saturday and €13.12 for Sunday, from €211.94 on Wednesday [4]. Our Ordinary Ledger has the arithmetic, and why it will not reach your bill this month.

**The river.** The Rhine at Kaub, the Middle Rhine gauge that barge operators watch, spent four weeks below 25 centimetres, the lowest daily reading of the 2010–2020 reference period, set on 22 October 2018 [3]. On Friday its daily mean of 15-minute readings reached 25.9 cm, just above that line; on Saturday it was back to 23.8 cm [3]. By 08:45 on Sunday it read 27 cm [3]. Desk arithmetic: the daily means are averages of the published 15-minute values.

**Why the storm did not fill the river.** Boelo's warnings, for wind and for rain, were for Britain and Ireland [1][2]. The Rhine is fed from the Alps, the Black Forest and the hills along its course. A storm can make the news in Glasgow and leave the gauge in Kaub almost where it was. That is why this desk reads the gauge, not the weather map.

**The constructive reading.** Three ledgers, three honest numbers: homes reconnected in Britain, megawatt hours of wind in Germany, centimetres of water at Kaub. Each was published by a public body, for free, within hours. Weather cannot be managed. Its bills can be read in advance, and that is most of what preparation is.
