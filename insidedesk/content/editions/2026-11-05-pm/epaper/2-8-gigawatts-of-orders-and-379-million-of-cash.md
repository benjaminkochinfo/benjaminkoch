Nordex, the Hamburg wind-turbine maker, published its third quarter on Thursday morning, and the numbers are those of a business that has learned to price its work. Sales rose 28.2% to about €2.19 billion; EBITDA more than doubled to €278.2 million, a margin of 12.7% against 8.0% a year earlier [1]. Net profit almost tripled to €147.1 million [1]. Free cash flow was €379.1 million [1].

**The order book.** Orders for new turbines reached 2,810 MW, up 29.5%, from ten countries [1]. The average selling price held at €0.90 million per megawatt [1]. The backlog stood at €19.3 billion at the end of September, against €14.9 billion a year earlier [1]. The full-year EBITDA margin target, already raised in October, is now 11.5% to 12.5% [1].

**The part that is not about money.** Nordex installed 331 turbines with 2,021 MW in the quarter, against 420 turbines and 2,576 MW a year earlier [1]. The company names the reasons: delays on the customer side, in obtaining transport permits, and in blade production in Turkey [1]. Desk arithmetic: installed capacity fell by about 22%, while orders rose by almost 30%.

That gap is the energy transition in miniature. Demand and finance are there. What paces the build-out is paperwork for heavy loads, factory capacity and the patience of local authorities. Those are fixable, but only by the dull people who fix them. Nothing here is investment advice.
