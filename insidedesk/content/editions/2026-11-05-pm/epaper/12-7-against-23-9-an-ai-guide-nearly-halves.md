When a doctor needs tissue from a lung nodule, a needle goes in under CT guidance. Each extra pass raises the chance of a pneumothorax, a partly collapsed lung. A multicentre randomised trial led from Huazhong University in Wuhan tested an AI navigation system, GPS-Lung, against standard guidance [1][2].

With the system, surgeons needed fewer punctures (2.0 against 2.5 on average), finished faster (20.4 against 23.2 minutes), used less radiation, and saw pneumothorax in 12.7% of patients against 23.9% [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is close to half the rate.

It is one trial, in one health system, of a tool partly developed with a device maker's researchers [1]. The welcome part is the method: registered in advance, randomised, published open access. That is how a promising tool earns its place in a German radiology suite.
