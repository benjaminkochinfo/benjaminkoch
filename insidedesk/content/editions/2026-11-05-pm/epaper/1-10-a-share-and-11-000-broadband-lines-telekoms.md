Deutsche Telekom reported its third quarter on Thursday, and the headline will sound familiar: more revenue, higher guidance, a bigger dividend. Revenue reached €31.1 billion, 5.5% more on an organic basis, and adjusted EBITDA AL rose 8.0% organically to €12.2 billion [1].

**The guidance moved by a hair.** Adjusted EBITDA AL for 2026 is now expected at about €47.6 billion, up from about €47.5 billion; free cash flow AL at about €20.1 billion, up from about €20.0 billion [1]. Desk arithmetic: both are increases of roughly 0.2% to 0.5%. The company says it is passing on higher expectations at T-Mobile US [1].

**The payout moved more.** The board plans a dividend of €1.10 a share, 10% more than for 2025, and buybacks of up to €2 billion in 2027 [1].

**The line worth reading twice.** In Germany, Telekom added 11,000 broadband customers in the quarter; a year earlier it lost 25,000 [1]. A record 190,000 new customers took a pure fibre-to-the-home line [1]. That is the slow machinery of a network business: digging, connecting, billing. It does not make headlines, but it pays dividends, literally.

One more number sits in the table: the group had 189,348 employees at the end of September, against 201,336 a year earlier, a fall of 6.0% [1]. Higher profits and fewer staff are part of the same story. Nothing here is investment advice.
