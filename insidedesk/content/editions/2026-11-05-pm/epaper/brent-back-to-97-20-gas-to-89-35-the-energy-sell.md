Brent crude rose about 2.7% to $97.20 a barrel by 14:30 CET on Thursday, after three sessions of losses that included a fall of more than 8% on Wednesday [1]. European gas, the Dutch TTF benchmark, recovered about 1.5% to €89.35 per megawatt hour, having dipped towards €85 earlier in the day [1].

The sell-off had been driven by hopes that the US midterm result would add pressure for an end to the US–Iran war, with Qatar mediating [1]. The rebound has more physical reasons: LNG deliveries from the Gulf remain disrupted ahead of winter, and forecasts of colder weather across north-western Europe point to higher heating demand on depleted storage [1].

Hope moves prices for a day. Tankers and thermometers move them for a winter.
