Artificial intelligence is usually sold as software. Underneath, it is a lease, a loan and a building site. On Wednesday the US data-centre developer TeraWulf said it had signed credit-support agreements with Google for the debt financing behind about $19 billion of contracted lease commitments with Anthropic at its Justified campus in Hawesville, Kentucky [1].

The mechanism is old-fashioned. A lender cares less about the promise of a technology than about who pays if the tenant does not. Google's parent Alphabet carries senior unsecured ratings of AA+ from S&P and Aa2 from Moody's, the company notes [1]. "Cost of capital is fundamental to infrastructure development," said TeraWulf's finance chief, Patrick Fleury [1].

The second agreement is with the engineering firm Fluor, for the engineering, procurement and construction of three data-centre buildings, with open-book cost reporting and guaranteed maximum pricing still being set as work progresses [1]. The company says phased completions are expected to begin in late 2027 and run through the second quarter of 2028 [1]. It also names labour availability as an increasingly important constraint on large data-centre builds [1].

Read it as an invoice rather than a headline. One AI customer's demand, turned into three buildings, needs a rated guarantor, a contractor with subcontractors, and a calendar that runs into 2028 [1]. "Megawatts alone do not create shareholder value," the chief executive, Paul Prager, said [1]. On that, at least, the press release and the sceptics agree. Nothing here is investment advice.
