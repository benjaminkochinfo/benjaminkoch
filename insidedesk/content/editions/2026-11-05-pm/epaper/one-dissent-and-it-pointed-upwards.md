The Bank of England's 8–1 vote to raise rates to 4% held one surprise. In contrast to expectations in a Reuters poll, the dissent came not from someone seeking to keep rates on hold, but from someone seeking more [2]. Catherine Mann preferred 0.5 points, to 4.25% [1].

Her reasoning, as the minutes record it, is about credibility: "an activist approach was needed" to stop inflation becoming embedded before price and wage-setting reach thresholds that could cause "non-linear responses" [1]. The other eight noted that evidence of such second-round effects has so far been limited [1].

A published, named vote is a small piece of institutional machinery. It lets you see the argument, not just the result.
