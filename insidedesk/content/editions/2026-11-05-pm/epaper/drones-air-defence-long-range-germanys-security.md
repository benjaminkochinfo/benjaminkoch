Germany's National Security Council, chaired by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, met on Tuesday and stressed the need for further growth in "unmanned systems in all dimensions", counter-drone capability, air defence and long-range weapons, government spokesman Stefan Kornelius said [1]. The council is a coordinating body, not a buyer: contracts and funding still run through the ministries and the Bundestag's budget committee, and the statement named no quantities, timetables or sums [1]. On Wednesday, at the Berlin Security Conference, Defence Minister Boris Pistorius and Lithuania's Robertas Kaunas signed a declaration putting air defence and counter-drone work at the top of their cooperation [2]. It is not a purchase agreement either; a separate action plan is to follow [2]. Germany's 45th Armoured Brigade in Lithuania is to reach about 5,000 personnel and civilians at full capability, with main infrastructure due by the end of 2027 [2]. Priorities are words. Watch for the budget lines that turn them into hardware.
