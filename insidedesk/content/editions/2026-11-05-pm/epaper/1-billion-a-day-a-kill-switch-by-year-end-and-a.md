China's ambassador to the EU, Cai Run, said on Thursday that if the EU insists on "restrictive and discriminatory measures against China, China will have to take countermeasures", Euronews reports [1]. The target of the warning is a tool the European Commission is drafting, nicknamed a "kill-switch", that could rapidly limit imports from a country causing serious disruption to the EU market [1].

The background is a number. The EU runs a record trade deficit with China of about €1 billion a day, Euronews writes, and Chinese imports have risen 25% over the past year according to Chinese customs [1]. EU leaders asked the Commission in October for such an instrument, and Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič told MEPs on Wednesday it should be presented by the end of the year [1]. Officially it is country-agnostic [1]. Leaders have also asked for measures, including quotas and tariffs, on Chinese chemicals and machinery [1].

Beijing, for its part, asked Brussels to assess the impact of new EU measures on low-value parcels, largely from retailers such as Shein and Temu [2].

Here is what the speeches leave out. Talks continue: since a 9 October deal to reduce imports of Chinese hybrid cars, officials have met at technical level, and Šefčovič is due to meet Commerce Minister Wang Wentao in March 2027 [1]. Meanwhile the Commission keeps imposing duties product by product, after investigations into pricing and subsidies [1]. Threats travel fast. Trade law travels by case file. For you, the case files are what end up on the shelf price.
