The headline initiative of COP31 is not a treaty clause but a ratio. Governments that sign the voluntary pledge drafted by the Turkish presidency will commit to raising electricity's share of total energy consumption to 35% globally by 2035 [1]. The pledge is due to be launched formally on Energy Day, 10 November, with the list of signatories; Australia is already on it and European countries are likely to join, while India could well say no and China is a maybe, according to Climate Home News [1].

The ratio is useful precisely because it is dull. Electricity today accounts for around 23 to 24% of final energy consumption, so reaching the target means adding roughly one percentage point a year [1]. On current policies, Climate Action Tracker estimates the share reaches only 28% by 2035, with transport falling furthest behind despite rising sales of electric cars [1].

Critics point out that the text does not say the power must be clean; it speaks of "diverse and sustainable energy sources" [1]. COP31 president Murat Kurum argues that wider use of electric vehicles and heat pumps will "automatically" cut fossil fuel use [1]. Experts quoted by Climate Home stress a more practical bottleneck: grids and storage, especially in developing countries, have not kept pace with fast-growing renewable capacity [1].

That is where to look. A heat pump is a purchase; a grid connection is a queue. Pledges get signed in Antalya. The percentage gets built in substations, transformer orders and permitting offices, and it is published every year, so you will be able to check.
