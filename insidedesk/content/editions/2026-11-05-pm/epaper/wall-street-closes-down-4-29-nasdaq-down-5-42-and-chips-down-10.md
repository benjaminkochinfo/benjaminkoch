New York has closed, and it finished what Frankfurt started. The S&P 500 fell 4.29%, the Nasdaq Composite 5.42%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 3.45% [1]. CNBC put the Dow down about 1,844 points, or 3.7%, into the final stretch, and said the Nasdaq closed more than 10% below last month's record — a correction [2]. The iShares Semiconductor ETF was down more than 10%, on course for its worst day since March 2020 [2].

The sorting inside the number is the story. Nvidia closed 8.94% lower at $182.23, AMD 16.03% lower at $410.42, and Western Digital 16.04% lower [1]. Broadcom and Nvidia each fell about 9% on CNBC's tape; Qualcomm dropped about 10% after a softer outlook, and CoreWeave about 11% [2]. Nationwide's Mark Hackett put the two drivers together: "Disappointing earnings and the Dem sweep potentially slowing the AI buildout are pressing sentiment" [3]. Brent settled about 3.9% higher at $98.29 a barrel as the Iran war moved back into focus [3]. The Cboe Volatility Index jumped about 62% to 26.90, a six-month high [1].

Yesterday's relief rally lasted one session. A red New York close after a red Frankfurt close is a mood, not a verdict on every data-centre order book. Nothing here is investment advice.
