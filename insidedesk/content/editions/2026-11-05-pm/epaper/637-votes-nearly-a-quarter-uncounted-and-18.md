Alaska's Senate seat is still open. As of Wednesday night, Democrat Mary Peltola led the Republican incumbent, Senator Dan Sullivan, by 637 votes, according to the Associated Press as reported by Al Jazeera, with nearly a quarter of votes uncounted [1]. Early on Wednesday she had 48% to his 47.7%; the other Dan Sullivan on the ballot, retired Petersburg teacher Dan J. Sullivan, had 1.7% [2].

Nobody needs to guess the timetable, because the state printed it. Alaska accepts mailed ballots for 10 days, or 15 if they come from overseas [2]. If no candidate has more than 50% of first-choice votes, the Division of Elections tabulates ranked choices on 18 November [1][2]. The state says it will publish its ballot data file during counting, so the AP could run its own tabulation and call the race sooner [2].

The noise is around the count, not inside it. The Justice Department sued to block certification if Sullivan loses, pending an inquiry into whether votes for the second Sullivan were cast by mistake; a judge dismissed the suit on Sunday, saying such an inquiry was impossible without violating ballot secrecy, and the department is appealing [2]. Ballot stacking is generally legal in Alaska, Alaska Public Media notes, and super PACs on both sides spent more than $2 million on "ballot composition" this year [2].

Democrats will hold at least 54 of 100 Senate seats either way [1]. What Alaska decides is the margin, and whether a slow, rules-first count still gets the last word. Its calendar says it will.
