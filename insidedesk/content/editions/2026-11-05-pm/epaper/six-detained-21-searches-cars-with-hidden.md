Poland's internal security agency, the ABW, said on Thursday it had detained six people suspected of working for Russian intelligence and of supplying Russia with "significant quantities" of military components, used in particular to make ammunition and weapons systems [1][2]. Between 21 October and 2 November its officers searched 21 locations in the Warmian-Masurian, Pomeranian and Greater Poland provinces [1].

Five of the detained are Poles aged 25 to 52; one is a 37-year-old Russian woman, wrote Tomasz Siemoniak, who coordinates Poland's special services [2]. Four, including the Russian national, have been charged with espionage and remanded for three months by a court; the other two face non-custodial measures [1][2]. All face charges of taking part in an organised criminal group and of breaking rules on trade in strategic goods and technologies [1].

According to the investigation, the scheme ran from June 2023 to December 2025 across Poland, Lithuania and Russia [2]. The group allegedly ordered and bought goods, including shell casings and projectiles, and drove them into the Kaliningrad region in vehicles with hidden compartments; some were intercepted by Polish border officers [2]. Prosecutors in Białystok supervise the case, and the ABW calls the investigation ongoing [1]. None of the suspects has been convicted.

The useful detail is the last one about the border. Sanctions are announced in capitals, but they are enforced by a customs officer opening a boot. Each intercepted box became evidence, and the evidence became warrants. That is how a long-running scheme ends: not with a speech, but with a file.
