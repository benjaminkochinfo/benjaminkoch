Few parents want to think about it, which is why the numbers help. At the end of 2025 about 231,000 children and young people under 18 in Germany were officially severely disabled, roughly 1.7%, or one in every 60 [1]. "Severely" means a degree of disability of at least 50, which can follow a serious chronic heart or lung condition as well as an accident [1].

Stiftung Warentest has now tested nine child disability insurance offers from five insurers [1]. All of them pay a lifelong pension of €1,500 a month once a disability of 50% or a degree of disability of 50 is established; three add a one-off lump sum, and the test winner pairs the pension with €36,000 [1]. Seven policies were rated good, two adequate [1]. The annual premiums, for a child insured from age one, range from €162 to €615 [1]. Good policies are available for under €400 a year [1].

The dividing line is cause. The testers recommend policies that pay equally for disability after an accident and after illness. Those that pay after accidents and only after certain named diseases were marked down [1]. A plain accident policy, they warn, usually pays nothing when disability follows illness [1].

Two dull practical points. Apply early, before any developmental problem or suspected illness appears, because insurers may then refuse cover [1]. And read the health questions carefully: the test rewarded forms that warn clearly about the consequences of a wrong answer [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
