China "has always maintained an open attitude" to buying more US farm goods, provided the agreed reciprocal tariff cuts covering $30 billion of goods on each side are implemented, Commerce Ministry spokeswoman Huang Ling said on Thursday, the South China Morning Post reports [1]. The cuts belong to the US-China Board of Trade arrangement [1]. An agricultural trader told the paper soybean purchases are on track to meet this year's target, while a separate $17 billion commitment remains largely unfulfilled [1]. Semafor notes that China recently restarted US soybean imports but that they remain far below levels before last year's trade war [2]. The remarks come after the Democrats' midterm win, which analysts told the SCMP raises questions ahead of this month's APEC summit about the administration's ability to honour commitments [1]. A trade deal is a promise. The tariff schedule is where it becomes an invoice.
