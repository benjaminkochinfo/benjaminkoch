German manufacturers took in 4.4% more orders in September than in August, after adjusting for prices, season and calendar, Destatis reported on Thursday [1]. Without large orders the rise was 5.1% [1]. Against September 2025 orders were up 3.3% [1]. That sounds like a rebound, and partly it is one. But August was revised to a fall of 10.1%, slightly less bad than the 10.6% first reported [1].

Desk arithmetic: a 10.1% fall followed by a 4.4% rise leaves September's order level roughly 6% below July's. So read Thursday's number as a repair, not a recovery.

The repair has an address. Car industry orders rose 10.5% on the month, after two months that Destatis says were low partly because of production changeovers and works holidays [1]. Machinery rose 8.3%, helped by one large order for engines and turbines [1]. Other vehicles, the category of aircraft, ships, trains and military vehicles that has carried many big contracts this year, fell 10.4% [1]. Consumer goods orders jumped 14.4%, capital goods 5.3%, intermediate goods 0.5% [1].

The geography is the quietly encouraging bit. Domestic orders rose 6.6%. Foreign orders rose 2.8%: from the euro zone up 4.5%, from outside it up 1.6% [1]. In a year of tariff headlines, your neighbours placing more orders is not nothing.

Now the steadier measure, the one Destatis calls less volatile: the three-month comparison. Orders in the third quarter were 0.2% higher than in the second; without large orders, 0.1% [1]. Real sales in September rose 0.2% on the month and 2.0% on the year [1]. That is a factory sector treading water. Not a boom, not the collapse August's headline implied. The dull work of filling order books goes on, one invoice at a time. Nothing here is investment advice.
