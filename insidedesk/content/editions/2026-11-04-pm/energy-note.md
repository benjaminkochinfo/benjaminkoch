This morning's relief lasted one delivery day. Today's day-ahead auction set an average of €257.38 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg for delivery on Thursday 5 November, the desk's average of the 24 hourly results SMARD published this afternoon [1]. That is €8.39 above Wednesday's €248.99, which had been the first day under €250 since 28 October [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-04pm]]

**The shape matters more than the average.** Thursday's night is cheaper than Wednesday's: the hour from midnight cleared €196.97, the lowest single hour since Wednesday 28 October [1]. The evening is dearer: €349.36 for the 17:00 hour and €346.14 for 18:00, the highest hourly prices since Thursday 29 October [1]. Desk arithmetic: the gap between Thursday's cheapest and dearest hour is €152.39, against €101.80 for Wednesday [1]. If you can move a wash, a charge or a heat pump's boost out of the early evening, Thursday is a day when it is worth the trouble.

**Wednesday's wind came from land, not sea.** In the first 15 hours of Wednesday, to 15:00, onshore turbines produced 238.3 gigawatt hours, SMARD's provisional figures show, already most of the 279.3 they made across all of Tuesday [1]. Offshore wind fell away: 30.3 gigawatt hours in the same 15 hours, against 89.5 for the whole of Tuesday [1].

**Gas has eased, not left.** Natural gas plants produced 182.3 gigawatt hours to 15:00 on Wednesday [1]. Desk arithmetic: at that hourly rate a full day comes to about 292, some 6% below Tuesday's 310.0 [1]. These figures are provisional and will be revised [1]. More wind trims the fossil hours at the margin; it does not yet empty them.

The auction does not reward hope or punish speeches. It prices the hours traders expect to be short, and on Thursday those are the hours when you come home. Nothing here is investment advice.
