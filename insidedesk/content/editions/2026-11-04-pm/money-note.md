Euro-area inflation reached 4.0% in October, up from 3.8% in September, according to Eurostat's flash estimate published this morning [1]. It is the first time the rate has hit 4% since September 2023, when it stood at 4.3% [2]. The figure was in line with what economists polled by Reuters had expected [2].

[[chart:ea-hicp-energy-dfr-04pm]]

**One line did nearly all the climbing.** Energy prices were 20.8% higher than a year earlier, up from 18.9% in September; a year ago, in October 2025, they were 0.9% lower [1]. Everything else barely moved. Inflation excluding energy stayed at 2.3%, and excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco it stayed at 2.5% [1]. Services rose to 3.3% from 3.2%, food, alcohol and tobacco to 1.6% from 1.3%, and non-energy industrial goods to 1.2% from 1.1% [1]. Energy carries a weight of 90.3 per thousand in the 2026 basket [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 9%, yet the headline has gone from 2.1% a year ago to 4.0% while the other 91% of the basket eased from 2.4% to 2.3% [1].

**Germany went the other way.** German inflation on the harmonised measure fell to 2.8% from 3.3%, with prices 0.1% lower than in September [1]. Italy jumped to 5.6% from 4.2% and Belgium to 5.2% from 4.5% [1]. Lithuania, at 6.1%, was the highest; Malta, at 2.3%, the lowest, and every member was above the ECB's 2% target [1][2]. The same oil price reaches 21 countries through 21 sets of tariffs, taxes and contracts.

**The ECB is weighing what it can see.** The bank left its deposit rate at 2.5% last month after a quarter-point rise in September [2]. Shortly before the release, markets priced a 71.8% chance of another rise in December; afterwards it was more than 72% [2]. Policymakers have said underlying inflation and wages are broadly stable [2].

For your household, the split matters more than the headline. If your bills are mostly rent, food and services, your inflation is closer to the quiet 2-something line than to 4%. If you heat with gas or drive long distances, it is not. Read your own invoices; the average is nobody's. Nothing here is investment advice.
