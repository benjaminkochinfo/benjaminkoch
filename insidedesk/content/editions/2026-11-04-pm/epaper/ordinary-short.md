Ask whether heating has got dearer and the honest answer is: it depends what you burn. Saxony-Anhalt's statistics office published its October figures for household energy on Wednesday, to mark the start of the heating season [1].

Taken together, electricity, gas and other fuels cost 0.8% more than in September and 3.6% more than in October 2025, putting the index at 151.6 [1]. Underneath that average, the picture splits. District heating was 0.5% cheaper than a year earlier and gas, including levies, 3.2% cheaper [1]. Solid fuels rose 7.7%, coal 2.8%, and heating oil, including levies, 54.8% [1].

Who pays which price matters. According to the 2022 census, 54.2% of the state's households heat with gas and 25.0% with district heating [1]. The households facing the sharpest rises are the minority on oil (12.8%), coal (0.7%) or wood (2.5%) [1]. And about 4% of people in the state said in 2025 that their household could not afford to keep the home adequately warm [1].

The useful lesson travels beyond one state. An "energy price" headline averages your neighbour's tank with your meter. The number that counts is on your own last invoice, and the dull habit that helps is reading it before the first cold week, not after. Nothing here is investment advice.
