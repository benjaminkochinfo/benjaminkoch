Researchers at UCLA and the University of Washington scanned 47 autistic and 45 non-autistic young people aged 8 to 15 while artificial grass and scratchy wool were rubbed on their forearm [1]. Before some blocks the screen said "feel"; before others it said "reporter", the cue to describe the texture objectively [1]. In the reporter blocks, autistic youth showed less activity in areas that register touch and in the insula, and even in the auditory cortex [1]. Their own ratings of how bad it felt did not change significantly [1].

It was one short training session in an MRI scanner, and the task was hard for younger children [1]. "This type of emotion regulation strategy could be incorporated into interventions and tested," said senior author Shulamite Green [1]. A small, testable tool, described without hype: that is how help usually arrives.
