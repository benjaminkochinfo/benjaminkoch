The US Treasury will sell $125 billion of notes and bonds next week to refund about $97 billion maturing on 15 November, raising about $28 billion of new cash from private investors [1]. The pieces are a $58 billion 3-year note on Monday 9 November, a $42 billion 10-year note on Tuesday 10 November and a $25 billion 30-year bond on Thursday 12 November, each auctioned at 13:00 New York time, 19:00 CET [1].

**The headline is the absence of one.** Treasury said it expects to keep nominal coupon and floating-rate auction sizes unchanged "for the next several quarters" [1]. Its table for November to January shows the same monthly sizes as August to October: $69 billion of 2-year notes, $70 billion of 5-year and $44 billion of 7-year [1]. TIPS sizes are held too [1].

**The changes are in the plumbing.** From March 2027 the 7-year note becomes a quarterly new issue with two reopenings, like the 10-year [1]. Treasury plans buybacks of up to $70 billion of older securities for liquidity support and up to $25 billion for cash management this quarter [1]. It assumes an $850 billion cash balance at the end of December, but says the account could peak at $1.025 trillion, plus or minus $50 billion, in late November and late January [1].

Bond markets, meanwhile, were reading the election. The 10-year yield slipped more than a basis point to 4.933% on Wednesday; JPMorgan's traders said Wall Street is focusing on "reduced deficit spending" after the midterms [2]. That may or may not arrive. What has arrived is a borrowing calendar with dates and amounts, and the next one is due on 3 February 2027 [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
