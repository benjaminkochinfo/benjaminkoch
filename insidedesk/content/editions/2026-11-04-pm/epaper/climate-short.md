The World Health Organization published a report on Wednesday that does something climate summits rarely do: it costs the boring things [1]. Its six measures are heat-health warning systems, climate-resilient drinking water, basic sanitation, hygiene and handwashing facilities, cleaner household fuels for cooking and heating, and efficient fossil-fuel pricing [1].

Scaled up worldwide, the WHO models, they could prevent 1.6 million deaths a year, about one in every 40 deaths, and avoid 16.7 billion tonnes of CO₂, equal to 31% of global annual emissions excluding land use [1]. Across five of the actions, every dollar invested is estimated to return $24 [1].

The standout is the cheapest. Heat-health warning systems are estimated to return $835 for every $1 invested, because they use systems that already exist [1]. Fossil-fuel pricing, meaning ending inefficient subsidies and taxing the fuels properly, brings the largest gains: an estimated 1.2 million deaths prevented and $5.4 trillion a year in fiscal benefits [1].

Read the small print too. The WHO itself lists the limits: "aspirational" scale-up scenarios, a still limited evidence base and uncertainty in long-range projections [1]. These are modelled estimates, not a promise. But as governments head to Antalya, it is a useful reminder that a text message before a heatwave can be worth more than a pledge.
