The dollar index fell to a one-month low on Wednesday and was last at 101.47; the pound rose 0.5% to $1.3471 [1]. By late morning the euro had climbed more than 0.6% towards $1.1260–1.1280, the top of its four-week range, helped by this morning's 4.0% euro-area inflation figure [2]. Convera's George Vessey said investors increasingly see the election as "a constraint on future policy rather than a fundamental challenge" to US growth [1].

Sterling's next test is domestic. Ebury's Matthew Ryan expects a quarter-point Bank of England rise on Thursday on a 7–2 vote, and calls swap pricing of four rises within twelve months "far too excessive" [3]. A 6–3 split, he writes, would be the pound's bearish risk [3]. Nothing here is investment advice.
