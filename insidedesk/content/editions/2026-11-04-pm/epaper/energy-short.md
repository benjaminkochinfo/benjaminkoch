The American Petroleum Institute's figures, cited by market sources on Wednesday, showed US crude stocks falling by 5.27 million barrels in the week to 30 October; eight analysts polled by Reuters had expected a rise of about 880,000 [2]. The government's own count arrived at 16:30 CET today. The US Energy Information Administration put the fall in commercial crude stocks at 865,000 barrels, to 409.8 million [1].

That is still a draw, and still the opposite of the forecast. But the shape of the week is different from the one the overnight headline suggested. Crude exports fell by 1.18 million barrels a day to 3.68 million, and net imports rose by about the same amount, to 2.70 million [1]. Refineries ran harder, taking in 16.73 million barrels a day, up 199,000 [1].

**The tighter shelf is diesel.** Stocks of distillate fuel oil, which includes diesel and heating oil, fell by 3.57 million barrels to 99.26 million, down 3.5% in a week and 11.0% below the same week last year [1]. Jet fuel fell 1.0% and petrol barely moved [1]. With winter heating demand ahead, that is the line a refiner, a haulier or anyone with an oil tank will read first.

**The reserve keeps shrinking quietly.** The Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell by another 399,000 barrels to 282.2 million, 31.1% below a year ago [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 127 million barrels fewer than the 409.6 million held in the same week of 2025 [1].
