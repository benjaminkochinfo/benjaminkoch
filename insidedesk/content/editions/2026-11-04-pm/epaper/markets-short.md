On a morning when most of Wall Street went up, one payments processor went sharply the other way. Fidelity National Information Services, the bank-software and payments group known as FIS, fell 17.2% in pre-market trading to $32.30, close to its 52-week low of $32.09 [2]. Its 52-week high was $68.37 [2].

The quarter itself was ordinary. Adjusted earnings of $1.61 a share matched the consensus of $1.60, and revenue of about $3.43 billion was in line with forecasts [2]. The trouble sat in the two lines below it.

**The year got smaller.** FIS now expects adjusted earnings of $5.82 to $5.89 a share for 2026, down from $6.15 to $6.24, and revenue of $13.445 to $13.505 billion, down from $13.630 to $13.695 billion [1]. Desk arithmetic: at the midpoints that is about 5.5% off earnings and 1.4% off revenue. Pro forma revenue growth is now put at 3.1% to 3.6%, from 4.5% to 5.0% [1].

**The finance chair empties tomorrow.** Chief financial officer James Kehoe steps down on 5 November and stays as a non-executive adviser until March 2027; chief accounting officer Alexandra Brooks becomes interim CFO [1]. The company also said meaningful share buybacks will resume only once gross leverage falls to about 2.8 times [1].

Investing.com notes that analysts had already made 21 downward earnings revisions in the 90 days before the report [2]. Expectations had been lowered, and the shares still fell. That is the quiet lesson: a forecast you meet is only half the story if the next one you write is lower. Read the guidance before the headline. Nothing here is investment advice.
