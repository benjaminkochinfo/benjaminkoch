At 20:00 CET on Tuesday, a 23-year-old supertanker was hit in its engine room by two unknown projectiles 14 nautical miles north-east of Limah, in Omani waters [1]. The maritime security firm Vanguard Tech named it as the Barbados-flagged VLCC Grit [1]. No crew were hurt; the damage was not yet known [1]. The ship appears on the blacklist kept by Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority [1].

Seatrade counts it as the fifth confirmed attack in the first three days of November: the tug Selat Storm and the tanker Era S in the strait on 1 November, the tanker Largo Atom in the Gulf the same day, and the Indian dhow Al Jusufi near Oman on 2 November, which killed one crew member [1]. Omani authorities rescued 13 others from the dhow [2].

Then there is the claimed list. On Wednesday Iran's Revolutionary Guards said four oil tankers had caught fire after hitting naval mines on a "prohibited southern route", and that two more had been intercepted [2]. BBC Verify has found no evidence that four tankers were destroyed [2]. The Guards also said they had shot down another US MQ-9 drone; after a similar claim on Monday night, US officials said the military had carried out limited strikes on Qeshm Island [2].

The difference matters for anyone pricing risk. A confirmed incident comes with a hull name, a flag, a time in UTC and a position in nautical miles, reported by the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre and checked by shipping firms [1][2]. A claim comes with a number. Insurers, crews and governments act on the first kind. You can too.
