Mike Johnson told his staff on a call on Wednesday morning that he will not run to lead House Republicans in the next Congress, according to four sources on the call quoted by NBC News [1]. He has served two terms as speaker since Kevin McCarthy was ousted in 2023 [1]. Republicans hold their internal election for minority leader next week, on 10 November [1].

On the other side of the aisle, Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become the first Black speaker of the House when the new Congress convenes on 3 January [2]. Democrats had a 228–139 majority on Wednesday morning, with 68 races still uncalled [2]. "This is not a partisan moment. This is a patriotic one," Jeffries told supporters [2].

In the Senate, John Thune, the outgoing majority leader, wrote that Republicans "will not stand idly by" and that "thankfully, we still have President Trump in the White House" [3]. Chuck Schumer, who will lead the new majority, offered Americans five words: "Relief is on the way" [3].

Those are the speeches. The dates are more useful: a caucus vote on 10 November, a swearing-in on 3 January, and then committee chairs, budgets and staff. A change of majority is mostly paperwork: who holds the gavel, who signs the subpoenas, who sets the agenda for each morning's sitting. That is less exciting than election night, and it is the part that lasts two years.
