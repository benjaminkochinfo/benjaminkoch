New York has closed, and it agreed with Frankfurt. The S&P 500 rose 1.72% to 7,698.86, the Nasdaq Composite 1.77% to 25,821.13, and the Dow 2.97%, or 1,534.89 points, to 53,321.61 [1]. Consumer discretionary led with a 5% gain; energy fell 5.1% [1].

The sorting inside the number is the story. Amazon rose 7% and Alphabet 3.8%, while the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 3.6% to a six-month low and the data-centre operators Hut 8 and IREN lost 24% and 12% [1]. AMD, which forecast faster growth for 2027 overnight, still closed down 0.4% [1]. Defence names fell on the prospect of a tougher road for the $1.5 trillion budget request, and a solar fund climbed 6.1% [1]. Citi's Scott Chronert put it plainly: change the pace of the data-centre buildout and you "change the fundamental trajectory" for its suppliers [1].

Gridlock is not a policy, but markets often price it as a relief. The order books of the suppliers will say more than tonight's close. Nothing here is investment advice.
