Sunday is the rest day in Antalya, and the delegates go into it holding a document that insists on what it is not. On Friday the COP31 presidencies, Türkiye as host and Australia steering the negotiations, published a "summary of reflections" on a possible cover decision [1]. The presidency stressed that it is "not intended to reflect consensus, nor the strength or prevalence of views expressed" [1]. It does not say which country proposed what [1]. Read it as an inventory, not an invoice.

The inventory is interesting. It includes the "transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems", the wording agreed at COP28 in Dubai and missing from the final decisions in Baku and Belém [1]. It floats efforts to limit the "magnitude and duration" of warming beyond 1.5°C, raising electricity's share of final energy use to 35 per cent by 2035, and welcoming roadmaps on moving away from fossil fuels and on halting deforestation [1]. A list is cheap. What matters is which lines survive a text that needs the consent of every country present [1].

The deeper argument is whether there should be a cover decision at all. Some large emerging economies say it is being pushed by the presidencies rather than by parties; Russia and the Arab Group are among those who say there is no mandate for one [1]. The European Union has warned there is little political appetite for new funding pledges in Antalya [1]. "The big question is what can survive that test of opposition," said Alden Meyer of E3G [1].

There is also a quieter dispute about the calendar of science. Some countries want the next major IPCC assessment ready for the global review of progress in 2028; Saudi Arabia, India and China have argued for 2029, saying more time is needed to include research from developing countries [1]. That sounds procedural because it is. Procedures decide what evidence is on the table when the stocktake begins.

On Saturday UN climate chief Simon Stiell urged countries to move swiftly towards agreement, and COP31 President of Negotiations Chris Bowen said a first draft of the overarching agreement is expected early next week [2]. The conference is due to end on 20 November, though these talks often overrun [1].

For you, the useful habit this week is to ignore the adjectives and compare drafts. On Monday or soon after there should be a text with brackets in it [1][2]. The brackets are where the negotiation actually lives.
