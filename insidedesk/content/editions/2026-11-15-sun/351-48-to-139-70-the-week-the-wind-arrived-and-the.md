Sunday's electricity in Germany and Luxembourg was bought on Saturday at an average of €139.70 per megawatt hour, the cheapest delivery day of the week [1]. The week began at €345.64 on Monday and set a record of €351.48 on Tuesday [1]. Desk arithmetic: from Tuesday to Sunday the daily average fell by €211.78, or about 60%. Against the previous Sunday, 8 November, at €242.13, it is €102.43 lower [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-15nov]]

**The shape of a cheap Sunday.** The dearest hour on Sunday is 17:00, at €178.12, as lights come on and the sun is gone; the cheapest is 12:00, at €99.79 [1]. No hour clears below €99, so this is a cheaper day, not a giveaway one. The pattern is the familiar winter one: a dip at midday, a hump in the early evening [1].

**Who took the shift.** SMARD's generation data tell the story better than any commentary. On Tuesday, wind turbines on land and at sea together produced about 101 GWh; on Friday, about 791 GWh, and on Saturday about 532 GWh [2]. Gas-fired plants produced about 418 GWh on Tuesday and about 183 GWh on Saturday [2]. Desk arithmetic: Friday's wind output was nearly eight times Tuesday's, and Saturday's gas output was 56% below Tuesday's. The expensive marginal plant had less to do, and the price followed.

**Demand helped, quietly.** Consumption was about 1,518 GWh on Wednesday and about 1,260 GWh on Saturday, a weekend drop of roughly 17% [2]. Desk arithmetic: the seven delivery days from Monday to Sunday averaged about €244.57, against about €256.59 for the seven days to 8 November [1]. A record on Tuesday and the cheapest day on Sunday add up to a week only a little cheaper than the last.

**For you.** If you are on a dynamic tariff, this week was a lesson in timing rather than luck: the same kilowatt hour cost very different amounts on Tuesday evening and Sunday lunchtime. If you are on a fixed tariff, the record and the slump both passed through someone else's books. Either way, the price you will see next year is built from weeks like this, averaged by the dull machinery of procurement. Nothing here is investment advice.
