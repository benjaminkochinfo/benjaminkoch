**The podium.** In Munich on Friday, Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel set out the case plainly. Euro-area inflation stood at about 2% when the year began and is now at 4%, he said; oil costs around a third more than before the Iran war began, European gas almost three times as much [1]. The ECB raised rates in June and September and held at 2.5% two weeks ago [1]. Then the line that moved the wires: "If the situation stays as it is, it seems highly probable that we'll have to act again" [1][3]. In Riga on Wednesday he had said "we will do what is necessary again" [4]; on Friday he put a probability on it.

**The print.** On the same day Eurostat published its second look at the third quarter. Euro-area GDP rose 0.4% on the quarter, after 0.6% in the spring, and 1.3% on the year [2]. Employment was unchanged on the quarter, after rising 0.1% in each of the three quarters before [2]. France's output fell 0.1%, Italy's was flat, Germany's grew 0.2%; Spain grew 0.6% [2]. The United States, for comparison, grew 0.8% [2].

**The gap.** It is real, but it is not a contradiction. Nagel did not hide the print: "So far, the economy has proved resilient," he said, citing the same 0.4% [1]. His argument is that higher policy rates "won't produce more oil or gas", but can stop energy prices feeding into wages and price-setting [1]. He also said underlying inflation and wage growth have so far been "broadly stable" [1]. Desk arithmetic: put the two together and the ECB is weighing a hike against an economy whose second- and third-largest members, France and Italy, produced no growth between them last quarter [2].

**What the print does not say.** A flat head count is not a falling one; year on year, employment is still up 0.4% [2]. And the flash covers states making up 97% of euro-area output, with revisions due on 7 December [2]. It is a reading, not a verdict.

**For you.** The podium tells you where the rate on your next loan or deposit is more likely to go. The print tells you how many new payslips there were to pay it: in the euro area last quarter, net, none. Keep both on your page. Nothing here is investment advice.
