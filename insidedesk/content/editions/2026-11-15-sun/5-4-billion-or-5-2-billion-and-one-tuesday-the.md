The 2027 budget was agreed in the small hours of Friday. The electricity tax was not in it [1]. That gap ran through the whole weekend.

Friedrich Merz says the goal stands. He wants to cut the electricity tax to the European minimum, "from next year, in full", as he put it on the edge of the EU meeting in Dublin, and he was due to address the CDU's Schleswig-Holstein party conference in Neumünster on Saturday, where SPIEGEL expected him to have to say something about it [1][2]. Several minister-presidents of his party pressed for the cut as well, among them his host, Daniel Günther [1][2].

The finance minister answers with a number. Lars Klingbeil says he is open in principle, but it needs "serious" counter-financing: the federal government would take in about €5.4 billion a year less [1]. DER SPIEGEL puts the sum needed at €5.2 billion [2]. Desk arithmetic: the two figures are €200 million apart, which is small next to the size of the hole and large next to anything already found to fill it. Even the Union's own budget specialists doubt whether that money can still be found in the budget, SPIEGEL reports [2].

The pressure inside the party is now on the record. A meeting of the Union's parliamentary group on Wednesday turned into what SPIEGEL calls a reckoning, because the cut was left out of the deal with the SPD leaders, and the paper reports a "threat" from within the party's own ranks to let the budget fail in the Bundestag at the end of November [2]. The decision is due at Tuesday's coalition committee and could be added during the budget week [1]. The background is not getting easier: in the same overnight briefing SPIEGEL notes economists' warnings of a severe budget crisis from 2028, after a 2027 budget shaped by record spending and more new debt [2].

For you: the household gain depends on one dull document, the counter-financing table. A lower rate needs a matching line somewhere else, a cut, a levy or a reallocation, with a sum beside it. If Tuesday produces that line, the promise becomes a measure. If it produces only another statement of intent, the budget week will open with the same €5 billion question it closed on.
