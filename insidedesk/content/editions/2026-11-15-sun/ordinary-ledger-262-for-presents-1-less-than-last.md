The Ordinary Ledger takes the week's big numbers and turns them into lines on a household budget. This Sunday the big number is small, and that is the news: on average you plan to spend €262 on Christmas presents this year, against €263 last year, according to a survey of more than 2,000 people by the Handelsblatt Research Institute for the retail association HDE [1].

**Who is cutting.** More than half of people plan to spend the same as last year, 10% plan to spend more and 24% plan to spend less [1]. Desk arithmetic: cutters outnumber spenders by more than two to one, and the average still barely moves. That is what a stable budget looks like from the inside: a minority tightening, a smaller minority loosening, and most people buying roughly the same list for roughly the same people.

**What the shops expect.** The retailers are gloomier than their customers. In a separate HDE survey of 300 retail companies, 56% of non-food retailers expect a worse or much worse Christmas than last year [1]. Still, the association forecasts nominal sales up 2% in November and December, to €129.2 billion in total [1]. HDE president Alexander von Preen said customers "sind in Weihnachtsstimmung" (are in the Christmas mood) and are planning stable spending on presents [1].

Note the word nominal. A 2% rise in euros, with a flat present budget per person, is not a story of more parcels under the tree. It is mostly a story of prices: of energy, staff and, in the HDE's own words, "ausufernder Bürokratie" (runaway bureaucracy) on the retailers' side of the counter [1].

**Where the money goes.** Online is the growth line. The HDE expects online Christmas sales to rise 4.1% to €23.1 billion [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 18% of the €129.2 billion forecast for the two months, and it is growing twice as fast as the whole.

**What you can do with this.** The ledger's advice is not to spend less or more. It is to write the number down. A budget of €262 that you set in November and check in December is a decision; a budget that you discover on your card statement in January is an accident. If you are among the 24% who plan to cut, the cheapest way is the oldest: fewer, better presents, bought before the last weekend, when the choice is wider and the delivery promise firmer.

**The bottom line.** The national Christmas is forecast to be 2% bigger in euros. Yours, on average, is €1 smaller. Both can be true, and the gap between them is the season's price list.
