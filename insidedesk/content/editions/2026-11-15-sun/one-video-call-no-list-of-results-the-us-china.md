The machinery met before the leaders do. China and the United States held the first meeting of their new trade council by video link on Friday, China's commerce ministry said on Saturday [1]. The council was agreed in May; Friday was its first sitting [1]. The White House says Donald Trump will meet Xi Jinping on Thursday on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen [2].

The readout used the usual adjectives: "candid, in-depth and constructive" exchanges on tariffs, the reciprocal tariff arrangement, agricultural trade "and other issues of mutual concern" [1][2]. It did not say whether anything was agreed [2]. Both sides will keep using the council to "shorten the list of issues", the ministry said, in the service of "a constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability based on respect, fairness and reciprocity" [1]. That is a long phrase for a short readout. The useful word in it is "list": a council that keeps one can be checked against it next time.

The central item is the one from September. At their eighth round of consultations, held in New York and Washington from 20 to 23 September, the two sides agreed to cut tariffs on about $30 billion of each other's goods, with tariffs on around 90 per cent of the covered products to fall to most-favoured-nation rates [1]. Reuters describes it as a $60 billion package that includes roughly $17 billion of US farm goods China imported in 2024; it has yet to be finalised [2].

The farm ledger shows the gap between agreement and delivery. China agreed to buy $17 billion of US farm goods a year through 2028, yet its purchases of US farm goods other than soybeans are on track for a seven-year low this year, according to a Reuters analysis of customs data [2]. Beijing calls the tariff cuts a prerequisite for buying more [2]. Each side, in other words, is waiting for the other's paperwork.

For you: you don't need to read Thursday's handshake. Watch for a published tariff list with product codes and an effective date. That is when a summit stops being a photograph and starts showing up in invoices, freight bookings and the prices on the shelf.
