On Sundays this desk puts the week back in the order it happened. Europe's total is tidy: the Stoxx Europe 600 rose 15.81 points, or 2.44%, to 664.14, its largest weekly gain since the week ending 3 July [4]. The order is less tidy, and more useful.

**1. Monday did most of the lifting.** The Stoxx 600 rose 1.26% to 656.52, snapping a two-day losing streak; it was still 0.60% short of its August record [1]. Desk arithmetic: Monday's 8.19 points were just over half of the week's 15.81 [1][4].

**2. Tuesday, a small step.** Desk arithmetic from Wednesday's figures: the index closed at about 658.32 on Tuesday, a rise of roughly 0.27% [2].

**3. Wednesday, the record.** Up 0.72% to 663.05, a new record close, and the largest three-day gain since June [2]. Year to date the index was up 11.97% [2].

**4. Thursday, the nudge.** Up 0.14% to 664.01, another record, four days up in a row, on the day Nvidia's founder resigned [3][11]. In Frankfurt, the DAX set its own record close at 26,771.88 [5].

**5. Friday, flat and still counted.** The Stoxx 600 added 0.13 points, or 0.02%, enough for a third record close and a fifth straight day up, its longest winning streak since August [4]. The DAX slipped 21.93 points but ended the week 3.60% higher at 26,749.95, its second-highest close ever [5]. The FTSE 100 finished 2.14% up at 10,902.56, 0.07% below its February record [6]; the CAC 40 gained 2.82% [7].

**The total, and what it is made of.** Investing.com put Europe's resilience down to a heavy weight in value and defensive sectors and a solid third-quarter earnings season, while Nvidia shares were heading for a weekly loss of about 11% [8]. Wall Street had a good week too: the S&P 500 rose 2.51% and broke a run of four losing weeks, but fell 0.40% on Friday [9]. Brent fell 2.52% to $95.84, its second weekly decline [10], which takes some pressure off company costs [8]. The caution came from someone who was paid to give it: "Investors tend to assume that what has performed well will continue to do so. History shows that isn't always the case," said Sean Peche of Ranmore Fund Management [8].

**For you.** Monday's jump made the headlines possible, and the four quieter days kept them. A week of records built on invoices and earnings is sturdier than one built on a single name, but it is still a week. Nothing here is investment advice.
