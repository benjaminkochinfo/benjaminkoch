Tariffs are argued about at podiums. They are measured in customs declarations. Eurostat's data, updated on Friday and summarised by Expansión, give the first nine months of 2026 [1].

To the United States: EU exports from January to September came to €383.5 billion, down 12.4 per cent on a year earlier, a fall of more than €54.5 billion [1]. Imports from the US rose almost 8 per cent to more than €292.3 billion [1]. The EU's surplus with the US was €91.2 billion, 36.5 per cent smaller than the nearly €143.8 billion of the same period last year [1]. In September alone, exports to the US fell 10.6 per cent while imports from the US rose 14.7 per cent [1].

With China, the gap runs the other way. EU exports to China fell 1.3 per cent to €145.7 billion; imports rose 10 per cent to more than €470.6 billion, leaving a deficit of about €324.9 billion, up 16.2 per cent [1]. Desk arithmetic: the deficit with China is about three and a half times the surplus with America.

Overall, extra-EU exports grew only 0.9 per cent to just over €2 trillion while imports rose 9.8 per cent, turning last year's €85.5 billion surplus into an €83.2 billion deficit [1]. The new agreements with Mercosur, India, Indonesia and Australia will take time to show up, the paper notes [1].

For you: a trade deal is a promise about the next decade. The customs ledger is a record of the last nine months. Read both, and trust the one with invoices behind it.
