Anthropic is offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, of which 801,351,354 are new shares and the rest are sold by existing holders, and it has applied to list on Nasdaq as "ANT" [2]. People familiar with the plan told CNBC it intends to price on 18 November and trade on 19 November, with about 20% of the shares aimed at retail investors, against a typical 5% to 10% [1].

The headline number is the valuation: up to $1.405 trillion fully diluted at the top of the range, up from $965 billion in a May funding round [1]. The more useful number sits a paragraph lower. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC have agreed to buy $26 billion of Class N stock in a private placement at the IPO price, shares that will not be subject to underwriters' lock-ups [1].

Read that list again: several of these names make the chips, memory and cloud capacity that AI labs run on. That is not sinister; suppliers have financed their customers for as long as there have been invoices. But it means part of this deal is a supply chain buying a seat at its customer's table, in the same month that the AI trade was being repriced.

The filing's own figures are the other half of the story: $33.24 billion of revenue in the nine months to September, and a $104 billion net loss, most of it from fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives [1]. Run-rate revenue was about $81 billion at the end of October [1]. Price the revenue, footnote the loss. Nothing here is investment advice.
