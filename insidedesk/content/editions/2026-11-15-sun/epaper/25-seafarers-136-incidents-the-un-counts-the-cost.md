Wars are counted in missiles and ministers. The UN counted crews. At least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the war on Iran began in February, assistant secretary-general Khaled Khiari told the Security Council on Friday, with figures running to 11 November [1]. "This situation cannot be allowed to become the new normal," he said [1].

The International Maritime Organization tracked 136 security incidents involving vessels in Hormuz and the wider Middle East between 1 March and 11 November: 35 of them in October, the highest monthly tally since the conflict began, and 10 in the first eleven days of November [1]. Desk arithmetic: the other 91 incidents were spread over the seven months from March to September, 13 a month, so October ran at about 2.7 times that pace.

The weekend added more. Iranian authorities said on Saturday that three oil tankers had hit mines and caught fire in "unauthorised" or "restricted" areas of the strait; Iran's navy warned that any "offending tanker" in the restricted area east of the strait "will be set on fire by hitting naval mines" [1]. The US military says it has cleared the main routes and that the waterway is open [1]. UKMTO puts shipping at about 65 per cent below pre-war levels [1].

Khiari named the bill: higher energy, transport and insurance costs, and "severely constrained" fertiliser shipments [1]. Somebody signs on to these ships every week. The insurance premium is the price of that decision, written down.
