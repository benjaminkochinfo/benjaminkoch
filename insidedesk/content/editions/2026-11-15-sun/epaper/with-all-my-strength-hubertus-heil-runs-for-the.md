Until Saturday the race for the SPD leadership had no runners. Then the former labour minister Hubertus Heil wrote to the party's regional branches and executive: he will stand for one of the two chairs at the federal party conference in Bremen in mid-January, where the whole leadership is re-elected [1].

The key sentence is about time, not policy. "That is why I want to devote myself with all my strength to a party chairmanship," he writes (desk translation) [1]. The ARD capital studio reads it as aimed at Lars Klingbeil, who is party chair, finance minister and vice-chancellor at once [1]. A motion quoted by SPIEGEL and seen by ARD says the federal chair "may not, in the current situation, be held at the same time as a ministerial office" (desk translation) [1].

The programme lines are familiar ground: families who can rely on their income, "affordable" living, respect for every kind of work, a "strong police", continued support for Ukraine [1]. Klingbeil has not taken himself out of the race. Anke Rehlinger, Boris Pistorius and Matthias Miersch have ruled out running; Manuela Schwesig, seen as the strongest contender for the female co-chair, has left it open [1].

For you: the real choice in Bremen is organisational. Should the person who runs the party also sign the budget? Both answers have costs. One chair with a ministry speaks with authority but little freedom. One without speaks freely but cannot deliver. Heil has picked his side of that trade.
