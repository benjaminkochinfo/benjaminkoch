On Friday the US Food and Drug Administration approved durvalumab (Imfinzi) with enfortumab vedotin before surgery, followed by durvalumab alone after bladder removal, for adults with muscle-invasive bladder cancer who cannot take or have refused cisplatin chemotherapy [1]. Up to half of such patients are ineligible for cisplatin, often because of kidney function, and for them the standard has been surgery alone [1].

The evidence comes from the VOLGA phase III trial, which randomised 695 patients at 182 centres in 25 countries [1]. In a planned interim analysis, the combination cut the risk of recurrence, progression, missed surgery or death by 49 per cent against the comparator (hazard ratio 0.51) [1]. An estimated 64 per cent were alive and event-free at two years, against 45 per cent [1]. The risk of death fell by 42 per cent; 78 per cent were alive at two years, against 69 per cent [1].

Now the other column. Grade 3 and 4 adverse events of any cause occurred in 64 per cent of patients on the combination and 44 per cent in the comparator arm [1]. Patient-reported quality of life was maintained after surgery, the company says [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is 20 points more serious side effects for 19 points more patients alive and event-free at two years. Both numbers belong in the conversation between patient and oncologist.

The source is the manufacturer's release, and the trial lead quoted has disclosed financial interests related to AstraZeneca [1]. Applications are under review in the EU and Japan [1]. A good trial is the start of a decision, not the end of one.
