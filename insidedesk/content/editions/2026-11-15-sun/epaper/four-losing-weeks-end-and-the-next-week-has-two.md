The S&P 500 rose 184.81 points, or 2.51%, last week to 7,561.67, its largest weekly gain since early August, and snapped a four-week losing streak [1]. It is still 3.29% below its 6 October record, and up 10.46% this year [1]. Friday itself was a 0.40% fall, the second down day in a row [1].

The calendar now sets two exams. Nvidia reports after the US close on Tuesday, its first results since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive and board member on Thursday; interim chief executive Colette Kress will take the call [2]. "That's a Nvidia question, not an AI question," said Cullen Rogers of Wedbush Fund Advisers [2]. On Wednesday evening Anthropic is expected to price its listing, and to start trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday as "ANT", selling 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each [2]. Desk arithmetic: that is roughly $58 billion to $64 billion of stock.

Underneath both sits the rate question. After Friday's producer prices, futures put a 90% probability on a quarter-point Federal Reserve rise in December, up from 85% a day earlier, according to CME FedWatch as cited by Kiplinger [2].

So you have one company's invoice, one company's price tag and one central bank's arithmetic in four days. The week just gone proved the market can survive a headline. The coming one asks it to read the numbers. Nothing here is investment advice.
