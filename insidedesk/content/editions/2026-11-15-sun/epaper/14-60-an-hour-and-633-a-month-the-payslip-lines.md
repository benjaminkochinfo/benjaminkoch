This week's ledgers were full of 1 January letters about gas, power and parcels. Here is a 1 January line that runs the other way. The statutory minimum wage rises from €13.90 to €14.60 gross an hour on 1 January 2027, as set in the fifth minimum-wage adjustment regulation [1].

Because the mini-job ceiling is tied to the minimum wage, it moves too, without anyone having to vote on it again. The rule since October 2022 is ten hours a week at the minimum wage: multiply the hourly rate by 130, divide by three and round up to a full euro [2]. That takes the ceiling from €603 to €633 a month, or €7,596 a year, up from €7,236 [2]. The midijob band now starts at €633.01 and still ends at €2,000 a month [2].

Desk arithmetic: €14.60 is €0.70, or about 5.0%, more an hour than €13.90. For a full-time job of 40 hours a week, using the same 130-over-three formula scaled up (about 173.3 hours a month), that is roughly €2,531 gross a month, about €121 more than this year.

For you, two checks. If you employ a cleaner, a carer or a helper on a mini-job, the hourly rate must rise on 1 January, and the hours that fit under €633 are still capped at about ten a week [2]. If you hold a mini-job yourself, an occasional unforeseen overrun is allowed in no more than two calendar months a year [2]. A pay rise written into a regulation is the dullest kind of good news. It also arrives on time.
