Germany's long-term care insurance will draw money from an additional federal loan for the first time this month, the health ministry told dpa; the amount is not yet fixed [1]. October was covered by €522 million left over from the original €3.2 billion loan for 2026 [1]. The Bundestag budget committee approved up to €700 million more in early October, paid out month by month as needed [1]. The reason is plain: spending rose faster than assumed because the number of people needing care keeps growing [1]. For 2027, minister Carsten Linnemann's savings package is meant to raise €6.25 billion against an expected €7.6 billion shortfall [1]. Desk arithmetic: that leaves about €1.35 billion uncovered. The care commission reports by the end of January [1].
