Most seasonal outlooks are sold on their headline. Germany's weather service sells its own on the caveats, which is why it is worth reading. The DWD's seasonal climate outlook, dated 19 October and covering November to March, sees a "moderate tendency" of 69 per cent for a warmer early winter, November to January, than the 1991–2020 average [1]. Warmer here means a three-month mean above 3.2°C, and the DWD rates the forecast quality for that period as relatively good [1].

Then the confidence falls away. For December to February it gives 61 per cent for a warmer winter (above 2.0°C), and for January to March 64 per cent (above 2.8°C) [1]. For both, it rates forecast quality as low and recommends using the observed climate instead: colder, normal and warmer treated as equally likely [1]. That is a public agency telling you its model is not better than history for those months. Few institutions print that sentence.

The context is in the same document. Last year's observed early winter was normal at 2.4°C, the winter normal at 1.7°C [1]. The WMO multi-model ensemble broadly confirms the DWD picture, with somewhat higher warm probabilities in the south and west, partly because it compares against a shorter, colder reference period [1]. The DWD's statistically selected runs point to slightly colder conditions in mid and late winter than the full ensemble [1].

What to do with it: nothing dramatic. A mild lean for the next weeks is not a promise for February, and the outlook says so itself [1]. Keep the boiler serviced and the gas bill budgeted for a normal winter.
