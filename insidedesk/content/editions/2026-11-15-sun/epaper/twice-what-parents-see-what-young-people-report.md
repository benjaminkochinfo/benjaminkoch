New research from the Ad Council and Surgo Health finds that twice as many young people report symptoms of depression or anxiety as their parents may recognise [1]. "When we ask young people directly, they tell us things even attentive parents can miss," said Surgo Health chief executive Sema Sgaier [1]. Her tracker also asks what helps: time with friends, being active, getting outside, creative outlets [1]. The release comes with a campaign, and the campaign's own figure is modest: parents aware of it were more likely to feel strongly confident about such conversations, 44 per cent against 30 per cent [1]. The advice is cheaper than any app. Ask, then listen longer than feels comfortable.
