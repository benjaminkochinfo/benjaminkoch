Brent crude for January delivery settled at $95.84 a barrel on Friday, down $2.48, or 2.52%, on the week [1]. Over two weeks it lost $21.80, or 18.53%: the largest two-week fall in dollars since the week ending 10 October 2008, and the largest in percentage terms since April 2020 [1]. It is 19.02% below its 2026 high of $118.35, set on 31 March, and still 57.50% higher than at the start of the year [1].

The week itself was not a straight line. Brent rose for three sessions in a row to Friday, adding $4.35, after Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi facilities and reports of tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz [1][2]. Yet the weekly total still pointed down, as an International Energy Agency report pointed to a rising global surplus and US crude and fuel inventories rose [2].

"It's clear the biggest fear a few months ago was a fear of shortage," said Phil Flynn of the Price Futures Group. "Now it looks like the biggest fear is a surplus" [2]. Andrew Lipow of Lipow Oil Associates described a market "torn" between more supply from the Middle East and unchanged negotiating positions between Washington and Tehran [2].

For you the useful number is not the swing but the level: oil at about $96 is cheaper than a fortnight ago and far dearer than in January [1]. Heating oil and diesel bills follow the level, with a lag. Don't budget on the swing. Nothing here is investment advice.
