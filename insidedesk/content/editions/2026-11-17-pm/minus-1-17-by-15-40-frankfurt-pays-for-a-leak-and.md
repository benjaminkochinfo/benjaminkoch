At 15:40 CET the DAX stood 1.17% lower at 26,286.89 points, 311.75 below Monday's close of 26,598.64 (Desk arithmetic) [1]. It had opened at 26,387.44 and found its low at 26,258.46 [1]. These are snapshot figures, not the settled close; Xetra trading ends at 17:30.

[[chart:europe-tuesday-17pm]]

Read the chart from the bottom. Deutsche Bank was down 4.48% at €31.44, the most traded DAX share by volume, after media reports built on leaked internal files [1][3]. SAP fell 4.26%, the software end of the AI worry [1]. Only four members were up at all, Zalando leading with 0.96% [1]. Around 14:40 CET the picture was the same across the region: Stoxx 600 −1.05% at 655.35, FTSE 100 −1.14%, CAC 40 −1.15%, Euro Stoxx 50 −1.19% [2]. Reuters put European tech 1.6% lower and linked it to Washington's tighter rules on Chinese access to AI chips [3].

The day's one piece of good paper came at 11:00. The ZEW index of German economic expectations rose 7.5 points to +35.6; its assessment of the current situation improved to −33.2 [4]. ZEW's president Achim Wambach called the recovery "verhalten", subdued, and said it is carried mainly by state spending, while worry about the sustainability of public debt grows [4]. A survey is not an order book, but it does not trade on Nvidia's mood either.

New York opened soft. By 15:53 CET the Dow was down 0.5%, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq 0.2% each; the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 5.15% and WTI crude was 1.8% higher at $87.30 [5]. Brent was at $93.03 at 14:46 CET [3]. The 10-year Bund yield was 3.363% in the morning [6].

Nvidia reports after the US close. Nothing about those numbers is known yet, and nothing here pretends otherwise [5]. Until then, the honest description of your portfolio is "awaiting invoice". Nothing here is investment advice.
