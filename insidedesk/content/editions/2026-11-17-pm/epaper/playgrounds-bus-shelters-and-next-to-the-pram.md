Rhineland-Palatinate's cabinet has approved a draft law that would ban smoking on playgrounds, in the outdoor areas of leisure and amusement parks and zoos, and at covered bus and train stops [1]. Vaping and heated products count as smoking, whether they contain nicotine, tobacco or cannabis [1].

The new clause is the quiet one: no smoking in the immediate vicinity of babies and toddlers, in a pram or a sling [1]. "Dabei wird bewusst auf einen starren Abstand in Metern verzichtet," said health minister Clemens Hoch: there is deliberately no fixed distance in metres; the situation decides [1]. Beer, wine and other festival tents would get a comprehensive ban too [1].

What does not change matters as much. Existing exceptions for pubs, shisha bars and discos stay, outdoor dining stays open to smokers, and small one-room pubs under 75 square metres can keep allowing smoking under current conditions [1]. Hoch calls it protection "mit Augenmaß", with a sense of proportion [1].

The timetable is long. After an external consultation the state parliament is to take up the bill in 2027, and if it passes it could take effect around the end of next year [1]. A law that hangs on judgement rather than a tape measure will live or die by enforcement, and by whether parents on a bench feel entitled to ask.
