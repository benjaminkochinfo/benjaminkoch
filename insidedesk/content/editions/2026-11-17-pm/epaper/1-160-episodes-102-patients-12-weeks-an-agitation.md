On Monday the US Food and Drug Administration approved Igalmi (dexmedetomidine), a film placed under the tongue or against the gum, for adults to use at home during acute agitation linked to schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder or bipolar I or II disorder, Teva says [1]. Until now it was cleared only under the supervision of a healthcare provider [1].

The numbers the company cites explain the interest: agitation affects 25% to 40% of patients with these conditions, and in up to 21% of cases it ends in an emergency department visit or hospital admission [1].

Read the trial for what it tested. The at-home study treated 1,160 agitation episodes in 102 patients over 12 weeks, and its primary endpoint was safety and tolerability [1]. The most common side effects were sleepiness, dry mouth, dizziness and numbness in the mouth, and there were no discontinuations for tolerability [1]. The evidence that the drug calms agitation comes from the earlier supervised trials, where scores fell by about 9.6 points at two hours against about 4.9 on placebo [1]. Labels warn of low blood pressure and slow heart rate; patients are told to sit or lie down after taking it [1].

Teva has not yet said when or at what price it will sell the at-home version [1]. The hard part of home treatment is rarely the molecule. It is the plan around it: who notices, who helps, and who is called if it does not work.
