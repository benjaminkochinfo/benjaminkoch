Home Depot reported quarterly sales of $43.4 billion on Tuesday, ahead of the $42.8 billion analysts had expected [1]. Demand from professional contractors and smaller repair jobs offset continued weakness in discretionary consumer spending, and the retailer raised its fiscal 2026 sales outlook to the top end of its earlier range, about 4.5% growth [1].

It also named its chief financial officer, Richard McPhail, as chief executive from 23 November [1]. He succeeds Ted Decker, who took medical leave in August, has since returned, and will become chairman [1].

The market's verdict was mild. Shares were about 0.9% lower before the open and down 2% early in New York trading, with Investopedia describing the outlook as disappointing [1][2].

This is the other test of the week, and arguably the more useful one for you. A chip maker's quarter tells you what data-centre builders plan to spend. A hardware chain's quarter tells you what plumbers, roofers and electricians actually billed, and whether households still fix the leaking tap themselves. The professionals are carrying the numbers; the do-it-yourself customer is not [1].

A finance chief in the top job is not a strategy. It is a signal that the next years will be run on margins and inventories rather than on speeches. Nothing here is investment advice.
