Tesla says it will invest €3 billion in its factory at Grünheide, near Berlin, so that it can build Cybercabs, its driverless taxis [1]. Construction is to begin in 2027 and the first vehicles are to leave the line in 2029 [1]. The company names a capacity of up to 1 million units, though DW notes the timeframe for that goal was unclear [1].

"With this, we are laying the foundations for the world's most technologically advanced Tesla factory here in Grünheide," the company said [1]. Brandenburg's premier Dietmar Woidke called it "an enormously important economic decision" [1].

Now the dull machinery. Tesla says it will submit the application for the new building by the end of the year [1]. Employment at the plant had dipped to around 11,000 before rising to 12,600 in October; the expansion is expected to add several hundred jobs [1]. The plant has a history of tension with neighbours over drinking water, and any new hall goes through Brandenburg's permitting process like the last one [1].

Note the order of events. The announcement came days after Tesla said it would stop building Cybertrucks, and while driverless Cybercabs are allowed in only a few places, Austin among them [1]. A factory can be planned before the law that lets its product drive; it cannot sell many cars before then.

For you, the number to watch is not the million. It is the date on the building application.
