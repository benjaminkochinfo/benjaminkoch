The Deutscher Wetterdienst's medium-range outlook, issued at 11:30 CET on Tuesday, has a two-word summary for the weekend: polar air [1]. Its headline reads "at first mild and very wet", then "markedly cooler" [1].

**Friday is the wet day.** A nearly stationary front across the country brings persistent rain on Friday, with 30 to 40, locally around 50 litres per square metre in 24 hours from southern North Rhine-Westphalia across Hesse to the Thuringian Forest and the Fichtelgebirge [1]. In the southern half, gusts reach Beaufort 7, on the hills 8 to 9 [1]. Highs run from only 4 to 7 degrees in the north to 13 in the south [1].

**Then the front moves.** Overnight into Saturday it pushes south as a cold front, and polar air floods the country, with highs of 4 to 9 degrees on Saturday [1]. Above 800 to 1,000 metres in the Alps, 5 and locally 10 centimetres of fresh snow are possible by Saturday morning [1]. Elsewhere the snow line hovers around 600 metres, meaning a little snow on high uplands and "only rarely flakes" below [1]. Nights bring widespread light frost, moderate frost into Sunday, and patches of black ice [1].

**The honest uncertainty.** The forecasters say confidence fades from Sunday evening, with the models disagreeing on a low in the south-west [1].

For you: this is the week to check the car's tyres and the scraper, and to move the frost-tender pots indoors, rather than a week to expect a white landscape.
