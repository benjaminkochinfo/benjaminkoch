The European Commission began consulting member states on Tuesday about a new package of sanctions against Russia [1]. Brussels calls the process "confessionals": one capital at a time, to test the waters and avoid surprises [1]. It will be the 22nd package since February 2022 and is to be formally presented before the end of the month [1].

The likely targets are Russia's banks, the cryptocurrency channels it uses to get round mainstream payment systems, and the ageing "shadow fleet" that carries oil past the price cap [1]. The fight against circumvention will feature prominently, backed by a dedicated sanctions office the Commission has set up for enforcement [1].

The most telling line is what will be missing. No major or headline-grabbing measures are expected, so that negotiations run smoothly and conclude in December [1]. The last package saw several governments use vetoes to water down, postpone or delete parts they disliked, and officials want to avoid a repeat [1]. "I don't think the appetite for more sanctions is diminishing among member states," a senior diplomat said [1].

On Wednesday the Commission presents a proposal for a €45 billion loan to cover Ukraine's 2027 financing shortfall [1].

For you: a sanctions package designed to pass quietly is not a weak one. Enforcement offices and payment channels are where sanctions actually bite; the grand announcements are mostly for the evening news.
