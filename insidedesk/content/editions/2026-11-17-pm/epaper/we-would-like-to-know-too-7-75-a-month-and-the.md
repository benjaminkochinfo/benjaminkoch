For a three-person household, the electricity tax cut Friedrich Merz has promised is worth about €70 to €90 a year, according to ARD [1]. Verivox puts it at €7.75 a month for a household using 4,000 kilowatt hours a year [2]. So far the tax has been cut only for industry, manufacturing and agriculture [1].

On Monday Merz and CSU leader Markus Söder said the coalition committee would decide the cut on Tuesday evening [1]. Asked what lay behind that agreement, people in the finance ministry replied: "Das wüssten wir auch gern", we would like to know too [1]. Merz has ruled out cheaper half-measures such as a temporary cut or a one-cent reduction [2].

Options on the table pull in opposite directions. The Federal Court of Auditors sees €17 billion of potential in cutting subsidies, though lobbies resist [2]. The Union suggested using surpluses on the renewables (EEG) account, which the SPD rejects; some in the SPD would rather raise corporation tax or the flat tax on capital income [2]. The vice-chancellor's ministry warns against "financial-policy experiments" [2].

For you: €7.75 a month is real money, but the thing to read tomorrow is the line underneath it. A promise without a funding line is a loan from the next budget.
