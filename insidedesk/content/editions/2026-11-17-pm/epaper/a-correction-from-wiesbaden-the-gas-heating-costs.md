At 14:00 on Tuesday, Destatis did something institutions are rarely praised for: it published a correction [1]. Its final consumer price releases for September and October must be revised because of errors in recording the operating costs of gas central heating [1].

The consequences are listed with care. The corrected values change aggregates that contain the item, such as housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels, and energy [1]. For October the overall index level changes too, and the harmonised European index is affected [1]. But the inflation rates, the year-on-year changes, stay unchanged for both months [1].

So the October picture stands: consumer prices 2.8% higher than a year earlier, and 0.1% lower than in September [2]. Within energy, light heating oil was 84.5% dearer than a year before, while electricity was 5.2% cheaper and natural gas, excluding operating costs, 1.2% cheaper [2]. Butter was 18.9% cheaper, eggs 15.4% dearer [2].

For you, the correction changes nothing on a bill. It matters for anything indexed to the price level itself, such as some rent or maintenance clauses tied to the consumer price index. If you have one, take the index figure from the corrected release, not from a copy saved last week. A statistic you can correct is a statistic you can trust.
