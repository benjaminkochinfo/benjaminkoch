Kaja Kallas began Tuesday's meeting of EU defence ministers with a progress report and a shopping list [1]. The European Defence Agency's steering board discussed the EU defence readiness report, which, she said, shows that investment in defence has increased "but at the same time, we have big defence capability gaps" [1].

On Ukraine, the plea was specific. "Ukraine urgently needs air defence," she said, asking countries with systems in stock to hand them over, so that Patriot missiles produced later would refill their own stocks [1]. The European Peace Facility is "finally unblocked", she added, and €1 billion goes to joint procurement "because we really need to push Member States to work together" [1].

The third item was the sea. The EU's naval Operation Aspides "really requires more naval assets to be operational", Kallas said, adding that the need is bigger than ever: "We have a lot of requests for escorting the ships" [1].

For you: the order of her list is the order of the problem. Missiles in warehouses help now; missiles on order help later; escorts help only if someone sends a ship. Readiness reports count money spent. Gaps are counted in things not yet delivered.
