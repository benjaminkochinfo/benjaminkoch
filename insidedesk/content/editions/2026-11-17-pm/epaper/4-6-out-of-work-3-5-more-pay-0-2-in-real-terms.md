Britain's unemployment rate fell to 4.6% in the three months to September, from 4.9% in the previous three months, the Office for National Statistics said on Tuesday [1]. The employment rate rose to 75.4% [1]. Pay told a quieter story: regular earnings excluding bonuses grew 3.5% a year and total pay 3.6% [1]. Adjusted for inflation (CPIH), regular pay rose just 0.2% in real terms [1]. The public sector led with 5.4% regular pay growth against 3.1% in the private sector, partly because of the timing of pay awards [1]. More people in work, each earning barely more in real terms: a labour market that is absorbing people rather than bidding for them. If you are negotiating a raise, the 0.2% is the number to bring.
