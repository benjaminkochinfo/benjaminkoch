The US dollar rose to its strongest level since April 2025 on Tuesday, Reuters reported, as markets treated a Federal Reserve rate rise in December as a certainty [1]. LSEG data put the chance of a quarter-point rise at 96% and of a half-point move at about 4% [2]. St. Louis Fed president Alberto Musalem said the Fed may need several hikes and should act soon; New York's John Williams and Governor Christopher Waller were due to speak later [1]. The euro was at $1.09, near its lowest since April 2025, and the yen hovered near 160 per dollar, with traders watching for intervention [1]. For you, the dull consequence is in the invoice currency: anything priced in dollars, from an American software subscription to a barrel of oil, costs a little more in euros this week.
