Wednesday's day-ahead auction has cleared, and SMARD now lists the price for delivery on 18 November: €146.25 per megawatt hour on average for Germany and Luxembourg [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €65.33, or about 31%, below Tuesday's €211.58, and the lowest weekday average since Monday 26 October, when the price was €116.57 [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-17pm]]

**The discount has a timetable.** The hourly list is more instructive than the average. Between midnight and 07:00 the price stays between €80.64 and €116.20; the cheapest hour of the day is 04:00 to 05:00 at €80.64 [1]. Desk arithmetic: those seven night hours average €99.01. Then the price climbs to €150.24 between 08:00 and 09:00, eases to about €120 around midday, and rises again from 15:00 [1].

**The evening keeps its price.** The dearest hour on Wednesday is 18:00 to 19:00, at €212.43 [1]. Tuesday's was €215.19 in the same hour [1]. Desk arithmetic: the evening hour everyone cooks and charges in is €2.76 cheaper than a day earlier, while the 08:00 hour is €113.32 cheaper (€150.24 against €263.56). The seven hours from 16:00 to 23:00 average €202.82 on Wednesday, roughly twice the night.

**What we can say, and what we cannot yet.** This morning's forecast from the grid operators had Tuesday's onshore wind building through the day and peaking late in the evening, and that is consistent with the cheap Wednesday night [2]. The operators' forecast for Wednesday itself was not yet on SMARD when we checked at 16:34 CET, so this desk will not tell you why Wednesday's evening stays dear [2]. A price list is a fact; an explanation without the generation data is a guess.

**For you.** On a dynamic tariff, the useful hours on Wednesday are the small ones: a washing machine or a car on a timer set for after midnight meets a price under €120 at the exchange, while 18:00 meets one above €210 [1]. On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches your bill this month. Nothing here is investment advice.
