The Netherlands still has a government. Rob Jetten's minority cabinet will stay in office after four days of crisis talks; D66, the VVD and the CDA said late on Monday that "intensive talks" had brought a "restoration of trust" [1]. The government information service announced the deal at around 22:00, after more than nine hours at the Catshuis, the prime minister's residence in The Hague [1]. Ministers held an extra cabinet meeting on Tuesday morning to hear what had been agreed [1].

The row was about money and procedure. D66 and the CDA made a deal with the left-wing PRO and Volt that raises wealth tax by €1 billion and uses it to cut income tax [1]. The VVD said that broke the coalition agreement, and on Thursday its MPs voted against their own cabinet's whole tax plan, which still passed with the smallest possible majority, 76 of 150 seats [1]. The next day VVD leader Dilan Yesilgöz called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment" [1].

According to NOS, the leaders discussed breaking up the coalition or carrying on without the VVD, and dropped both [1]. No other party joins [1]. Instead they brought in Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Council of State and a former CDA leader, to help reach working agreements with opposition parties on the budget; he will advise rather than strike deals himself [1].

The opposition is not impressed. "The big question for the whole country is: do we still have a cabinet?" Klaver asked, calling the VVD's vote "totally irresponsible" and demanding a debate [1]. Municipalities' association VNG, the logistics lobby Evofenedex and property developers' group Neprom had urged the cabinet to settle its differences and serve its term [1].

For you: the coalition holds 66 of 150 seats, so it needs opposition votes in both houses for any law [1]. That is not a flaw the deal fixed; it is the condition the deal accepted. Judge it by the senate vote before Christmas, not by Wednesday's speeches.
