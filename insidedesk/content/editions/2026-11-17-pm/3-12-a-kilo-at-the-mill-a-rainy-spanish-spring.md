The weather story of the Spanish olive harvest is told in spring, not in November. Spain's agriculture ministry expects 1,602,596 tonnes of olive oil in the 2026/27 season, 23% more than last year, and says plainly why: mild temperatures and abundant rain in the main growing areas gave a good flowering and fruit set [1]. Andalusia alone is put at 1,261,200 tonnes, 29% up and 79% of the national total; Jaén province at 575,000 tonnes [1].

**The price is the other half.** On Tuesday the farm paper Agrónoma reported the Poolred figures for the week of 10 to 16 November: extra virgin at €3.12 a kilo at origin, virgin at €2.90, lampante at €2.78, from 60 deals and 3,583 tonnes [2]. Prices "continue without recovering" in a thin market where producers are harvesting rather than selling [2]. Desk arithmetic: a week's sales were about 0.2% of the forecast harvest, which is why one week's price is a signal, not a verdict.

**Same oil, different provinces.** Official data for week 45 show extra virgin at €2.979 a kilo in Jaén, €3.049 in Córdoba and €3.500 in Seville [2]. Some new-harvest oils already fetch more than €3.50, while last season's stocks trade around €3 [2].

**The dull machinery.** From November, mills must start storing oil towards a compulsory withdrawal of 12% of the total harvest, a mechanism meant to hold prices or at least stop the fall [2]. Desk arithmetic: 12% of the ministry's estimate is roughly 192,000 tonnes kept off the market for now. The ministry itself warns that the estimate is preliminary: the coming weeks' weather and the effect of the summer heat on unirrigated groves will set the final yield [1].

**For you.** A good harvest takes months to reach the supermarket shelf, and the shelf price is not the mill price. What the numbers do say is that this season, the weather has been on the side of supply.
