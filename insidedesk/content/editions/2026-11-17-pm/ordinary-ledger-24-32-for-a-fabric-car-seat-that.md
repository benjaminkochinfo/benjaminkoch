Some of the most important consumer information in Germany arrives as a crash-test report. On Tuesday Stiftung Warentest published one: two fabric harness systems, sold online as substitutes for a child car seat, failed in its frontal crash test [1].

**The two products.** One, a "portable" polyester harness for children from 0 to 4 years, cost €24.32 from the website Verodie [1]. The other, a five-point harness for children from 1 to 12 years, cost €29.33 from the website Wecro [1]. Both are strapped onto the car's own seat [1].

**The milliseconds.** In the frontal crash the plastic buckles of the Verodie harness broke within 56 milliseconds, and the straps holding it to the seat tore as well [1]. The Wecro model was then tested at a lower speed of only 30 km/h; its plastic buckles gave way after about 66 milliseconds [1]. In both cases the dummy flew forward almost unbraked, which the testers call life-threatening for the child and for the other passengers [1].

**What was missing.** The fabric seats have no side wings and no head support, and lack the buckles with a red release button that are mandatory for such seats in the EU [1]. There was no instruction manual and no approval label [1]. The products are not approved as child car seats and should not be sold in the EU at all; yet variants are easy to find, the testers say, including on Amazon and kaufland.de [1]. Neither seller answered when confronted with the results [1].

**For you.** Before you buy a child seat, look for the approval label and the red button, and if you can, buy in a shop where you can check the fit and the label in person, as Stiftung Warentest recommends [1]. If a seat costs €25 and carries no approval label, the price is not the bargain. It is the warning.
