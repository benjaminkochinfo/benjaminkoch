Qatari mediators presented Iran with a new draft deal on Sunday to end the eight-month war with the US and Israel, an official briefed on the negotiations told Reuters [1]. It sets a seven-day timeline of daily confidence-building steps that each side must take, could begin early next week, and would have several countries acting as guarantors; Tehran's response is expected by the end of the week [1]. The official said the Qatari team had coordinated with Washington before going to Tehran [1].

Shortly afterwards a senior Iranian official involved in the talks said Doha had presented only "unacceptable ideas and not an offer", and that Iran's refusal to show flexibility on the nuclear issue was unchanged [1]. The sequencing is the quarrel. Washington wants the nuclear programme in the talks from the start; Iran wants the US blockade of its ports ended and oil sanctions lifted first [1].

This is the second seven-day plan in two months. In late September Iran put forward its own, under which the Strait of Hormuz could reopen if conditions were met; Donald Trump rejected it [1]. An interim agreement in June unravelled quickly [1]. Meanwhile the price of not agreeing is written down daily: oil rose more than 1% to above $115 a barrel on Wednesday after a Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery [1], and Pentagon data reviewed by Reuters put US spending on the war above $47 billion [2].

Read the two statements together and they are less contradictory than they sound. "Not an offer" is what a negotiator says before replying to one. The draft exists, the reply has a deadline, and mediators with a guarantor list are doing the patient clerical work wars eventually end with. You do not need to believe either side's tone; you need to see whether a reply arrives by Friday. Nothing here is investment advice.
