Two researchers at the University of Warsaw asked 200 young Polish Facebook users to imagine missing a get-together of family and friends [1]. Half read a version in which they had not seen these people for a long time and then saw photos of the party. The other half read a version in which they had seen everyone recently and had more meetings planned [1].

Only the first group's mood fell, from an average of 3.88 to 3.52 on a five-point scale [1]. That group also reported more bodily activation and more changeable emotions. Measures of reflective processing did not differ [1]. The authors conclude that FOMO is "tied primarily to our intuitive, automatic functioning rather than cognition" [1]. It was a convenience sample of mostly young people, and the scenarios were imagined, as the authors note themselves [1].

The useful detail is in the control story. The difference was not the missed party. It was not knowing how your people were and having nothing planned. The study did not test remedies, but the hint is plain: booking the next meeting may do more for you than scrolling through the photos.
