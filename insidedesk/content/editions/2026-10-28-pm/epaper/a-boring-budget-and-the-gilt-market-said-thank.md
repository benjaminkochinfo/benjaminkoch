Britain's finance minister John Healey delivered his first budget on Wednesday, and markets barely moved, Reuters reports, taking comfort from his pledge to stick to the government's borrowing rules [1]. Healey put the headroom, the room for extra spending or tax cuts under the fiscal rules, at £15 billion, in line with a Reuters poll but below the £23.6 billion of March [1].

**The bond market's verdict.** The ten-year gilt yield fell 3 basis points to about 5.31% and the 30-year 4.5 points to about 5.70% [1]. "The bond market is calling it a very boring budget," said Kathleen Brooks of XTB [1]. Mike Bell of BlueBay Asset Management said the point was "to avoid a Liz Truss moment", a reference to the 2022 mini-budget that set off a bond rout [1]. The pound slipped 0.1% to $1.3295 [1].

**The banks' bill.** The surcharge banks pay on profits above a threshold rises from 3% to 6%, partly reversing a cut from 8% three years ago [1]. That was less than feared: Lloyds, Barclays and NatWest rose between 3.3% and 3.6%, and the FTSE 100 was up 0.3% [1].

Desk arithmetic: the headroom shrank by £8.6 billion since March, and yields still fell. Investors were not paid for drama; they were paid for arithmetic that added up. A finance ministry that bores its creditors is, for you as a taxpayer, the cheaper kind. Nothing here is investment advice.
