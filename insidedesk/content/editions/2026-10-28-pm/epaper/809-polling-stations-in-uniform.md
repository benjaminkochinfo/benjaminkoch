Turnout among Israeli army personnel was 79.9%, up 6.2 points on 2022, the IDF said; it ran 809 polling stations on bases and in combat zones [1]. Israelis normally vote only at their assigned station, but soldiers are among those allowed to use special ones, and those ballot boxes go straight to the Knesset for checks before counting [1]. That is part of why roughly 600,000 double-envelope votes were still outstanding on Wednesday afternoon, to be added over the next couple of days [2]. The committee also says the last 7% of ordinary ballots need extra oversight [2]. Slow is not suspicious. It is what a count looks like when somebody checks it.
