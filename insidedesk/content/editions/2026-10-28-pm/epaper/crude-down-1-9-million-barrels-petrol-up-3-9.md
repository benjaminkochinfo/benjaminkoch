The US Energy Information Administration's weekly count, published at 15:30 CET this afternoon, put commercial crude stocks 1.892 million barrels lower in the week to 23 October, investingLive reports [1]. Analysts had expected a build of 15,000 barrels [1]. The private American Petroleum Institute had reported a draw of 2.031 million barrels late on Tuesday [1].

**The other tank.** Petrol went the other way. Gasoline inventories rose 3.870 million barrels, against an expected fall of 0.942 million [1]. Distillates, the family that includes diesel and heating oil, fell 1.462 million barrels, a larger draw than the 0.858 million expected [1]. Stocks at Cushing, Oklahoma, the delivery point for US crude futures, fell 0.624 million barrels, and refinery utilisation slipped 0.6 percentage points [1]. Desk arithmetic: crude and distillates together fell about 3.35 million barrels while petrol rose about 3.87 million, so the three together rose by roughly half a million barrels.

**Why read the boring table.** US crude was trading at $102.29, up $2.40 on the day, at the time of the report [1]. Prices move on the headline number. The table underneath says more: refiners ran a little less and stocks of petrol still rose sharply, which, investingLive notes, raises questions about the balance between petrol supply and demand [1]. One week of stock data is a snapshot, not a trend. Nothing here is investment advice.
