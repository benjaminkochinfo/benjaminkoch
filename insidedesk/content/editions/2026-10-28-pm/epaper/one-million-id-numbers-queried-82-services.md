Between 19 and 23 October, attackers queried about one million personal identification numbers through Moldova's government digital services, and data on roughly 750,000 people may have been extracted, the country's IT and cyber security service STISC said, according to the state news agency MOLDPRES [1]. The categories at risk include identity data, family members, pensions, benefits, driving licences, vehicles, and some financial details such as IBANs and tax debts; not everyone had every category exposed [1]. Bank card data was not affected, the authorities say [1].

The extraction was stopped at 11:30 on 23 October, and 82 public services and more than 30 systems were disconnected as a precaution [1]. The attack allegedly exploited services that could be reached without authentication [1]. The lesson is old and unexciting: every public form that answers without asking who you are is a door. Citizens are being told to distrust unexpected calls and never share codes [1].
