The Bank of Canada raised its target for the overnight rate by 25 basis points to 2.5% on Wednesday, at 14:45 CET [1]. Markets had expected a rise [2]. The interesting part is the reasoning, which is less about speeches and more about costs.

**What it saw.** CPI inflation has been around 3% since April and is expected to rise to about 3.5% by the end of the year, the Bank says [1]. Excluding petrol, inflation was 2.2% in September, and core measures remain close to 2% [1]. So the problem is not yet everywhere. But "cost pressures are building", it writes: fuel raises the cost of production and transport, and imported industrial and electronic inputs are dearer [1]. Businesses have mostly absorbed those costs so far, and the Bank's own surveys suggest more will be passed on over the next year [1].

**Why it moved now.** The Bank now expects inflation to average 3% in 2027, a percentage point above its July projection, before returning to 2% in the middle of 2028 [1]. It raised rates to reduce the risk that high energy prices spread to other goods and services, and it says there "could be a need" to go further, but will "proceed carefully" [1].

**The useful bit for you.** Ottawa did not wait for inflation to be broad. It acted on surveys of what firms say they will charge. That is a central bank reading invoices before they are written. Whether Washington tonight and Frankfurt on Thursday read the same papers the same way is their decision to make, and ours to report when they do. Nothing here is investment advice.
