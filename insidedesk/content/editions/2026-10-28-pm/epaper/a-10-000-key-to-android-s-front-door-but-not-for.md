Google has opened applications for its Registered App Stores programme, Ars Technica reports [1]. Approved third-party stores will be able to install apps with a single tap, as the Play Store does, and update them silently in the background [1]. The programme goes live on 1 December [1].

**The paperwork is the product.** To qualify, a store must protect intellectual property, remove illegal content, publish privacy policies for users and developers, keep up ongoing security measures, and hand Google documentation in English plus copies of all its apps [1]. Participation costs $10,000 a year, payable before Google reviews the application, with possible extra review fees if Google finds malware problems [1].

**Where it applies.** Not in the US, where a court order already lets rival stores be distributed inside Google Play, and, by Ars Technica's account, not in Europe either; the programme covers dozens of countries in most of the rest of the world [1]. It began as Google's alternative to the court-ordered remedies after it lost the Epic Games antitrust case [1].

**The catch is the update.** Support starts only with Android 17 QPR2, expected on Google's own Pixel phones in the coming weeks [1]. Pixels are not sold in most of the countries covered, and the phones people do use there tend to get updates slowly, Ars Technica notes; many may never receive a compatible version [1]. An open door is only as useful as the hinge. Here the hinge is a software update that many phones will not get.
