In Germany, the local default supplier, the Grundversorger, may change its prices at the start of a month, but must announce it publicly at least six weeks ahead [1]. For 1 January 2027, the last day to do so is 20 November 2026 [1]. A tracker of 116 default tariffs and price areas counts seven announced changes so far, all of them rises [1].

**The early ones are steep on gas.** Stadtwerke Hamm raises its default gas price from 11.29 to 13.97 cents per kilowatt hour on 1 January, 23.4% more, about €351 a year for a household using 12,000 kWh [1]. Stadtwerke Magdeburg and NEW raise gas from 1 December, by 22.1% and 15.0% [1]. On electricity, Hamm's rise is 12.5%, NEW's about 6% [1]. Most large suppliers, from Munich to Hamburg, are listed as "noch offen", not yet announced [1]. The comparison site Verivox said this month it expects gas suppliers broadly to pass on higher purchase costs at the turn of the year, with rises of 10% to 20% depending on tariff [2].

**The quiet right that comes with the letter.** When a default supplier changes its prices, customers may cancel without notice to the day of the change; those who show a new contract within a month of cancelling do not pay the new prices, the tracker notes, citing the electricity supply ordinance [1]. A price letter is not a verdict. It is a deadline, and deadlines favour people who read their post. Nothing here is advice.
