On Wednesday, the Irish Nurses and Midwives Organisation counted 824 patients in Irish hospitals waiting for admission to a bed, up from 777 on Tuesday. It is the highest daily figure the union has recorded this year [1]. The worst-affected hospitals were University Hospital Limerick (97), Cork University Hospital (81) and St Vincent's University Hospital in Dublin (65) [1].

The Health Service Executive put Wednesday's figure at 599 [1]. The gap is mostly about method. The HSE counts patients on trolleys or on extra beds placed in unsuitable spaces on wards, while the union also counts patients waiting on wards, in corridors or on chairs elsewhere in the hospital [1]. Desk arithmetic: the two counts are 225 apart. The union says overcrowding has kept rising since the bank holiday weekend [1].

The union wants all elective activity in acute hospitals stopped, with private capacity bought to do that work [1]. Its general secretary, Phil Ní Sheaghdha, also pointed to hiring: at least four HSE regions now need sign-off from the Department of Health before they can recruit [1]. "Hospital activity must be aligned with the workforce available," she said [1].

The record is still 931, set on 3 January 2023 [1]. Winter has not started. The useful question is not whose count is right. It is who signs the recruitment forms, and how quickly.
