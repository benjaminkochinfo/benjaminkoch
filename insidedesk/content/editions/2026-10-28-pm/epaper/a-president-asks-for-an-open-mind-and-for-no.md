Israel's president spoke on Wednesday like a man who has read the tally. "We must do everything we can, certainly after yesterday, to avoid another round of elections," Isaac Herzog said, urging the parties to keep "an open mind" [1]. Once the count is final, it is his job to pick the leader best placed to form a government; until then Benjamin Netanyahu stays on as caretaker [1].

The offers so far are pointed in different directions. Yousef Jabareen, head of the Arab-majority Joint List, said anyone who wants "a substantive and genuine alternative" to Netanyahu "will have to talk to us" [1]. Netanyahu, early on Wednesday, called on his opponents to join him in a broad government to "remove the Iranian threat once and for all" [2]. Gadi Eisenkot's change bloc has to choose between the Joint List, a unity government with Netanyahu, or new elections, the Guardian writes [1].

There is precedent for the first: in 2021 Mansour Abbas took Ra'am into a coalition led by Naftali Bennett, who now sits in the opposition bloc [1]. Precedent is not a promise. What you are watching this week is not a winner but a negotiation, and the useful habit is to judge it by what gets signed, not by what gets said on stage.
