"Vessels which do not need to transit the area are advised to remain well clear to avoid unnecessary delays to their voyage," US Naval Forces Central Command said on Wednesday in an advisory issued through the Joint Maritime Information Center [1]. Ships that do cross between the Gulf of Oman and the Strait of Hormuz should "plan additional transit time" for heavier traffic and "military blockade enforcement operations" [1]. Neutral vessels, laden or in ballast, may be questioned, visited and searched to confirm their intentions [1].

It is not clear what prompted the notice; the last similar public one came in mid-July, when the blockade of Iranian shipping began [1]. The numbers around it point both ways. Shuttle tankers and dozens of crude carriers willing to take the risk have lifted oil flows out of the Gulf to near pre-war levels, while the blockade has pushed Iranian crude exports close to zero [1]. At the same time Iran has raised its attacks on ships to almost 30 in October, a wartime high, Bloomberg reports [1].

For a shipowner the advisory is not a headline but a line item: more days at sea, more boarding paperwork, more charter hire. For you it arrives later, in delivery times and freight surcharges. The calm response is the clerical one: watch whether the notice is withdrawn, extended, or followed by a new one. Nothing here is investment advice.
