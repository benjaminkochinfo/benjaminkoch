Germany's consumer climate, measured by the NIM with GfK, slipped by 0.2 points to -30.8 for November, from -30.6 [1]. A year ago it stood at -22.5 [1]. The picture underneath is split: households' economic expectations rose for the sixth month in a row, while their willingness to buy fell slightly again and their income expectations dipped [1]. Worry about rising prices is growing, the institute says, and the willingness to save eased moderately [1].

That is a sensible household, not a gloomy one. People can see that the economy is steadying and still wait for the payslip to prove it. For the ECB, which decides on Thursday, it is a reminder that Germany's shoppers are not behaving like an overheating economy. Nothing here is investment advice.
