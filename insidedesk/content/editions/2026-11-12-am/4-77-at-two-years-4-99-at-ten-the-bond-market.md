On Wednesday the US cash bond market was shut for Veterans Day. The reply to Tuesday's hot inflation figures therefore arrived on Thursday, as trading resumed in Asia. The two-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to 4.77%, the 10-year was little changed at 4.99% and the 30-year fell 2 basis points to 5.33%, according to Saxo [1]. Bloomberg had the 30-year at 5.32% and the 10-year briefly at 4.98% [2].

**What the shape says.** A rising short end and a softer long end is what traders call a twist-flattening [1]. The two-year tracks what the Federal Reserve is expected to do next; it had already jumped as much as 20 basis points on Tuesday, the biggest one-day move since April 2025 [1]. The longer maturities picked up a little safe-haven demand as Nvidia's boardroom troubles rattled share futures [2].

**The price of the next move.** Traders now fully price a quarter-point Fed rise in December and assign a small chance of a half point, Bloomberg reports [2]. Options on SOFR, the overnight rate that much US lending is priced off, traded about 80% above their 20-day average on Tuesday, with most new positions protecting against further tightening, Saxo says [1]. A CIBC strategist told Bloomberg that a 50-basis-point move or faster tightening is "now real" as a discussion [2].

**The euro's side.** The dollar held near an 18-month high [2]. The euro was 0.2% lower at $1.1073 in Bloomberg's snapshot; AFP had it at $1.1097 slightly earlier [2][3].

**For you.** Your mortgage is not priced in Washington, but the world's benchmark borrowing costs are, and a dearer dollar makes oil and other dollar-priced imports cost more in euros. If you are fixing a loan or a deposit, the useful habit is to compare offers this week rather than wait for a headline. Nothing here is investment advice.
