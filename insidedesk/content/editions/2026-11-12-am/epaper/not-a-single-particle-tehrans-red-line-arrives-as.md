The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday evening that US Vice President JD Vance was considering in-person talks with a top Iranian security official, and that President Donald Trump had expressed openness to the idea; the paper said discussions remained preliminary, according to Investing.com [1]. Tehran has largely denied that any direct talks with the US have taken place [1].

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei set out a line in an interview with Lebanon's Al Mayadeen: Iran would not allow "even a single particle" of enriched nuclear material to leave the country, and there would be consequences in the event of new US strikes, Reuters reports [2]. Washington says nuclear issues must be part of any initial negotiations aimed at ending the eight-month-old war [3]. Analysts have noted a more conciliatory tone from Trump since the Republicans' defeat in the 3 November midterms [2].

The region did not pause for the diplomacy. On Wednesday Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles and drones launched towards Riyadh, Mecca and other regions; the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed three cruise missiles headed for the Riyadh region, two drones aimed at Mecca and southern areas, and two missiles aimed west, while the Houthis denied targeting Mecca [2].

Red lines stated before talks are normal. They tell you where the bargaining starts, not where it ends. The more useful sign would be a venue and a date for the Vance channel. Until then, the interceptor count is the record that matters.
