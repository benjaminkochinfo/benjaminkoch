Gold fell as much as 1.2% on Wednesday to trade below $4,000 an ounce during US hours, after hotter-than-expected US inflation and hawkish remarks from Fed officials [1]. On Thursday morning spot gold was 0.2% lower at $3,979.70 [1]. That followed a 2.6% fall on Tuesday, Saxo notes, with bullion "trading more as an expression of the rates narrative than a traditional haven" [2].

The context: the Bloomberg dollar index is near its highest since May 2025 and traders fully price a quarter-point Fed rise in December [1][2]. A metal that pays no interest looks dearer when cash pays more.

For you: if you bought gold as insurance, remember insurance has a premium, and this week it is paid in rates. Nothing here is investment advice.
