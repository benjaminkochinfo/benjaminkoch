If an email in the name of the Volksbanken Raiffeisenbanken is sitting in your inbox with the subject "Aktualisierung Ihrer Kundendaten zwingend erforderlich", it is not from your bank [1]. The consumer advice centre says criminals are behind it; the message was first reported to it on 5 November [1].

**How it works.** The email claims the bank must regularly check customer data for legal reasons, asks you to confirm via SecureGo plus, the banks' real approval app, and offers a blue button reading "Daten jetzt bestätigen" [1]. Confirmation is demanded by 12 November, today, with transfers supposedly restricted until then, and it is said to take only about two minutes [1]. Similar fakes have used the names of Deutsche Bank, Consorsbank and Commerzbank [1].

**The tell-tale signs.** The consumer advisers list an impersonal greeting, a dubious sender address, no account details, no reason why you in particular must check your data, and a confirmation link inside the email [1].

**What to do.** Do not reply and do not click; move it to spam [1]. If in doubt, check only through your bank's official app or the website you already know, and forward the email to the real bank [1]. If you have already clicked, run a virus scan and change passwords, including on other accounts with similar ones [1]. If you entered bank details, tell your bank at once; cards can be blocked round the clock on 116 116, and a police report is advisable [1].

A real bank does not run on two-minute deadlines. Its processes are slow, written down and dull, which is exactly why you can trust them more than an urgent blue button.
