World leaders arrived in Antalya on Wednesday for the first day of COP31's Leaders' Summit, and the negotiating rooms carried on underneath them [1]. The Earth Negotiations Bulletin's daily report shows where the arguments now sit.

**A number in a draft.** In talks on research and systematic observation, Russia, India and the Arab Group proposed deleting "1.47 ± 0.13 °C", the figure given for global mean temperature in 2026, and streamlining the text on overshooting 1.5°C [1]. The EU wanted the text to emphasise the state of the climate system and the role of Earth observation [1]. A measured number does not change because a paragraph is shortened; what changes is whether a decision text admits it.

**A fund that shrank.** On guidance to the Global Environment Facility, developing countries expressed "strong disappointment" with a 27% decrease in support under its ninth replenishment [1]. The EU, Canada and the Environmental Integrity Group suggested welcoming the outcome and encouraging extra contributions from countries "in a position to do so" [1].

**Where there was agreement.** Broad support emerged for continuing the Just Transition Work Programme beyond 2026, and for continuing the Mitigation Work Programme, though views differed on whether it should run to 2030 or 2040 [1]. Outgoing UN Secretary-General António Guterres called for a windfall tax on fossil-fuel profits [1].

**Why it matters to you.** Most of COP is not a speech but a document, negotiated comma by comma. The useful habit is to read what is in the text at the end, not what was said on the podium at the start.
