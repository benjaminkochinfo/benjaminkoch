The Dutch TTF front-month contract, Europe's benchmark gas price, rose 2.7% by 09:30 in Amsterdam on Wednesday to about €87 per megawatt hour [1]. Earlier in the week it had touched a one-month low of about €82, after President Donald Trump signalled he was not planning another attack on Iran "at this point" [1]. On Wednesday, oil rose and gas followed, as traders grew less hopeful about a US–Iran deal amid continuing tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz [1].

**Below the peak, above last year.** Wednesday's price was well below the four-year high of more than €101.82 at the end of October, but still far above where it stood a year ago [1]. Desk arithmetic: €87 is about 14.6% under that October high.

**The supply side has not moved.** LNG flows through Hormuz remain well below pre-war levels, and QatarEnergy has extended force majeure on its deliveries until at least the end of December [1]. EU storage sites were 72% full on 10 November, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe, below the five-year average for the start of the heating season, and withdrawals have begun in most EU countries [1]. The International Energy Agency said last week that the global market could stay tight well into next year [1].

**What it means for you.** A wholesale price that moves five euros in two days is a headline, not a bill. Household tariffs are bought months ahead and smooth these swings out. The number worth watching is the storage level as the cold arrives, because that, not one morning's trade, is what decides how nervous the market stays this winter.
