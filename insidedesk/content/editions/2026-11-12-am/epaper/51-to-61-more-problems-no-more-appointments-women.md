Nearly half of the post-9/11 veterans in a new study screened positive for depression, anxiety or PTSD, and about four in ten showed signs of elevated suicide risk [1]. The data come from 2,083 veterans in the BRAVO cohort, which deliberately recruited more women and veterans from ethnic minorities; the paper is in Military Psychology [1].

Women reported 51% to 61% more types of mental-health and substance-use problems than non-Hispanic white men, yet after accounting for the number of problems there was no significant difference in mental-health visits between groups [1]. Their extra physical conditions, by contrast, generally came with more appointments [1]. Lead author Jordan Davis says the study cannot say why, naming possible reasons such as too few providers and long waits [1]. A need that does not turn into an appointment is a scheduling problem before it is a medical one.
