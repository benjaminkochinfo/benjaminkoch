Within their first three years in the job, one nurse in seven in Germany thinks every day about leaving the profession, according to the third nursing study by the health insurer Barmer and the Institut für Betriebliche Gesundheitsberatung [1]. More than half, 55.5%, show a medium risk of burnout, and 36.5% often feel emotionally exhausted [1]. The study, reported by the trade press on Wednesday, looks at two questions: how to keep people in the early years, and how culturally mixed teams work together [1][2].

The constructive finding is about the dull machinery of a workplace. Nurses who are more satisfied with their work and feel more attached to their employer think less often about quitting [1]. Help comes mainly from colleagues: about six in ten get support from their team often or always, while support from managers is perceived much less often [1]. On teams, 27.1% of nurses showed low cultural competence, 48.4% medium and 24.5% high, and higher competence went with better ratings of team support, leadership, appreciation and job satisfaction [1].

Two cautions. The trade press notes that the release leaves open how many nurses were surveyed, how they were recruited and how representative they are, and that the training ideas it lists have no proven effect yet [2]. And an association is not a cause. Still, the cheapest retention policy in the study costs no money: a ward manager who asks, on a Tuesday, how the new colleague is doing.
