For the German Nursing Day, the German Hospital Federation (DKG) warned on Wednesday that changes to how hospital nursing is financed could undo recent progress [1]. Hospitals have "significantly increased" nursing staff and improved pay structures, said deputy chair Henriette Neumeyer, and they want full reimbursement of collectively agreed pay rises to continue [1]. The federation says it has been in talks with health insurers, the German Nursing Council and the ver.di union, on behalf of the health ministry, about reforming the system: changes are necessary, but "with a sense of proportion" [1].

The number to keep is the dull one: nursing staff spend about three hours a day on bureaucracy, the DKG says, time missing from direct care [1]. It calls the draft law to cut red tape "far behind expectations" [1]. A federation's statement is a position, not an audit. But three hours is a figure a ward can check against its own rota.
