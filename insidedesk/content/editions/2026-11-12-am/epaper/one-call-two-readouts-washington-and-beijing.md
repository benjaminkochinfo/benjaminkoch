US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi spoke by phone on Wednesday to prepare President Donald Trump's trip to the APEC summit in China next week and President Xi Jinping's visit to Florida for next month's G20 summit, the State Department said [1]. Its readout said the two "agreed on the importance of meaningful leader-level conversations to advance goals such as peace and denuclearization, including at any potential trilateral leaders meeting with Russia" [1].

China's earlier readout made no mention of denuclearisation or a trilateral summit, Reuters notes [1]. Xinhua called the call "positive and constructive", and quoted Wang describing the current stability in relations as "a rational choice" by both countries [1]. Last month Beijing's foreign ministry said it would be "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic" to ask China to join trilateral nuclear disarmament talks at this stage [1].

The trade file sits underneath. In September the two leaders agreed a two-month extension of a trade truce that had been due to expire in November, but disputes over tariffs, Chinese purchases of US goods, rare-earth exports and technology restrictions remain unresolved, Reuters reports [1]. Trump said on Sunday he thought he would meet Xi and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen [1].

Two readouts of one phone call are not a crisis. They are a negotiation showing its working. The useful question for next week is not who smiles at the summit, but which of the unresolved items ends up with a date and a signature.
