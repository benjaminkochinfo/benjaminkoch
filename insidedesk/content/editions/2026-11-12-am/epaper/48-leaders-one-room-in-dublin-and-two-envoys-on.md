The ninth summit of the European Political Community opens in Dublin on Thursday, co-chaired by European Council President António Costa and Taoiseach Micheál Martin [1]. Forty-eight heads of state and government have been invited: the 27 EU members plus countries from Albania to the United Kingdom, with Ukraine, Türkiye and Switzerland among them and Canada as a guest [1]. Under the theme "Europe – strong, secure and connected", leaders are to discuss competitiveness, including affordable energy, and the defence of a democratic Europe [1]. Arrivals start at 11:00 CET and the opening plenary is at 12:30 CET; an informal meeting of EU leaders follows on Friday [1].

The timing matters more than the agenda. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday and then plan to fly to Ireland to see Volodymyr Zelenskiy, who is attending the summit [2]. Speaking beside Martin on Wednesday, Zelenskiy said Kyiv hoped for concrete results this year, but that much depended on how the US approached Moscow [3].

A forum with 48 chairs and no treaty behind it is easy to mock. It produces no binding decisions. What it does produce is a room where the leaders of Armenia and Azerbaijan, or Serbia and Kosovo, can be photographed without a crisis. Next time the forum meets in Switzerland, then Greece [1]. Routine is underrated.
