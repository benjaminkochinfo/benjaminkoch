The Bundesnetzagentur opened the second auction for long-term controllable capacity on 10 November [1]. Bids for 3,840.031 megawatts are due by the end of 29 December, with a ceiling of €244,000 per megawatt per year and a 15-year commitment [1]. The volume is below the 4.5 gigawatts first planned because the first round awarded about 5,160 megawatts; the overshoot is deducted now [1]. In that first round the volume-weighted average was €155,296 per megawatt per year [1]. The plants are meant to cover gaps from 2031 when wind and sun fall short [1].

The economy ministry plans an ordinance under which converting coal plants to gas qualifies only if they must close by the end of 2029; critics warn such less efficient plants could raise wholesale prices [2]. Desk arithmetic: at the ceiling, the full volume would cost about €937m a year. An insurance premium, put out to tender, with the price set by bids rather than speeches.
