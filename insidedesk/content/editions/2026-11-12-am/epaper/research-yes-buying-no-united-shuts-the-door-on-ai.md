United Airlines said on Wednesday it would restrict AI bots' access to its digital platforms, citing data security [1]. The carrier will act directly on its own website and app and indirectly through travel agencies and aggregators, with changes to its booking, ticketing and distribution policies [1]. According to United's help pages, AI tools may still research flights and fares, but the purchase has to be completed by the customer [2].

**The reasons given.** United warned that sharing account credentials with third-party assistants could expose sensitive data and lead to unintended purchases, account changes or unauthorised use of frequent-flyer miles [1]. It also flagged a regulatory point: check-in requires a human to acknowledge the list of prohibited items, such as explosives, and an automated tool doing that could create compliance risks [1].

**A divided market.** Companies are choosing sides. Amazon has blocked Meta's AI agent Muse while Walmart has partnered with it; Expedia and Booking work with OpenAI [1].

**For you.** An assistant that can spend your miles holds the keys to your account. Until airlines and agents agree rules on who is liable for a mistaken booking, the safest division of labour is the one United now enforces: let the machine search, and sign the invoice yourself.
