Japan's corporate goods price index, the prices companies charge each other, stood at 136.8 in October (2020 = 100), up 7.1% on a year earlier, the Bank of Japan said on Thursday [1]. That is 0.3 points faster than September and 0.6 points above private forecasts, Nikkei reports [1].

**Where it came from.** Petroleum and coal products rose 15.9% on the year, 4.4 points faster than in September, as crude climbed again after the summer with the prolonged US–Iran conflict [1]. Electricity, gas and water rose 15.8%, as fuel costs rose and subsidies on bills shrank [1]. The AI build-out shows up too: non-ferrous metals such as copper rose 31.7% and information and communications equipment 15.9% [1].

**The other way.** Farm, forestry and fishery products fell 25.0%, after −14.9% in September, as rice prices dropped [1]. A Bank of Japan official noted that some firms reprice quarterly or half-yearly, which weighed more heavily in October [1]. Of 515 items, 469 rose and 35 fell [1].

**The yen.** Import prices in yen rose 24.8% [1]. The yen traded around 159.82 per dollar on Thursday morning [2].

**For you.** Factory-gate prices travel slowly to shop shelves, but they travel. If you buy Japanese goods or hold yen, a weak currency and dearer inputs are two sides of one invoice. Nothing here is investment advice.
