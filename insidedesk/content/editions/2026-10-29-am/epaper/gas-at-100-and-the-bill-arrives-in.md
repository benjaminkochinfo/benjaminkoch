The Dutch front-month TTF contract, Europe's benchmark for natural gas, briefly traded at €100 per megawatt hour at about 14:35 CET on Wednesday, RTÉ reports, citing ICE data [1]. That was up about 4% on the previous session and the first time at that level since December 2022 [1]. Prices are around triple their level before the Iran war, as disruption to Middle East LNG shipping keeps supply tight [1].

**The cushion is thin.** EU storage is nearly 75% full and Germany's around 60%, well below last year [1]. "Europe has less of a storage cushion than needed," said Mathieu Utting of Rystad Energy, who noted LNG imports 30% below last year in the week to 9 October [1]. The German front-year baseload power contract rose 1.7% to €159.30 per MWh, the highest since June 2023 [1].

**Who pays, and when.** Chemicals, fertiliser and steel makers buying near wholesale feel it at once, and some have cut output [1]. Households feel it later: retail contracts lag, so most of the hit to bills comes in 2027, RTÉ reports [1]. German gas tariffs for new customers are already up 55% this year, and EU governments have spent €17.9 billion this year cushioning oil and gas costs, according to a European Commission note [1].

Desk arithmetic: today's €100 is still less than a third of the roughly €340 peak of August 2022 [1]. A lag is not a gift, but it is time. Use it to check your contract's end date. Nothing here is investment advice.
