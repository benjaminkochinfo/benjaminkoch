Researchers at the Medical University of South Carolina asked 189 young people aged 8 to 15 to give a three-minute speech to a camera, as if auditioning for a TV show, and measured cortisol in their saliva [1]. 107 had experienced at least one potentially traumatic event before the age of six; 82 had experienced none [1].

Among those with early trauma, a smaller cortisol rise predicted a shift over the next two years towards anger and rule-breaking rather than anxiety; a larger rise predicted a shift towards anxiety [1]. The two groups' cortisol responses did not differ on average. The authors call the pattern "complex", and the sample is small [1].

The plain lesson is about labels: the same stress response can point to different troubles in different children, so the history matters before the diagnosis.
