Germany's trade-union federation, the DGB, presented its own pension plan on Wednesday [1]. Its commission wants the pension level raised from today's 48% to 53%, paid for in part by an occupational pension for all employees [1]. The retirement age would stay at 67 for those born from 1964, and the penalty-free pension after 45 years of contributions would remain [1].

It is a reply to the government commission, whose recommendations have been on the table since June: link the retirement age to life expectancy, eight months more work for every extra year of life, and phase out the early pension after 45 years [1]. "A good pension for all generations is a question of political decisions," said DGB chair Yasmin Fahimi [1]. Two written proposals on the same desk is how a payroll question should be argued.
