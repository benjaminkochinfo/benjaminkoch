Alphabet reported negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion for the third quarter and kept its full-year capital spending forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion, NDTV Profit reports [1]. Its adjusted earnings per share and revenue missed expectations [1]. The shares fell about 10% after hours, wiping nearly $400 billion off its market value and pulling Nasdaq futures down 1%, Reuters reports [2]. Microsoft, by contrast, rose 3% on better-than-expected cash flows, helped by its Azure cloud business [2] [1].

**Same boom, different invoices.** "Both of Alphabet and Microsoft confirmed that the AI revenue generation is still solid," said Jamie Hannah of VanEck [2]. His point about Alphabet was narrower: its advertising business is being hit by "the very thing which is the AI boom" [2]. The market did not punish AI. It punished a cash-flow line that turned negative.

**The quieter note.** Moody's Ratings warned in its Private Credit Quarterly that "refinancing risk will become more visible" as loans written in 2020–22 approach maturity, AFP reports [3]. Those loans were underwritten when rates were low, "often with higher leverage and more aggressive terms," Moody's said, and pressure is likely "even in the absence of additional rate hikes" [3].

Neither item is a crash. Both are the same lesson in two sizes: what you spend today has to be paid for at tomorrow's rate. Nothing here is investment advice.
