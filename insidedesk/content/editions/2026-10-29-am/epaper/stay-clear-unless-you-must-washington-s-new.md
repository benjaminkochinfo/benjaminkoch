US Naval Forces Central Command issued an advisory on Wednesday through the Joint Maritime Information Center. "Vessels which do not need to transit the area are advised to remain well clear to avoid unnecessary delays to their voyage," it said, and those that do should "plan additional transit time" for traffic density and "military blockade enforcement operations" [1].

The notice also spells out the paperwork of a blockade. Neutral ships carrying neutral cargo, or sailing empty between neutral ports, may be questioned, visited and searched to confirm their intentions [1]. It was not clear what prompted it; the last similar public notice came in mid-July, when the blockade of Iranian shipping began, and Centcom did not immediately answer Bloomberg's questions [1].

The context is mixed. Oil flows out of the Gulf have climbed back to near pre-war levels, helped by shuttle tankers that run in and out to meet larger ships waiting in the Gulf of Oman, while Iran's own crude exports are close to zero [1]. At the same time, Bloomberg counts almost 30 Iranian attacks on ships in October, a wartime high [1].

For you, the practical reading is in the logistics, not the rhetoric: longer voyage times, more boardings, harder questions from insurers. Those are the costs shipping firms and their customers carry. Nothing here is investment advice.
