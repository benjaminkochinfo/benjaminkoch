If your regular income stopped tomorrow, how long could you keep paying the rent, the groceries and the electricity? A representative survey for the insurer Debeka, reported by Der Spiegel on Wednesday, put that question to adults in Germany, leaving pensioners out [1]. Only 51% said they could cover their running costs for at least three months [1]. Twenty-two per cent said they could manage for one month; just under a quarter, 23%, could bridge half a year [1].

Single bills are a test too. Thirty-seven per cent said they could not pay an unexpected €3,000 at short notice without getting into difficulty, most of all those aged 18 to 29 [1]. Only about one in seven would dip into pension savings in an emergency [1].

The more encouraging line is in how people say they would react. Under pressure, 54% would first cut their spending, while only 14% would reduce their saving or pension contributions [1]. In other words, most households protect the long-term ledger and squeeze the short-term one. That is the right order.

For you, the survey suggests a modest, unheroic target. A buffer does not have to be six months on day one. One month of fixed costs, kept in an account you do not see every day, already moves you out of the most exposed group. Then a second month. The dull standing order beats the good intention every time.
