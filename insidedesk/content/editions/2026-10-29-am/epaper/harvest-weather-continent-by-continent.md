Bread prices begin in fields, and the fields depend on the week's weather. DTN's meteorologist John Baranick set out the picture on Wednesday [1].

In the US Midwest it remains warm and mostly dry, good for harvesting. Scattered showers over Iowa and Illinois from Friday could slow work locally, but a much bigger system next week is expected to bring widespread and possibly heavy rain [1]. On the Plains that may disrupt fieldwork, but it could also help against the ongoing drought and improve soil moisture for winter wheat [1].

In central Brazil, a stalled front is bringing showers that are "sorely needed" for soybean planting, even if amounts are only near normal; southern Brazil, too wet of late, is drying [1]. Argentina's soils are mostly in good shape, but cold has slowed winter wheat and corn, and soybean planting in November could be pushed back if it stays chilly [1].

Europe is mostly drier this week. Spain has stayed dry, "too common this fall", while a system over the eastern Mediterranean could bring rain to Italy and south-eastern Europe from the weekend [1]. Ukraine's soil moisture is favourable, but western Russia looks dry, which DTN calls "concerning" for winter wheat establishment [1]. China's corn and soybean harvest has had good weather [1].

None of this sets a price on its own. It is the slow ledger of a growing season, written a week at a time. Nothing here is investment advice.
