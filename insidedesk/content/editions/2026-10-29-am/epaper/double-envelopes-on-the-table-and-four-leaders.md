Israel's Central Elections Committee started counting the "double envelope" ballots at 01:00 local time, midnight in Berlin [1]. These are votes cast by soldiers, diplomats, hospital patients and prisoners, and the committee expects between 430,000 and 480,000 of them, with counting due to finish by Thursday evening [2].

With 90.4% of votes counted, the seats have not moved: Netanyahu's bloc 53, the Eisenkot-led bloc 48, the Arab-majority parties 19 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the opposition's 48 and 19 make 67, six more than the 61 needed; the bloc without the Arab parties is 13 short.

The politics waits on the arithmetic. Senior figures in Eisenkot's bloc told Haaretz they are "operating in the dark" and that their four leaders will not decide before the full seat distribution is known [1]. Yair Golan of the Democrats said he would meet Eisenkot and Yair Lapid on Thursday, which would be the first formal talks [2]. Likud has accused Arab parties, without evidence, of "stealing" the election; the committee says it will examine the complaint in its routine post-election review [2].

The sensible order is the one the committee is following: count, check, publish. Then President Herzog consults the parties. Everything said before that is a bid, not a result.
