The European Union is considering import restrictions on Chinese chemicals, machinery and furniture, in the form of quotas or tariffs, and could propose them as early as the first quarter of 2027, Industrylinqs reports, citing Bloomberg [1]. The Commission would use so-called safeguard measures, which must comply with World Trade Organisation rules [1]. The exact list of sectors may still change, and the Commission had not commented [1].

**The template is a number.** Brussels wants to build on its approach to hybrid cars, where it recently agreed with Beijing on a cap of 350,000 imported Chinese hybrids a year [1]. A safeguard can be a fixed quota, or a tariff that applies only above a set quantity [1]. The aim is the trade deficit with China, about €360 billion last year according to Bloomberg [1].

**The reply is a chemical.** China's Commerce Ministry has opened an anti-dumping investigation into imports of para-nitrotoluene from Europe just before sensitive trade talks over European tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, the Beijing Post reports [2]. Earlier this month China warned that the EU should not underestimate its willingness and ability to protect its interests [1].

Quotas and probes make poor headlines. They make good contracts: a quota is a number a purchasing department can plan around, which a threat is not. If your firm imports from China, the sector list is the document to watch, not the speeches.
