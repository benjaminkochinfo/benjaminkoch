The rate held at 3.75% to 4%. The more lasting decision on Wednesday was about the diary. Chairman Kevin Warsh said the Federal Open Market Committee will keep eight meetings a year, but in 2027 one of them "will focus exclusively on broad monetary policy strategy, with neither a vote on the federal funds rate nor a policy statement anticipated" [1]. In 2028 he expects two such meetings and six for setting policy [1].

His reasoning was plain. Policy needs regular, "information-rich" check-ups on the near term, he said, but the committee also needs "time and space" for "thinking anew about big questions", which he called focusing "on the left side of the decimal point" [1]. He described the schedule as "more than some administrative detail" and "an institutional choice" [1].

It fits his other habit. In the same press conference he repeated that he does not favour forward guidance "absent exigent circumstances, absent a crisis" [2]. CNBC read the calendar change as part of an effort to reduce the Fed's day-to-day pull on markets [2].

For you, the practical effect is small and slightly reassuring. Fewer dates on which a rate can move means fewer days on which markets wait for one. And a committee that books time to argue about its own framework is doing the unglamorous work that keeps a framework credible. Nothing here is investment advice.
