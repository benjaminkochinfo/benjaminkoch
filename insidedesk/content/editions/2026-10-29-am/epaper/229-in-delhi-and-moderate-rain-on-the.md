Delhi's air quality index rose to 229 on Wednesday, in the "poor" band, from 197 on Tuesday, ending four days of moderate air, according to the Central Pollution Control Board [1]. Of 58 stations, 41 read poor and one, Vivek Vihar, reached 316, "very poor" [1]. Transport was the largest single local source, at 19.3%; stubble burning contributed 0.9% [1]. Satellites counted 129 farm fires across six states on 27 October [1].

The Air Quality Early Warning System expects moderate air from 29 to 31 October, and the India Meteorological Department forecasts moderate rain on Thursday [1]. Light winds still trap pollution, so the six-day outlook runs from moderate to poor [1]. If you are in Delhi, check the station nearest you, not just the city average: the spread on Wednesday ran from moderate to very poor.
