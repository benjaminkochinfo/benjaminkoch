Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said on Wednesday that Tehran remains "in close and ongoing contact with intermediaries" on ideas to implement its seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz "in even less than a week", AFP reports [1]. In the same post on X he said the armed forces were "fully prepared to defend the Homeland" [1]. Yesterday evening's note reported Qatar's draft and Tehran's "not an offer". This morning brings three new signals, and they do not point the same way.

**The letter.** US Vice President JD Vance wrote to Mohsen Rezaei, head of Iran's Security Council, to revive peace negotiations, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing US officials, according to Reuters [2]. **The denial.** President Donald Trump denied reports that he had directed Vance to begin peace talks, suggesting the contact concerned Tehran's need for "help with medical supplies", Al Jazeera reports [3]. **The pressure.** Axios reported that mediators led by Qatar were pushing for a deal to restart US–Iran talks and prevent a resumption of war after the US midterms on 3 November [1].

**What each side asks first.** Iran's plan, conveyed through Qatar in September, asks Washington to lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, unfreeze Iranian assets and lift oil sanctions, in exchange for reopening Hormuz [1] [4]. Washington insists that Iran's nuclear programme be on the table from the start [4]. Qatar is trying to bridge the two: UN inspectors at Iran's bombed nuclear sites in return for some sanctions relief, according to the Axios report [1]. Iran is expected to give its final position on the Qatari draft by this weekend, Reuters reports [4].

**The market's reading.** Brent fell 0.7% to $114.50 overnight on the letter report [2]. "However, the decline remains limited," said Yuki Takashima of Nomura [2]. A letter and a denial on the same night are not a contradiction. They are what talks look like before anyone is willing to call them talks. The dull indicator to watch is not a speech but a date: a written answer from Tehran by the weekend.
