Germany's fuel discount came into force on 1 October, and October's final price figures show it working where it was aimed. Motor fuels were 5.5% cheaper than in September, super petrol 5.8% and diesel 5.1% [2]. Year on year, fuels were still 28.9% dearer, but that was down from 37.1% in September [1].

The discount does not reach the tank in the cellar. Light heating oil rose 4.6% in the month and was 84.5% dearer than in October 2025, after 74.9% in September [1][2]. Electricity, by contrast, was 5.2% cheaper than a year earlier, and natural gas excluding service charges 1.2% cheaper [1].

Desk arithmetic: on those year-on-year rates, a heating-oil fill that cost €1,000 in October 2025 would cost about €1,845 at October 2026 prices, while the same household's gas bill, had it heated with gas, would be about €12 lower per €1,000. These are national averages, not your dealer's quote.

"Energy" is not one price. The forecourt, the electricity bill and the oil delivery are three different invoices, moving in different directions. Nothing here is investment advice.
