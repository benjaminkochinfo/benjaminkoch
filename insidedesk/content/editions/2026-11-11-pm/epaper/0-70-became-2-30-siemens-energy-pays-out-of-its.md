Siemens Energy proposes a dividend of €2.30 a share for the year to September, up from 70 cents, and plans to buy back up to €9bn more of its own shares by the end of fiscal 2030, on top of €3.1bn of buybacks in the year just ended [1]. The shares rose 7.7% on Wednesday morning [3].

The money comes from somewhere specific. Orders reached €68.1bn, 18.8% more than a year earlier on a comparable basis, and the order backlog a record €164bn [1]. Revenue rose 15.5% to €44.2bn, and net income to €4,129m from €1,685m [1]. Demand for turbines and grid equipment to power data centres has given the group "a huge boost", Reuters notes [2]. For 2027 the company expects net income of around €5bn [1].

Now read the small print. Siemens Gamesa, the wind unit Reuters calls "long-loss-making" [2], reached break-even on profit before special items [1]. For 2027 it guides to revenue down 1% to 3% and a margin of 0% to 2% [1]. Gas Services, by contrast, expects revenue growth of 24% to 26% [1]. Desk arithmetic: the backlog is about 3.7 times this year's revenue.

The lesson is old-fashioned. A dividend tripled out of cash and an order book is a different thing from a valuation tripled out of a story. Nothing here is investment advice.
