Since 2011, hepatitis B vaccination has been part of the Dutch national childhood programme, so more and more people entering work are already fully vaccinated, usually without an antibody test [1]. The Health Council (Gezondheidsraad) now advises that those starting jobs with a risk of blood exposure, in health care, funeral services, police and fire brigades among others, receive a booster followed by a titre check, a blood test to confirm enough antibodies [1].

The council says childhood vaccination gives very good protection; the point is that occupational vaccination needs "maximum certainty" [1]. Employers have a legal duty of care, and the national infection-control (LCI) guidelines will be updated in line with the advice [1]. A small, boring rule that closes a gap before anyone falls into it.
