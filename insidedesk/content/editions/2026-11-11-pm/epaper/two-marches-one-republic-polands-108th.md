Thousands marched through Warsaw on Wednesday to mark 108 years since Poland regained independence in 1918 [1]. The annual Independence March is still divisive because of past violence and the presence of nationalist and far-right groups, yet it has become more mainstream; organisers hoped for up to 500,000 people [1]. President Karol Nawrocki walked in the procession. By mid-afternoon, TVP Info reported no major incidents beyond widespread red flares [1].

Prime Minister Donald Tusk stayed away and attended a smaller event in Szczecin. "We may hold different views … yet, anyone who truly cares about Poland must understand that this space, defined by independence, freedom, and a secure Republic, is a common good," he said [1]. The 2027 parliamentary election is less than a year away [1].

A national holiday split between two cities is not ideal. A quiet afternoon with no major incidents is still a small win for the institutions both men lead.
