A joint draft report on the Commission's proposed Cloud and AI Development Act was presented in the European Parliament on Wednesday [1]. It calls for common EU rules for cloud and AI infrastructure, more support for European providers, faster approval of new data centres that still respect environmental rules, and stronger protection for critical sectors such as health, energy and transport [1].

**The number behind it.** According to the Commission, three non-European hyperscale providers control more than 70% of the European cloud market [1]. Under its proposal, the EU aims to triple data-centre capacity within five to seven years [1].

**The sharpest line.** Dutch Green MEP Reinier Van Lanschot put the worry bluntly: "Our critical systems run on someone else's technology, and someone else can switch it off." His remedy was not a subsidy: European firms "are not asking for subsidies, they are asking for customers" [1]. The report wants public authorities to buy more cloud services from European providers [1]. Spanish conservative MEP Diego Solier framed it as sovereignty that "does not consist in closing Europe to the rest of the world" [1].

**Why the dull part matters.** Procurement is where this becomes real. A ministry's tender, a hospital's IT contract and a grid operator's renewal are invoices that can build a market far faster than a strategy paper. Permits are the other half: a data centre that waits years for a connection is capacity on paper only.

**The calendar.** The draft report is expected to be published on Thursday and discussed in committee on 1 December; a committee vote is provisionally set for mid-March 2027 and a plenary vote for April [1].
