The federal cabinet on Wednesday approved a draft law for a national gas reserve [1]. It is meant to hold 24 billion kilowatt hours, about a tenth of Germany's storage capacity, and the Bundesnetzagentur could draw on it only once an emergency stage has been declared [1]. The draft sizes it to cover a 20-day loss of all Norwegian pipeline imports, a 40-day loss of all German LNG terminals, or full supply of households and businesses for ten days in an extremely cold winter and 18 days in a normal one [1].

**Not for this winter.** Building the reserve is to start for winter 2027 and take several years [1]. The background is this year's storage season: with gas expensive because of the Iran war, filling stores in summer barely paid, and the 70% fill target was clearly missed [2]. The fill-level rules will not be extended; the ministry says supply this winter is secure, mainly through ongoing imports [2].

**The invoice, in advance.** A levy on gas customers is to pay for it from 1 October 2027 [1]. E&M reports annual costs of €247m to €308m in the draft; Handelsblatt's summary puts the upper end at €405m [1][2]. About 40% would fall on households and businesses, 60% on industry [1]. For a flat using 13,000 kWh a year, the draft puts the extra at €3.77 a year; for a house using 20,000 kWh, €7.20 [1]. The levy is disputed within the coalition, and the municipal utilities' association VKU wants it paid from the federal budget instead [1].

**Alongside it.** A key-points paper, also approved, would oblige suppliers to actually use storage capacity they have booked, with sanctions, to protect households, hospitals and care homes [2].

Desk arithmetic: €3.77 is about 31 cents a month. A reserve you hope never to use, priced on the bill like a fuse, is dull. It is meant to be.
