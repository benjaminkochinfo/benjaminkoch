Portugal's Civil Protection counted 1,630 weather incidents across the country on Wednesday, 881 of them floods, against 538 by noon [1]. Greater Lisbon, the Setúbal peninsula and the Algarve were hit hardest; one person was assisted and four rescued after climbing onto roofs to clean them "at the last minute" [1]. The government has ended the state of alert [1]. IPMA's strongest gust was 123 km/h at Fóia in the Serra de Monchique; Lisbon district had 60 to 80 mm of rain in six hours, red-warning territory [1]. The storm split and weakened, and IPMA's Jorge Ponte said prevention was "adequate" for the data it had on Tuesday [1]. A warning that turns out larger than the storm is not a failure. It is the premium on a policy you hope never to claim.
