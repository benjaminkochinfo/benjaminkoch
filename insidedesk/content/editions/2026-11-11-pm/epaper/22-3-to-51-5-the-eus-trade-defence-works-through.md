No speech came with it. On Wednesday the EU's Official Journal published two Commission regulations imposing provisional anti-dumping duties, the slow, evidence-based tools of trade policy [1].

**Acrylic esters.** Regulation (EU) 2026/2512 sets provisional duties of 22.3% to 40.3% on butyl acrylate and 2-ethylhexyl acrylate from China, Saudi Arabia, South Africa and the United States; Chinese producers face 22.3% to 29.0% [1]. The duties apply from 10 November, a day before publication [2]. These chemicals feed adhesives, paints, coatings and technical textiles, so downstream buyers pay at customs from now on [2].

**Copper tubes.** Regulation (EU) 2026/2497 imposes provisional duties of up to 51.5% on refined copper tubes in level-wound coils from China, Mexico, Vietnam and Uzbekistan [1].

Two details are worth noting. First, the US is on the acrylics list. Trade defence follows case files, not alliances. Second, these duties are provisional: the Commission can make them definitive, change them or drop them when the investigation ends [2]. Last Friday, 6 November, the Commission also opened a safeguard investigation into PET resin chemicals after a 27% rise in imports [1].

For you: if your company buys these inputs, check your contracts for clauses that pass on tariff costs, and your customs broker's start date. A tariff in a regulation number is less dramatic than one in a speech, but it is the kind you will actually pay. Nothing here is investment advice.
