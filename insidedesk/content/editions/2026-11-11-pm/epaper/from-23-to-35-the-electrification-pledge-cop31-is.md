At the UN climate talks in Antalya, Türkiye is pushing a "35 by 35" pledge: electricity's share of global energy consumption up to 35% by 2035 [1]. Today it is 23%, according to Australia's climate and energy minister Chris Bowen, who said reaching 35% "would require more than a four-fold increase in the rate of electrification" [1].

The sticking point is the source. The draft wording does not require the extra electricity to be clean, saying pathways "will differ according to different starting points" [1]. Sceptics say fossil-fired electrification would lock in fossil fuels; IEA chief Fatih Birol argues that cost would push the pledge towards lower emissions anyway [1]. Analysts cited by ABC estimate a purely clean route could take 0.3C off projected warming [1].

A pledge is a speech. The grid connections, transformers and meters that follow are the invoice. Watch for those.
