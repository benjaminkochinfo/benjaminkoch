By 16:31 CET on Wednesday, four AIS-visible commercial ships had crossed the Strait of Hormuz since midnight UTC: three heading into the Gulf, one heading out, and only one of them a tanker, according to the hormuz.now counter [1]. Its seven-day average is 6.9 crossings a day, against a pre-crisis baseline of 84.8 [1]. Another tracker, using Windward data, logged 10 transits on Tuesday, the last complete day, about 11% of the roughly 88 a day before the war; half of them ran with transponders off [2].

The diplomacy is just as thin. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei called reports of talks in Qatar "a pure lie" and said the strait stays shut until Washington answers Tehran's seven conditions; Washington's first reply, he said, was "insufficient and deceptive" [3]. The full list has not been published. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says the strait could reopen within two days if the demands are met [3]. In Paris, Marco Rubio said there was no need for "major combat operations" while "the economic campaign" continues [3].

Meanwhile, ships are still being hit. The IRGC says three tankers struck mines on Tuesday on an "unauthorised route"; UKMTO received reports involving two tankers, one hit by an unknown projectile off Oman [3].

For you: two counters with different methods give different numbers, and both say the same thing. When the figures disagree on the detail but agree on the direction, trust the direction.
