Storms Bruna and Charles are not only a story of red warnings. In the province of Seville they are also a rainfall ledger. AEMET station data from 00:00 on Sunday 8 November to 08:00 on Wednesday put Guadalcanal top with 122.2 litres per square metre, followed by Cazalla de la Sierra with 101.7, both in the Sierra Norte [1]. Tomares had 73.2, and the city's two stations, Tablada and the airport, 59 and 58.2 [1].

**An uneven gift.** The same days left La Roda de Andalucía with 4 litres, Écija with 5.9 and Lora de Estepa with 9 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Guadalcanal received about 30 times as much rain as La Roda in the same period. Rain is an average only on a map; on a farm it is a postcode.

**Into the dams.** On Wednesday morning the reservoirs managed by EMASESA, Seville's water company, held about 468.47 cubic hectometres, or 73.1% of capacity [1]. Aracena recorded 76.4 litres in the catchment, and the first estimate of the inflow from this episode is about 3 hm³, still to be validated [1]. Desk arithmetic: 3 hm³ is well under 1% of what the reservoirs already hold. One wet week is a top-up, not a reset.

**What comes next.** AEMET expects the episode to last at least until Thursday 12 November, with rain possible on Friday [1]. The number to watch is not the next day's rainfall record but the validated inflow figure. That is the one the water company plans with, and so, quietly, does everyone who turns on a tap in Seville.
