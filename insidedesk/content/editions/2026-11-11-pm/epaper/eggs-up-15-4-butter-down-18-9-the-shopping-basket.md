Food is no longer what drives German inflation. In October, food prices were 0.4% higher than a year earlier, according to the final figures the Federal Statistical Office confirmed today [1]. But a small average can hide large moves, and the supermarket had plenty.

Up: eggs cost 15.4% more than a year ago, fresh vegetables 11.5% more, and fish, fish products and seafood 4.5% more [1]. Down: butter was 18.9% cheaper, edible fats and oils 9.1%, dairy products 4.7%, fresh fruit 2.8%, and coffee, tea and cocoa 3.9% [1][2].

For a household that cooks, that is a mixed message with a practical edge. The fridge door, with butter and milk, has become cheaper; the vegetable drawer has not. Services, which include package holidays and restaurant meals, rose 2.5%, a slower pace than in recent months [1].

Desk arithmetic: a 250-gram pat of butter that cost €2.00 in October 2025 would cost about €1.62 at the average fall; a box of ten eggs at €3.00 would now be about €3.46. Your own shop and brand will differ.

"That is good news for consumers," said Cyrus de la Rubia, chief economist at Hamburg Commercial Bank, of the cooler core rate, while noting it means companies have less power to raise prices [1]. A receipt kept for a month, compared with the same month next year, tells you more than any of us can.
