Ship counts in the Strait of Hormuz look like a closure. Barrel counts tell a different story. According to Kpler, cited by Reuters columnist Ron Bousso, crude through Hormuz has averaged 10.5 million barrels a day since the start of October, against 13.5 million before the war. Including routes that bypass the strait, total Gulf crude exports average 16.7 million, just short of the pre-war 17 million [1].

The workaround costs money: convoys along Oman's coast under US Navy protection, ship-to-ship transfers, and freight and insurance at about ten times pre-war levels [1]. The International Maritime Organization counted 131 confirmed incidents near the strait between 28 February and 9 November, including 35 in October, the highest monthly total of the war [1].

So the oil arrives. Getting it out is what costs more. That shows up in invoices more than in headlines. Nothing here is investment advice.
