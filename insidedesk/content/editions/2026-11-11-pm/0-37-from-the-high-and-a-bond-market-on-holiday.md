Europe spent Wednesday close enough to its records to see them, and did not quite touch them. At 16:16 CET the Stoxx Europe 600 stood at 660.97, up 0.40% on the day, against a 52-week high of 663.41; its best moment of the day was 663.36 [1]. Germany's DAX was up 0.91% at 26,587.90 at 16:31 CET, after a day's high of 26,659.42, beneath a record of 26,692.90 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the Stoxx 600 was 2.44 points, or about 0.37%, short of the high, and the DAX 105 points, or about 0.39%. These are afternoon values, not closes; the cash session ends at 17:30 CET.

You will read "near record" in a lot of places tonight. It is accurate, and it is also the least interesting thing about the day. What moved Europe was mostly a set of invoices.

[[chart:europe-sectors-11pm]]

**The invoices.** The first came from New York overnight. CoreWeave, the AI cloud provider that has become the market's thermometer for AI spending, reported better-than-expected revenue and strong demand, and European chip equipment makers rose in sympathy: BE Semiconductor and ASM International were up more than 4% each in the morning, ASML and STMicroelectronics more than 3% [3]. ASM added a share buyback of up to €300 million [2]. By 16:16 CET the Stoxx 600 technology index was up 1.85% [1].

The second came from Munich. Siemens Energy rose 7.7% in the morning [2] after a year in which revenue rose 15.5% to €44.2bn, operating profit before special items more than doubled to €5.49bn, and the dividend was raised to €2.30 a share from 70 cents, with up to €9bn more in buybacks to 2030 [7]. That is not a story about a mood. It is a story about turbines and grid equipment ordered, built and paid for.

Not every invoice was welcome. Vestas fell 8% after narrowing its outlook, Brenntag 4.5% despite confirming its own, and CSG 23% after cutting its margin forecast [2][3]. The market can still tell one balance sheet from another. That is a healthier sign than any record.

**The oil.** The Stoxx 600 oil and gas index rose 2.22% by 16:16 CET, the best sector on the board [1]. Brent was up $1.32, or 1.4%, at $92.81 a barrel, after Iran's security chief said Tehran would not allow enriched nuclear material to leave the country; Washington wants nuclear matters in any initial negotiation [6]. Brent has still fallen about 21% since the start of the month, CNBC notes [6]. One sentence from Tehran moved a dollar and change; eleven days of diplomacy have moved about a fifth of the price. Keep the proportions.

**The quiet one.** On the other side of the Atlantic, US stocks opened on Veterans Day, and the US bond market did not: SIFMA had recommended a full close for dollar-denominated government and corporate bond trading [5]. At 15:49 CET the S&P 500 was up 0.76% at 7,578.07 and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.16% at 25,354.34, while the Dow was down 0.13% at 52,162.06 [4]. CoreWeave was up 12.7% and Nvidia 2.4%, after Nvidia had shed about $430 billion of market value in the previous two sessions [4]. Traders still treat a Fed rate rise in December as certain, with an 11.5% chance of a half-point move, according to the CME FedWatch tool cited by Reuters [4].


**The dull machinery.** In Wiesbaden, the statistics office confirmed October German inflation at 2.8%, down from 3.3% in September, with prices down 0.2% on the month and core inflation, excluding food and energy, slowing to 2.1% from 2.4% [8]. In Riga, Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel said interim assessments pointed to "a stronger recovery than we were expecting in the summer" and that growth "is likely to accelerate in the fourth quarter", while warning the recovery is "not yet broad-based" [9]. Neither line will trend. Both matter more than a record.

**For you.** A day near the top is a good day to read what is underneath it: order books, dividends paid out of cash, and a price index that stopped climbing. Records come and go; the paperwork stays. Nothing here is investment advice.
