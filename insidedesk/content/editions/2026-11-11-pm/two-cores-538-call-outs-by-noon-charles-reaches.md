On Tuesday this desk reported that Storm Charles might come ashore near Lisbon with a possible sting jet. On Wednesday it did something less dramatic and more useful: it split. IPMA meteorologist Patrícia Gomes told Lusa the depression divided into "two distinct low-pressure cores" (our translation) and lost strength, one heading for Lisbon, the other running along the Algarve coast from Sagres to Vila Real de Santo António [1]. The civil protection commander said a sting jet had become less likely [2].

**The wind came down; the rain stayed.** Warnings were cut accordingly. By early afternoon Lisbon and Setúbal were on red for rain only until 15:00 Lisbon time (16:00 CET), and Leiria, Castelo Branco, Beja and Portalegre went from red to orange [1]. Faro remained the only district on red for wind [1]. Gusts above 120 km/h were still recorded on exposed coast and in the Serra de Monchique [1]. Between midnight and noon Lisbon time, civil protection counted 538 incidents on the mainland, mostly floods, fallen trees and road clearances, chiefly around Lisbon and Setúbal [2]. On Tuesday it had been 1,240 [1].

**What the day cost, so far.** By 11:00 Lisbon time, 360 flights had been cancelled at Lisbon, Madeira, Faro and Porto, most of them at Lisbon [1]. Eight national roads were restricted by 14:00, including the Eixo Norte-Sul access in Lisbon [1]. The grid held: E-REDES, the distribution operator, said the night passed "without significant incidents", with about 2,000 customers without power at 07:00 Lisbon time, after it had put generators, mobile power units, helicopters and drones on standby [3].

**Was it too much?** Asked whether the state had overreacted with closed schools and services, interior minister Luís Neves said no, and that "prevention is always the best remedy" (our translation) [1]. You can disagree about the dose. But note what the closures bought: the incidents that came were small ones, handled by crews already on shift. A forecast that softens a day later is not an embarrassment for IPMA; it is what forecasts do when the atmosphere changes its mind.

**For you.** If you fly to or from Portugal this week, TAP has let Wednesday passengers rebook without penalty for flights until 17 November [1]. The heaviest rain was expected between 15:00 and 18:00 Lisbon time, in places until 21:00 [2], so the full tally of damage will come on Thursday, and with it the first insurance claims.
