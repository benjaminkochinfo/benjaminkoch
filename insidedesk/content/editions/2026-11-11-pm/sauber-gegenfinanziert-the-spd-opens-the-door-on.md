The coalition is "close to a final agreement" on the 2027 federal budget, says Dirk Wiese, the SPD's parliamentary whip. Budget and finance politicians from the CDU/CSU and SPD had worked together "really extremely constructively" in recent days, and "a lot of open points" had "already been cleared away", he said in Berlin on Wednesday [1]. The budget committee finalises the plan on Thursday; the Bundestag could pass it at the end of November [1].

The new sentence was about bracket creep, the *kalte Progression*: the effect by which a pay rise that only matches inflation pushes you into a higher tax rate. The finance ministry's first draft did not offset it. Wiese said he was open to the CDU/CSU demand to do so, but it must all be "sauber gegenfinanziert" (properly funded); "that is the decisive point" [1]. ZDF puts the cost of a further offset at about €5 billion in 2027 [3]. So the door is open, and the condition is the costly part.

Meanwhile, the finance ministry's amendment paper to the committee, seen by Handelsblatt, shows the less visible changes [2]:

- **Electric cars:** the purchase subsidy rises from €800 million to €1.7 billion, because take-up has been "much more dynamic" than expected [2].
- **Defence:** about €11 billion is reshuffled. A €2 billion unallocated "global spending cut" is dissolved, while frigates and the Bundeswehr's tactical wide area network (TaWAN) slip, with spending €1.34 billion and €1.18 billion lower than planned [2].

None of this will make a slogan. It is still the budget. Money moving from projects that run late to programmes people are actually using is how a ledger reflects reality. A subsidy that runs out faster than expected tells you something about demand. A frigate that slips tells you something about shipyards.

For you: on Friday, ignore the press-conference adjectives and read the two lines that matter. What changed for your tax bill, and which line pays for it. If the second line is missing, the first is not finished. Nothing here is investment advice.
