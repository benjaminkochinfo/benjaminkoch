The second day of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended on Wednesday after a session of just under two hours [3]. Nobody claimed a breakthrough, and the BBC found "no hint" of one [1]. What the round did produce is smaller and more useful: a humanitarian deal in principle and a calendar.

**The swap.** Both Moscow and Kyiv confirmed that a large-scale prisoner-of-war exchange is being prepared and could happen soon [1]. Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said "mutual understanding" had been reached on some issues and that more talks would follow [2]. Zelenskyy listed "an important humanitarian arrangement regarding preparations for a new exchange" as the first result [2]. This morning a swap was a line in a source's notes. Tonight both governments say it on the record.

**The calendar.** Jared Kushner said he and Steve Witkoff will meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, then fly to Dublin to debrief Zelenskyy, who is attending the European Political Community summit [2]. He hopes to "finalise" what he called "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners" in Moscow [2].

**The hard file.** Kushner said "the territorial issues … will be the hardest" and that "there is no trust right now between the sides" [1][2]. Ukraine's Davyd Arakhamia called this round "one of the hardest", said the Russians came "with very strict directives", and still spoke of "good momentum" [1]. Donetsk remains the knot: Moscow wants the rest of it, Kyiv wants the line frozen [1].

**The empty chair.** Europeans were in Abu Dhabi and met the US and Ukrainian teams, but the Kremlin said it had no contact with them [1]. Kushner said getting Europeans "added to the format" is on his agenda for Putin [1]. Medinsky was cool on the idea [2]. A German government spokesperson said talks between Chancellor Merz and Putin "may ensue" on the basis of an understanding reached among European, US and Ukrainian officials, not with Russia [2].

For you: ignore the adjectives, which were "substantive" on one side and "strict" on the other, and follow the logistics. A prisoner list, a flight to Moscow and a meeting in Dublin can all be checked by Friday. That is a modest scorecard, and an honest one.
