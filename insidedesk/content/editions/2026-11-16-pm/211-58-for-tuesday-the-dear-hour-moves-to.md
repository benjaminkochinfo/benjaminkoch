Monday's auction has priced Tuesday. Electricity for delivery in Germany and Luxembourg on 17 November will average €211.58 per megawatt hour [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €17.09 more than Monday's €194.49, and €139.90, or about 40%, less than Tuesday 10 November, when €351.48 set the record of the autumn [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-16pm]]

**An ordinary number, for once.** The industry association BDEW puts the average day-ahead price of the past 30 days at €213.6 [2]. Desk arithmetic: Tuesday sits about two euros below that. After a week that ran from €351 down to €140, a day that simply lands on its own monthly average is the market behaving like an institution rather than a headline.

**The hours have moved.** This morning's edition pointed you to Monday's dearest hour, 18:00 to 19:00, at €268.29 [1]. On Tuesday the same evening hour costs €215.19, and the peak sits at breakfast instead: 08:00 to 09:00 at €263.56, with 07:00 to 10:00 all above €254 [1]. The cheapest hour is the last one, 23:00 to midnight, at €142.62 [1]. Desk arithmetic: a spread of €120.94 inside one day, about seven times the gap between Monday and Tuesday's averages.

**What we do not know yet.** At the time we read the data, SMARD had not yet posted its wind and generation forecasts for Tuesday [1], so we will not tell you why the morning is dear. The price list is published; the explanation is not, and a desk that guesses the second spoils the first.

**For you.** On a dynamic tariff, Tuesday's rule is simple and printed: wash, charge and heat water late in the evening, not over breakfast. On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches this month's bill. Nothing here is investment advice.
