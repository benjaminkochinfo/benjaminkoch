The World Health Organization used Monday to put a number on a slow emergency: drug-resistant infections could cause 39 million deaths over the next 25 years, and could make routine surgery and childbirth life-threatening again [1]. "Unlike COVID-19, which was very visible, drug-resistant infections have been taking people's lives for years without us noticing it," said Jean-Pierre Nyemazi, acting head of the WHO's antimicrobial resistance department [1]. In 2021 alone, resistance was directly responsible for more than a million deaths and linked to about 4.7 million others, on WHO figures [1].

The more useful number is a smaller one. A survey of 183 countries this year found that nearly nine in ten have a national action plan against resistance, against roughly four in ten in 2017 [1]. But only 42 countries, fewer than one in five, have a plan that is financed, implemented and monitored, said Pravasha Prakash, an AMR technical officer at the agency [1]. Desk arithmetic: 42 of 183 is about 23% of the countries surveyed. The rest have the document; they do not yet have the machinery.

The surveillance data explain why the machinery matters. In 2023, one in every six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections reported to the WHO was resistant to the antibiotic used to treat it [1]. "Without testing, clinicians must treat without knowing the cause of infection or which antibiotic will work," said Sylvia Bertagnolo, who heads AMR surveillance at the agency [1]. A doctor who guesses tends to guess broad, and broad guesses breed the next resistance.

So the WHO's answer on Monday was not a speech but two working documents: one defining which tests should be available at each level of a health system, from the primary-care centre to the referral hospital, and one compiling 73 laboratory procedures, from taking the sample to testing which antibiotic still works [1]. The agency's levers are equally unglamorous: infection prevention, vaccination, clean water and sanitation, and using antimicrobials only where they are needed [1].

None of this will trend. A swab, a culture plate and a line in a ministry's budget are how a 39-million projection stays a projection. Watch the 42, not the 39.
