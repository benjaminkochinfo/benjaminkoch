Novo Nordisk on Monday released the full results of a late-stage trial of its Wegovy injection in children aged six to under 12 with obesity [1]. After 68 weeks, body mass index fell 17.4% among children on the drug and rose 2.2% among those on placebo, an estimated difference of 19.7 percentage points, assuming children stayed on treatment [1]. Counting every child, including those who stopped, the estimated difference was 16.2 points [1]. All 167 children also followed a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity [1].

The starting point was serious: 85% began with moderate to severe obesity, and nearly half already had at least one related complication, such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol or sleep apnoea [1]. More than 40% of the treated children were no longer classed as having obesity after 68 weeks [1]. Novo said it saw no safety concerns related to growth or pubertal development [1]. Wegovy is approved from age 12; nothing is approved below that [1].

The most useful sentence came from the company itself. The study does not show whether treating obesity earlier changes its long-term course, or what treatment and maintenance should look like as children grow up. "That is something that is still an evidence gap," said Andrea Traina, Novo's medical director lead for obesity [1].

A 68-week result is a chapter, not a childhood. The regulators will ask for the rest of the book, and they should. Nothing here is investment advice.
