Germany's gas storage was 57.3% full on Saturday 14 November, according to the industry association BDEW's Monday update [1]. That is 0.8 percentage points lower than on 10 November, and BDEW reports that the stores are now being drawn down rather than filled [1]. This is what storage is for: the gas bought in summer starts to earn its keep in November.

On the supply side, BDEW reports rising volumes landed at Germany's LNG terminals, with the first gas fed in through the new terminal at Stade since 6 November [1]. Domestic production accounts for 5% of consumption so far in 2026, above last year's output [1]. Flows into Germany are slightly above the previous year's level [1].

**The price of now.** BDEW puts the THE spot price at €91.7 per megawatt hour on 13 November, and the front-year contract at €73.2 [1]. Desk arithmetic: gas for immediate delivery costs €18.50 more than gas for next year, a premium of about a quarter. The association attributes the high level to the Iran war, while noting that the rises are not yet comparable to those at the start of the war in Ukraine [1].

**For you.** A storage level is not a forecast of shortage; it is an inventory, counted and published. Watch the weekly drawdown, not the daily headlines. Nothing here is investment advice.
