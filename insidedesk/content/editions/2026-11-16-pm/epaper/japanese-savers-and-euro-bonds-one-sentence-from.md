Amid Monday's AI warnings, ECB Vice-President Boris Vujčić made a quieter point about who holds Europe's government bonds. "Large Japanese investors may further reduce euro area government bond holdings if Japanese yields rise," he said in Frankfurt [1]. Hedge funds, meanwhile, have become "a significant but procyclical source of liquidity" [1].

He also asked whether heavy AI-related borrowing could compete with governments for capital, "including whether AI-related debt issuance could put upward pressure on financing costs or crowd out other investment" [1].

The geoeconomics here are plain. A budget is financed by buyers, not by votes, and the buyer base is becoming more price-sensitive, in his words [1]. When a finance minister promises a measure, the bond market's question is who in Tokyo, London or a hedge fund will lend for it, and at what rate.
