United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket suffered "an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight" on Sunday and failed to put 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites into orbit, the company said [1]. It was the first outright failure of a ULA rocket since the Boeing–Lockheed Martin venture was formed in 2006, and only the fifth Vulcan flight, the first in nine months [1]. The cause is not yet known; Spaceflight Now noted that the first stage's BE-4 engines appeared to burn longer than the pre-launch timeline, and that something went wrong during the Centaur upper-stage firing [1].

The cost is measured in queue time. Sunday was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has bought for Leo [1]. Amazon has also booked 24 launches on Blue Origin's New Glenn, which is itself waiting to fly again after an on-pad explosion in May, 24 on Europe's Ariane 6 and 13 on SpaceX's Falcon 9 [1]. More than 3,300 satellites are needed to complete the constellation; Sunday's flight would have brought the total launched to 436 [1].

Desk arithmetic: 38 + 24 + 24 + 13 = 99 booked launches across four rockets, and 62 of them sit on the two American rockets now waiting on failure fixes, Vulcan and New Glenn.

ULA promised "full transparency" to its customers, and NASA's administrator offered help with the investigation [1].

For you: Amazon spread its bookings across four rockets for exactly this kind of day. Diversified suppliers do not stop a failure; they decide how long it hurts.
