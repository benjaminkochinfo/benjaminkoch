Tropical cyclone Xavier has strengthened to Category 2 over the Northern Cook Islands, two weeks into a South Pacific season that officially runs from 1 November to 30 April [1]. The Fiji Meteorological Service, the official regional centre in Nadi, placed it about 170 km north of Manihiki at 10:00 UTC on Monday (11:00 CET), with a central pressure of 983 hPa and sustained winds of about 95 km/h near the centre, gusting to 130 km/h [1]. A gale warning is in force for Manihiki and Rakahanga, with winds of 85 km/h gusting to 120 km/h; Pukapuka and Nassau are under a cyclone alert [1].

The forecasts do not agree, and the agencies publish that openly. New Zealand's MetService expected Xavier to hold at Category 2; Nadi forecast a severe tropical cyclone of 90 knots by 18:00 UTC on 17 November; the US Joint Typhoon Warning Center forecast a peak of 95 knots, measured on a one-minute average, before weakening from 18 November [1]. The US Embassy in New Zealand said the storm should stay in the area until Thursday 19 November [1].

The atolls are low-lying coral, about 1,300 km north of Rarotonga; Manihiki is known for its black pearl farms [1]. Strong El Niño years tend to push South Pacific cyclones east towards the Cook Islands, and in 1997 Cyclone Martin's storm surge destroyed almost every building on Manihiki and caused 19 deaths in the islands [1].

Three agencies, three numbers, one warning on the ground. Disagreement in the open is not confusion; it is the honest width of a forecast.
