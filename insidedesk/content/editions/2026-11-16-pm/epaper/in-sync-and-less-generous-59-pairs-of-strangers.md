Synchrony is meant to be the warm thing: two people's heart rates or sweat responses rising and falling together, and, says much earlier research, more cooperation as a result [1]. A study in Proceedings of the Royal Society B, written up by PsyPost on Sunday, found the opposite in one setting [1].

Researchers at Leiden paired 122 young adults into same-sex pairs of strangers; 59 pairs were analysed [1]. One player sent between 0 and 6 euros, which was tripled; the other decided how much to send back [1]. Trustors sent an average of €4.14 and trustees returned 61.6% of the tripled sum [1].

Seeing each other helped: trustees returned a larger share face to face than behind a barrier [1]. But within the face-to-face rounds, stronger skin-conductance synchrony went with lower reciprocity [1]. "We expected that people whose bodies were more in sync would be more generous toward each other, and we found the opposite," says lead author Fabiola Diana [1].

The caveats are the authors' own. Synchrony was observed, not manipulated, so cause is not shown; the arousal explanation came from an unplanned analysis; heart-rate synchrony never beat chance; and the effects were small to medium [1]. "Please don't conclude that 'being in sync is bad'," Diana says [1].

For you: feeling attuned to someone is information, not a verdict on them. What you tune into matters more than the tuning.
