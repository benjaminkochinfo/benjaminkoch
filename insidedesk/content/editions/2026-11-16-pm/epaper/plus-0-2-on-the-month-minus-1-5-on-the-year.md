Euro-area industrial production rose 0.2% in September from August, seasonally adjusted, after a 0.5% fall in August, Eurostat said on Monday in its first estimate [1]. Across the whole EU, output rose 0.5% [1]. Against September 2025, however, euro-area production was down 1.5%, and EU production 0.8% [1].

The split by goods tells you where the slack sits. On the month, intermediate goods rose 0.7% and durable consumer goods 1.3%, while energy fell 1.7% and capital goods were flat [1]. On the year, non-durable consumer goods were down 7.5%; every other main grouping was up [1].

Germany, so often the laggard, rose 1.3% on the month and 0.7% on the year [1]. Ireland fell 5.5% on the month and 19.1% on the year, the largest annual drop among the countries reporting [1]. The Netherlands posted the largest annual rise, 8.8% [1].

Desk arithmetic: four of the five main groupings are above last year's level. The headline minus is real, but it comes from one line.

For you: a monthly factory number is a draft, not a verdict. Eurostat revises it as late data arrive; the August figure was confirmed unchanged at −0.5% this time [1].
