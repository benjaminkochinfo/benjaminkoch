March cocoa rose for a fourth straight session early on Monday as West Africa dries out, the broker ADMIS wrote [1]. Ivory Coast's output lags last year even though the harvest started a month early, and World Weather Inc. expects only sporadic, light rain over the next seven to ten days, mostly near the coast [1]. The country's national weather agency has said an exceptionally strong El Niño could cut its 2026/27 cocoa production by 21–25% [2]. Coffee runs the other way: Brazil is expected to turn wetter, with rain nearly every day, which traders see as good for the crop if it does not become excessive [1]. Brazil exported 5.02 million bags of green coffee in October, a record, industry group Cecafé reported [1].
