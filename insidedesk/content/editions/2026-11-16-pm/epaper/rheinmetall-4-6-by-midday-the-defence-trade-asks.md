Aerospace and defence was Europe's best sector on Monday morning, up 1.3%, as Rheinmetall, Renk and CSG tried to recover from sharp losses earlier in the month, Reuters reported [1]. By midday Rheinmetall was up 4.6%, Thales 2.6% in Paris and Leonardo 2.7% in Milan; in London BAE Systems and Babcock both gained 2.2% [2]. AJ Bell tied the move to a Morningstar report suggesting recent volatility had made valuations in the sector more attractive [2]. By 15:42 CET Rheinmetall's gain had narrowed to 2.77%, at €786.70, still the best in the DAX [3].

The reason the sector needed a second look is worth reading slowly. Reuters noted that defence has lagged this year on concerns that contractors "are struggling to convert orders into strong revenue and cash generation", even as governments are expected to spend more on equipment [1]. That is the dull machinery at work: a budget line is not an order, an order is not a delivery, and a delivery is not cash until the invoice is paid.

One fund manager was openly keen. "We have some very interesting investments in that we're pretty excited about," said James Rasteh of Coast Capital [1]. Enthusiasm from a holder is information about the holder.

For you: if you hold defence shares, the number to watch is not the next procurement headline but the next cash-flow statement. Nothing here is investment advice.
