The average Black Friday budget this year is around €328, according to a survey by the price-comparison site Idealo cited by SWR on Monday [1]. Three in four respondents plan to shop on the day, and about one in five now uses an AI chatbot to hunt for bargains [1].

Consumer advocates are relaxed about the chatbots. Andrea Steinbach of the Verbraucherzentrale Rheinland-Pfalz says her team tried them and the AIs "reliably" found the cheapest price; she still advises comparing two different ones and staying sceptical [1].

**The rule worth knowing.** Under Germany's price-indication regulation, a shop advertising a reduction must also show the lowest price of the previous 30 days [1]. The Verbraucherzentrale Baden-Württemberg warns that many retailers use the manufacturer's recommended retail price as the crossed-out figure instead, which makes the discount look larger and sidesteps that rule [1]. Studies cited by SWR find that the real savings around Black Friday are smaller than many expect, and that discounts tend to be on older electronics models rather than the newest [1].

**And the fakes.** Oliver Buttler of the Baden-Württemberg centre says complaints about fake shops have been rising for years [1]. Never pay in advance, and the advice centres' free "Fakeshop-Finder" will check a suspicious web address [1].

For you: a countdown clock is a sales tool, not a price. The 30-day figure is.
