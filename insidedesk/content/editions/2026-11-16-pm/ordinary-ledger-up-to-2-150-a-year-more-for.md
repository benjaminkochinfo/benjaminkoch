The ledger's entry tonight is a line most tenants never read: the fixed charge on a district-heating bill. Enercity, Hannover's municipal utility, has announced changes to its district-heating prices, and on Monday the Verbraucherzentrale Niedersachsen, the state's consumer advice centre, put numbers on them [1].

By its calculation, the higher fixed costs in the base price can add up to €2,150 a year for a detached house [1]. In blocks of flats it finds increases of about €200 to €530 per flat per year [1]. Hardest hit are households with a high heat demand, for example in unrenovated buildings with a correspondingly large connected load [1]. Which cost factors drive the rise, and how much of it is the real cost of expanding the network, remains unclear, the advocates say [1].

**The monopoly line.** "District-heating customers in the Hannover area cannot simply switch supplier or move to another heating system because of enercity's monopoly position," says René Zietlow-Zahl, the centre's energy-law expert [1]. That is why, he argues, price rises should be systematically checked by someone independent [1]. It is not an argument against district heating. He calls affordable, traceable prices important for its acceptance and for the success of the heating transition [1].

**The discount with a string attached.** Enercity's pricing includes a 15.09% discount, calculated by comparing district-heating costs with a gas heating in basic supply; if district heat is dearer, the discount partly closes the gap [1]. It softens this rise considerably [1]. But the advocates point to the mechanism: if gas prices rise, the gap narrows, the discount shrinks, and district-heating increases could be amplified [1].

**For you.** The one lever a customer holds is the connected load, the capacity in your contract, which now carries a larger share of the cost [1]. The centre suggests checking whether it matches your real need; a heat-load calculation can show whether it can be reduced [1]. A smaller number on one dull line of a contract is worth more than any amount of outrage at the headline price.
