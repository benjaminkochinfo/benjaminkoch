**The podium.** Opening Euro Finance Week on Monday morning, ECB Vice-President Boris Vujčić said the risk of a market repricing "rather than abating, has further increased" since a year ago [1]. His central sentence: "A reassessment of the AI narrative may trigger an abrupt repricing in markets and significant valuation losses" [1]. He then spelled out the route. Euro-area investment funds have "significantly increased their exposures to AI", so a repricing "could lead to a surge in investment fund redemptions, increasing the risk of forced asset sales" [1].

**The print.** Six hours later, at the New York open, the Nasdaq Composite fell 282.9 points, or 1.13%, to 24,853.35; the S&P 500 lost 0.63% and the Dow 0.15% [2]. A little later CNBC TV18 had the Nasdaq down 1.4%, with Micron off 6%, Intel 5% and AMD 4%, ahead of Nvidia's results after Tuesday's close [3].

**The gap.** On the surface, wide: "abrupt" and "significant" against a 1% opening dip. But Vujčić was not forecasting Monday. He was describing a mechanism, and the print shows only that the first part of it, the reassessment, is being priced in instalments rather than all at once [1][2]. The second part, redemptions turning into forced sales, does not show up in an index at all. It shows up in the liquidity terms of the funds you hold. His own prescription is dull and precise: funds should better align "redemption terms with the liquidity of underlying assets" [1].

**For you.** Find the fund factsheet for your largest holding and check two lines: how much of it sits in AI-linked names, and how quickly you can redeem. Neither is a reason to sell tonight. Both are reasons to know before Wednesday's Financial Stability Review [4]. Nothing here is investment advice.
