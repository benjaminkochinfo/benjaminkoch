Russia's military says it intercepted and destroyed 2,049 Ukrainian drones and four Flamingo long-range missiles over 24 hours, the biggest Ukrainian drone attack of the war by its count [1]. The overnight figure from the defence ministry was 1,336 drones [2]; around 600 flew towards Moscow alone, according to its mayor, Sergey Sobyanin [1]. Drones struck the Kerch bridge to occupied Crimea, Vladimir Putin's signature project, leading to traffic restrictions; Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said: "Minor damage is being assessed" [1][3]. Ukraine's general staff said it hit the Ilsky refinery in Krasnodar region and an oil depot in Azov, in Rostov region [3].

The other direction was just as busy. Kyiv came under a record 17 air-raid alerts in a single day, and at least seven people were killed by Russian strikes, the BBC reports, with attacks on energy infrastructure leaving 50,000 people without power [1]. Ukraine's deputy energy minister Artem Nekrasov reported new outages in six regions, from Vinnytsia to Odesa, and counted 116 deliberate Russian attacks on energy, oil and gas infrastructure over the past week, with 161 incidents of damage recorded [3].

Both sides are now aiming at the same dull thing: the other's energy system. Sberbank's first deputy chief executive told Reuters that drone attacks on Russian refineries, warehouses, data centres and ports were "meaningful factors" this year, and that the bank expects the rouble to weaken by 10% against the dollar [3].

For you: interception counts are claims made by the side doing the intercepting, and nobody publishes the count of what got through. The weekly tally of damaged substations, and how fast the repair crews close it, is the figure that decides how this winter goes.
