Brent's December contract expired on Friday at $117.64 a barrel, up $2.76 on the day and its highest level since the end of April, Reuters reported [1]. For October, Brent rose 13.6% and US crude 15.2% [1]. The more active January contract closed at $113.43 [1]. Desk arithmetic: $4.21 lower, so the market is paying a premium for barrels now over barrels later. Behind it: a limpet-mine attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, and Houthi claims of strikes on Saudi refineries [1]. A quieter line in the same report: Saudi crude exports rose in October, according to two of three ship-tracking firms [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
