OpenAI is now seeking up to $20 billion in its latest funding round, down from an earlier target of up to $30 billion, CNBC confirmed on Saturday after the Financial Times first reported the cut [1]. The FT said the round values the ChatGPT developer at $1.25 trillion after the money comes in, and that OpenAI had set a Friday deadline for investors to commit, with some reducing their allocations [1]. The round has not closed and the figures could change, a person familiar with the deal told CNBC [1]. Investors from the United Arab Emirates are leading it alongside Coatue [1].

**The week around it.** Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon all issued capex figures or forecasts that came in below analysts' estimates, CNBC notes [1]. Alphabet had its worst trading day on record on Thursday; on Friday Intel fell 29%, Qualcomm 11%, Broadcom more than 5% and Nvidia 4%, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 2.9% [2]. Amazon kept its 2026 capex forecast at $220 billion and reported negative free cash flow for a second quarter running [2].

**How to read it.** A smaller round at a very large valuation is not a collapse; it is a negotiation that the buyers won by a third. Desk arithmetic: $10 billion off $30 billion. For the companies building data centres on the strength of contracts with model makers, the relevant question is not the mood on Friday but whether those customers keep raising enough cash to pay their bills. This week offered a small, priced answer. Nothing here is investment advice.
