On Thursday, before the ECB's rate decision, Christine Lagarde told the Governing Council and European Council President António Costa that she will step down on 7 February 2027 [1]. Reuters noted that the rate decision was published only hours later [2].

**What the ECB said.** The press release is short. Her successor will be appointed by the European Council under the procedure in Article 283 of the EU treaty [1]. And: "The Governing Council thanked President Lagarde warmly and gave her a long standing ovation" [1]. No drama, no list of candidates, no hint of a change in policy.

**What did not change.** The meeting went ahead as planned, with rates held and a December hike clearly signalled [2]. Lagarde stressed that governments must comply with EU fiscal rules to qualify for help from the ECB's bond-market backstop, the Transmission Protection Instrument [2]. That condition belongs to the institution, not to its president.

**Why it is reassuring.** A central bank is built so that a change at the top is a matter of procedure, not of nerves. The treaty sets out who nominates, who is consulted and who appoints. Names will be floated in the coming weeks; the rule that decides between them was written long ago. That is what institutions are for: to make the departure of one person a scheduling question.
