New months bring new rules, and November's are unusually practical.

**Parcels from outside the EU cost more.** From 1 November, a handling fee of €2 applies for each product category in a parcel from a non-EU country [1]. A parcel with a T-shirt and a hair clip pays €4; several T-shirts pay only €2 [1]. Together with the extra duty introduced in July, a parcel can cost at least €5 more [1]. The sender pays first but may pass the fee on [1]. Desk arithmetic: one order split across three categories means €6 in fees before the goods are counted.

**Families travel at a flat price.** From 1 November, Deutsche Bahn sells its family ticket permanently: €99.99 for a return journey for up to five people, with an ICE seat reservation included [1]. It was meant only for the summer holidays; demand kept it [1].

**Pay-later gets a credit check.** From 20 November, providers such as PayPal and Klarna must check more carefully whether customers can afford to repay "buy now, pay later" purchases and small loans up to €200 [1]. The aim is to protect people from over-indebtedness [1]. A slower checkout is a small price for that.

**And a deadline.** The car-insurance switching date is 30 November in most cases [1]. The brief beside this piece has the arithmetic.

None of these is dramatic. Together they are the sort of thing a household ledger is for.
