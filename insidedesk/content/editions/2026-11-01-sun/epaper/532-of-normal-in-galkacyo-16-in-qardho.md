By 26 October, 42 Somali rain stations with a long record had already reached their October normal, the FAO's weekly bulletin says: Galkacyo at 532%, Baidoa at 282% [1]. In the same country, Burao stood at 26% and Qardho at 16% [1]. On the Shabelle, Belet Weyne rose above its 6.50 m moderate flood-risk level on 26 October and stood at 6.70 m by the 29th, with the daily rise slowing [1]. The IOM projects that up to 874,319 people could be displaced between October and December, 92% of them by floods; by 21 October it had secured about 14.4% of the US$16.62 million it asked for to prepare [2]. Desk arithmetic: 14.4% is about a seventh. The rain is measured every day; the money to prepare for it, at last count, was a seventh of the way there.
