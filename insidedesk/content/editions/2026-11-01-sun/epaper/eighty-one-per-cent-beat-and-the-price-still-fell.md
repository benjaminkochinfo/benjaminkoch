By Thursday, 253 of the S&P 500 companies had reported for the third quarter, and 81% of them had beaten analysts' expectations, according to LSEG data [1]. Analysts now expect aggregate earnings growth of 31.0% on the year [1]. Those are not the numbers of a weak quarter.

Beating is not the same as being rewarded. Caterpillar topped profit estimates but left its growth targets unchanged, and its shares fell 9.3% [1]. Ford rose 12.4% on a revenue beat and Eli Lilly 6.6% after lifting its outlook [1]. The market is paying for the next line, not the last one. Nothing here is investment advice.
