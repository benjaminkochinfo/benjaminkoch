Cyclone Arnab, classed by the India Meteorological Department as a "very severe cyclonic storm", brought exceptionally heavy rain to southern Odisha on 28 October [1]. The IMD's gauges give the scale better than any adjective: 546 mm at Patrapur in Ganjam district, 503 mm at Sorada, 462 mm at G Udayagiri in Kandhamal and 458 mm at Mahendragarh in Gajapati [1].

The Rushikulya and Mahendratanaya rivers swelled, and floods and landslides washed away roads and bridges across seven districts [1]. State officials count 299 affected villages in Ganjam and 711 in Gajapati, with around 42,500 and 20,000 people affected respectively [1]. Rescue teams from the national and state disaster forces and the fire services moved 11,882 people in Ganjam and 17,441 in Gajapati into flood and cyclone shelters [1]. Some villages are still cut off; food is arriving by helicopter, and the Ganjam collector says boats have been arranged for marooned residents [1].

There is a quieter line too. After a dam release of 350 cubic metres per second into the Ghodahada river, home to around 100 mugger crocodiles, residents were warned; the forest range officer says no injuries have been reported [1].

The watch item: the IMD's 30 October release expected a low-pressure area in the Andaman Sea to concentrate into a depression around 31 October, with isolated heavy rain likely over coastal Andhra Pradesh [1].

The constructive reading is in the counting. Shelters, evacuation numbers by district and a published rain gauge for each town are what a state that has learned from past cyclones looks like.
