On Friday the US Food and Drug Administration granted accelerated approval to Voceryq, from Inovio Pharmaceuticals, for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis [1]. It is the first plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy approved in the US [1].

**The disease.** HPV types 6 and 11 cause wart-like growths in the airways, mostly around the voice box. They bring hoarseness, coughing and, in severe cases, difficulty breathing. Surgery removes them, but the virus stays in the tissue, so many patients go back to the operating theatre again and again over years [1].

**The medicine.** Voceryq delivers two small rings of DNA into the body's cells: one carries instructions for HPV proteins, the other for an immune-boosting molecule, IL-12. It is injected into muscle with a device that uses brief electrical pulses to open cell membranes [1].

**The evidence.** The trial was open-label and enrolled 32 adults who had needed at least two surgeries in the previous year [1]. Of 26 patients who had had at least three such surgeries, seven, or 27%, needed no surgery at all in the year after treatment [1]. The most common side effects were injection-site reactions, headache, fatigue, high blood pressure, nausea and dizziness [1].

**The condition.** Accelerated approval rests on an intermediate endpoint, fewer surgeries, judged reasonably likely to predict real clinical benefit. The manufacturer must run further trials to confirm it [1]. That is the system working as designed: a door opened early for a rare disease, with a receipt still to be presented.
