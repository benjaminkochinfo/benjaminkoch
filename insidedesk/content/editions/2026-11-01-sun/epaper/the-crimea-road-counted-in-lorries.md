Wars are supplied by lorry, and lorries can be counted. Anatoly Tsurkin, who heads the freight carriers' association in occupied Crimea, said on Saturday that the number of trucks travelling from Russia's Rostov region to Crimea had fallen from 2,000 to 3,000 a day to 600 to 800 a day [1]. He said the M-14 highway is the only land route lorries can use, and that Ukrainian drone strikes have also made drivers less willing to make the run [1]. ISW reports his claim; it did not count the trucks itself [1].

Treat it as one man's figure, not a measurement. But it comes from the side with every reason to understate the damage. Logistics is where long-range strikes show up first: not in maps, but in the number of drivers who turn up for work.
