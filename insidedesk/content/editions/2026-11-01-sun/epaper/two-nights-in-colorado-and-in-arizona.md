A week after Europe's clocks went back, a useful preprint, not yet peer reviewed. Researchers compared Fitbit sleep data from 1,522 people around 13 clock changes in Colorado, New Mexico and Utah with the same dates in Arizona, which does not change its clocks [1]. After the autumn change, sleep timing moved about 28 minutes earlier [1]. Large departures from someone's usual sleep length typically returned to normal within two nights, but almost the same two-night pattern appeared in Arizona after similarly large departures [1]. The authors' point: a fast return to baseline is not the same as recovery from the clock change [1]. People whose sleep varied most beforehand were likeliest to still be off a week or two later [1]. A steady bedtime is the cheap defence.
