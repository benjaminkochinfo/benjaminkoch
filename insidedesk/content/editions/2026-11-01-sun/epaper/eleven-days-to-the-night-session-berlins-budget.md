The German federal budget for 2027 is not decided in speeches but in a calendar, and the Bundestag publishes it [1]. This week the 42 members of the budget committee take the transport and digital ministries on Wednesday 4 November, then housing, research and the Foreign Office on Thursday 5 November [1]. The Bundestag's own budget and the finance ministry follow on 11 November [1].

Then comes the Bereinigungssitzung on Thursday 12 November, the "clean-up" sitting where the most contested questions, set aside until then, are settled; in the past it has often run into the early hours of the next day [1]. The final four-day debate runs from Tuesday 24 to Friday 27 November [1].

The money on the table is smaller than the hopes. The autumn tax estimate gives the federal government €4.1 billion more for 2027 than expected, and all levels of government €10.9 billion more [2]. But a better economy also lowers the borrowing allowed under the debt brake's cyclical component. "For 2027 we gain no new room overall, but we also don't have to close new gaps," Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil said [2]. Still open, by n-tv's count: compensation for bracket creep, about €4 billion next year, and the promised cut in electricity tax for households and small firms, about €5.4 billion a year [2].

The constructive reading: a parliament that names the date of its hardest night in advance is a parliament that intends to finish. Watch 12 November, and read the committee's recommendations, not the press releases.
