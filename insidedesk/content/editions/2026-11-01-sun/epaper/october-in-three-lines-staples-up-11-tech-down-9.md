October is over, and the month's ledger is easier to read than any single day of it.

**The averages.** The Nasdaq fell 5.3% in October, its steepest monthly drop since March 2025 [1]. The S&P 500 slipped 0.9%. The Dow, heavier in banks, oil and household brands, gained 1.7% [1].

**The rotation.** Nasdaq Dorsey Wright counts the consumer-staples fund XLP up 11% in October, its best calendar month on record, while technology fell more than 9% [2]. On Friday alone, Walmart and Coca-Cola each rose more than 1% and the staples fund touched an all-time intraday high [1]. People still buy toothpaste in a tech sell-off. Investors remembered that.

**The casualties.** The hardest falls were among companies that borrow to build AI capacity. CoreWeave lost 37% in the month and Nebius almost 35%; a memory-chip fund fell 20% [1]. "Last year was hope," Keith Buchanan of Globalt told CNBC. This year the question is "how to finance [it] and who gets the economics" [1].

**What to make of it.** The market did not fall apart. It sorted itself: cash-generating businesses up, cash-hungry ones down. Dorsey Wright notes that November to May has historically been the stronger half of the year for the Dow [2]. An average is a description of the past, not a promise; the sorting is the more useful lesson. Nothing here is investment advice.
