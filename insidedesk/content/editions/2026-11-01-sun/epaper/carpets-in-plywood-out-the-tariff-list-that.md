At 12:01 a.m. Eastern time on Friday, 05:01 in Berlin, the list of Canadian goods paying an extra 50% to enter the United States changed [1]. The proclamation, signed on 23 October and published on Friday, takes some products off the list in one annex and puts others on in another [1]. The duty comes on top of any Section 232 tariffs [1].

Canada News Media, which went through the annex, counts 22 lines removed and 100 added [2]. Off come seven lines of thin plywood, corrugated cartons, paper sacks, coated paperboard, plastic plates and bags, some food-packing machinery, and edible seaweed [2]. On go carpets and floor coverings, 43 lines by themselves, cocoa powder with 13, plus paints, soaps, shampoos, lubricants, window air conditioners, chest freezers of up to 800 litres, canoes and archery equipment [2]. It is the third change to these duties in as many months, according to Flexport as cited by the outlet [2].

The annex gives no reason for each choice, and no official figure for the value of trade affected has been published [2]. An importer has already challenged the duties at the US Court of International Trade, and the two governments are working on a narrower deal on steel and autos [2].

For you, the lesson is about where trade policy really lives. A speech says "Canada". A proclamation says HTSUS codes, with an effective minute. For a Canadian carpet exporter, the week's diplomacy arrived on Friday as a line in a customs schedule. If you want to know what a tariff does, read the annex. Nothing here is investment advice.
