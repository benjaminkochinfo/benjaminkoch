On the first Sunday of November the ledger turns from the pump to the boiler. Both matter this week, and both have numbers you can check yourself.

**The boiler.** Verivox puts the average price of a new gas contract at about 14 cents per kilowatt-hour, for a household using 20,000 kWh a year, as of 30 October [1]. Across all customers, old contracts and new, the average was about 12.7 cents on 1 October [1]. In January, Verivox's table showed a family in a 180 m² house paying 9.87 cents and €1,974 a year [1]. Many suppliers are raising their prices, says its energy expert Thorsten Storck, and anyone who receives an increase should compare it with the offers available now [1].

Desk arithmetic: 20,000 kWh at 14 cents is €2,800 a year; at 12.7 cents it is €2,540. The €260 between the two is the price of signing a new contract this month rather than sitting on an older one, which is a reason to read your price guarantee's end date before you switch, not after.

**The pump.** ADAC's latest weekly reading found Super E10 at €2.084 a litre, down 2.6 cents, and diesel at €2.289, up 3.4 cents; diesel now costs over 20 cents more [2]. The club blames dearer crude, a tight diesel market and rising heating-oil demand as the weather turns [2]. The tank rebate, a cut of about 17 cents a litre including VAT, runs from the start of October to the end of December [2]. The government wants a fuel-price cap by 1 January 2027 at the latest [2].

**The kitchen, for balance.** Food prices rose just 0.4% on the year in October, Destatis said on Friday; household energy rose 12.2% [3]. This winter's squeeze is in the meter cupboard, not the fridge.

**What you can do with that.** ADAC's advice still holds: fill up shortly before noon, since prices may only rise once a day at 12:00, and compare stations in its app [2]. Verivox's advice is the gas version of the same habit: compare when the letter arrives [1]. Neither is glamorous. Both are worth more than a forecast.
