Weekends are usually where German power gets cheap. Factories idle, offices empty, and the auction clears lower. Not this one. Friday's day-ahead auction priced Saturday 31 October at an average of €251.42 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD figures [1].

[[chart:de-october-saturdays-30pm]]

**The comparison.** Last Saturday, 24 October, cost €30.12, on a day when onshore wind turbines generated 1,009.2 gigawatt hours [1]. Desk arithmetic: this Saturday is a little over eight times that. Across 2026, no Saturday has come close: the next-dearest was 3 October at €169.01 [1]. Saturday is also the fourth-dearest delivery day of the whole year so far, behind Thursday's €314.85, Friday's €268.62 and 14 September's €256.55 [1].

**The shape of the day.** The cheapest hour on Saturday is 12:00 to 13:00 CET, at €220.25, and the dearest is 18:00 to 19:00 CET, at €298.97 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Saturday is €17.20 cheaper than Friday on average, so the weekend discount is there, just very small.

**The background.** On still days it is often gas plants that set the price, and gas is dear: Dutch TTF futures for December rose 4.9% to €102.78 per megawatt hour on Friday, CNBC reported, the highest in almost four years [2].

**The calm point.** The receipt is printed a day ahead, hour by hour, for anyone to read. If you have a dynamic tariff, Saturday lunchtime is still the cheapest slot, and the washing machine does not care what day it is. Nothing here is investment advice.
