Germany's respiratory season has started the way it usually does: with the common cold. The Robert Koch-Institut's weekly report for 19 to 25 October puts acute respiratory illness at a moderate level in the population and in GP surgeries, and at a low level in hospitals [1]. Activity has been fairly stable since the beginning of October [1].

The viruses doing the work are the unglamorous ones. Of 76 sentinel samples sent to the national influenza reference centre, 50 contained respiratory viruses, mostly rhino- and enteroviruses (43%), then parainfluenza viruses (14%) and adenoviruses (7%) [1]. Influenza and SARS-CoV-2 were each found in 3% of samples; RSV was not detected at all [1]. Among hospital patients with severe acute respiratory infections, 2% each had a flu or Covid diagnosis, and fewer than 1% RSV [1].

The direction is the news. The RKI says SARS-CoV-2 and influenza activity is still low in all its systems but has been rising gradually in recent weeks, and there are signs that SARS-CoV-2 is already circulating nationwide [1]. Notified Covid cases rose slightly on the previous week, while notified flu cases dipped slightly [1]. In wastewater, the SARS-CoV-2 load is still low but has been trending up for several weeks; flu shows a slight rise and RSV stays low [1]. The recombinant variant XFG remained the most commonly detected lineage [1].

None of this is a forecast, and the RKI makes none. It is a timetable. If you are in a group for which an autumn flu or Covid jab is recommended, a low and rising curve is the calm moment to book it, before the waiting room fills.
