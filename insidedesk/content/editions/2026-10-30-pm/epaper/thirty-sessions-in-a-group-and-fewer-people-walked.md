Fear of criticism, a strong need to lean on others, a rigid drive for order: when such lifelong patterns get in the way of work and love, they can be diagnosed as "cluster C" personality disorders. They account for up to 30% of patients in mental-health services but draw far less research than borderline disorder, Psychreg reports [1].

**The trial.** Researchers at the University of Amsterdam and ten Dutch mental-health centres randomly assigned 369 outpatients, most with avoidant personality disorder, to group schema therapy (30 weekly sessions of 90 minutes), individual schema therapy (50 sessions) or specialist usual care, according to the trial published in BMJ Mental Health [1]. After one year both schema formats had reduced the severity of the disorder more than usual care, by a small to moderate margin; by two years all three groups had improved a lot and the gap had closed [1].

**The ledger.** After one year, 93% of the individual-therapy group and 85% of the group-therapy patients were still in treatment, against 78% in usual care [1]. Only the individual-therapy gap was statistically significant at that point; by two years both formats had kept more patients than usual care [1]. The group format treats five to nine people with two therapists [1].

**For you.** In therapy, as in most things, a treatment only has a chance to work if people keep coming back to it. An economic evaluation is under way; that, not a headline, will decide what services offer [1].
