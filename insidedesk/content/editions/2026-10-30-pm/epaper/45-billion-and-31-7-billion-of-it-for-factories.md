The EU's Ukraine Support Loan is worth €90 billion up to the end of 2027, and half of it is meant to be paid out in 2026 [1]. On Friday the European Commission set up the framework for member states to approve the other €45 billion [1]. Ursula von der Leyen had announced the proposal on Thursday, in the same post in which she condemned the strike on Dnipro [2].

The split is the interesting part. Of the 2027 money, €13.3 billion is to go in budget support and €31.7 billion to financing Ukraine's defence industrial capacity [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 70% of next year's tranche is earmarked for production rather than salaries and pensions.

"With today's proposal, we are providing Ukraine with much-needed predictability and helping maintain essential public services," von der Leyen said [1]. The Commission also gave Ukraine's financing strategy a positive assessment [1]. The allocation still has to pass the EU's approval procedures before any money moves [2].

For you, the useful word is predictability. A ministry that knows in October what it can spend next year can sign contracts, pay suppliers on time and plan its winter repairs. A pledge can be undone with the next speech. An approved tranche with a written split is far harder to talk away, and that dull certainty is worth more than the headline figure.
