Data centres run on contracts as much as on chips. Amazon's quarterly filing says that at the end of September it held energy contracts covering about 690 million megawatt hours that it must value at market prices, with a weighted-average remaining life of about 18 years; most of that power is to be delivered more than ten years from now [1]. Some contracts run 20 years [1].

Because Amazon may settle in cash rather than take the power, these count as derivatives, and their revaluation produced unrealised gains of $91 million in the quarter, booked mainly against AWS [1]. Desk arithmetic: 690 million MWh is a little more than the 1.36 million MWh Germany's grid used on Thursday [2], multiplied by 500. The cloud's electricity bill is being written decades ahead, and it is in the footnotes.
