New York closed October on Friday with a sell-off in the shares that had carried the year. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00, its worst day since June, CNBC reported; the S&P 500 lost 1.82% to 7,580.07 and the Dow 1.09% to 51,770.31 [1]. Intel fell almost 29% after a weak outlook, and chipmakers such as AMD, Nvidia and Broadcom went down with it [1].

The other half of the tape is the more useful half. Consumer staples and healthcare rose: Walmart and Coca-Cola each gained more than 1%, and a staples fund touched an all-time high [1]. For the month, the Nasdaq lost 5.3%, its steepest fall since March 2025, while the Dow added 1.7% [1].

The pattern is an old one. When the question turns from what AI might earn to how it is paid for, money moves to companies whose invoices arrive every week. Nothing here is investment advice.
