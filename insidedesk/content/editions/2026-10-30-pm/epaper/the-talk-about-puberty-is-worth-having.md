Researchers at Beijing Normal University followed 1,977 seventh-graders, average age 12.7, at a rural middle school in China; 1,911 were seen again a year later, Medscape reports of a study in the Journal of Adolescent Health [1]. Teenagers who said their parents gave them information, talked about puberty and offered emotional support had fewer depressive symptoms at follow-up, whatever the timing of their puberty [1]. Among girls, early puberty was linked with more symptoms only when parental support was low [1].

The effects were small, the support scale had only three items and all pupils came from one low-income school [1]. Still, the authors suggest clinicians could give parents brief guidance on normalising bodily changes [1]. One awkward conversation is a modest price.
