A Norwegian group reports in The Lancet that people with irritable bowel syndrome do not seem to benefit from a faecal transplant meant to renew the gut microbiome [1]. In the REFIT2 trial at five hospitals, 448 adults with moderate-to-severe IBS were analysed; at 90 days, 40% of those given donor stool and 38% of those given their own stool as placebo improved by at least 75 points on the symptom score, a difference that was not significant [2]. At one year it was 44% against 45% [2].

The donor microbes did take hold, shifting recipients' gut profiles towards their donor's; it simply did not relieve symptoms, and no "super donor" stood out [2]. A 2024 US gastroenterology guideline already advises against conventional transplants for IBS outside trials [2]. If a clinic offers you one for IBS, ask for the trial it is part of.
