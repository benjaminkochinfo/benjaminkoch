Gas got the attention this week, but lignite did the heavy lifting. On Thursday 29 October, the year's dearest power day, German lignite plants generated 295.3 gigawatt hours, against 283.3 GWh from gas, according to SMARD's provisional figures [1]. On Monday lignite had generated 162.1 GWh [1]. Desk arithmetic: an increase of about 82% in three days, while onshore and offshore wind together fell from about 826 GWh to about 109 GWh [1].

The point is dull and important: on windless days, brown coal is still one of the machines Germany turns up when the turbines stop. Thursday's figures are provisional and may be revised; the next update appears on SMARD [1].
