Kyiv, a city of around three million, has seen its bridges over the Dnipro hit by Russian drones again and again. Each strike stops the traffic and leaves people stuck in long queues [1]. On Friday the transport minister, Mykola Kalashnyk, told lawmakers the government had approved the "rapid construction of alternative bridge crossings over rivers, including across the Dnipro" [1]. "In total, the implementation of 21 bridge crossings is planned. Work in Kyiv has already begun," he said, adding that the first would open at the beginning of the year [1].

This is resilience in its least glamorous form: a second way across, built before the first one fails. It will never make a headline the way an intercept does. But when a bridge is closed on a weekday morning, a spare one is what keeps the bus running.
