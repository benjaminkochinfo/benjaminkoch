Germany had 2,955,000 people registered as unemployed in October, 40,000 fewer than in September, the Federal Employment Agency (BA) said on Friday [1]. The rate fell 0.1 points to 6.3% [1]. That is the usual autumn upturn, and the BA's own headline calls it weak [1]. Adjusted for the season, unemployment rose by 4,000; on the year it was 44,000 higher [1].

"The economic conditions are still not sufficient for an improvement in the labour market," said the BA's chair, Andrea Nahles [1]. That is a plain sentence from an institution that does not need to sell anything.

**Under the headline.** The broader measure of underemployment, which also counts people in schemes and on short-term sick leave, fell by a seasonally adjusted 9,000, to 3,592,000 [1]. Firms notified short-time work for 22,000 people between 1 and 26 October [1]. In August, the latest month with actual figures, short-time pay went to 98,000 workers, 20,000 fewer than in July and about 66,000 fewer than a year earlier [1]. Employers had 654,000 vacancies registered, 31,000 more than a year ago [1].

**The other side of the payroll.** Unemployment benefit went to 1,065,000 people, 88,000 more than a year earlier, while people on basic income support able to work fell by 172,000, to 3,659,000 [1]. Desk arithmetic: more people drawing the insurance they paid into, fewer on the last-resort safety net. A labour market that is soft but not breaking looks like that. Nothing here is investment advice.
