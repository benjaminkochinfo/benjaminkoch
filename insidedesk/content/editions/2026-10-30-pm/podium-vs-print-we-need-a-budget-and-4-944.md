**The podium.** On Friday morning France learned that its economy shrank by 0.1% in the third quarter, after a flat second quarter and a 0.2% fall in the first [1]. Roland Lescure, the economy and finance minister, went on franceinfo and called the economy "à l'arrêt", at a standstill, after an "unprecedented" mix of shocks, from the summer heatwave to the fuel prices driven by tension around the Strait of Hormuz [1]. Then he made his case: "We need a budget, we need a deficit of 5% or less. It is absolutely indispensable," to find growth again and to reassure investors and firms that "France is not heading for crisis" [1]. Without that budget, he said, "on ira dans le mur": we will hit the wall [1].

**The print.** The bond market answered before lunch. France's 10-year yield rose 6 basis points to 4.944% after the GDP figure, Reuters reported, underperforming the rest of the euro zone [2]. Analysts polled by Reuters had expected growth of 0.1%, not a fall [2]. Exports fell 1.7%, mostly because exports of transport equipment, chiefly aircraft, dropped 9.1% after a 20.8% jump in the spring; investment fell 0.6% [2].

**The gap.** The minister and the market are not, in fact, disagreeing about the diagnosis. They disagree about the cure's odds. The budget he wants is still in the National Assembly, aiming to cut the deficit to 5% of GDP from 5.4% this year, and France 24 calls its adoption "very uncertain" with no majority and six months before a presidential election [1]. A yield is the price of that uncertainty, quoted daily. Desk arithmetic: 6 basis points is 0.06 percentage points, about 1.2% of the yield itself. Small for a day, but Reuters says France paid more of it than its neighbours [2].

**What the speech left out.** The same INSEE release carried a quieter number: household consumption rose 0.4% in the quarter, after 0.2%, with purchases of electric cars helping [2]. Across the euro area, growth was 0.4%, and Spain grew 0.6% [3]. The shop till is not at a standstill, even if the export ledger is. For you, the lesson is the usual one: read the release, then the price, and only then the interview. Nothing here is investment advice.
