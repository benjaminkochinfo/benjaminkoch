Amazon's press release on Thursday told the story of a record quarter. Its Form 10-Q, accepted by the SEC on Thursday evening and public this morning, tells the story of contracts. Two tables in it deserve more attention than any slide.

**What customers owe.** Amazon says customers have committed to approximately $578 billion of future services under contracts longer than a year that it has not yet booked as revenue, "primarily related to AWS", with an average remaining life of 6.1 years [1]. Some of it is large and named: in the first quarter AWS expanded its arrangement with OpenAI by $100.0 billion over eight years, and in the second it expanded its commitment with Anthropic by more than $100.0 billion over ten years [1].

**What Amazon owes.** The other column is longer. Principal contractual commitments at the end of September totalled $883.6 billion, the filing shows, including $159.6 billion for property and equipment, $63.9 billion of it due in 2027, and $164.9 billion for leases that have not yet started [1]. Long-term debt rose from $65.6 billion at the end of 2025 to $173.1 billion [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 2.6 times as much in nine months. Interest expense in the quarter was $1.69 billion, against $538 million a year earlier [1]. The filing also describes a secured loan facility from suppliers of up to about $80.0 billion for technology infrastructure, available from early 2027 [1].

**Reading the two together.** The columns are not a balance: the commitments include leases, debt interest and media rights, and the backlog depends on customers actually using what they signed for, as the filing itself says [1]. But they show the shape of the bet more honestly than a forecast does. Amazon is borrowing and signing now against revenue that is contracted, not yet earned.

**For you.** You don't need an opinion on artificial intelligence to read this. You need two numbers and a date, and the company has to update all three every quarter. Apple, whose results come on Monday, will publish its own [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
