The tanker was outbound in the Strait of Hormuz, 25 nautical miles north-east of Oman's Khasab, when a small boat attached two explosive devices to its hull on Thursday, the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said [2]. The blasts started a fire in the engine room and left the vessel temporarily immobilised [2]. By Friday UKMTO said the crew were safe, the fire was out, there was no environmental impact and the ship was continuing to its next port of call [2]. Nobody has said who did it [2].

Oil reacted on the contract with the least time left. Brent futures for December, which expired on Friday, rose $2.50, or 2.2%, to $117.38 a barrel in early trade [1]. The more active January contract gained $1.39 to $112.19, and US WTI rose $2.04 to $104.09 [1]. Brent was on course to finish October 13% higher and WTI 15%, a fourth straight monthly gain [1].

Desk arithmetic: the expiring December contract stood $5.19 above January. A front month that much dearer than the next one usually means buyers are paying for barrels they need now. It tells you less about where prices will be by Christmas.

Two other details from the same report are worth your attention. ExxonMobil and Chevron have cancelled plans for their executives to attend next week's big oil show in the United Arab Emirates, according to two Reuters sources [1]. And Iran has received a letter from Vice President JD Vance but has not answered it, Iran's semi-official Tasnim agency said [1]. One is a travel diary and the other an unanswered letter. Neither is a market forecast, but both say something about how safe the people who run the trade feel.

The tanker sailed on and the price went up, and both are true. The gauge to watch is the one the trade keeps for itself: whether ships keep their schedules. If a ship can be hit, put the fire out and still make its next port, the trade is under strain but still running. Nothing here is investment advice.
