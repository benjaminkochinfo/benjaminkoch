The clocks went back in Europe last Sunday, and the evenings have turned dark. A good week, then, for a study about the body clock and mood, and a good week to read it slowly.

**The claim.** Researchers at the University of Sydney's Brain and Mind Centre, writing in the British Journal of Psychiatry, propose that some people have "circadian" depression, in which a disrupted body clock is a core feature, National Geographic reported on Friday [1]. Their test is six self-reported traits: seasonal mood swings, late sleep, being a night owl, oversleeping, waking up very tired, and "social jet lag", the gap between weekday and weekend sleep. Anyone with at least three counts [1]. Of a cohort of more than 11,000 people, about a quarter fitted [1]. Their depression tended to start earlier and to be more severe, and they were more likely to say antidepressants had not worked well [1].

**The caveats, from the people themselves.** The traits were self-reported, with no wearable data or melatonin measurements to confirm a disrupted clock [1]. Co-author Jacob Crouse concedes the poor response to drugs might have other causes [1]. David Curtis of the UCL Genetics Institute says the checklist may simply pick out more severe depression, and that a poor response to one drug "doesn't mean they'll respond better to a different treatment at all" [1]. Whether the clock drives the low mood, or the low mood scrambles the clock, is still open [1].

**What is already known.** A 2025 review cited in the report found that among more than 800 people with depression that was not seasonal, more than 40% reached remission with bright light therapy, against 25% in the control group; the evidence for melatonin is weaker [1]. The Sydney team is now running a melatonin trial in young adults who fit the profile [1].

**For you.** You don't need a new diagnosis to use an old remedy: morning daylight, a steady bedtime and a weekend that does not run three hours late are cheap and safe. If low mood has lasted weeks, the right first step is still a GP, not a checklist.
