The final results confirmed what the count had been saying all week. Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc has 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc 48, and the two Arab-led lists, Ra'am and the Joint List, a record combined 19 [1][2]. Likud is the largest party with 27, ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar with 21 and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad with 12 [2]. Sixty-one makes a majority [1].

Friday was a day of refusals. "It is forbidden to form a government with them," Netanyahu posted, about the Arab-led parties [1]. Avigdor Liberman, on the opposition side, put it as "Neither Bibi nor Tibi", ruling out both Netanyahu and Ahmad Tibi of the Joint List [1], and later said: "We will not sit with supporters of terror" [2]. Shas leader Aryeh Deri ruled out joining an opposition-bloc government without Likud [2]. Meanwhile Netanyahu met Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich, and the opposition held separate talks of its own [2].

Desk arithmetic: Netanyahu's bloc needs 8 more seats; Eisenkot's needs 13. The only block large enough to close the opposition's gap on its own is the 19 Arab-led seats, which the loudest voices on both sides have just ruled out. Netanyahu's route runs through persuading parts of the opposition to cross, and analysts quoted by Al Jazeera say Bennett and Liberman do not currently look willing [1].

The cracks are where you would expect. Ra'am has indicated it is open to joining an Eisenkot-led government, analysts say, and the Joint List has left open supporting a minority government from outside [1]. Eisenkot has set conditions for partners, including recognising Israel as a Jewish and democratic state, without saying whether the Arab-led parties meet them [1]. Rabbi Dov Landau, a senior ultra-Orthodox figure, said: "We are not in anyone's bloc" [2].

For you, the sensible reading is patience. President Isaac Herzog has asked the parties to come "with an open mind" and to avoid another election [3]. A refusal on the day after a count is an opening position, not a final one. What counts is the list of recommendations each MK gives the president, and that list is still to come.
