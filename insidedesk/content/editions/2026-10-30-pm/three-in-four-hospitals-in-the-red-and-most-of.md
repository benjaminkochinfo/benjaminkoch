The consultancy Roland Berger has published this year's edition of its annual German hospital study, based on interviews with around 600 managing directors and medical directors [1]. The headline is a ledger, not a slogan. In 2025, 73% of hospitals were in the red, and at more than half of them (56%) the year-end deficit came to over 10% of revenue; at some it reached a quarter [1].

Then comes the paradox. Hospitals have become much better at predicting their own results: 84% met the annual result set in their business plan in 2025, against roughly half in earlier years [1]. Roland Berger is blunt about why. "The higher hit rate is no sign of recovery," the firm said (our translation, as are the quotes below); almost four in five loss-making hospitals met their plan because the losses had already been budgeted [1]. A target met is not the same as a business that lasts.

The reserves are thinning. Many hospitals have largely used theirs up, the study says, and co-author Jonas Hollacher expects the number of insolvencies "will probably rise sharply" [1]. Respondents expect the GKV contribution-rate stabilisation law, the government's savings package for statutory health insurance, to cut their revenue by 7% on average [1]. More than one hospital in two is postponing investment to cope, said senior partner Peter Magunia, which helps results and cash in the short run but not for long [1]. The outlook is grey: about two-thirds expect worse results and weaker liquidity through 2029, and only one in four expects an improvement by 2031 [1].

Read it the stubborn way. This is a survey of managers, not an audit, and those who run institutions have reasons to sound alarmed before a savings package. But forecasting is a skill, and the hospitals have learnt it; the question Roland Berger now puts to boards is not whether a target was met but whether the planned result is sustainable, and Magunia says no hospital will secure its future without "strategic and structural" measures [1]. For you, a deferred investment is a lift that waits, a scanner that ages, a ward that is not refurbished. Nobody will announce it. You will notice it at the reception desk.
