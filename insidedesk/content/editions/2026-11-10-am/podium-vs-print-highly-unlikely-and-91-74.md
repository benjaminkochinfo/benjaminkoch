**The podium.** Over the weekend Donald Trump told CNN that a resumption of strikes on Iran is "highly unlikely" if the United States sees progress in negotiations toward ending the war, AP reports [1]. It was a conditional sentence, and the condition matters: "if" carries most of its weight.

**The print.** The oil market took the sentence at face value first and read the condition later. Brent settled at $92.57 on Monday, down $5.75 or 5.85%, and US crude at $86.85, down 4.38% [2]. Early on Tuesday Brent slipped another 0.9% to $91.74 and US crude 1% to $85.97 [1]. Asia followed Wall Street higher. At 04:28 CET, Kaohoon International had Seoul's Kospi up 4.46%, Tokyo's Nikkei up 2.89% at 60,475.78 and Shanghai up only 0.14% [2].

[[chart:asia-tuesday-10nov]]

**The gap.** The footnote came from the same government. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Sunday that Iranian demands had sometimes been "outrageous and ridiculous" [1]. So the podium spoke with two voices, and the print chose the softer one. That is not irrational: a barrel prices probabilities, and a lower chance of strikes is worth something even before any deal exists. Seoul and Tokyo were led by chipmakers and AI names [1]: oil was the tailwind, not the engine.

**What the speech left out.** The households. In the same Asian morning, Japan reported that household spending fell 6.8% on the year in September, against a 3.1% fall expected, its tenth monthly decline in a row [3]. Desk arithmetic: Monday's figures imply a Friday settle of $98.32 ($92.57 + $5.75), so Tuesday's early $91.74 is $6.58, or about 6.7%, lower. A cheaper barrel helps those budgets only after it has travelled through refineries, pumps and invoices, which takes weeks, not a weekend interview. For you: watch the negotiators' documents, not the interviews about them, and the CPI at 14:30 CET. Nothing here is investment advice.
