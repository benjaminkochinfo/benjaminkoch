SoftBank Group reports its second-quarter results at 06:30 GMT, 07:30 in Berlin, Reuters says, as relayed by Startup Fortune [3]. Its shares did not wait: they jumped 15% in Tokyo on Tuesday ahead of the release, AP reports, after falling to their lowest level in a year on Monday [1]. Reuters had the stock down 4.2% on Monday, 11% on Friday and 23% for the year [3].

The reason everyone watches is a single holding. On 1 October SoftBank paid the third and last $10 billion tranche of its follow-on investment in OpenAI, taking its cumulative investment to $64.6 billion for a stake of about 13% [2]. It funded that tranche with foreign-currency bonds, and says that all borrowings under its $40 billion bridge facility have been repaid, with no undrawn commitments left [2]. That is the tidy part of the ledger, and worth noting: a bridge that was meant to be temporary was, in fact, temporary.

The less tidy part is the rest of the balance sheet. At the end of June, net interest-bearing debt stood at 10.8 trillion yen, about $68 billion, up from 6.6 trillion yen a year earlier, Startup Fortune reports, and the cost of insuring the group's debt has reached a four-year high [3]. Analysts' forecasts for the quarter range, per Reuters, from a profit of 233 billion yen to a loss of 113 billion yen [3]. A range that wide tells you the result will depend on how holdings are marked, not on anything sold.

There is one sober voice inside the group worth hearing. Junichi Miyakawa, chief executive of the mobile arm SoftBank Corp., told investors that "the major developments in the US are not going to work particularly to our advantage", and that if conditions worsen sharply "we may launch in other countries first", Bloomberg reported [3]. His own unit's cloud and AI revenue still rose 37% in the half-year [3].

For you, the useful habit this morning is the old one: read the valuation note before the headline profit, and the debt table before either. A mark-to-market gain is an estimate signed by auditors; interest is an invoice. Nothing here is investment advice.
