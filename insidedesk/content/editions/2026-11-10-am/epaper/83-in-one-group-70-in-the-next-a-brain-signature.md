Researchers led by Kanhao Zhao and Yu Zhang of Lehigh University trained a model on resting brain scans of 129 people in Mexico, 71 with cocaine use disorder and 58 without; it told the groups apart with 83% accuracy [1]. On an independent group of 82 patients and 81 controls from the US and Mexico, accuracy fell to 70%, but the same connectivity differences reappeared [1]. In a small trial of 45 patients, 25 given active magnetic stimulation (rTMS) and 20 a sham, the signature predicted craving reductions only in the active group [1]. The study is in Nature Mental Health [1].

The drop from 83% to 70% is the honest number: that is what replication looks like. The samples were mostly male, the sites varied, and the design cannot say whether the pattern causes the disorder or follows from it [1]. A screening tool is a hope for later, not a test you can book [1].
