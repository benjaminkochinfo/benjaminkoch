The EU's 27 leaders meet in Dublin on Friday for their first joint meeting on artificial intelligence, POLITICO reports [1]. The speeches will be about speed. The paperwork is about slowness.

Under the AI Act, each member state had until August 2025 to appoint a national authority to enforce it. Six countries, Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Slovakia and Hungary, have not selected one, and ten more have only a provisional decision that still needs adopting, the Commission confirmed to POLITICO [1]. That makes 16 capitals short of the finish line.

The infrastructure side has slipped too. The deadline for consortia to express interest in seven EU-backed AI compute hubs, a €30 billion project, has been extended for the second time, "to give member states the necessary time to complete their national budgetary process" [1]. The initial November deadline had already moved to February [1].

Green MEP Sergey Lagodinsky has asked the Commission to start a formal procedure against the late capitals. "Stop blaming the EU for missing the race if you miss your own deadlines," he told POLITICO [1].

It is the right complaint. A law without an enforcer is a press release, and a compute plan without national budgets is a brochure. If Dublin produces only one result, the most useful would be sixteen names on sixteen office doors.
