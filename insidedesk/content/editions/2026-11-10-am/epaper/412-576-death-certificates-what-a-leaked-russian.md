More than 400,000 Russians have been killed fighting in Ukraine since February 2022, according to data the Guardian says was leaked from Russia's state statistics agency, Rosstat [1]. The material, obtained by the Belarusian opposition-linked hacker group Cyber Partisans and the Ukrainian collective Silent Crow, contains more than 11 million death certificates; 412,576 of them explicitly link the death to the war, through cause-of-death codes or written references to combat [1].

The caveats come first. The Guardian could not independently verify the whole dataset; Rosstat confirmed it had been hacked but denied that sensitive information was obtained [1]. The figure is broadly consistent with other estimates: British intelligence put Russian military deaths at about 500,000 in September, and the independent outlet Mediazona has identified nearly 286,000 by name [1].

"The data looks authentic, and it's very rich," said Janis Kluge of the German Institute for International and Security Affairs (SWP) [1]. Registration lags deaths by about six months, he said, and in several months of late 2024 and 2025 more than 15,000 deaths were recorded, about 500 a day [1].

The burden is uneven. In Buryatia, Tuva and Zabaykalsky Krai, war-related deaths were about a third of all deaths among men aged 18 to 49 from the invasion to mid-2024; in Moscow and St Petersburg about 10 percent [1]. A register is a dull institution. That is why it is hard to argue with.
