The Group of Seven foreign ministers opened their fourth in-person meeting of the year in Paris on Monday, NHK reports [1]. They reaffirmed their opposition to unilateral attempts to change the status quo by force or coercion in the East and South China Seas and the Taiwan Strait [1].

The economic line is the one to keep. Ministers agreed to cooperate in countering market-distorting policies and practices, arbitrary export restrictions and economic coercion [1]. They also agreed to work together against cybercrime such as cryptocurrency theft, and restated the commitment to North Korea's complete denuclearisation under UN Security Council resolutions [1].

None of this is new in wording. Its value is repetition: supply chains are planned on the assumption that rules hold, and statements like this are how governments tell firms they still intend to enforce them.

Ukraine took the rest of the oxygen. EU ministers pressed US Secretary of State Marco Rubio to make sure Europe is represented at the Abu Dhabi talks, the Guardian reports [2]. Germany's Johann Wadephul put it plainly: "Europe belongs at this table" [2]. Rubio said the peace process still has "ways to go" [2].
