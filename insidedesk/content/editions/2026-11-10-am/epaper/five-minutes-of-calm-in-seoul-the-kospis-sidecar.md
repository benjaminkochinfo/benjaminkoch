Seoul had the loudest morning in Asia. The Kospi surged more than 5% at one point, and the Korea Exchange activated its "sidecar", suspending programme-driven buying orders for five minutes to moderate volatility [1]. By 04:28 CET the index stood 4.46% higher at 5,242.49 [1].

The engine was chips. Samsung Electronics gained 4.6% and SK Hynix 5.6%, AP reports; Taiwan's Taiex rose 3.4% [2]. That came despite Nvidia falling 4.3% in New York on Monday [2].

The sidecar is worth a moment of appreciation. It does not stop anyone from buying a share. It simply makes the algorithms wait five minutes while humans catch up. Rules like that rarely make headlines, and they are a large part of why a 5% morning stays a morning rather than becoming a story. Nothing here is investment advice.
