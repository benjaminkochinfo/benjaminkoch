Chancellor Friedrich Merz receives Hungary's prime minister Péter Magyar at the Chancellery today: a one-to-one talk, then a working lunch with ministers from both sides on Europe, defence, economic cooperation and support for Ukraine, and a joint press conference planned for about 13:30 CET [1][2]. Magyar's Tisza party won April's election and ended 16 years of Fidesz government under Viktor Orbán; he has led a pro-European government since May [1].

The delegation brings the foreign, economy and energy, defence and interior ministers; Germany remains Hungary's main trading partner [3]. In October the EU released €4.2 billion of funds after rule-of-law reforms; a further €12.4 billion is pending while Brussels checks the remaining measures [3]. Analyst Daniel Hegedus of the Institute for European Politics told AFP that Magyar has largely restored Hungary's position, and cited the expulsion of ten Russian diplomatic staff [3].

The unfinished part is energy. Hegedus does not expect Hungary to meet the EU's end-2027 deadline to stop importing Russian gas and thinks it will ask for flexibility [3]. That is a forecast from one analyst, not a policy. What makes a change of government real is less the press conference than the slow paperwork: reforms checked, funds released, contracts replaced.
