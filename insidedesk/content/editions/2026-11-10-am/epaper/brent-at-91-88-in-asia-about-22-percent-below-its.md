Brent crude futures fell 70 cents, or 0.75 percent, to $91.88 a barrel by 03:46 CET on Tuesday, Reuters reported; US crude (WTI) lost 78 cents to $86.07 [1]. Both had been down about 1 percent earlier in the Asian session, with Brent quoted at $91.56 at one point [1][2]. This follows Monday's 5.85 percent fall to the lowest settlement since 31 August [1].

The trigger is a sentence, not a barrel. President Trump said he plans to hold off on new military strikes against Iran "at this point", after appeals from countries in the region, while keeping the naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz in place for now [1]. Brent has given up about 22 percent since the six-month high of $118 on 30 October [1].

Analysts quoted by Reuters are careful. "Despite the recent selloff, prices are unlikely to fall below $90 per barrel for Brent without more durable signs of de-escalation and a US-Iran deal," Gregory Brew of Eurasia Group wrote [1]. Nick Twidale of ATFX Global warned that "markets have moved too far, too quickly" if talks falter [2].

The quieter number sits on the supply side: a Reuters survey found OPEC output rose in October, led by Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq [1]. For you, the pump and the heating-oil tank follow with a lag and with taxes on top; a lower barrel helps, slowly. Nothing here is investment advice.
