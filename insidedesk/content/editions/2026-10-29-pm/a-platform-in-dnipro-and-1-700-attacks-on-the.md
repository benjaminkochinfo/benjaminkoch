At least 14 people were killed when a Russian jet-powered drone hit a crowded platform at the main railway station in Dnipro on Thursday, local officials said, according to the BBC [1]. Dnipropetrovsk regional head Oleksandr Hanzha said at least 14 others were wounded, including an 11-year-old boy [1]; six were in critical condition [2]. Ukrzaliznytsia, the state railway, said a passenger train bound for Zaporizhzhia was standing at one of the platforms [1]. Russia's military had not commented; Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians and says its strikes on railways aim at infrastructure used to move troops and equipment [1].

An elevated threat level had been announced in Dnipro before the strike, said the railway's head, Oleksandr Pertsovsky. "But unfortunately there were still a large number of people on the platform," he said [1]. That sentence is the whole problem of a working railway in wartime: trains run because people need them, and people wait on platforms because that is where trains stop.

The railway keeps a ledger. Of 521 locomotives hit since the start of the full-scale invasion, almost 60% were hit this year, Ukrzaliznytsia told AFP, and attacks on railway facilities rose from 1,206 in 2025 to 1,700 so far this year, the BBC reports [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 300 locomotives this year, and the 2026 count of facility attacks is already about 40% above last year's full total.

The same day brought other strikes: two 15-year-old girls were killed in a missile attack on central Chernihiv, regional head Vyacheslav Chaus said [1], and one man was killed in the Rivne region [2]. Overnight Moscow's mayor said 613 Ukrainian drones had been launched towards the capital region, most shot down, while it hosted the Russia–Africa summit [1].

Zelensky called for further US, European and G7 sanctions [1]. For you, the useful thing to follow is not the rhetoric but the railway's counts: they are compiled by the people who repair the track, and they tell you whether protection of stations, trains and passengers is keeping up.
