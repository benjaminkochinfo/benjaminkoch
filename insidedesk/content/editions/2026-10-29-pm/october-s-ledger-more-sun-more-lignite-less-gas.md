Days make headlines; months make the bill. So here is the month. Between 1 and 28 October, German solar panels generated 4,742.6 gigawatt hours of electricity, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD figures, against 3,244.9 GWh on the same days last year [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 46% more, in a month when the sun is low and the days are short.

[[chart:de-october-mix-29pm]]

**Who stepped back.** Gas-fired plants generated 4,223.2 GWh over the same 28 days, against 4,852.7 GWh in 2025 [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 13% less. That fits the price of the fuel. The Dutch TTF benchmark settled at €97.35 per megawatt hour on Wednesday and is on course for a monthly rise of about 34%, Investing.com reports [2]. A gas plant that has to buy at those prices is often the last one anyone wants to run.

**Who stepped forward.** Lignite, Germany's brown coal, generated 6,081.3 GWh, against 5,111.5 GWh a year ago [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 19% more. Hard coal fell slightly, to 1,889.4 GWh from 1,993.2 GWh [1]. Wind was mixed: onshore turbines produced 11,231.5 GWh, about 7% less than last October, and offshore farms 2,924.6 GWh, about 14% more [1].

**The shares.** Grid load was almost the same in both years, 36,126.3 GWh this October against 36,365.3 GWh [1]. Desk arithmetic: wind and solar together covered about 52% of it, up from about 49%, while lignite, hard coal and gas covered about 34%, against about 33%. Both shares rose, which leaves less of the load for everything else: biomass, hydro, other plants and cross-border trade. The price was the other story: the day-ahead average was €157.82 per MWh over these days, against €84.62 a year ago [1].

**The calm point.** There is good news and a trade-off in one table. The good news is that the solar fleet now carries real weight even in October. The trade-off is that when gas is dear, it is lignite, the most carbon-heavy fuel in the mix, that fills the gap. Neither is a verdict; both are receipts, published every morning, and you can read them yourself. Nothing here is investment advice.
