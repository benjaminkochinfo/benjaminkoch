The third Russia–Africa summit ended in Moscow on Thursday with representatives of 48 African countries, 22 of them heads of state, according to The Moscow Times [2]. Opening the plenary in the Grand Kremlin Palace, Vladimir Putin said Russia's trade with African states "exceeded $27 billion last year" and had grown by 50% since the previous summit [1]. A day earlier he said it could pass $50 billion in the near future, which, as Vedomosti noted, means at least doubling it [3].

Now read the same numbers from the other side of the desk. The Moscow Times reports that the $27 billion was 2.4% less than in 2024, and that trade with the entire continent accounts for just 3.9% of Russia's foreign commerce [2]. Putin has promised a doubling since at least 2019 [2]. None of that makes the relationship small for the countries involved; it makes the target a speech, not a forecast.

The more interesting line was about factories. "Our companies are simply interested in moving certain production operations to African countries," Putin said, adding that this fits Russia's interests "when scores of sanctions are placed on the Russian economy" [2]. He named no company [2]. If it happens, it will show up in the dull places first: investment approvals, customs codes and certificates of origin in the host countries.

The paperwork that did appear is real enough. Presidential adviser Anton Kobyakov said more than 60 agreements were signed, not counting documents classed as commercial secrets, and that some 1,200 business representatives from about 630 companies took part [3]. In the transcript, Putin also put the rouble's share of trade settlements with African countries at 85% and Russian grain deliveries to Africa at more than 18 million tonnes in 2025 [1].

For you, the practical point is modest. Summits produce targets; customs offices produce statistics. The next set of annual trade figures will say whether 60 signatures became shipments. Nothing here is investment advice.
