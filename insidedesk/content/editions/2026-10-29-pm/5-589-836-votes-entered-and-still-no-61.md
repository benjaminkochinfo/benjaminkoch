Israel's Central Elections Committee said on Thursday that the counting of the double envelopes, the ballots of soldiers, diplomats, patients and prisoners, had been completed, with final results to follow [1]. By then results from 12,644 polling stations had been entered, containing 5,589,836 votes: 5,564,764 valid and 25,072 invalid [1]. Desk arithmetic: fewer than one ballot in 220 was spoiled.

The seats did not move. With 97% counted, Haaretz reported Netanyahu's bloc at 53, the Eisenkot-led bloc at 48 and the parties representing Palestinian citizens of Israel at 19, a record, according to the Guardian [2]. Neither camp reaches the 61 needed for a majority on its own [2]. The opposition faces a plain choice: an arrangement relying on the Arab parties, which Eisenkot's bloc has ruled out, or a unity government with Netanyahu, which Eisenkot vowed never to join [2]. The Joint List has offered tactical support to an Eisenkot minority government without joining it [2].

What you can actually hold on to is the timetable. The committee has asked the Shin Bet to look into alleged foreign interference and wants findings within a week, ahead of the 4 November deadline for certifying results [2]. President Isaac Herzog said that once official results are in he will meet representatives of all political groups, and asked them to "keep an open mind" [2]. The new Knesset is due to be sworn in on 10 November, and Eisenkot warned against using the interference allegations to delay it [2].

Each of those steps belongs to an institution, not a candidate: a committee that counts, a president who consults, a parliament that convenes on a fixed date. Coalition talk before 4 November is bargaining. The certified result is the only document that binds.
