On Thursday, Likud's claim that parties representing Palestinian citizens of Israel had "stolen" the election met a series of institutional replies. Likud says it has evidence of bribery and intimidation at polling stations but has not presented it to the public; the government's claims centre on Qatari money, for which there is so far no evidence, the Guardian reports [1].

The acting director general of the Central Elections Committee, Dean Livne, said there was "no basis for claims of systematic violations of election integrity" [1]. Police dismissed the claims too; one source quoted in Hebrew-language media said: "Election day passed with very few irregular incidents" [1]. Qatar's foreign ministry spokesman, Majed al-Ansari, called the accusations "ridiculous and dangerous in equal measure" [1]. Mansour Abbas of Ra'am said his party had "no connection whatsoever" and would cooperate fully with investigators [1].

President Isaac Herzog asked for the allegations to be "thoroughly investigated", and in the same breath urged leaders to avoid "tarnishing an entire community that sought to exercise its right to vote" [1]. The committee has asked the Shin Bet for findings within a week [1].
