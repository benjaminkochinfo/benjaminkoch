US real GDP grew at an annual rate of 3.4% in the third quarter, up from 2.2% in the second, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said at 13:30 CET [1]. Investment, consumer spending and government spending did the lifting; exports fell [1]. Sales to private domestic buyers rose 4.9% [1].

The month underneath is quieter. In September, headline PCE inflation rose to 3.6% from 3.4%, and inflation-adjusted consumer spending was virtually unchanged, ActionForex reports from the same day's income and spending data [2]. Real disposable income fell 0.3% [2]. Desk note: a strong quarter can end on a flat month, and the households are the ones paying for the fuel. The next GDP estimate is due on 25 November [1].
