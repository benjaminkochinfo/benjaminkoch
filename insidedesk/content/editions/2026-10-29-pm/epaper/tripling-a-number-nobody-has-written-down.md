Last year's COP30 agreed to "at least triple" finance for climate adaptation in developing countries by 2035 [1]. It did not say tripled from what, to how much, paid by whom, or delivered how [1]. A promise without a baseline is a speech. A baseline is an invoice.

That is what the African Group of Negotiators is now asking for. In a letter to UN climate chief Simon Stiell, Ghana, which chairs the group this year, requested three extra agenda items for COP31, which opens in Antalya on 9 November: tripling adaptation finance, trade and climate change, and the financing arrangements of the Green Climate Fund [1]. The group's chair, Antwi-Boasiako Amoah, wrote that "a dedicated item is needed", noting that June's talks in Bonn ended without agreement on where to take the goal [1].

The least-developed countries want the goal defined as $120 billion a year by 2030, which would triple an earlier target of around $40 billion that was meant to be reached in 2025 and is likely to have been missed [1]. The EU's agreed position reaffirms tripling but places it inside the broader finance goal agreed at COP29 [1]. The UK told the Green Climate Fund in May it would deliver only half of its £1.62bn pledge for the current period [1].

Any government can object to extra agenda items, and disputed ones usually go to consultations run by the presidency [1]. That sounds like procedure. It is. Procedure is where a slogan becomes a number someone can be held to.
