Brent slipped to $114.10 a barrel at 07:48 UAE time on Thursday, 04:48 in Berlin, after settling at $115.32 on Wednesday, up 1.3% [1]. West Texas Intermediate traded above $101 after a Wednesday close of $102.78 [1]. The trigger was the reported letter from Vice President JD Vance to Mohsen Rezaei; President Trump played it down, saying it concerned "medical supplies and various things" [1].

The headline moved the price by cents. The more durable argument came from a bank research note. UBS expects Brent to stay near $110 at the end of the year [1]. "Global inventories have fallen sharply, with pledged strategic oil reserves releases largely completed," wrote its commodity analyst Giovanni Staunovo, adding that "record freight costs, constrained tanker capacity, and recovering Chinese imports" were other factors likely to keep prices high [1]. UBS also says the uncertainty around its forecast "remains elevated" [1].

That is the useful split for you. Diplomacy changes the mood of a trading session; inventories, freight rates and tanker availability change what a refinery pays and, later, what a filling station charges. Emirates NBD's Nick Stadtmiller put the market's state plainly: prices "remain volatile" [1]. The Wall Street Journal reported that Washington has told several Gulf states to prepare for a possible renewed US campaign after the 3 November midterms, according to The National [1].
