The local ceasefire in Oleshky, a town under Russian occupation, had been in effect for 54 hours by Thursday morning, Ukraine's human rights commissioner Dmytro Lubinets said, with "no ceasefire violations" recorded [1]. It began at 03:00 Kyiv time on 27 October, 02:00 in Berlin [1]. Routes for the humanitarian mission are being cleared of mines, and an aid shipment is planned for Friday, he said [1]. The Kremlin said it hoped aid would arrive on Thursday or Friday, Interfax reported [2].

Lubinets credited his contact with his Russian counterpart, Yana Lantratova [1]. The arrangement is small, one town and six days, on the same day a drone killed passengers in Dnipro. But it rests on two ombudsmen talking, a cleared road and a dated convoy: the plain tools by which bigger agreements are later tested.
