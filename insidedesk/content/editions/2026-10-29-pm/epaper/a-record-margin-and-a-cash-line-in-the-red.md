Amazon reported after the New York close, and the podium line was easy to find: operating income of $28.1 billion and an operating margin of 13.9%, which the company calls its highest ever [1]. AWS sales grew 39% to $46.0 billion, the fastest in nearly five years by Amazon's own account [1].

The print sits further down. In the quarter Amazon spent $77.3 billion buying property and equipment, against $35.1 billion a year earlier, while operations brought in $42.4 billion [1]. Over twelve months free cash flow, as Amazon defines it, was an outflow of $42.4 billion, against an inflow of $14.8 billion a year before; the company says the rise in spending "primarily reflects investments in artificial intelligence" [1]. The same statement shows $48.1 billion of new long-term borrowing in the quarter [1]. It still expects about $220 billion of cash capital spending for 2026 [1].

Total sales of $201.9 billion and earnings of $1.89 a share were a little below analysts' estimates, and the shares fell more than 7% in extended trading [2]. Andy Jassy offered the long view, that Amazon "may have two" trillion-dollar revenue businesses in five to ten years [1]. The dull test arrives quarter by quarter: whether the cash line turns back up. This is reporting, not investment advice.
