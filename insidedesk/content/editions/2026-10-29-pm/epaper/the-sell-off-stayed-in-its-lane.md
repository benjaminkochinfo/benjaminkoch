Alphabet fell more than 13% in early US trading on Thursday after its results showed disappointing search revenue and a sharp rise in cash burn on AI, Reuters reports, and the Nasdaq opened 1.3% lower [2]. That was the loud part. The quiet part is what did not happen.

**No rush for the exits.** In a flight to safety, money leaves shares for government bonds and gold. ActionForex's cross-asset read found the opposite [1]. S&P 500 futures fell less sharply than Nasdaq futures; the DAX and the CAC 40 both gained more than 1%; Treasury yields were broadly steady; and gold weakened by about 1%, with silver, platinum and bitcoin also lower [1]. In mid-October, it notes, AI and credit worries coincided with a substantial Treasury rally; this time they did not [1].

**Sorting, not fleeing.** Microsoft's stronger revenue and Azure growth were a counterpoint to Alphabet, ActionForex says, which suggests investors are "becoming more discriminating about AI investments" [1]. "The bulk of the risks in portfolios are driven by the AI infrastructure cycle," SEB Investment Management's Carl Hammer told Reuters, while keeping a constructive view on equities [2].

**The calm point.** A market that punishes one balance sheet and rewards another is doing its job. The test of the spending, for every company in the race, will be in the cash-flow lines of filings, not in the keynotes. Intel and Amazon report after the US close tonight [2].
