More than 60 agreements were signed at the Russia–Africa summit and economic forum in Moscow, presidential adviser Anton Kobyakov told the closing press conference, "without counting documents that constitute a commercial secret" [1]. He put attendance at more than 3,500 people from 74 countries, with over 1,200 business representatives from about 630 companies [1]. One concrete example: Russia and Uganda signed a mutual visa waiver for holders of diplomatic and service passports [1].

Putin, for his part, said 47 African countries qualify for the Eurasian Economic Union's tariff preferences, and that duties have been cut to zero for 32 of them [2]. Those are the numbers worth checking next year, in customs data rather than communiqués. A visa waiver for diplomats is small; it is also the kind of agreement that actually gets used.
