Cognizant, one of the large firms that write and run software for banks, insurers and hospitals, reported on Thursday. Its chief executive, Ravi Kumar S, said "over 50% of our code" is now AI-assisted [1]. If you have heard that AI will empty the offices of such firms, the next page of the release is worth reading.

Total headcount at the end of September was 359,900: 3,200 more than in June and 10,100 more than a year earlier [1]. Revenue rose 4.6% to $5.66 billion [1]. Voluntary attrition in tech services was 12.6%, the same as a year earlier [1].

The payroll is changing shape rather than shrinking. Under a programme called Project Leap, Cognizant now expects $150 million to $200 million of severance and other personnel costs, part of total charges of $180 million to $250 million running into early 2027 [1]. In the third quarter it booked $46 million of employee separation costs [1]. At the same time it says it plans to hire 1,500 US college graduates [1].

The clearest number is a small one. A seven-person Cognizant team used an AI software engineer to convert an old logistics system for a client; the company reports that delivery throughput rose by roughly a third and the client recorded a 37% net cost saving against the conventional approach [1]. That is a case study written by the seller, so treat it as a claim, not an audit.

Desk arithmetic: severance at the top of the range would be about 3.5% of one quarter's revenue. The machinery here is not dramatic. It is contracts, separation costs, graduate intakes and an attrition rate that did not move. Nothing here is investment advice.
