In a forsa survey for Germany's federation of consumer organisations, 87% of 14- to 25-year-olds said they can easily recognise advertising on social media. Yet 40% said they had bought a food product at least once because an influencer recommended it [1]. The survey of 547 young people ran from 4 to 12 October [1].

The vzbv's reading is the psychologically interesting part: influencer marketing works precisely because products are woven into personal stories and daily routines, and advertising shapes purchases even when it is recognised as advertising [1]. A label tells you what something is; it does not stop it working on you.

The group wants the federal government to ban influencer ads for food that fails the World Health Organization's nutrient criteria for children when the audience is mostly young; 57% of those surveyed agreed influencers should not advertise unhealthy food [1]. Board member Ramona Pop said politicians "must protect young people better" (our translation) [1].
