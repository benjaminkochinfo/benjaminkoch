The day-ahead auction held on Thursday has set Germany's wholesale power prices for Friday 30 October, and SMARD, the Bundesnetzagentur's data portal, has published all 24 hours [1]. Desk arithmetic: they average €268.62 per megawatt hour, about €46 or 15% below Thursday's €314.85, which was the dearest day of 2026 so far [1].

**The timetable.** Friday's cheapest hour is 12:00 to 13:00 CET, at €215.00 [1]. The night hours run between €229.95 and €240.76 [1]. The dearest hour is 17:00 to 18:00 CET, at €345.93, against €559.63 in the same hour on Thursday [1]. Desk arithmetic: Friday's evening peak is about 60% above its midday low; on Thursday the peak was about 2.4 times the night-time low of €231.00 [1].

**What it means for you.** Wholesale €345.93 per MWh is about 35 cents per kilowatt hour before network charges, taxes and levies (desk arithmetic). On a dynamic tariff, Friday lunchtime is the cheap window, and the hour after work is the one to avoid. On a fixed tariff, your supplier bought most of this power months ago, so a single auction reaches you slowly and averaged out.

**The calm point.** An auction price is a timetable, not a verdict on the grid. It falls when the wind or the sun returns and rises when they leave, and it is published a day in advance precisely so that people, and their dishwashers, can plan. Nothing here is investment advice.
