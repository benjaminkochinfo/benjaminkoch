When Microsoft says rented capacity arrived late, the renters are companies such as CoreWeave, IREN and Nebius, which CNBC names among the firms Microsoft relies on [1]. Their shares fell after Wednesday's reports, as did Oracle's [1].

The issue is the financing, not the demand. These "neoclouds" need billions in outside capital but do not have investment-grade credit ratings, and with interest rates rising and concerns about their debt mounting, lenders are pulling back, CNBC reports [1]. Gartner analysts told CNBC they expect that by 2030, 80% of neocloud companies will either be absorbed or cease operations [1]. The Australian neocloud Firmus withdrew its plans to go public earlier this month [1].

The lesson is an old one. Capacity is built on credit, and credit has a price. Nothing here is investment advice.
