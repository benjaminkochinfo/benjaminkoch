**The podium.** The European Central Bank left its three key rates unchanged on Thursday: 2.50% on the deposit facility, 2.65% on main refinancing operations and 2.90% on the marginal lending facility [2]. Opening her press conference, Christine Lagarde gave the reason in one sentence: "While the full effects of the energy shock are still unfolding, underlying inflation has remained broadly stable so far and wages do not show a material response at this stage" [1]. Asked about it, she added that the Governing Council had "debated whether a hike was appropriate" before deciding unanimously to hold [4].

**The print.** Eurostat keeps the ledger. Euro-area inflation was 3.8% in September, from 3.2% in August [3]. Energy prices were 18.9% higher than a year earlier [3]. Everything else in the basket, all items excluding energy, rose 2.3%, against 2.1% in August and 2.5% in September 2025 [3]. Services, the part most tied to wages, were at 3.2%, against 3.0% in August and 3.2% a year before [3]. Lagarde's own statement cites compensation per employee up 3.3% in the second quarter, down from 3.6% in the first [1].

[[chart:ea-hicp-energy-dfr-29pm]]

**The gap.** Small, and honest about its edges. Desk arithmetic: in 2026 the ex-energy rate has moved only between 2.1% and 2.4%, while the headline went from 1.7% in January to 3.8% [3]. That is what "broadly stable" looks like on paper. The words "so far" carry real weight, though. September's ex-energy figure ticked up two tenths, and the statement itself says "the early stages of the pricing chain point to upward pressures" [1]. A stable line can still be a line that has started to lean.

**The verdict.** Podium and print agree today. For you, the useful reading is the dull one: fuel and heating are doing the rising, food prices are up only 1.3% [1], and services at 3.2% are the line to keep an eye on, because that is where wages would show first [3]. The rate stays where September's rise put it [5], and the next test is not a speech but tomorrow's German flash figures [6]. Nothing here is investment advice.
