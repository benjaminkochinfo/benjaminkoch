This morning's piece called Tuesday's €155 the end of the storm discount. The midday auction has since priced Wednesday, and the answer is steeper. The German-Luxembourg day-ahead price for delivery on Wednesday 28 October averages €249.21 per megawatt hour, according to SMARD, the Bundesnetzagentur's data portal [1]. That is the highest daily average since 14 September, when it reached €256.55 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Wednesday costs about 61% more than Tuesday's €155.11 and a little over twice Monday's €116.57.

**No cheap hour left.** On Tuesday the midday hour, 12:00 to 13:00 CET, cleared at €43.50 [1]. On Wednesday the cheapest hour, 13:00 to 14:00 CET, clears at €189.91, and the dearest, 18:00 to 19:00 CET, at €343.09 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the evening peak is now about 1.8 times the cheapest hour, against six times on Tuesday. The spread narrowed because the floor rose, not because the evening got cheaper.

**Who came back.** The storm weekend showed what the system does when wind is plentiful: it switches off its oldest fuel. Lignite plants generated 54,346 megawatt hours on Saturday 24 October, the lowest day since 14 June, and 62,470 MWh on Sunday [1]. By Monday they were back at 162,086 MWh [1]. Desk arithmetic: lignite output roughly tripled in two days.

[[chart:de-lignite-27pm]]

**The month in brown coal.** The weekend dip hides the longer line. From 1 to 26 October lignite plants generated about 5,592 gigawatt hours, against about 4,932 GWh on the same days of 2025 [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 13% more this October. A dear gas market makes the old coal blocks competitive again, and the carbon bill follows.

None of this is a failure of the market. A price of €343 at 18:00 is a loud, public signal to anyone with a battery, a flexible factory shift or an interconnector to sell into. The answer to a still, dark evening is not a speech. It is storage that charged when Sunday's wind was nearly free. Nothing here is investment advice.
