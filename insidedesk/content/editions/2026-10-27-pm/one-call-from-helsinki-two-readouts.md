Alexander Stubb and Jonas Gahr Støre spoke to Vladimir Putin by telephone from Helsinki on Tuesday [1]. Norway's government said the call followed the two leaders' meeting in Oslo on 20 October, was held "in consultation with Ukraine" and was meant to convey messages directly to Putin about the coming negotiations in Abu Dhabi [1]. The Kremlin said the call came at the Finnish and Norwegian request [2].

**Where the accounts agree.** Both sides tie the call to Abu Dhabi. The Kremlin wrote that the three "welcomed the agreement to resume trilateral talks" between Russia, Ukraine and the United States there in early to mid-November [2]. Both mention security in the Baltic Sea and the north; the Kremlin's version adds that the two Nordic leaders stressed "international maritime law and the freedom of navigation" [2]. And the Kremlin records that the three agreed to keep in direct telephone contact [2].

**Where they do not.** The Nordic text stresses what it was not: Stubb and Støre "did not speak on the behalf of Europe as a whole," and they repeated their support for Ukraine [1]. "Ending the war requires direct discussions," Stubb said; Støre spoke of "enormous human and material costs" and of de-escalation [1]. The Kremlin text instead has Putin restating the war's "root causes" and saying a lasting peace requires Russia's interests to be considered "in full and without any conditions attached," while blaming EU and NATO policy for prolonging the fighting [2].

The Kremlin also used the call to announce something concrete: a US-mediated humanitarian operation under way near Oleshky, with a temporary ceasefire in the Kherson sector [2]. Reuters reported that Ukraine's human rights commissioner confirmed Kyiv was honouring the halt, even as strikes continued elsewhere, including on Kyiv overnight [3].

Two neighbours with long borders opened a channel without claiming a mandate they do not have, and agreed to keep it open. That is modest. Modest channels are how the larger ones get built.
