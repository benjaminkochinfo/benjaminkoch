This morning's tech note was about a chip maker standing behind the value of its own hardware. By mid-morning there was a second variation on the theme. Nvidia said on Tuesday it will invest $3 billion in Meta's equity offering, and AMD announced $1 billion, CNBC reports [1].

**The offering.** On Monday Meta said it wants to raise $95 billion for AI infrastructure: $35 billion first, through underwritten public share offerings, then another $60 billion through an at-the-market programme [1]. Meta's announcement said Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom were interested in buying up to $5 billion of the shares [1]. Desk arithmetic: the two confirmed stakes come to $4 billion, about 4% of the total Meta wants to raise, and about 11% of the first $35 billion.

**What comes with the cheque.** Nvidia said Meta would expand its deployment of Vera Rubin systems and Vera processors, and that the two would deepen work on Muse, Meta's AI agent [1]. AMD said its Helios racks were running at Meta and its EPYC processors powered Muse [1]. So the money and the orders travel together. That is not improper; it is common in industries where supplier and customer need each other for years. It does mean you should read a supplier's sales figures with its investment portfolio open beside them.

**A habit, not a one-off.** Nvidia has invested in OpenAI, Anthropic, Intel and xAI, and has taken part in 121 investment rounds so far this year, up from 13 in 2022, according to Dealroom data cited by CNBC [1]. AMD has been in 25 rounds this year, against two in 2022 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Nvidia's count is more than nine times what it was four years ago.

The market's verdict on Tuesday was mild: AMD up 3%, Nvidia flat, Meta down 1% [1]. That calm is the right register. Vendor finance is old machinery, used for aircraft, ships and telephone networks. It works best when everyone can see who owes what to whom, which is why this week's earnings filings matter more than this week's press releases. Nothing here is investment advice.
