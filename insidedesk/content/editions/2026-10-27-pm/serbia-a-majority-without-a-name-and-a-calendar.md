Serbia's Republic Electoral Commission has put numbers on Sunday's upset. On preliminary results from 8,187 polling stations, the Student List won 2,267,615 votes, 52.71%, and 140 seats; Aleksandar Vučić's United Serbia list won 39.17% and 104 seats [1]. About 4.3 million people voted [1]. Turnout was 68.78%, the highest in more than 30 years, according to the commission's Sunday figures [2]. A majority needs 126 of the 250 seats [2].

Desk arithmetic: 140 seats is 14 more than a majority, so the winners need no coalition partner.

What the movement does not yet have is a name at the top. It ran deliberately without a hierarchy and named no candidate for prime minister [2][3]. Ilija Srdanović, the Novi Sad cardiologist who headed the list, is widely seen as the likeliest choice, but the ministries of interior, finance and justice are open [2]. One Belgrade analyst told the Guardian the movement's decision-making also needed to "be sped up", since choosing candidates "took a long time – months" [3].

The constitution will help with that. The new National Assembly must meet within 30 days of the final result; a government must be formed within 90 days after that [2][3]. Separately, because Vučić resigned as president on 27 September, Ana Brnabić, the assembly's president and acting head of state, must call a presidential election by 27 December [2].

The programme is institutional rather than rhetorical: an independent police unit answerable to prosecutors, a ban on appointing party loyalists to state companies, and independent scrutiny of large infrastructure contracts [3]. Those are dull promises, which is their virtue. They will be tested in public institutions staffed, as the Guardian puts it, with civil servants loyal to the outgoing regime [3]. A movement born of a collapsed railway canopy in Novi Sad now has to show that procedure, not personality, can carry a country.
