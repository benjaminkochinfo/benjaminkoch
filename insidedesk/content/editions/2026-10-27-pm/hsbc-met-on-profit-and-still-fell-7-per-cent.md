HSBC's shares fell nearly 7% on Tuesday and touched a six-month low, after third-quarter revenue missed estimates, partly because its wealth business grew more slowly [1]. Pretax profit was in line with expectations [2]. The bank index fell 1.1% and the FTSE 100 0.8% in the morning; the STOXX 600 was down 0.1% at 640.07 by 10:20 CET [1]. By early afternoon it had edged up 0.1% [2].

**Why a bank can earn and still disappoint.** "Banks have been the winners of the latest quarter before this one," said Ipek Ozkardeskaya of Swissquote Bank, but "short-term yields rising more than long term" is "eating into the income revenue" [2]. That is the dull machinery behind the headline: a bank borrows short and lends long, and when the short end climbs faster, the margin narrows. No scandal, just arithmetic.

**The rest of the ledger was better.** Novartis raised its full-year guidance for core operating income and rose 1.7% [1]. SIG Group, which makes packaging, jumped 9.4% after lifting its margin guidance [1]. BE Semiconductor rose 8.5% after Applied Materials said it had raised its stake in the Dutch maker of chip-packaging equipment to 20% [1]. "The earnings results, generally speaking, have been pretty solid," said Richard Flax of Moneyfarm [1].

**France, the quiet weight.** French consumer confidence fell five points to 80 in October, its lowest since June 2013, as student protests spread to unions [1]. French shares were flat after falling more than 2% last week [1].

For you, the lesson of the day is that one large company can move a headline and very little else. Results season is a long queue of invoices being added up: more than a third of S&P 500 companies report this week [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
