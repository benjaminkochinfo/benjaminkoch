The eighth summit of the Gas Exporting Countries Forum ended in Moscow on Tuesday with the adoption of a Moscow Declaration, according to the Kremlin's record of the plenary session [1]. Around the table sat, among others, Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian and Algeria's prime minister, Sifi Ghrieb [1].

**The host's figures.** Vladimir Putin told the session that forum members account for 70% of the world's natural gas reserves and around 40% of global exports [1]. He said global gas consumption grew by 1.2% last year to 4.2 trillion cubic metres, and cited a projection of 5.5 trillion cubic metres a year by the middle of the century [1].

**The host's asks.** He proposed expanding consultations within the forum on responding to crises in the gas market, including physical threats to infrastructure, and pointed to OPEC Plus as a model of cooperation on the oil side [1]. He argued that investment "must be underpinned primarily by long-term contracts" [1]. He also said prices "could fall just as sharply" as they have risen [1].

**How to read it.** A forum of sellers asking for long contracts and coordinated responses is doing its job: it speaks for sellers. Buyers have their own dull machinery: storage, tenders and contracts with more than one seller. A declaration sets a tone. The cargoes and the contract terms set the bill. Nothing here is investment advice.
