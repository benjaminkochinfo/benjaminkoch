The Turkish parliament's Foreign Affairs Committee on Tuesday approved the bill to ratify the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement between Türkiye, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan [1]. The pact was signed in Mecca on 7 August by President Erdoğan, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif; Saudi Arabia and Pakistan have completed their own ratification [1]. It treats an attack on one as an attack on all three [2].

Deputy Foreign Minister Levent Gümrükçü told the committee the agreement was "entirely defensive" and "envisions integration rather than bloc-building" [1]. It provides for regular consultations, joint training and exercises, defence-industry co-production and technology sharing [1]. The committee chairman, Fuat Oktay, answered the obvious question: was this an alternative to NATO? "Absolutely not," he said [1].

The timing gives the paperwork weight. Since the Houthis stepped up attacks on Saudi Arabia this month, neither Türkiye nor Pakistan has announced strikes on the group, and on Monday the Saudi defence minister, Pakistan's army chief and Türkiye's intelligence chief were in Tehran trying to calm things [2].

What you are watching, then, is a parliament doing the slow part. A treaty signed by leaders in August becomes binding only when legislators have read it, questioned a minister and voted. That is the order things should happen in, and it is happening in that order.
