When the ECB drew up its projections in September, its adverse scenario had oil rising to about $100 a barrel and gas to €75 per megawatt hour in the fourth quarter. Current spot prices are about $115 and €95, MUFG Research notes [1]. The bad case has become the base case.

**And yet: a pause.** MUFG expects the deposit rate to stay at 2.50% on Thursday, because sovereign yields rose sharply and financial conditions tightened [1]. "A pause here is reasonable," writes Henry Cook, who expects a hike in December, when new projections arrive, and another one after that [1]. Markets price about 20 basis points of tightening for December [1]. Economists polled by Reuters see a 70% chance of a December hike [2].

**Why wait, if energy is dearer?** Because the ECB reacts to energy only if it seeps into everything else. In October's purchasing managers' survey, manufacturing input costs accelerated sharply, but services output prices fell back, MUFG says [1]. Household inflation expectations for the medium and long term were unchanged [1].

Desk arithmetic: spot oil is 15% above the adverse scenario, gas about 27% above. For you, that gap shows up on the heating bill before it shows up in any rate decision. Nothing here is investment advice.
