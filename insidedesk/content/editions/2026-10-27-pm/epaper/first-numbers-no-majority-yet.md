Polls closed at 22:00 local, 21:00 in Berlin, and the first numbers did what this evening's lead said they would: arrive loudly and settle nothing. Channels 11, 12 and 13 all gave Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc 50 of the Knesset's 120 seats and the Jewish-led bloc opposing him 52 or 53 [1]. A government needs 61 [1].

Likud is projected to be the largest party with 24 or 25 seats, down from its current 32, ahead of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar on 21 to 23 [1]. Kan's poll has Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad third on 14 [2]. Arab-led parties are projected to win 17 or 18 seats, a record; they hold 10 today [1]. Turnout had reached 74.3% by 20:00 local, against 66.3% at that hour in 2022, the Central Elections Committee said [4].

Desk arithmetic: 52 or 53 plus 17 or 18 is 69 to 71 seats opposed to Netanyahu, which is why the Times of Israel counts a clear anti-Netanyahu majority of 70 [1]. But some Jewish-led opposition parties have ruled out governing with Arab parties, which may close that path [1]. Two pro-government channels showed Netanyahu's bloc doing better, at 60 and 55 seats [1].

Remember 2022, when the first exit polls under-counted the winning bloc and gave a party seats it never got. The committee publishes tallies through the night and the official result by 4 November [3]. Until then, read every claim of victory as a forecast. Nothing here is advice.
