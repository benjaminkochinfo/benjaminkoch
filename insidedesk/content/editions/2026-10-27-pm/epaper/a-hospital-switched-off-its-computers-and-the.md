On Monday, the University of New Mexico's health system in Albuquerque detected a network breach at around 11:30 local time (18:30 CET), according to a message to staff reported by the university's student newspaper [1]. About 40 minutes later, staff were asked to shut down and unplug computers and turn off Wi-Fi on mobile devices [1]. The system runs the state's largest teaching hospital and its only Level 1 trauma centre [1].

By 14:30 local time (21:30 CET) it was diverting ambulances to other hospitals and had cancelled all clinic appointments for the day [1]. A few hours later it said systems were coming back in phases; a final update that day said all its hospitals were open and clinics would resume Tuesday's appointments and procedures [1]. Tuesday's classes at the medical, nursing and pharmacy schools were cancelled [1].

An extortion group called BYOD has claimed the attack and says it stole student and employee data, including US social security numbers; those claims are unconfirmed [1]. Because it is an active law-enforcement investigation, the university said it is "limited in what we can share" [1].

Two details are the real news for patients. Ambulances had somewhere to go: the university thanked "the health systems in Albuquerque and Santa Fe" that took its patients [1]. And the hospitals were open again within a day [1]. Pulling the plug quickly, diverting cleanly and restoring in phases is not luck. It is a drill someone practised.
