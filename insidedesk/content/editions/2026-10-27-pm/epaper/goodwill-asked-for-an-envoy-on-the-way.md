Qatar's foreign ministry used its weekly briefing on Tuesday to put the burden of proof where clerks like it. "We have heard positive statements from the Iranian side," its spokesman Ibrahim bin Sultan Al Hashmi said, "but they still need to show goodwill", meaning an end to threats against neighbours and a reopened Strait of Hormuz [1]. Asked whether Qatar's delegation had carried a new proposal to Tehran, he said only that messages and ideas were still being exchanged, and announced no breakthrough [1].

Beijing added a name to the list of mediators: China said on Tuesday it will soon send an envoy to countries in the region to help avoid another round of escalation [2]. Regional sources told The National that Monday's four-way Tehran meeting produced a promise to keep talking, and that a second round, possibly in Turkey, has not been ruled out [3].

Against that, the ledger is unchanged. Tehran wants the blockade of its ports lifted and oil sanctions removed; Washington wants enrichment cut [1]. ANZ research puts crude flows through the strait at around 60 per cent of normal [1]. An envoy is a travel plan, not a term sheet. Watch for a date for the second round and for any change in that 60 per cent. Nothing here is advice.
