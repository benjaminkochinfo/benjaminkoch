Three US releases arrived on Tuesday and all three beat the consensus. Durable goods orders rose 1.8% in September, against an expected 1.7% [1]. New home sales were 676,000, down 0.6% from August but above the 653,000 economists polled by Dow Jones had pencilled in [1]. Consumer confidence rose to 91.7 in October, against an estimate of 81.0 [1].

**The companies said the same, mostly.** Coca-Cola and United Parcel Service, a bellwether for the economy, raised their forecasts, Reuters reports [2]. Hilton and JetBlue showed the other side: rising costs from the Middle East conflict [2]. More than a third of S&P 500 companies report this week [2]. That is the useful kind of noise: hundreds of firms adding up what they sold and what it cost.

**The bond market took note.** The ten-year Treasury yield rose to 5.003% and the two-year to 4.741% as the Fed's two-day meeting began [1]. Fed funds futures price about a 90% chance of a hold on Wednesday, according to the CME FedWatch Tool [1]. Good news on spending is, for a central bank fighting inflation, a reason not to hurry.

Desk arithmetic: consumer confidence came in 10.7 points above the estimate. Surveys can surprise; invoices settle the argument later. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, PCE, is due on Thursday [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
