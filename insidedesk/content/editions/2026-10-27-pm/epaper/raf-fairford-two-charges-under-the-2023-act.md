The Crown Prosecution Service said on Tuesday that two men, both 24 and from Westminster, London, have been charged under the National Security Act 2023 after a Counter Terrorism Policing investigation into an incident involving RAF Fairford [1]. Mohmed Abdulkarim is charged with conduct likely to materially assist a foreign intelligence service between 31 August and 28 September [1]. Nouman Essghir is charged with being in the vicinity of a prohibited place on 22 August for a purpose prejudicial to the UK [1]. Both were due at Westminster Magistrates' Court on Tuesday [1].

Counter Terrorism Policing said it believes the intelligence service involved is Iran's [2]. Its head, Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, said police had seen "a range of incidents in recent months" they believe were directed by the Iranian state, and asked the public to report anything that worries them [2]. A third man, a 25-year-old UK-Iranian dual national, was released without charge but remains on police bail, as do five other men [2].

The CPS was explicit about what a charge is and is not. Its decision is "not in any sense a finding of, or implication of, any guilt", and it asked that nothing be reported or shared that could prejudice the proceedings [1]. So this desk stops here. The useful point is procedural: the 2023 Act's offences are now being put before a court, which is where its definitions will be tested.
