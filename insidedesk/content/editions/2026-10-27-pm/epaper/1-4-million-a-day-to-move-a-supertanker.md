Benchmark earnings for very large crude carriers from the Middle East to China are nearing $1.4 million a day, more than 12 times pre-war levels, according to Reuters [1]. Freight has eased from its peaks, but the cost still squeezes refining margins and pushes producers to offer discounts, Bloomberg reports [1].

The response is a change in contracts, not in speeches: Aramco and Adnoc have started selling some cargoes delivered to China and India, taking on shipping arrangements that buyers would normally handle [1]. Eleven supertankers were damaged this month alone, more than 30 since the war began [1]. A seller who pays for the voyage is a seller who wants to keep the customer. That is how trade survives a war: one contract clause at a time.
