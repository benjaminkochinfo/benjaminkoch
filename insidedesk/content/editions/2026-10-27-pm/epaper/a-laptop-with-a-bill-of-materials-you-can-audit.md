The defence company Anduril and the chip designer Qualcomm announced a computer called BlueChip on Tuesday, TechCrunch reports [1]. The first model is a hardened laptop for the military, government agencies, first responders and police; preorders open in 2027 [1].

**The selling point is the list.** About 90% of the components in the first generation come from the US or allied countries, including the processor, a Snapdragon X Series chip built at TSMC's new facility in Arizona [1]. "We have a technical roadmap to get to 100% domestic over time," said Anduril's founder, Palmer Luckey [1]. He named the display as one of several parts that, by his account, are still made only in China; he has found an American supplier for the cover glass [1].

**The honest price tag.** No price yet, but Luckey estimates about 20% above typical high-end PCs [1]. He was candid about the consumer case: an ordinary buyer without a particular need would be "better off buying a Dell laptop" [1]. Rugged computers are already about a $5 billion market, according to TechCrunch [1].

**Why it matters beyond the barracks.** Government buyers face rules that require them to buy US-made computers, and the pitch here is a supply chain the customer can track and audit [1]. That is a procurement story more than a gadget story. An auditable bill of materials is dull paperwork, and it is exactly what security buyers have lacked. Whether the last 10% can be made at home is a question of factories, not slogans. Nothing here is investment advice.
