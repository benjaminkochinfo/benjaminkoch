The US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has added a 30-day, all-cause readmission rate after sepsis to its Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program, the seventh condition on the list; it covers Medicare patients aged 65 and over [1]. Hospitals get confidential early-look reports for the 2028 and 2029 programme years; financial penalties start in fiscal 2030 [1]. Studies show that about a quarter of sepsis survivors are readmitted within 30 days, with a median of 11 days [1]. Because the measure counts every unplanned return, a return with heart failure or a drug side effect counts as much as a relapse [1]. The advice from clinicians is plain: plan discharge from day one, check the infection is actually cleared, and tell patients which symptoms mean "call" and which mean "come in" [1]. One nurse noted that patients' toothbrushes often stay in the wrapper; brushing helps prevent pneumonia [1]. A penalty is crude. A checklist with a toothbrush on it is not.
