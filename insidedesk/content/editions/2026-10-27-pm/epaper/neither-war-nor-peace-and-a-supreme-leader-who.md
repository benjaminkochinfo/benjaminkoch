Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei met Mohsen Rezaei, his top national security official, to discuss the Islamic Republic's conditions for ending the war with the United States, according to a post on the supreme leader's account cited by CNBC [1]. The current state of "neither war nor peace" needs to end, he said, and the two discussed "safeguarding national and regional security by maintaining control" over the Strait of Hormuz [1].

**Diplomacy, in layers.** On Monday Saudi Arabia's defence minister held direct talks with President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran, with senior military and intelligence officials from Pakistan and Turkey present [1]. Qatar's representatives travelled to Tehran on Sunday, Reuters reported [1]. Tehran described the talks as a step towards regional security cooperation, with further consultations possible, according to the state outlet Tasnim [1].

**The other half of the table.** President Donald Trump told Fox News on Sunday that he was approaching a decision on whether to strike a deal with Iran or intensify military operations [1]. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia continued over the weekend [1].

The market's verdict was modest: Brent futures fell 1.7% to $113.88 by 16:10 CET, a second day of losses [1]. Earlier in the day they had been down 1% at $114.74 [2]. A leader saying a war should end is not a treaty. But conditions are what treaties are made of, and someone has started listing them.
