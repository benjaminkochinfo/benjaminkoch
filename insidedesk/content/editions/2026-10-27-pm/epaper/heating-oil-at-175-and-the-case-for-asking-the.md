At 11:52 CET on Tuesday, 100 litres of heating oil cost €175.04 on the German average for a 3,000-litre order, up from €173.15 at Monday's close, according to the price service HeizOel24 [1]. That is about €1.75 a litre [1]. Desk arithmetic: a 3,000-litre tank at that average comes to about €5,251, some €57 more than a day earlier.

**Where it costs more.** Among the large cities, buyers in Cologne paid the most, €1.80 a litre, and buyers in Nuremberg the least, €1.71 [1]. Most Länder got dearer; Bremen and Hesse got slightly cheaper [1]. Desk arithmetic: nine cents a litre between those two cities is about €270 on a 3,000-litre order.

**Why the bill is sticky.** Transport keeps the bill high even when crude eases. The International Energy Agency reported that crude bound for Asia cost on average $93 a barrel at loading in October but $131 delivered, HeizOel24 notes, and such freight costs can keep diesel and heating oil dear in Europe even when Brent falls [1]. Austria paid €185.76 per 100 litres, Switzerland 178.46 francs [1].

**The dull tip that works.** If you only need a small amount, HeizOel24 suggests joining neighbours in a collective order [1]. One lorry, one trip, several tanks: the delivery cost is shared. Comparing three quotes on the same morning costs a few minutes. Nothing here is advice.
