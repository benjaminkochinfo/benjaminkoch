On Tuesday the federal law raising Germany's tobacco tax was published in the Federal Law Gazette. It sets out steps up in the tax for 2027 to 2030 [1]. Its final clause reads: "This law enters into force on the day after its promulgation" [2]. That day was Wednesday, 21 October 2026 [1]. Read literally, the higher rates applied from this morning, more than two months before anyone intended.

The finance ministry confirmed the error on Wednesday. Bild had reported it first [2]. A spokesman called it a technical mistake in the date of entry into force [2]. At the government press conference, the ministry added a detail that matters more than the apology: the error was already in its own draft bill, and it was not caught anywhere in the procedure, in government or in parliament [2][3]. Asked how many people had read the text, the ministry said the number of hands and eyes could not be counted on two hands [3].

**What the machinery did next.** The striking thing is not the slip. Long laws carry slips. It is the order in which the repair arrived. On the day of publication, the customs administration, which collects the tax, put a notice on its website. It called the clause an "obvious inaccuracy" and stated plainly what applies [1]. The rates in force on 19 October continue unchanged until 31 December 2026 [1]. The new tariff for cigarettes, fine-cut, heated tobacco and substitutes starts on 1 January 2027, and for cigars, cigarillos, pipe and water-pipe tobacco on 1 March 2027 [1]. The notice also explains why the law entered into force early at all: so that the new tax stamps can be designed and ordered in time [1].

That is a small document doing a large job. Anyone at a tobacco wholesaler, a kiosk or a tax office who wanted to know what to do on Wednesday morning did not need a minister. They needed a dated notice from the authority that sends the bills, and it was there.

**The legal argument.** At the press conference the finance ministry said the will of the legislator was clear from the law itself [3]. The explanatory memorandum names 1 January 2027 in 13 places, the ministry said, and the amended section 2 of the tobacco tax law begins each time with "for the period from 1 January 2027" [3]. The ministry said it is aiming to use the correction procedure under §61 of the joint rules of procedure of the federal ministries, so that the Bundestag does not have to vote again, because the error is obvious [3]. It also said it was working on instructing the administration not to collect the higher rates [3]. Details were not yet settled on Wednesday [3].

Asked who was responsible, government spokesman Stefan Kornelius pointed to the ministry that drafts the text; the chancellery coordinates, he said, and the federal president checks constitutionality [3].

**The industry's view.** The tobacco makers were relieved and cross. Maximilian van Ackeren of the VdR, a tobacco industry association, called it "dilettantism" [2]. Jan Mücke of the BVTE, another industry association, warned that the sector would spend weeks "in a grey zone" tolerated by the authorities and asked for legal certainty quickly [2]. Both points are fair. Tolerance is not the same as law, and a correction that takes weeks would leave honest firms technically on the wrong side of a statute.

**What is actually at stake.** Tagesschau notes that the reform could raise the price of a pack of 20 cigarettes by about four euros to €11.78 by 2030 [2]. The tax is expected to bring in about €1.5 billion extra next year, against €17.6 billion collected in 2025 [2]. None of that changes because of the slip. What changes is a few weeks of paperwork.

**A second correction, same room.** The same press conference produced a quieter repair. This desk reported this morning that the "more than €220 billion" announced at Tuesday's investment summit was a total for the year, not a summit haul. On Wednesday the economy ministry gave the exact figure, €228.6 billion. It covers large investment projects of €100 million or more announced by companies during 2026 [3]. Some were announced at the summit, but not all, and the ministry said it has no figure for the share that is new [3]. That is less impressive than the headline, and more useful: you now know the method.

**The argument.** Two numbers were wrong or loose on Tuesday, and both were made precise on Wednesday, on the record, in answer to plain questions. That is how institutions are supposed to work. A free press asks; a ministry answers; an agency publishes a notice that a shopkeeper can read. You will hear more today about embarrassment. The better story is the speed of the repair. Watch whether the correction is formally published, and how soon. That will be the receipt.
