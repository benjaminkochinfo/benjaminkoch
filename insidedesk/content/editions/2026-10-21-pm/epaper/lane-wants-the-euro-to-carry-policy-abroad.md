Speaking in Shanghai on Wednesday, ECB chief economist Philip Lane said greater international use of the euro "supports the ability of the ECB to achieve price stability" by extending the reach of its policy to foreign demand [1]. More euro invoicing would also make euro-area import prices less sensitive to outside shocks, he said [1]. "The monetary-policy case for liquidity lines is reinforced by their dynamic interaction with other policy areas," he added [1]. It is an argument for plumbing, made by an economist, which is the best kind.
