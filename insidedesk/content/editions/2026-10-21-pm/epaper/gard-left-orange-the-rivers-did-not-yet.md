After Var and Alpes-Maritimes, Gard came out of orange on Wednesday, Météo-France announced in its latest bulletin; no département was left in orange [1]. The forecaster called it a Mediterranean episode "qui aura été remarquable" [1]. It caused flooding, closed roads, evacuations and power cuts, and two people died, in Saint-Mitre-les-Remparts in Bouches-du-Rhône and in Haute-Corse [1].

The daily gauge records explain the order in which warnings came down. At Nîmes-Courbessac, 134.3 mm fell in the 48 gauge days from 1 September to 18 October, then 151.6 mm in the single gauge day to 08:00 on 20 October [2]. Mont Aigoual recorded 191.5 mm in that day and Montpellier airport 111.7 mm [2]. Desk arithmetic: at Nîmes, one day brought more rain than the seven weeks before it.

Water like that falls in hours and drains through a catchment over days. Rain warnings come down first; river warnings follow when the gauges allow. Wednesday's map is the system completing that sequence, step by step.
