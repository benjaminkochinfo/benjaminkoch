Sébastien Lecornu chaired a meeting at 10:30 on Wednesday with the main French unions, the CFDT, CGT, FO, CFE-CGC, CFTC, UNSA and FSU [1]. The ministers for public accounts, labour, education and the interior attended, together with the heads of the national police, the gendarmerie and the Paris police prefecture [1].

Leaving Matignon, CGT leader Sophie Binet said the government had "announced no measure" and had not taken the unions' demands into account [1]. She relayed the joint union call for a general strike on 5 November [1].

Two other dates are now on the calendar. Education minister Édouard Geffray told the National Assembly that a "first series of decisions" would be presented to schools, regions and pupils on Monday morning [1]. In the Senate, Lecornu said he was "lucid" about the "climate of social tension and anxiety" and refused "announcement effects" [1]. Speaking from Doha, President Emmanuel Macron said a state of emergency was not needed [1].

The useful reading: a meeting with no measures is not a failure if it produces a timetable. Monday's education decisions are the first thing you can actually check.
