UK public sector net borrowing was £24.5 billion in September, £1.8 billion more than a year earlier and £1.9 billion above the Office for Budget Responsibility's forecast, the Office for National Statistics said on Wednesday [1]. It was the second-highest September on record, behind 2020 [1].

Public sector net debt was provisionally estimated at £3,005.0 billion at the end of September, above £3 trillion for the first time [1]. As a share of the economy it was 94.0% of GDP, 0.7 points lower than a year earlier, because nominal GDP grew faster than the debt [1].

The detail worth reading is the interest. Central government debt interest was £10.9 billion, the highest September since records began in 1997 [1]. Of that, £4.0 billion was the inflation uplift on index-linked gilts, largely from a 0.6% rise in the Retail Prices Index between June and July [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is a little over a third of the month's interest bill. Inflation does not only reach households through the pump; it reaches the Treasury through the gilt register.

For the financial year so far, borrowing was £97.8 billion, £4.4 billion less than a year earlier but £6.0 billion above forecast [1]. The Budget is on 28 October, with new OBR forecasts [1]. Nothing here is advice.
