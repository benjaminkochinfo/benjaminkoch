The US State Department has ordered all non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia, CBS News reported on Wednesday evening [1]. The spokesperson, Tommy Pigott, gave the reason in one line: the Houthis have "increasingly engaged in hostilities against Saudi Arabia", including attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure [1]. The order follows Sunday's round of embassy alerts in Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, the UAE and several other countries, which warned that the fighting could spread without warning [1].

The same live page carries the quieter half of the evening. Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, shut on 10 October, saw a limited number of Saudia and flynas flights on Wednesday, while Lufthansa and Air France have cancelled flights through mid-November [1]. And the US Treasury sanctioned a Panamanian-Greek law firm and shipping agents in Singapore and the UAE over Iran's "shadow fleet" of tankers [1].

A warning is a paragraph; an ordered departure is a list of names, seats and leases being closed. It tells you more about how a government reads the risk than any speech this week did. Nothing here is advice.
