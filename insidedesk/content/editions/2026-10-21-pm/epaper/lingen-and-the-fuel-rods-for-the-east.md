At Wednesday's government press conference in Berlin, ZDF asked how warnings about Russian sabotage fit with a permission for Rosatom, Russia's state nuclear company, to take part in the fuel-element factory at Lingen [1]. The question followed a statement by Lower Saxony's environment ministry that the approval had been given [1].

The federal environment ministry said the decision was taken by the Land ministry. The plant is run within a multinational web of companies with links to a Russian firm, it said, and EU law permits manufacturing material for nuclear power supply as a business [1]. The security situation is reassessed regularly; the business activity is not in itself an automatic threat [1].

Government spokesman Stefan Kornelius added the economic logic. Fuel elements of this type made in Lingen mainly supply power plants in eastern Europe, he said, which could become more independent of deliveries from Russia [1]. The permission was granted by Lower Saxony in consultation with Berlin [1]. The federal government wants the sanctions regime extended to cases like this, Kornelius said, but there is no majority for that in the EU [1].

So the dull question is not who is in the boardroom. It is whose reactors stop needing Russian rods, and when.
