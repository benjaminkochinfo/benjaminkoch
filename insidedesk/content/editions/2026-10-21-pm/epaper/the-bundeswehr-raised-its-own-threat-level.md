An internal security analysis seen by WDR and SZ rates the threat of Russian sabotage against the Bundeswehr as "erheblich" (considerable) for the first time, the second-highest level on its scale [1]. For the first time, the armed forces assume they themselves could be a target of Russian attacks inside Germany [1].

The trigger was a farmer's chance find in mid-September: a crashed drone near the Wunstorf air base, carrying a can of high explosive [1]. It resembles the drone used in the failed attack at Leipzig/Halle airport in early August [1]. Technical analysis by the Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) reportedly found the Wunstorf drone had lain there for about two years and may have been aimed at military aircraft [1]. The paper says the threat can no longer be limited to particular sites [1]. Russia has always denied involvement in sabotage [1].

At the government press conference on Wednesday, a defence ministry spokesman confirmed the "considerable" rating [2]. He gave examples of the response: more staff on guard and patrol, and extra technical protection such as infrared cameras and fence monitoring [2].

The reassuring part is the boring part: a written threat scale, a rating that moved, and fences that get cameras.
