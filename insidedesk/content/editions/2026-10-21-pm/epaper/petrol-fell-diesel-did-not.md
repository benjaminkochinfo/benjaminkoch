A litre of Super E10 cost €2.110 on the national average on Wednesday, 4.1 cents less than a week earlier, according to the ADAC [1]. Diesel went the other way, up 0.8 cents to €2.255 [1].

The oil did not help either fuel. Brent reached its highest level since May during Tuesday's trading and closed at about $109 a barrel, roughly $3 more than a week before [1]. For diesel, the ADAC adds a seasonal factor: demand for heating oil, a close cousin, rises as the weather turns colder [1].

Petrol's fall despite dearer crude, the club argues, shows there was room for price cuts on E10, and it says most of the fuel-tax rebate now seems to be reaching the pump for petrol [1].

Desk arithmetic: diesel is now 14.5 cents a litre dearer than E10. On a 50-litre fill, E10 is €2.05 cheaper than a week ago and diesel €0.40 dearer.

The ADAC's standing observation is that prices are lowest shortly before 12:00 and jump at noon [1]. It is a pattern, not a promise. Nothing here is advice.
