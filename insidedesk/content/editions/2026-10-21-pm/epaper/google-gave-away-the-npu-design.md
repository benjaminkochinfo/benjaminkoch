Synaptics introduced its Astra SR200 series on 20 October, three AI microcontrollers rated at 200 to 400 GOPS and built around Google Research's open-source Coral NPU design [1]. They are aimed at wearables, battery-powered sensors, connected cameras and industrial vision [1].

TechRepublic's useful distinction is between what is announced and what is orderable. The SR200 chips remain in sampling, with no public price or firm production date [1]. What developers can buy now is a separate board, the Coralboard SL, which moved to full availability through DigiKey the same day and runs a 1-TOPS version of the Coral NPU on Synaptics' existing SL2619 processor [1].

The licensing is the quieter story. Google released the Coral NPU architecture under the Apache 2.0 licence, so chipmakers can build it into their processors without royalties [1]. The pitch is that a model optimised once could run across chips from different vendors; TechRepublic notes that this remains a goal rather than something shown across competing commercial silicon [1]. Synaptics' own speed figures for small language models have not yet been independently tested on shipping SR200 hardware [1].

A press release describes a chip. A distributor's stock line describes a product. This week Synaptics has one of each.
