Kenya had eight confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases, including one death, as of 20 October, the ECDC says [1]. The first was a Kenyan citizen living in the DRC who travelled home via Uganda on 3 October; the case was reported on 6 October, and the patient died [1].

Then the tracing shows up in the timeline. The second case, on 14 October, was a close family contact who was already in quarantine [1]. The third, on 16 October, was another close relative, also in quarantine [1]. On 17 October four more were confirmed: one relative and three health workers, all of whom had been in contact with the first case and had been placed in quarantine [1]. The eighth case, reported on 18 October, was a patient attendant, according to the health ministry's director-general [1]. The ECDC does not say whether that person was on the list.

Desk arithmetic: six of the seven cases after the first were found among people already in quarantine. That is what containment looks like from the inside: new cases, but on a list someone wrote in advance. UN News, citing WHO on Wednesday, gives nine Kenyan cases linked to the DRC [2]; the two counts are a day apart and may simply differ in timing.
