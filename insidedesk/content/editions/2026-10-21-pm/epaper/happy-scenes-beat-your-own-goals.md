Psychologists at Hull and York St John gave 187 students one of four seven-minute tasks [1]. In those with more depressive symptoms, imagining positive future scenes raised their sense of control over their goals; only the general happy scenes kept the effect ten minutes later [1]. Imagining their own goal successes left some ratings lower after ten minutes [1]. One session, students not patients, not preregistered [1]. A small, honest result, and labelled as such.
