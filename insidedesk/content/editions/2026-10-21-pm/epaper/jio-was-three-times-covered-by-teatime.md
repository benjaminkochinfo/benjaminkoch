The initial public offering of Jio Platforms was subscribed 3.02 times by 5:24 pm Indian time on its first day, with bids for 59.55 crore shares against 19.75 crore on offer, according to BSE data reported by Moneycontrol [1]. (A crore is ten million.) The issue, India's largest at about Rs 30,200 crore, has a price band of Rs 1,065 to 1,119 a share and closes on 23 October; listing is slated for 28 October [1].

The split matters more than the total. Qualified institutional buyers subscribed their portion 9.02 times; non-institutional investors 1.59 times; retail investors only 0.69 times [1]. Before the issue opened, Jio raised Rs 8,109.84 crore from 212 anchor investors, including BlackRock, Fidelity, GIC and Norway's sovereign fund [1].

Grey-market trading implied a premium of about 22–23% over the top of the band on Wednesday morning [1]. That is an unofficial number from an unregulated market, and it is worth exactly what an unsigned quote is worth. The signed numbers are the order book, and they say the professionals came first.
