Brent crude futures rose $2.06, or 1.9%, to $110.85 a barrel by 10:48 GMT on Wednesday, and US crude rose $2.13 to $99.95, Reuters reported [1]. On Tuesday Brent had touched its highest level in five months before falling back on talk of mediation between Iran and Pakistan [1].

Wednesday's move came from words. Foreign minister Abbas Araqchi told the Financial Times that Iran was fully prepared to defend itself, and President Masoud Pezeshkian told state media that the United States could not expect dialogue "while blockading and threatening Iran" [1].

The more durable information is in the shipping counts. Kpler data showed at least 18 vessel attacks over the past two weeks, and LSEG analysts said Iran was targeting tankers well beyond the Strait of Hormuz, into the Gulf of Oman [1]. On the other side of the ledger, industry figures from the American Petroleum Institute showed US crude stocks down 4.9 million barrels in the week to 16 October, with gasoline and distillates also lower [1].

Speeches move the price for an afternoon. Insurance premiums, tanker routes and inventories move it for a season. The official US inventory count from the Energy Information Administration follows the industry estimate; that is the one to wait for.
