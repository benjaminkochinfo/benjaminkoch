**The podium, in its own words.** On Saturday Abbas Araghchi said Tehran had sent Washington, through Qatar, "a specific seven-day plan": if Iran's conditions are met, the strait can reopen and normal traffic resume within seven days. "The choice is now America's," he said [1]. He also said US military action had prevented implementation of the June memorandum of understanding [1]. An Iranian lawmaker told local media that indirect talks were continuing through Qatar and Oman, and that both sides had exchanged conditions [3].

**The print.** The market has not been waiting. Gulf producers have pushed tanker transfers as far as India's coast: Vortexa counted 82 ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Kutch last month, equivalent to about 680,000 barrels a day, roughly two-thirds of it refined products [2]. The IEA says exports out of the Middle East have recovered to pre-crisis levels through this makeshift network, even as it buckles under congestion and attacks [2].

**The gap.** The plan has a date in it, which is more than most speeches offer, and dates bind in a way adjectives do not. But its conditions were not published, and a deadline whose terms you cannot read is only half a sentence. The detour, by contrast, is fully legible: it has a location, a count and a volume.

**Verdict.** Not a contradiction, a sequence. The podium describes a door; the print describes people already climbing through a window. If the door opens, the window gets quieter and cheaper. Until then, the window is the supply chain, and it is more expensive than a door.
