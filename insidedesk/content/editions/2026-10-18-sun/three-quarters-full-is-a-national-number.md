EU gas storage was 74.3% full on 16 October, about 842 TWh, according to GIE's AGSI+ data as compiled by GasRadar [1]. GIE's platform reports daily for every underground store in the EU [2]. That is the number that will be quoted all winter.

Look under it. On the same data Italy was 89.5% full and France 87.4%, while Germany stood at 60.7% [1]. A comfortable-looking average can sit on top of a thin national number, and a thinner store means leaning harder on daily imports through the winter. European LNG terminals sent out about 3,610 GWh a day on 16 October [1]: ships, not caverns, are the margin this winter.

On the oil side, the IEA said this week that the diesel shortage triggered by the war would persist well into next year [3]. Gas and diesel are different molecules with the same lesson: a stock is a buffer you build in summer and spend in winter, and the bill for a thin buffer arrives as a price, not as a shortage.

The constructive part is that this is the most transparent energy system Europe has ever run. Every store reports daily, in public, for free. You can watch the winter being spent one gas day at a time, which is more than any podium will tell you.
