At the EU summit, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy asked leaders for an additional €70 billion in financial and military support next year to counter Russia's escalation, Euronews reported [1]. The request upends the bloc's plans, which assumed last year's €90 billion loan would cover both 2026 and 2027. Leaders asked the Commission to propose how to meet Kyiv's needs [1].

The leaders promised to keep sanctions in place for as long as Russia pursues its full-scale invasion, and condemned the US–Russia diesel deal, with Bulgaria's prime minister breaking ranks [1].

Filling the gap is the hard part. Soaring borrowing costs make joint debt unpalatable, the immobilised Russian assets remain divisive, and bilateral contributions risk leaving the burden on a few wealthy countries [1]. Security, again, is first a financing question.
