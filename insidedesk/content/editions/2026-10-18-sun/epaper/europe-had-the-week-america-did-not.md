The STOXX Europe 600 rose 2.09% on the week to 644.74, its largest weekly gain since the week ending 3 July, according to Dow Jones Market Data. It still sits 2.39% below its record close of 660.51 from 11 August [1]. Over the same five days the S&P 500 fell 1.95% [3].

Frankfurt shows where the money went. The DAX closed Friday at 25,622.61, up 0.95% on the day and 2.13% over five days. Rheinmetall led with a gain of 6.3%, and Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz and BMW rose between 2.8% and 3.5%, as "traditional industrial sectors were generally in demand", dpa-AFX reported [2]. Sören Hettler of DZ Bank put the selling in one line: the parts of the AI trade under pressure are "primarily those that are highly valued and heavily debt-financed" [2].

That is not a verdict on Europe against America. It is the same rate question asked of different companies. Where earnings arrive as cash this quarter, a higher cost of money is a nuisance. Where they arrive as a promise, it is the whole argument.
