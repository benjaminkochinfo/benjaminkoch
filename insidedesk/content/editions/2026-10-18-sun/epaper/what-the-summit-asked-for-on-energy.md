After talks that ran into Thursday night, EU leaders called for a coordinated response to the energy crisis and asked the Commission for targeted, temporary measures for households and businesses, including extending support to the hardest-hit industries, Euronews reported [1]. They also asked it to improve the capacity and efficiency of European oil refineries, "which have been in decline since 2009", and showed interest in voluntary joint energy purchasing [1].

The argument underneath was about carbon pricing. The Visegrád four and Italy pushed to reassess the Emissions Trading System; Finland, Denmark and Sweden pushed back, and the attempt to reopen the rules lacked a qualified majority [2]. One EU official said the day's drafting was "not resulting in any new measures" [2].

For this desk, the refinery sentence is the one to keep. It names the physical constraint the IEA keeps pointing to. Turning it into capacity is a project measured in years, not in summit communiqués.
