In R+V's long-running study "Die Ängste der Deutschen", 59% of respondents fear that living costs will rise sharply, up seven points from 52% in 2025. It is the top fear for the 16th time in 35 surveys [1]. Fear of tax rises or benefit cuts came second at 53%, and fear of a worse economy rose ten points to 51%, the largest increase of any worry [1].

Two numbers point the other way. Only 20% of people in work fear losing their job, the lowest since the question was first asked in 1992. And the average across all fears is 38%, only two points above the calmest year on record, 2021 [1]. Interviews ran from 11 May to 29 July 2026 with about 2,400 people [1].

So Germans are not panicking. They are adding up the bills. A population afraid of prices but not of unemployment is telling you where the pressure sits: in the shopping basket, not on the payroll.
