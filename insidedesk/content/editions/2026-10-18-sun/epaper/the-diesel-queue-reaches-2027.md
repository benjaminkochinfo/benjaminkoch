The International Energy Agency's October Oil Market Report says the global diesel and gasoil market will stay tight "well into 2027". Even with refinery runs recovering quickly towards year-end, gasoil supply falls short of demand in the fourth quarter, so rebuilding lost stocks "is unlikely before 2H27" [1].

The numbers behind that sentence are plain. Global diesel exports in September were 700,000 barrels a day lower than a year earlier. A loss of 1.3 million barrels a day from Russia and the Middle East was only partly offset by other suppliers [1]. Wholesale diesel topped $200 a barrel in Singapore in mid-September and reached all-time highs of around $220 in Europe and the United States [1]. Prices have eased since, but benchmarks "remain nearly double the price of crude oil and their pre-crisis levels" [1].

Refineries are the bottleneck. Global runs are set to hit a seasonal low of 81.3 million barrels a day in October, during autumn maintenance, before rebounding to 84.6 million by year-end [1]. Diesel demand, the IEA notes, is "relatively price inelastic": lorries, tractors and buses keep running when the price rises, helped by government relief measures [1].

That is why the German pump moves the way it does. ADAC's weekly average put diesel at €2.247 a litre [2]. A tax cut can lower that number at the margin; it cannot add a refinery. The IEA also warns that delivered diesel prices in importing regions, Europe among them, could rise again in the coming weeks as freight costs climb [1].

The useful habit here is to watch capacity, not headlines. A ceasefire that lasts four days does not refill a tank farm. A refinery that comes back from maintenance does, slowly, one cargo at a time.
