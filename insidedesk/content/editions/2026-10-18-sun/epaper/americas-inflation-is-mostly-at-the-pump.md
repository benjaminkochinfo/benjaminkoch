The US consumer price index rose 0.6% in September on a seasonally adjusted basis, after 0.4% in August, and 3.7% over twelve months, up from 3.4% [1]. The gasoline index rose 9.1% in the month and accounted for over half of the overall increase. Energy prices are up 20.4% on the year [1].

Without food and energy the picture is calmer, but not calm. Core prices rose 0.3%, as they did in August, and 2.6% on the year, up from 2.4% [1]. Shelter rose 0.4%. Food was unchanged on the month [1].

Traders raised their bets on further US rate rises after the release on Wednesday [2], and on Friday Fed Chair Kevin Warsh declined to signal his next move [3]. A committee can look through a petrol spike for a while. It cannot look through a core rate that keeps rising. The October figures are due on 10 November [1].
