EU leaders spent much of Thursday on the 2028–2034 budget and, as expected, made little headway, Euronews reported. The net contributors and the "Friends of Cohesion" from the south and east dug in [1].

Two things did change. The negotiating box tabled by the Irish presidency a few days earlier, which proposed cutting €141 billion from the overall budget to win over the net contributors, is "now officially dead" [1]. And European Council President António Costa will take over the talks. He is due to present a new compromise in the coming weeks, which many expect before the special summit he has called for 26 November [1].

Euronews expects Costa to pursue three lines: keep national contributions as low as possible, rework the package of EU-wide taxes and restore the flexibility instruments the Irish draft cut [1]. The more worrying sentence comes from the leaders themselves: on both sides, they say openly that they feel no pressure to close the deal by the end of the year [1].

This desk's rule for budgets is the same as for central banks. A process with a named owner and a date is better than one without. As of Friday this one has both, Costa and 26 November, and one thing it lacks: a cost to missing the date. Without that, no one is forced to give ground first.

For households the stakes are slow but real. The seven-year budget is where cohesion money, farm support and the EU's share of energy and defence spending are fixed. A late deal will not change your gas bill this winter. It decides who pays for the next one.
