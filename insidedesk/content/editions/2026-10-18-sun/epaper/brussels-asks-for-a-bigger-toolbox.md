At their summit on 15–16 October, EU leaders pressed the Commission to prepare new trade defence instruments, including safeguards with quotas and tariffs, to protect the chemicals and machinery industries from Chinese competition, Euronews reported [1]. The bloc runs a trade deficit of about €360 billion with China, and Chinese customs data show exports to the EU up 25% over the past year [1].

"The toolbox will be bigger. It will also be applied as necessary," Chancellor Friedrich Merz said [1]. Ursula von der Leyen told leaders the Commission would begin work on such a tool. It is unclear whether it will be ready before the end of the year [1].

Last week's deal with Beijing on hybrid-car exports was negotiated; this would be unilateral. A safeguard has a date, a quota and a tariff. Until those three exist on paper, the toolbox is a podium item.
