On 15 October MSCI opened a consultation on reclassifying the MSCI Indonesia Indexes from Emerging Market to a Standalone Market. Feedback runs until 30 April 2027, and a decision is due on or before 30 June 2027 [1]. The freeze already in place stays: no increases to foreign inclusion factors or share counts, no additions to the investable market index and no upward moves between size segments [1].

The reasons MSCI gives are about how the market works, not how fast the economy grows: "persistent opacity in shareholding structures and suspected coordinated trading behavior" [1]. Jakarta has announced reforms, including disclosure of holders above 1% and a roadmap to a 15% minimum free float. Investors, MSCI says, want evidence that they are enforced [1]. Indonesian shares had their biggest drop since 1998 on the news, Reuters reported [2].

Passive funds do not read speeches; they read index rules. A country can argue with a rating agency for years. With an index provider, the argument has a calendar, and this one ends in June.
