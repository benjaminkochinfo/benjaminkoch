September 2026 was the second-warmest September on record, at 16.27°C, 0.82°C above the 1991–2020 average and 1.63°C above the pre-industrial level, Copernicus reports. It is the second month in a row above 1.5°C [1].

The ocean is doing much of it. The average sea surface temperature between 60°S and 60°N was a September record at 21.05°C. In the Niño 3.4 region, used to monitor El Niño, the monthly temperature reached an all-time high of 29.70°C, above the peaks of the very strong events of 1982/83, 1997/98 and 2015/16 [1]. Copernicus expects the run of months above 1.5°C to continue into 2027 [1].

"How far we overshoot, and for how long, is still up to us," said Carlo Buontempo, director of the Copernicus Climate Change Service [1]. A strong El Niño is weather on a planetary scale. The trend underneath it is not.
