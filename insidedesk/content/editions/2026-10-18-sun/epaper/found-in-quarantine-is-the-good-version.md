Kenya confirmed its second and third cases of Bundibugyo Ebola on 14 and 16 October. Both patients are close family members of the country's first case, a man who had travelled home from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and died on 5 October, the WHO says [1]. Both were already in quarantine when they fell ill [1].

Health teams listed 130 contacts of the first case. By 16 October, 127 of them were in quarantine, with temperature and symptom checks every day for 21 days [1]. There is no licensed vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo Ebola yet, so early supportive care matters [1]. "This is the response working as designed," said Dr Patrick Amoth of Kenya's health ministry [1]. The WHO advises against closing borders or restricting trade [1].

Kenya has trained nearly 5,000 health workers since May [1]. Preparation is invisible until the week it is needed. Then it looks exactly like this: a short list, a daily check, and no surprises.
