**What happened.** Two in five UK adults with mental-health or neurodevelopmental conditions say recent policy debate and headlines have left them feeling "under attack", according to a survey of 2,033 UK adults commissioned by Clinical Partners, a provider of NHS and private mental-health services, and conducted by PF Global [1]. The research follows the Timms and Milburn reports into NEETs and PIP claims [1].

**The numbers.** Among respondents with lived experience of these conditions, 66% say the debate has left them feeling misunderstood, 64% judged, 63% ignored, 63% powerless, 60% not believed, 60% hopeless and 56% unfairly treated [1].

[[chart:uk-under-attack-26nov]]

**The other side of the ledger.** The wider public is not hostile. Almost three-quarters (73%) believe people should get the support they need, including financial support, whatever the type of condition; only 15% think people whose conditions affect behaviour and emotions need less support than those with physical ones [1]. And 42% say the debate has made them more motivated to seek help for themselves, 44% for their children [1]. A gap remains: 61% would be likely to seek treatment or diagnosis for a mental-health condition, against 71% for a visible physical condition [1].

**Why it matters.** "We can see that stigma remains a real problem," said Dr Alice Parshall, chief medical officer at Clinical Partners; patients, she said, "are much more likely to downplay difficulties" than to play them up [1]. Andy Bell of the Centre for Mental Health called it "deeply disturbing" that so many feel "under attack from inaccurate or hostile public debate", while noting "important signs of progress" in help-seeking [1].

**What to take from it.** A commissioned survey from a provider should be read as one input, not a verdict. Its useful finding is not anger but mismatch: a sympathetic public and an anxious minority, separated by headlines. You can reform a benefit and still choose words that do not make the claimant feel like a suspect. Precision is cheaper than repair.
