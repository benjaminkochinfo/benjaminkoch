**The podium.** In Wednesday's general debate on the 2027 budget, which carries around €12 billion in aid for Ukraine, Friedrich Merz asked the question many voters ask: are we spending too much on Ukraine's defence? [1] His answer: "Aber es geht hier, meine Damen und Herren, nicht allein um die Ukraine, es geht eben auch um unsere Freiheit. Denn mit der Unterstützung der Ukraine unterstützen und schützen wir uns auch selbst." [1] He went on to describe Russian escalation in the Baltic, in Poland, on the Black Sea and in Germany [1]. Earlier he had said "Russland will mehr als die Ukraine" [3].

**The print.** Körber-Stiftung's annual Berlin Pulse survey, carried out by Forsa in September among 1,501 eligible voters and published on Monday, put that exact proposition to the public. Half (50%) agree that Ukraine, by defending itself, also protects the security of Germany and Europe; 48% do not [2]. German military support for Ukraine "findet keine Mehrheit mehr": 46% in favour, down from 59% a year earlier, and 53% against, up from 39% [2]. At the same time 82% still see Russia as a military threat [2].

[[chart:koerber-ukraine-aid-26nov]]

**The gap.** Desk arithmetic: support fell 13 points in a year and opposition rose 14. The chancellor's sentence is not contested by the data on threat; four in five agree Russia is one. It is contested on the link between that threat and the cheque. tagesschau noted that the Dresden polarisation barometer now ranks Ukraine aid above immigration as the country's most divisive issue, and concluded that the case "offenkundig besser und mehr erklärt werden muss" by the government [1].

**The dull part.** Persuasion here is not rhetoric but bookkeeping: what was delivered, at what price, with what effect, and what the alternative would cost. The Körber figures also show 61% think Germany and Europe are doing too little for peace talks, while 64% reject normal relations with Russia during the war [2]. That is not a nation of pacifists. It is a nation asking for the receipts.
