**What happened.** Russian drones struck the centre of Pavlohrad in the Dnipropetrovsk region, killing at least five people, Ukrainian authorities said on Thursday [1]. A later update from the regional authorities put the number of wounded at 67, eight of them children; two rescue workers were also hurt [1]. Shops, commercial pavilions and vehicles were damaged or destroyed [1]. Pavlohrad, an industrial and railway hub, lies roughly 80 kilometres from the front line [1].

**The capital.** Late on Wednesday Russian forces attacked Kyiv with ballistic missiles, hitting a shopping centre and two other buildings on the east bank of the Dnipro, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said, according to Reuters [2]. Three people were wounded, one seriously, and one building was badly damaged [2]. Earlier this week Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukrainian intelligence showed Russia was preparing a major new strike [2]. Both sides deny targeting civilian sites [2].

**The heat.** In the Poltava region, a Russian attack on Naftogaz gas production infrastructure caused a large fire and halted operations at the site, chief executive Sergii Fedorenko said; he accused Russia of deliberately targeting gas production to deprive Ukrainians of heat in winter [3]. Reuters also reported strikes on Dnipro, Sumy and Mykolaiv, and two people killed at a sunflower-oil facility in Dnipro [1].

**What to take from it.** Each of these is a local tragedy and a line in a long ledger. The ledger's subject is endurance: who can keep the lights, the gas and the shops running through the next four months. That is why the boring requests, interceptors, transformers, spare parts, matter more than any communiqué.
