In a Rhode Island Voices survey by Blue Cross & Blue Shield of Rhode Island and Brown University's School of Public Health, 91% said more open discussion of mental health would be good for the state, but 76% said their communities lack enough mental-health and substance-use providers [1]. While 64% were at least somewhat confident they could find a suitable provider, only 19% were very confident; 34% cited long waits and 31% out-of-pocket costs [1]. Wait problems hit 46% of under-50s against 22% of those 50 and over [1]. The survey ran from 31 August to 22 September among 755 panel members [1]. Openness is the easy part; appointments are the hard one.
