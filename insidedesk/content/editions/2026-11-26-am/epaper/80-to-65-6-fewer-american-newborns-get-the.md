The share of US babies vaccinated against hepatitis B within a day of birth fell from a median of 80.0% in 2020–2022 to 65.6% in June 2026, according to a Public Health Alerts report from NEJM Evidence and CIDRAP based on Truveta data covering 1.3 million infants [1]. The decline began in mid-2023; federal advisers replaced the universal birth-dose recommendation almost a year ago [1]. The authors estimate about 360,000 infants a year are born to mothers with no evidence of screening, and more than 640 a year to mothers with an untested infection [1]. The birth dose, they write, is "a critical safety net" [1].
