Asian shares "eked out a rare rally" on Thursday, ABC News reported with Reuters, with Japan's Nikkei up 1.6% and South Korea's Kospi up 1.9% at the time [1]. Now the context. For November, Japan was down 8%, South Korea had shed 16% and Taiwan's main index 15%; Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan were still off 9% for the month [1]. Seoul's Wednesday alone had cost 6.16% before Thursday's partial recovery, by the Minutes market tracker's close data [2]. A day's gain of under 2% against a month's loss of 16% is a rest, not a reversal. You do not judge a ledger by its last entry. Nothing here is investment advice.
