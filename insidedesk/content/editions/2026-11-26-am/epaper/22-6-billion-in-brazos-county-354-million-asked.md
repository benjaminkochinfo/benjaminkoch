TSMC is seeking an estimated $354 million tax break for a proposed $22.6 billion chip plant in Brazos County, Texas, according to documents filed with the Texas Comptroller's Office, Manufacturing Dive reports [1]. The 3,200-acre site would include at least one factory making 2-nanometer chips for AI, high-performance computing and mobile devices [1].

The application, under the state's Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation (JETI) programme, calls the property-tax limit a "determinative and compelling factor" in choosing Texas [1]. It also names the alternative: an expansion in Arizona, close to TSMC's existing fabs, with "synergies" in workforce, logistics and suppliers [1].

TSMC says the Texas project would generate $64.4 billion in economic output over 40 years and at least 2,099 direct jobs by 2032 when fully operational [1]. Desk arithmetic: the requested break is about 1.6% of the investment. The College Station school board holds a public meeting on 10 December; both the district and the governor must approve before a tax agreement can be signed [1].

TSMC's US pledges across 2024 to 2026 already total $265 billion for 12 facilities, according to the White House, the Commerce Department and NIST as cited by Manufacturing Dive [1]. The headline is the AI boom. The file is a county tax roll, a school district and a deadline. Factories still get built through the dullest offices in the land.
