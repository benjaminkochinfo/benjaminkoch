Two counts arrived on Wednesday, and they pointed in different directions. The US Energy Information Administration said American crude inventories rose by 1.1 million barrels to 418.9 million in the week to 20 November, a smaller build than the 1.5 million barrels analysts had projected [2]. At the same time, Iranian state media reported that two oil tankers struck mines and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz, and a statement from the Iranian army carried by Fars said a tanker hit a naval mine and caught fire inside a "restricted zone" [2].

The market chose the strait. Front-month Brent for January settled at $94.61, up 3.51% on the day and $5.24 or 5.86% over two sessions, the largest two-day gain since 23 October [1]. Yet the same Dow Jones sheet shows Brent 20.06% below its 2026 high of $118.35, set on 31 March, and down 19.58% month to date [1].

Thursday is a thin day. There is no WTI settlement because of the US Thanksgiving holiday [2]. Kotak NEO's Anindya Banerjee told Moneycontrol his operating range for Brent runs from $87–88 on the downside to $97–98 on the upside [2]. OPEC+ ministers meet on 29 November to map out 2027 policy [2].

A strait closes a headline. A storage tank fills a ledger. Both are true at once.
