German private consumption rose 0.1% in the third quarter, adjusted for prices, season and calendar, Destatis said on Wednesday; households make up about 70% of all consumer spending [1]. They held back more than in the second quarter on consumables, which include food and energy products such as fuel, while government consumption grew 0.3% [1]. The economy as a whole grew 0.1%, a tenth less than first reported, partly because September retail sales came in weaker than assumed [1]. Trade, transport and hospitality lost 0.6% of value added [1]. A quarter like this is not a slump. It is a household reading the receipt twice before it buys.
