Péter Magyar's trip to Transcarpathia on Tuesday was the first visit to Ukraine by a sitting Hungarian prime minister since 2015, the Kyiv Post reports [1]. It produced no grand declaration. It produced a list: gas and diesel supplies to Ukraine, Hungarian companies' use of Ukrainian gas storage, rebuilt and new border crossings, and direct trains on new routes [1]. Humanitarian demining was also discussed; the UN estimates more than 1 million mines and pieces of unexploded ordnance contaminate about a quarter of Ukraine's territory [1].

"On all issues, we have reached specific agreements," Volodymyr Zelensky said, adding that teams had been instructed to implement them promptly [1]. Magyar, for his part, said: "We have managed to take important steps towards strengthening trust" [2].

The road here was paved with legislation rather than speeches. Relations soured in 2017 over a Ukrainian schools-language law; in June the two countries agreed on minority rights, and last month Kyiv passed a law addressing Budapest's concerns about the Hungarian minority, after which Hungary let the EU open new areas of accession talks [1]. Magyar said he would keep raising language and education questions with Kyiv [1].

Not every neighbour moves in step: on 22 November Slovakia's Robert Fico said his country would not take part in the EU's new €45 billion loan programme for Ukraine for 2027 [1].

Trust between states is rarely announced. It is built in timetables, storage contracts and border posts that open on time.
