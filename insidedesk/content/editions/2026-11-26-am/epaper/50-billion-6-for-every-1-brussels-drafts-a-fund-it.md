Alongside its Quantum Act, the European Commission on Wednesday proposed a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, STEF, meant to drive up to €50 billion of investment in quantum, AI and other technologies it calls critical to European sovereignty [1][2]. The design is seed capital: the Commission hopes to draw up to €6 of private money for every €1 of public money put in by member states, the European Investment Bank and itself [1]. It wants STEF running as soon as possible and by 2028 at the latest, when the next long-term EU budget starts [1].

The gap it aims at is private, not public. Annual public investment in quantum in the EU is expected to reach €8.6 billion this year, against €13.6 billion in China and €5.3 billion in the US, but private investment last year was €1.1 billion in Europe against €7 billion in America, the Commission says [1]. The fund is meant to work alongside the European Competitiveness Fund, due from January 2028 with a total budget of €234.3 billion [1].

The industry consortium QuIC welcomed the plan and asked for a dedicated quantum share open to start-ups and scale-ups [1]. The proposal now goes to the European Parliament and the Council [1].

Desk arithmetic: at the hoped-for ratio of 6 to 1, €50 billion would need roughly €7 billion of public money. A ratio is a hope until the first cheque clears. The budget talks are where this fund becomes real, or does not.
