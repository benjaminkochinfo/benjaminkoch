Health Canada has warned of a "very rare but serious" risk of vision loss with semaglutide drugs such as Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy, CBC News reports [1]. Its own safety review, following similar warnings in the United States and Europe, found a possible link with non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, or NAION: sudden, permanent loss of sight caused by reduced blood flow to the optic nerve, with no known treatment [1].

The advice is practical. Anyone with sudden vision loss or rapidly worsening eyesight while taking semaglutide should "seek urgent medical attention", and clinicians should refer such patients urgently and stop the drug if NAION is confirmed [1]. The department notes the loss usually affects one eye, is often noticed on waking and is usually painless [1].

Scale explains why a rare event earns a label. An estimated three million Canadians take GLP-1 medicines, CBC reports [1]. The endocrinologist Daniel Drucker put the risk at perhaps "one in 5,000, one in 10,000 or even less" and urged caution until it is known for sure; for people with serious type 2 diabetes or obesity, he said, "the numbers are overwhelmingly favourable for benefit" [1]. The drug-safety epidemiologist Mahyar Etminan noted that with a very large sample, "even a rare event" can be significant [1].

This is pharmacovigilance doing its dull job: reports collected, a review completed, a label changed. If you take one of these medicines, the sensible next step is a conversation with your doctor, not a decision on your own.
