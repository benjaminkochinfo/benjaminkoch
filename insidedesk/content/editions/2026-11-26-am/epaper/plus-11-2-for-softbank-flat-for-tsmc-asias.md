The rebound in Asia on Thursday was not a tide that lifted everything. It was a list. SoftBank Group, one of OpenAI's largest backers, was up 11.2% by Tokyo's lunch break [1]. AFP named the memory maker Kioxia alongside it as the standouts, both up by double figures [3]. CNBC counted Advantest up 7.3%, Lasertec and Disco more than 6%, and Tokyo Electron 4.6% [2]. Handelsblatt noted that the two biggest winners were among the names that had fallen deepest in the slide [1].

In Seoul, SK Hynix rose more than 4% and Samsung Electronics 2.1%, according to CNBC [2]. In Taipei, by contrast, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing traded at the previous day's level [1], even as the Taiex rose.

Two pieces of news that might have mattered did not, at least by midday. Handelsblatt reported that S&P Global's threat to downgrade Nvidia's credit rating after its leadership turmoil left Asian investors cold, and that Washington's new tariff plans for Japan, South Korea and Taiwan were barely a topic [1]. A strategist at Resona Bank, quoted via the Nikkei newspaper, said the heavy sellers appeared to have withdrawn [1].

The lesson is in the shape, not the size. Money returned first to the stocks that had fallen most, not to the one company that sits under the whole AI build-out. That is what short-covering and bargain-hunting look like. Whether it is more than that, Friday's short session in New York will begin to say.
