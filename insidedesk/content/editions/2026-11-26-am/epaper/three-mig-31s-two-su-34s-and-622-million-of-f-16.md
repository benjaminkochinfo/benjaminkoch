Ukraine's general staff says its forces struck the Savasleyka airbase in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region and damaged five military aircraft: three MiG-31 jets and two Su-34 fighter-bombers, which it says are out of action "for some time", The Independent reports [1]. Russia had not immediately responded to the claim [1]. Kyiv has increasingly aimed at military facilities deep inside Russia to weaken Moscow's ability to launch air strikes [1].

On Wednesday, the US State Department said it had approved a potential $622 million sale of F-16 equipment to Ukraine [1]. The package covers aircraft sustainment and related equipment: the maintenance and logistical support needed to keep the fighter jets in the air, with a principal contractor to be chosen through US government procurement [1].

The approval comes after Zelensky criticised Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth for not authorising Patriot interceptors despite critical shortages [1]. The Pentagon says it has always "supported efforts to deepen industrial cooperation and strengthen global supply chains" for Patriot production [1].

The Independent also reports that the UK is moving military aircraft into hangars at night over possible drone threats [1]. Read the two halves together: a war in which aircraft on the ground are targets, and in which the less glamorous contract, spare parts and servicing, decides how many of yours are still flying next spring.
