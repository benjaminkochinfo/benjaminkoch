Of 47 critical components the Commission has identified for quantum technology, nine currently have no EU supplier at all, Euronews reports from Wednesday's Quantum Act [1]. The Commission and member states would monitor supply chains to find critical suppliers, while public procurement stays tied to World Trade Organisation rules: non-EU firms may bid if they have a European-controlled presence and follow EU rules [1]. The act would also require member states to move to post-quantum cryptography by 2030 for high-risk areas such as critical infrastructure, and by 2035 for all other systems [1]. IBM's Jean Marc Leclerc said Europe "should not have to choose" between its own capabilities and the best technology from abroad [1]. A parts list is less glamorous than a fund. It is usually more honest.
