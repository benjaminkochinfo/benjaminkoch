The Bank of Korea raised its base rate by 25 basis points to 3.25% on Thursday, its third rise this year after increases in July and August and a pause in October [1]. CNBC called the move expected [2]; Aju Press wrote that markets had priced an extended pause [1]. Either way, the board's message was not hedged: growth "has further strengthened", inflation is "projected to remain above the target level for a considerable time", and "financial stability risks persist" [1].

The forecasts explain the confidence. The bank now sees growth of 3.9% this year and 4.1% in 2027, up from 3.3% and 2.9% in August, with core inflation at 2.6% for both years [1]. The engine is overseas demand for chips [1]. Five of seven board members voted for the rise; two wanted to hold at 3.00% [1]. Seven of the 21 dots in the six-month projections sat at 3.75%, a level no member had pencilled in before, and none sat below the new rate [1].

Aju Press said the move narrows the gap to the upper end of the Fed's range to 0.75 percentage points, ahead of an expected Fed rise in December [1]. Three-year government bond yields rose 5.2 basis points to 3.635% by midday [1].

Desk arithmetic: 3.25% plus 0.75 points puts the upper end of the Fed's range, as Aju Press describes it, at 4.00%.

A central bank that raises rates because the economy is strong is doing its dull job. That is the reassuring kind of hike. Nothing here is investment advice.
