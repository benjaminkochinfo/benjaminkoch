You can stop guessing about December. According to CME's FedWatch tool, cited after Wednesday's inflation data, futures put the probability of a 25-basis-point rise by the Federal Reserve in December at 97.8%, and of no change at 2.2% [1]. CNBC TV18, quoting the same tool a few hours later, put the odds of a hike on 9 December at 99% [2]. Reuters, via ABC, said futures "still imply a 98% chance" [3]. Different snapshots, one answer.

The more interesting number is the one that moved down. Reuters reported that speculation of a half-point increase had faded, and that the US 10-year Treasury yield was at 4.848% after touching an 11-week trough [3]. For January, FedWatch showed a 58.4% probability of a cumulative 50 basis points of tightening and 40.7% for a single 25, "not significantly different from before the data release" [1]. In plain words: the market believes one rise is done, half-believes a second, and does not believe in a large one.

This is the quiet consequence of a 3.3% core rate [4]. It did not shock anyone. It confirmed a path already priced, and on a day when stocks fell hard at the open, the bond market bought safety rather than panic.

Note the calendar, because the Fed cannot. BEA's next personal income and outlays release, with November's PCE prices, is due on 23 December [4]. The Fed meets on 9 December [2]. So the December decision will be taken on October's prices, the ones you already know. Whatever November holds arrives two weeks too late to change it.

Desk arithmetic: 97.8% for a hike leaves 2.2% for no change; the gap between the FedWatch snapshot and CNBC TV18's 99% is 1.2 points, small enough to be the difference between two refreshes of the same screen.
