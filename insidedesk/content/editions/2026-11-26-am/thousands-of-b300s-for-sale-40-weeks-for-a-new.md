**What happened.** Sellers of Nvidia hardware are seeking new buyers after the US tightened its rules on Chinese companies' remote access to advanced AI computing, three people told CNBC [1]. An unusually high volume of Nvidia GPUs has been offered since the measures were announced on 16 November; the Commerce Department's interim final rule took effect on 19 November [1]. Deals for thousands of B300 GPUs, one of Nvidia's most advanced products, have been floated since, according to Jack Duhamel, president of the marketplace GPU Mart, and one other person [1]. Nvidia declined to comment [1].

**The reason.** The chips, the people said, appear to have been bought for overseas data centres where Chinese companies would rent access [1]. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC the rule was meant to close "loopholes": "smuggling through places like Southeast Asia or remote access from data centers in third countries" [1].

**The other queue.** On the same day TrendForce, citing Taiwan's Commercial Times, reported that lead times for new B300 orders now range from 26 to 40 weeks, against 14 to 26 weeks for the GB300 [2]. Most new orders placed over the past two quarters are not expected to be filled until the second or third quarter of 2027 or later, and memory makers are prioritising server DRAM over graphics memory [2].

**What to take from it.** Desk arithmetic: 40 weeks is more than nine months. So the same week offers two pictures: chips looking for a buyer, and buyers waiting most of a year for chips. A rule did not destroy demand; it redrew who may use the supply. The interesting question for you is not the share price but the paperwork: who can lawfully rent what, where, and how quickly the second-hand market clears.
