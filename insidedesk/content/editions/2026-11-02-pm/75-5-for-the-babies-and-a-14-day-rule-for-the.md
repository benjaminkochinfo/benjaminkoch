Trials told us the RSV vaccine given in pregnancy works. The harder question is whether it works in ordinary clinics, with ordinary timing and ordinary paperwork. A new review of real-world studies, presented at the IDWeek 2026 meeting and reported today, gives a calm answer: yes, if you are not late [1].

The review pooled seven studies from Argentina, the UK and the US, covering 3,029 vaccinated mother-and-baby pairs [1]. When mothers were vaccinated at least 14 days before birth, the vaccine was 75.5% effective against hospital admission for RSV in the baby's first six months, compared with unvaccinated controls [1]. Protection was highest in babies aged three months or younger, at 80.8%, and 72.4% across the first six months [1]. It is a "living" review: the estimates are updated as new studies come out [1].

Then comes the number that matters for the calendar. When the jab was given less than 14 days before birth, effectiveness fell to 28% [1]. That figure comes from a single study, as the lead author, Ariel Bardach of the Institute for Clinical Effectiveness and Health Policy in Buenos Aires, points out, but it fits the biology: a mother needs time to make antibodies and pass them on [1]. An outside expert, Natasha Halasa of Vanderbilt University Medical Center, said clinicians "should not wait until the end of the eligible window to offer it" [1].

The limits are stated too. The studies are observational, they measured hospital admissions rather than intensive care, and they say little yet about premature babies or low-income countries, where the RSV burden is greatest [1]. The study was funded by Pfizer, which makes the vaccine, and several co-authors are Pfizer employees [1]. Families also have a second option, antibody injections for the baby, and should be told about both [1].

If you are pregnant, or about to be a grandparent, the practical point is small and dull. Ask about the jab at the antenatal appointment, not at the last one. Two weeks is the difference between most of the protection and little of it.
