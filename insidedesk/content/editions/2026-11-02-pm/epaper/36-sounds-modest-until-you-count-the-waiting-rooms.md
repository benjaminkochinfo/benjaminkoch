A flu jab that works one time in three is often waved away. A US analysis presented at IDWeek 2026 shows why that is the wrong reflex [1]. Researchers from the CDC's VISION network studied 76,815 emergency and urgent-care visits by children and teenagers last winter, a season dominated by a drifted H3N2 strain [1]. The vaccine cut the risk of a flu-related visit by 36% overall, by 47% in children aged six months to four years and by 29% in those aged five to 17 [1]. Protection was 40% in the first two months after the jab and 34% after four months [1].

The authors concluded that even moderate effectiveness can substantially reduce the flu burden among children [1]. The findings do not necessarily represent the CDC's official position [1]. For a parent, the point is simple: fewer nights in a waiting room, starting with the youngest.
