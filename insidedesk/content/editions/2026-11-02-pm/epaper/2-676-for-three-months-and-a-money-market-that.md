Rates on Frankfurt's euro money market were little changed from last week, n-tv reported on Monday: overnight money traded at 2.42–2.62%, the same range as before [1]. The three-month Euribor was fixed at 2.676% on 30 October, up from 2.643% the day before; the twelve-month rate was 3.306%, against 3.308% [1]. The ECB kept its deposit rate at 2.5% on Thursday [2].

Dealers welcomed the falling oil price and lower yields, and one said the latest German inflation data had shown "how strongly the numbers depend on energy prices" (desk translation) [1]. For your savings account or variable-rate loan, a quiet money market is good news: the price of a month's money is not jumping about. Nothing here is investment advice.
