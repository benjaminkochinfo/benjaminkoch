Alstom's shares lost 27.1% in Paris on Monday morning after the French train maker cut its forecast again, n-tv reported [1]. The company now expects an operating margin of only about 1%, 5.5 percentage points below its previous guidance. It also expects €1.5 billion in cash to flow out; until now it had expected cash to come in [1].

The reason is not a recession or a rate. It is a train. Alstom blamed problems with its Coradia Max regional trains in Germany [1]. It had already cut its forecast seven months ago, citing delays and problems on several large orders [1]. Preliminary first-half figures show a loss, with a margin of minus 4% against 6.4% a year earlier [1]. Martin Sion, chief executive since April, announced savings and debt reduction, and said the aim is calmer waters from next year [1].

What should you take from it? Big order books are not profit. A contract to build trains is a promise to deliver them on time and to specification. Penalties, rework and late payment all arrive when that fails. Desk arithmetic: a 27.1% fall in one morning is the market repricing those promises at once. Watch for the slow work after it: the delivery schedules, the settlements with operators, and whether the cash outflow turns out smaller than €1.5 billion. Nothing here is investment advice.
