The four German transmission system operators, 50Hertz, Amprion, TenneT and TransnetBW, have set their provisional national grid fee for 2027 at an average of 3.54 cents per kWh at the extra-high and transformer levels, up from 2.86 cents in 2026 [1]. That figure assumes a €5.525 billion federal subsidy from the climate and transformation fund, about €1 billion less than this year's [1]. Without it, the fee would be 6.52 cents [1].

The law for the subsidy has not yet passed. If there is no legal certainty by 7 December 2026, the operators expect the final 2027 fees to rise accordingly [1]. Desk arithmetic: the 2.98 cents between the two figures is the subsidy's work. The date that decides it is in a parliamentary calendar, not on an exchange.
