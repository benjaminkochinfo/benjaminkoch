The law set a number for 1 November: German gas storage sites were to be 70% full on average. On the day, they were at about 61.2%, according to the latest data from the European AGSI platform reported by tagesschau [1].

The authorities' answer was calm and specific. A spokeswoman for the economy ministry said supply is secure this winter even at current levels, because what matters is not only how full the stores are but also how much is withdrawn, ongoing imports and other precautions [1]. Bundesnetzagentur president Klaus Müller said: "It is no longer essential for a secure gas supply that the storage targets are reached" [1]. The regulator also noted that Germany now has LNG terminals to import through [1].

Not everyone agreed. The Green energy politician Michael Kellner accused economy minister Katherina Reiche of poor preparation, saying storage levels are "lower than ever" and there is no safety buffer [1].

The trade side pointed to the ledger. Tim Kehler of the gas and water industry association said missing the target brings no sanctions, and that traders have stored the gas ordered for their customers' winter and are still doing so [1]. He put the gas in storage at around 150 terawatt hours, "noticeably more" than was drawn from storage last winter [1]. The regulator could in law withdraw booked storage capacity if needed. It says that is "currently not the case" [1].

A target is a safety net, not a promise. The figure to watch through the winter is how fast the 150 terawatt hours come out.
