The local ceasefire around occupied Oleshky, which began on 27 October and was the most significant truce of the full-scale war, ended at midnight [1]. Early on Monday Ukrainian authorities in Kherson reported several Russian drone attacks; a 62-year-old woman and a 73-year-old man were taken to hospital with blast injuries [1]. Ukraine's general staff said Russian forces had used the pause to bolster drone defences and restock ammunition [1].

In Kyiv, ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets, the lead civilian negotiator for the mission, gave the human ledger [1]. The oldest evacuee brought to Ukrainian-held territory was 94; 104 pets were rescued, among them four parrots [1]. Exact evacuation totals are being withheld to protect those who chose to stay; about 2,000 civilians were in the town when the truce began [1]. "We are dog-tired," he said [1].

He also said he hoped the operation would be a "pilot model" for other missions and add momentum to three-way talks between Ukrainian, Russian and US officials expected this month [1]. Kyiv hopes to deliver aid to those who stayed, but the mechanism is still being worked out [1].

A truce that ends is not a truce that failed. It moved people, it ran to its agreed end, and it left behind a procedure. Procedures are what longer agreements are built from.
