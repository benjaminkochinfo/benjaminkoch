Treatment-resistant depression is defined as an inadequate response to two or more courses of antidepressants at the right dose for long enough [1]. On Monday the US patient group Depression and Bipolar Support Alliance and the biotech company Compass Pathways, which is developing a psilocybin treatment for the condition, published a survey of 261 patients and 73 caregivers [1]. Read it with both facts in mind: it is small, and its sponsor has a product in trials.

The patient findings are stark. 95% described themselves as functioning but struggling, surviving day to day or barely getting by; over 70% said they felt hopeless about the future; nearly nine in ten said they had felt judged or misunderstood because they had not got better [1]. More than half had cut their working hours, and more than a third had resigned from a job [1].

The caregiver line is the one that rarely gets counted. About 70% of caregivers said the condition affects the whole family's health [1]. Roughly half reported emotional exhaustion within three years of their relative's diagnosis; after three years or more, nearly all (95%) did [1].

If you are the one helping, that last number is not a verdict on you. It is a measure of time. Support for carers, from respite to a doctor who asks how you are, works best when it starts before year three.
