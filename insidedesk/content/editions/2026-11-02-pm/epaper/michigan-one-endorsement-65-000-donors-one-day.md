The weekend's biggest surprise in the US midterms came from Michigan. On Sunday, Representative Haley Stevens, who lost the Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed his Republican opponent, Mike Rogers [1]. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called it a "despicable betrayal", and Nancy Pelosi said El-Sayed "must win" [1].

Then came the ledger. El-Sayed's campaign says it raised $2.4 million on 1 November, from more than 65,000 people, nearly 30,000 of them first-time contributors, with an average gift of $36.74 [1]. Desk arithmetic: 65,000 donors at $36.74 is about $2.39 million, so the figures hang together.

The last New York Times/Siena poll put El-Sayed on 53% and Rogers on 42%, but it was taken before the endorsement [1]. Nobody yet knows what one politician's switch does to a statewide electorate, and this desk will not guess.

What you can see is how a democracy absorbs a shock in two days: a statement, a counter-statement, and tens of thousands of people each sending the price of a dinner. The answer comes tomorrow, in ballots counted under Michigan law, and possibly not before Wednesday morning in Berlin.
