This evening's Markets note ended on a bounce. It did not last the session. The S&P 500 had been up as much as 1.1% early on Monday and was virtually unchanged in late trading, according to AP; with a little under an hour left, the Dow was up 403 points, or 0.8%, and the Nasdaq was down 0.3% [1]. Yahoo Finance's last update before the bell had the Dow up 0.7% and the Nasdaq down 0.4% [3]. Settled closing figures were not yet published when this went to print, so treat these as late-session numbers.

What turned it was not a speech. It was the financing. Reuters reports Oracle down 8.6% after it set out plans to raise $20 billion by selling shares and bonds, Marvell down 13% on a $115 billion agreement to buy Lumentum, which fell 18%, and Intel down 8% after Friday's 29% fall [2]. Meanwhile, AP notes that most stocks on Wall Street still rose [1].

So the split is the familiar one. Ordinary companies had an ordinary good day; the build-out had to show its invoices. "The bar for performance is rising," said Mark Haefele of UBS Global Wealth Management [2]. Apple reports after the close tonight and the midterms are on Tuesday [2], so tomorrow's morning edition will have the settled numbers. Nothing here is investment advice.
