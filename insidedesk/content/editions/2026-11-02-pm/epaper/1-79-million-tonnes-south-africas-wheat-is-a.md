Drought rarely makes a headline on the day it matters. It shows up months later, in a crop estimate. South Africa's Crop Estimates Committee released its third winter crop estimate on 27 October and cut the wheat figure by a further 1% from September, to 1.79 million tonnes [1]. That is 6% lower than last year and the smallest harvest in about eight years [1].

Fewer hectares are only part of it. The national average yield is about 3.67 tonnes per hectare, also the lowest in eight years, which the analysis links to drier weather in the Western Cape [1]. Roughly two-thirds of the country's winter wheat is planted there, so a dry season in one province shows up nationally [1]. Silo data already show the harvest moving: by 23 October farmers had delivered about 150,061 tonnes since the marketing year began [1].

The cushion is elsewhere. South Africa imports roughly half its wheat and may import around 2.0 million tonnes this season, up from 1.98 million [1]. The International Grains Council expects the world harvest at 820 million tonnes, down 3% but above the five-year average, after last season's record of 845 million [1]. The difficulty, the analysis says, has been moving the grain, with stocks building in the Black Sea region [1].

The same report notes that farmers intend to plant 2.4 million hectares of maize this summer, down 12% and the lowest in eight years, with an El Niño-linked drought named as a major risk [1]. The next official planting figures are due on 27 January 2027 [1]. Food price inflation there was 1% in September [1]. A drought is cheaper to live through when the warehouses were filled in a good year.
