"Sold out" is the hardest-working phrase in the chip trade this autumn, and it does not always mean the same thing.

SK hynix told investors on 28 October that its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) output is effectively sold out through 2029 [1]. Samsung said on 29 October that its 2027 HBM supply is sold out, with price talks concluded at target levels [1]. A Korean trade newsletter that set the two claims side by side found different levels of commitment underneath [1].

SK hynix has long-term agreements with 14 customers, and confirmed contracts alone fill more than half of its 2027–2028 capacity; the 2029 framing adds volumes still under negotiation [1]. Samsung has multi-year deals with 10 major customers and 10 more in talks, and aims for 60–70% of capacity under long-term contracts by 2030 [1]. Its claim is narrower, covering HBM in 2027, but it is priced [1]. A KB Securities report quoted in October estimated that Samsung had already secured about 80% of its planned 2027 memory output through long-term agreements [2].

The contracts were signed while the share prices fell. At the 30 October close, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.20% and SK hynix 7.08% [1]. The newsletter also noted that Korean equipment and materials suppliers mostly earned operating margins of 9–21%, against 75–80% at the memory makers [1].

You do not buy HBM. You may buy a laptop or a phone whose memory is priced in the same market. When you read "sold out", ask whether the contract was signed or is still being talked about. Nothing here is investment advice.
