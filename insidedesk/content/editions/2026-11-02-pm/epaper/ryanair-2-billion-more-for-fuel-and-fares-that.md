Ryanair's profit after tax fell 6% to €1.61 billion in the July–September quarter, as analysts had expected, n-tv reported [1]. Fares in the peak season were on average 2% lower than a year earlier, but they are edging up again in the current quarter [1].

The interesting number is the next one. For its financial year to March 2028, the airline is budgeting a fuel bill about €2 billion higher, n-tv reported [1]. "If there is no quick resolution of the Middle East conflict, we expect jet fuel prices to stay high well into 2027," said chief executive Michael O'Leary (desk translation) [1]. Fares would have to rise 20% to cover that, he said; there will be double-digit increases at Ryanair and across Europe's airlines, but perhaps not that large [1].

The rest of the gap goes onto other invoices. Ryanair could freeze pay from April, O'Leary said, and wants to save through shorter crew hours, fewer administrative staff thanks to artificial intelligence, and lower airport charges [1].

For you, that is the honest version of a price outlook: a company naming its cost, the share it can pass on, and who carries the rest. If you fly next year, budget for dearer tickets; if you work for an airline or an airport, the wage round has already begun. Nothing here is investment advice.
