Vaccine programmes are not won in a week; they are won in the weekly table. England's latest one, from the UK Health Security Agency, covers data up to 25 October [1].

Flu is circulating at low levels and activity was stable across most measures [1]. Laboratory positivity was 6.6%, against 6.4% a week earlier, and the hospital admission rate for flu was stable at 2.47 per 100,000 people [1]. Covid activity fell and is also low; RSV rose but remains at baseline levels [1].

The vaccine table is where the work shows. Compared with the same week last season, uptake is lower among people aged 65 and over (53.6% against 56.9%), among under-65s in clinical risk groups (24.8% against 26.2%), and among two- and three-year-olds (29.7% and 29.5%, against 30.8% and 30.7%) [1]. The exception is pregnant women, at 29.1% against 27.8% [1]. For Covid, 42.5% of people aged 75 and over had an autumn 2026 dose by the end of the week [1].

The agency also flags a data problem: vaccination data were unavailable or incomplete in weeks 41 and 42, and that has been corrected [1]. It says an issue with attributing jabs to the place they were given should not affect the overall uptake estimates [1]. The official monthly uptake statistics for 1 September to 31 October are due on 26 November at 15:00 CET [2].

Desk arithmetic: 56.9 minus 53.6 is a gap of 3.3 points among the over-65s. That is not a crisis. It is a reminder letter that has not yet been answered, and a low, stable flu curve is the easy time to answer it.
