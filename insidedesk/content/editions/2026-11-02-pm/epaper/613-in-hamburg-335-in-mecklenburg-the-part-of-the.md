Electricity network fees pay for the wires, the meter, the reading and the billing. You cannot switch away from them, because your local grid operator charges them whoever supplies you. Verivox puts their share of the household electricity price at about 29% [2].

For 2027, Verivox's analysis of the provisional fees, covering grid areas with 84% of households, finds an average rise of 1.9%, from €428 to €436 a year for a three-person household using 4,000 kWh [1]. One reason is the federal subsidy for transmission costs, which falls from €6.5 billion in 2026 to €5.525 billion in 2027 [1].

The average hides the map. Hamburg's fees rise most, by 9% or €52, to €613 a year, the highest in the country [1]. Saarland follows at €530 after a rise of €38 [1]. Fees fall by €11 in Thuringia and by €5 in North Rhine-Westphalia [1]. Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania has the lowest at €335 [1]. In the eastern states the average falls by €3 to about €386, while in the western states it rises by €10 to about €445 [1]. Since 2025, regions with a lot of wind and solar no longer carry the cost of that grid alone; it is shared across the country [1].

The part you can shop for is the rest of the price. Verivox's average household electricity price was 34.4 cents per kWh in October; new customers could sign for about 33 cents on 30 October [2]. Desk arithmetic: at 4,000 kWh, 1.4 cents is about €56 a year, seven times the average rise in network fees.
