**The podium.** Germany's final manufacturing purchasing managers' index for October came in at 54.2, up from 53.9 and the same as the flash estimate, n-tv reported [1]. Phil Smith, the S&P Global economist who comments on the survey, said German industry was "proving its resilience" and had kept up its upward trend despite rising cost pressure (desk translation) [1]. He put the demand down to higher state spending on defence and infrastructure and to stronger export sales [1]. The PMI is a private survey; we quote it, we do not chart it.

**The print.** Earlier that morning, the statistics office had published the other side of the economy. Retail sales fell 2.0% in real terms in September against August, after calendar and seasonal adjustment, and 1.5% in nominal terms [2]. Against September 2025, real sales were down 2.3% even as nominal sales rose 1.2% [2]. Economists had expected growth of about 0.4%, n-tv reported [1]. "That is a data shock to start the week," said Alexander Krüger, chief economist at Bank Bethmann (desk translation) [1].

[[chart:de-retail-sep-02pm]]

**The gap.** The podium and the print are not in conflict; they describe two different customers. The factories are selling to ministries and to buyers abroad. The shops sell to you. And you, in September, bought less in every aisle the office measures: food down 1.4%, non-food down 2.4%, online down 2.2% [2]. Petrol stations are the plainest case: their real sales fell 3.4% while their nominal sales rose 2.1% [2]. Desk arithmetic: forecourt customers paid more and took home less.

**What the speech left out.** The till. Desk arithmetic: a 1.2% nominal rise against a 2.3% real fall over the year means prices did more of the work than shoppers did. One caveat in fairness: Destatis replaced its retail sample this month, and figures since January may be revised [2]. A month is a month. But a recovery led by defence orders and exports will feel thin at home until the receipts come back. Nothing here is investment advice.
