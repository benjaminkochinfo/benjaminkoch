**The podium.** After Wednesday night's coalition talks, SPD parliamentary leader Matthias Miersch said the partners had managed to clear "große Brocken", big chunks, and stressed above all that cuts to family benefits had been dropped [1]. Housing benefit and the child supplement would largely stay as they are, he said, according to tagesschau [3].

**The print.** The finance ministry's 23-page supplement to its clean-up template turns that into a single new line: a "global extra expense" of €600 million [2][4]. The note beneath it splits it in two. €300 million reverses the cuts to housing benefit "while keeping the suspension of indexation in 2027"; another €300 million reverses the cuts to the child supplement [2]. The money sits in one provision that may be drawn on, with the finance ministry's consent, to cover extra needs in the child-benefit and housing-benefit titles [2]. Elsewhere in the same document, the line for the basic-income benefit is cut by €1.06 billion, from €27.70 billion to €26.64 billion, to reflect developments since the draft and the government's October autumn projection [2].

**The gap.** Both are true. The cuts were reversed; the benefit was not raised. Housing benefit goes to 1.3 million households, and tagesschau reports its level will not rise for at least a year [3]. The FAZ adds the cost on the other side of the ledger: the forgone savings and the dropped plastics tax weigh on later years, and the €10.7 billion reserve from which the ministry hoped to save about €4 billion for 2028 could roughly halve [4]. Desk arithmetic: €600 million is about 11% of the €5.4 billion the electricity-tax cut for everyone would cost, which is still unfunded [1].

**For you.** If you receive housing benefit, plan on the same amount next year, not more [2][3]. If you are waiting for a lower electricity tax, the print still has no line for it; the coalition leaders meet again on Tuesday [1].
