America's federal drought service published a status update for Arkansas, Louisiana and Mississippi on Thursday, and its first line is blunt: all three states had their warmest August-to-October period on record, with near-record dryness on top [1]. Arkansas had its 3rd-driest such period, Louisiana its 4th and Mississippi its 5th [1].

The map has followed. According to the US Drought Monitor, 94% of the three-state region is in moderate to exceptional drought, including 97% of Arkansas, almost half of which is in the exceptional category, 95% of Louisiana and 92% of Mississippi [1]. About 9.2 million residents live in drought, NOAA estimates [1]. Louisiana went from drought-free to exceptional drought in three months [1].

**Where it lands.** Not in a headline but in a farm ledger. The US Department of Agriculture reports 76% of pastures in the region in poor to very poor condition; ranchers are feeding hay daily and hauling water as stock ponds dry up [1]. Brewer Lake near Conway, Arkansas, has dropped nearly 15 feet, and some places have imposed mandatory water curtailments and lawn-watering limits [1]. Dozens of counties and parishes have burn bans [1]. Arkansas wetlands are short of the water needed to flood fields for the duck season [1].

**What the river does.** On the Mississippi itself, high flows from upstream are temporarily easing low water downstream, but key points such as Baton Rouge remain below their long-term average, the update says [1]. Without significant rain upstream, levels could fall below normal and affect navigation and industry [1].

**What the forecasters say.** NOAA's seasonal outlook expects drought to improve across much of the region by the end of January 2027, and possibly to be removed entirely in parts of eastern Mississippi and south-east Louisiana [1]. Rain in the lower valley rarely lifts the river much, it adds [1].

**For you.** This is the quiet side of climate news: no storm name, no landfall, just a regional office, a weekly map and a list of who is paying. It is also how you know where help is needed before the bill comes due.
