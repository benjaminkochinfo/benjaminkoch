On Thursday morning we told you the regulator's forecast expected about 479 GWh of wind for the day. The meters have now had the whole day. Onshore and offshore turbines together delivered about 480.0 GWh on Thursday, against 479.4 GWh forecast [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 0.1% more than promised, and about 3.3 times Wednesday's 145.6 GWh [1].

The total hid a swap. Onshore wind came in at about 324.8 GWh, roughly 7% under its forecast; offshore delivered about 155.2 GWh, roughly 19% over [1]. Desk arithmetic, both. Land disappointed, sea compensated, and the sum landed where the forecasters said it would. Nobody gave a speech about it.

**Five auctions in a row.** Read the week as a ledger and the effect is plain. Delivery on Monday 9 November averaged €345.64 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg; Tuesday a record €351.48; Wednesday €316.65; Thursday €199.05; Friday €161.74 [2]. Desk arithmetic: the five-day average is €274.91, against €258.83 for Monday to Friday of the week before, and Friday is about 54% below Tuesday's record [2].

**Friday started windy.** In the first four hours of Friday, provisional SMARD data show about 142.0 GWh of wind, 114.6 GWh of it onshore [1]. Desk arithmetic: four hours of Friday night produced almost as much wind power as the whole of Wednesday. The offshore forecast for the full day is about 137.4 GWh; the onshore and solar forecasts for Friday had not been published when we checked at 05:17 CET [1]. So we cannot tell you how the rest of the day will run, and we will not guess.

**For you.** A cheap week is not made by policy or by press release. It is made by a forecast, an auction and a meter, published in that order and in public. On a dynamic tariff, the prices for Friday were fixed on Thursday and are already visible hour by hour [2]. On a fixed tariff, your supplier bought long ago and nothing changes this week. Nothing here is investment advice.
