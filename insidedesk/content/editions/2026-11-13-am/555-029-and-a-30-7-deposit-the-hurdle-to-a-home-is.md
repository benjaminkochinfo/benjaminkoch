Every time bond yields jump, the headline asks whether you can still afford a home. The Frankfurt mortgage broker Hüttig & Rompf has published an answer from its own files, and it is more interesting than the headline [1].

**The monthly payment held.** Owner-occupiers financing through the broker in the third quarter paid an average monthly instalment of €2,005, 6.2% more than a year earlier. Yet that instalment took 27.8% of household income, against 27.4% a year before, because the people borrowing had higher average incomes [1]. The total cost of the average property bought or built rose 6.9% to €555,029 [1].

**The deposit did the work.** Owner-occupiers brought in 17.7% more of their own money than a year earlier, and their equity ratio rose 2.8 percentage points to 30.7% [1]. Desk arithmetic: 30.7% of €555,029 is roughly €170,000. "The real hurdle is the equity," managing director Benjamin Papo said (our translation), adding that many people find it hard to save enough in the current economic climate [1].

**Rates, and how people lock them.** Ten-year fixed mortgage rates averaged above 4% in the third quarter, driven by a ten-year Bund yield of more than 3.6% at the end of September, and eased slightly in October, the broker says [1]. Borrowers have responded by locking in: 85.2% of owner-occupier loans carried a ten-year fixed rate, ten percentage points more than a year earlier [1]. Investors, who had held back in the second quarter, returned: their share of financings rose from 15.7% to 21.0%, back to the usual level of about 20% [1].

**For you.** These are one broker's clients, not the whole market, so read them as a well-kept ledger rather than a census. The lesson is plain and old-fashioned: the deposit is the part of a mortgage you control, and it is built in years, not in a rate cycle. Papo's advice is to clarify early how much equity you have and to secure terms once the property and the financing fit [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
