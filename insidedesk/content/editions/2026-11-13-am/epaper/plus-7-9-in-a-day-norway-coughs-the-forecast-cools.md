Thursday morning, gas was having a quiet third day of gains. By the evening it was not quiet at all. The Dutch TTF benchmark ended late European trading up 7.88% at €93.693 per megawatt hour, after touching a low of €86.045 in the early morning (06:04 CET) and climbing steadily from there [1]. Britain's NBP gained 8.09% to 233.520 pence per therm; EU carbon allowances rose 0.93% to €90.39 a tonne [1].

Three reasons were offered, none of them dramatic on its own. Temperatures across Europe are expected to fall from 17 November, with further cold spells possible in late November and early December [2]. Norwegian flows fell as outages worsened, notably at Troll, where the operator Gassco said the duration remained uncertain [2]; on Wednesday it had put the Troll outage at 7.3 million cubic metres a day until Friday [3]. And storage is thin: EU reserves were below 72% against a five-year seasonal average of 89%, and Germany's below 60% [2].

Oil, meanwhile, went the other way. Brent was little changed at $95.41 a barrel at 03:42 CET on Friday and is set for a weekly fall of 2.4% after a 16% drop the week before [4]. Gas is a local winter problem; oil is a global war problem. This week they were priced separately.

For you: one afternoon on a futures screen is not a heating bill. What it does show is that a cold week, a Norwegian valve and a thin tank are still enough to move the price of next month's gas by close to 8% in a day. Nothing here is investment advice.
