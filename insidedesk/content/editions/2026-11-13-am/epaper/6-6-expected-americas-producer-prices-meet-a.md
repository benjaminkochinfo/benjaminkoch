The speeches have been made; on Friday the invoices speak. The United States publishes its producer price index for October, and economists polled by Dow Jones expect a rise of 0.4% on the month and 6.6% on the year, NDTV Profit reports [1]. Producer prices are what companies charge each other: the steel, freight and packaging long before anything reaches a shop shelf.

The bond market comes to the number already bruised. A sell-off that began on Tuesday with hotter-than-expected consumer prices carried the two-year Treasury yield up 13.96 basis points on Thursday to 4.867%, its highest since 14 October, and the ten-year to 5.105% [2][3]. Fed funds futures price 29 basis points of rate rises in December, Reuters reports, which leaves only slim odds of a half-point step [2].

The officials have not tried to talk that down. New York Fed President John Williams said on Thursday that inflation remains high and that policymakers have to take further action, according to Bloomberg [3]. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said further rises would probably be needed, Bloomberg reported [4].

So the week's headlines were about a chip company's chief executive, while the week's pricing was about the cost of money. A producer price index is unglamorous by design; it is a survey of what firms actually charged. That is exactly why markets watch it. Nothing here is investment advice.
