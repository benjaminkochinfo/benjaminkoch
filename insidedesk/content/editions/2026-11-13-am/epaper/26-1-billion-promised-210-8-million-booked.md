The AI chip maker Cerebras reported third-quarter revenue of $210.8 million on Thursday, up 55% on a year earlier, and "core" revenue, its own adjusted measure, of $230.9 million, up 70% [1]. Its cloud business grew fastest: GAAP cloud and services revenue of $141.9 million was up 265% [1]. The company expects fourth-quarter core revenue of about $310 million to $330 million and raised its full-year core forecast to $940 million to $960 million; Bloomberg says the quarterly forecast exceeded analysts' estimates [1][2].

The order book is the striking line: $26.1 billion of remaining performance obligations at 30 September, and 700 megawatts of data-centre capacity secured for delivery by the end of 2027, including new capacity in Norway and Finland [1]. Desk arithmetic: the contract book is roughly 124 times the quarter's GAAP revenue.

The ledger has a second column. On a GAAP basis Cerebras lost $224.1 million from operations, an operating margin of minus 106%, and $161.2 million net; even on its own core measure the operating margin was minus 14% [1]. It holds $7.8 billion of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments [1]. The release also flags its dependence on a small number of significant customers, including OpenAI [1].

So read it as a business with a very large promise and a very real bill. A contract is not cash until it is invoiced and paid, and a forecast is the company's, not ours. On a week when one founder's departure shook the industry's leader, the competitor's numbers are a reminder that the build-out is run on contracts, not personalities.
