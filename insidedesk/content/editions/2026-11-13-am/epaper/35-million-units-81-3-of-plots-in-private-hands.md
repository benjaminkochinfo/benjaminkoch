If you own a flat, a house or a field, you filled in a form for the property tax reform. On Thursday the Federal Statistical Office published what all those forms add up to: the first statistics of property-tax values, from the first complete revaluation of land in Germany in almost 60 years, as of 1 January 2022 [1].

The count covers more than 35 million so-called economic units: about 31 million plots, built and unbuilt, and 4.4 million farms and forests [1]. Of the plots, 81.3% belong outright to a single person or a married couple; of the farms and forests, 73.6% [1]. The cities are different. In Hamburg and Berlin, companies and other business entities are the largest owner group by share of total value, at 39.4% and 39.5%; in Saarland private individuals and couples hold 77.6% [1].

Five states — Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Hamburg, Hesse and Lower Saxony — use their own valuation rules, which is why the office compares only shares, not absolute values, between states [1].

The useful part for you is the method, set out in three steps. The tax office sets a property-tax value; multiplying it by a statutory rate gives the assessment amount; your municipality then applies its own multiplier, the Hebesatz, and that produces your bill [1]. The value is for tax purposes only and can differ from what your property would sell for [1]. The first two steps are set by the tax office and by law. The third is set by your own municipality, which is where to ask if the bill surprises you.
