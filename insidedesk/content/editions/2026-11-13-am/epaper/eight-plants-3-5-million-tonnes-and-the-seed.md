Some industries can pause like a shop closing for lunch. Oilseed crushing is not one of them. Kernel, Ukraine's largest producer and exporter of sunflower oil, said on Thursday it had shut down operations at all of its oil extraction plants after Russian drones damaged production buildings, equipment and storage tanks [1][2].

The company's reasoning is an engineer's, not a politician's. Extraction is "a continuous technological cycle with an elevated fire hazard", it said, so a strike on running equipment threatens a large fire and the lives of staff [1]. The plants have been taken down under standard safety protocols, and Kernel has also suspended all seed purchases [1].

The scale: Kernel owns eight plants with combined annual capacity to process 3.5 million tonnes of sunflower seeds [1]. Two of them, in Kharkiv region, have been out of action since 2022 [1]. Before the full-scale invasion, Kernel ranked first in the world in sunflower-oil production, at about 9% of global output, and in exports, at about 12% [1].

The steadying part is in the small print. Kernel says it remains operationally and financially stable, continues to ship existing inventory from its warehouses and is meeting its obligations to Ukrainian farmers and partners [1]. In fiscal 2026 its net income rose 35% to $322 million on revenue of $4.404 billion [1].

For you: the cooking oil on your shelf is already pressed. What matters next is how long the crushers stay cold, and whether farmers who planned to sell seed to Kernel find another buyer.
