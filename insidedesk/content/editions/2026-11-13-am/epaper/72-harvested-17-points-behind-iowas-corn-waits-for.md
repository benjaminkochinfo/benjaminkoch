While the Mid-South dries out, the western Corn Belt has the opposite problem. Iowa's corn harvest was 72% complete by Sunday 8 November, 17 percentage points behind average; Nebraska's was 74%, 10 points behind [1]. From 11 September to 9 November much of the western and northern Corn Belt had 10 to 15 inches of rain [1].

September brought Iowa 7.83 inches, 4.35 above normal, its fourth-wettest September in 154 years of records; in the week to 8 November the state had 1.47 inches, more than triple the normal 0.48 [1]. A drier pattern is now helping, DTN reports, though slow-drying fields and roads will still slow the last leg [1].

A late harvest is not a lost one. It is a longer one, and the cost shows up later, in the farm accounts rather than the headlines.
