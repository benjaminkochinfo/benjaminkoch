The European Medicines Agency has begun examining the supply-chain weak points of more than 200 active substances on the EU's list of critical medicines, according to its 2026 half-year report as reported by Redacción Médica [1]. Its shortages steering group (MSSG) met six times in the first half of the year, twice as often as a year earlier, and completed two vulnerability analyses with recommendations [1]. The aim is to catch structural fragility before it becomes an empty pharmacy shelf [1].

Spain's generics association gives the plain arithmetic of fragility: the price of 40 mg furosemide, a common diuretic, has fallen 75% since 2021, while marketed presentations shrank from 19 to 11 [1]. "Shortages do not begin on the day a medicine is missing from the pharmacy," says its secretary general, Elena Casaus (our translation) [1]. Spain's health ministry agrees that "the lowest price does not always represent the lowest cost" (our translation) [1].

Fewer suppliers is an inventory problem before it is a patient problem, which is why it is worth counting now.
