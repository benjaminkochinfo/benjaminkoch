Speeches at a climate summit are written for the room. Draft texts are written for the record, and that is where to look. By day four at COP31 in Antalya, more than 100 agenda items had become 44 with some sort of draft text, 11 postponed or deferred and 48 with no text yet, Carbon Brief counts; it calls that "pretty normal" for day four [1].

The draft on the global goal on adaptation opens with bracketed, not yet agreed, language on the "era of 1.5C overshoot", which "makes adaptation more difficult" [1]. Further down sit three options for the pledge made at COP30 last year to triple adaptation finance: no text at all; moving the discussion to a separate climate finance work programme; or defining the tripling as a baseline of $40bn in 2025 rising to $120bn by 2035 [1]. The "no text" option, Carbon Brief notes, means some countries do not want finance mentioned in this track at all [1].

On cutting emissions, the draft for the mitigation work programme disagrees over whether it should run until 2030 or 2040 [1]. It also carries bracketed text on a permanent "mitigation committee", an idea pushed by Switzerland; Panama, speaking for the AILAC group, said such a body "could be helpful", while Carbon Brief's reporter heard opposition from the Arab Group and South Africa [1].

For you, the useful habit is the dull one: ignore the adjectives from the podium and watch which of the three options survives in paragraph 63. A number in a treaty text can be counted later. Silence cannot.
