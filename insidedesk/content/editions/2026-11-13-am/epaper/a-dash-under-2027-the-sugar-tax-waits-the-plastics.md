Budgets speak in dashes as well as digits. In the coalition groups' amendment for the 2027 budget, the sugar-tax revenue for 2027 is now "ein schlichter Strich", a plain dash, the FAZ reports [1]. The tax on heavily sweetened drinks moves from 1 July 2027 to 1 January 2028 [2].

It comes back with three rates per 100 millilitres: €0.25 for five to just under seven grams of total sugar, €0.30 up to just under nine grams, and €0.35 from nine grams [1]. In later years it is still expected to raise about €1.15 billion [1].

The new plastic-packaging tax will not be levied next year; the plan had been €550 a tonne from mid-2027 [2]. The FAZ puts its expected yield at €800 million in the first year and €1.5 billion a year later [1]; tagesschau cites €670 million for 2027 from the draft law [2]. The two figures differ, so treat both as estimates.

The bill arrives later. The FAZ says the forgone savings and the dropped plastics tax will weigh on the budget permanently, and that the reserve of €10.7 billion, from which the ministry wanted to save about €4 billion for 2028, could roughly halve [1]. It notes the compromise came on the same day the German Council of Economic Experts urged the government to bring spending and revenue back into line [1].

For you: the lemonade price stays as it is until 2028. The 2027 budget, about €555 billion in the draft, goes to the Bundestag for its final vote in the week from 23 November [3].
