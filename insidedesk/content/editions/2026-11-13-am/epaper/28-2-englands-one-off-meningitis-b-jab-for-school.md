After an outbreak of meningitis B in Kent and clusters in Dorset and Berkshire caused three deaths earlier this year, the UK government announced in June a one-off, time-limited programme offering young adults two doses of a MenB vaccine [1]. UK Health Security Agency figures now show just 28.2% of 18- and 19-year-olds have had a first dose [1].

The deadline for that first dose is 31 December; second doses, given at least 28 days later, remain available until 31 March 2027 [1]. Those who start tend to finish: almost 70% of first-dose recipients have already had a second [1]. Eligibility covers everyone born between 1 September 2007 and 31 August 2008, plus under-25s who started university or residential further education for the first time this autumn [1]. UKHSA says freshers face a risk about seven times higher than peers who do not go to university [1].

"MenB vaccines can't stop all cases, but they are still highly effective," said Prof Andrew Pollard of the Oxford Vaccine Group, who called the low uptake "very worrying" [1]. Dr Eliza Gil of the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine noted that invasive MenB typically peaks from December to March [1]. Some experts argue for reconsidering the December cut-off, and pharmacists for an annual campaign in place of a one-off [1].

The overall risk remains low, Dr Gil stresses [1]. But a vaccine offer is only as good as the appointment book. If someone in your household is eligible, the useful thing to do this week is boring: check the dates and book.
