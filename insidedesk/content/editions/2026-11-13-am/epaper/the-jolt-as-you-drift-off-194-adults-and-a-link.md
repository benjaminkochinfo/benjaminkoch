Researchers at Paris Lodron University Salzburg surveyed 194 adults in Austria and Germany online: 96 who had abrupt "sleep starts", or hypnic jerks, plus at least one other symptom weekly, and 98 controls [1]. The sleep-starts group reported poorer sleep quality, more severe insomnia, more unhelpful beliefs about sleep, and higher anxiety and depressive symptoms [1]. Together with age and gender, those factors were associated with 26.9% of the differences in how often people had sleep starts [1]. Participants reported more sleep starts in periods of stress and emotional strain; lack of sleep mattered less [1].

The caveats are the authors' own: cross-sectional self-report, and home-made questionnaire items that "now need to be confirmed with validated instruments and objective sleep recordings," says first author Tanja Grünberger [1]. "For most people, sleep starts are a harmless feature of falling asleep," says senior author Manuel Schabus [1]. If yours come with real distress, that is worth mentioning to your GP.
