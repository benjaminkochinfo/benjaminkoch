On Wednesday the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.88% and the S&P 500 1.11%, after US consumer prices for September came in hotter than expected [1]. On Thursday the S&P 500 rose 0.33% to 7,744.06 [2]. That is the number on the evening news. It is not the number that describes the day.

The equal-weighted S&P 500 rose 1.36% and the Russell 2000 of smaller companies 1.38% [2]. Healthcare gained 1.86% and financials 1.38%, while Nvidia fell 1.84% and the semiconductor ETF 1.04% [2]. The Dow, which owns less of the AI trade, rose 1.08% [3]. Money did not leave the market. It left the most crowded room in it.

[[chart:ust10-above-five]]

The reason has a number. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.00% on 15 September, its first close at that level this year, and at 5.31% on 5 October [4]. A builder that borrows to put up data centres pays for that number before it sells a single hour of compute. On Thursday, after producer prices rose 6.3% on the year, the highest since December 2022, yields actually fell, briefly below 5.04% [2]. Rates gave the market a day off, not a verdict.

The desk reading: a rotation is not a crash, and a cap-weighted index is a weighting decision, not a census. If your pension or savings plan tracks the headline index, you own the crowded room in its full size. That is a fact about your portfolio, not a recommendation to change it.
