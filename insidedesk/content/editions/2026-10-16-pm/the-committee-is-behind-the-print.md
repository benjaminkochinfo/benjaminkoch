Eurostat confirmed this morning that euro-area inflation was 3.8% in September, up from 3.2% in August [1]. Energy prices were 18.9% higher than a year earlier. Strip out energy, food, alcohol and tobacco and the rate was 2.5%; services ran at 3.2% [1].

The ECB's deposit rate is 2.50%, after increases that took effect in June and September [2]. Against headline inflation, that is a real rate of about minus 1.3 points. If you think the committee is asleep, read what it says it is doing.

[[chart:ea-hicp-vs-dfr]]

"We do not react to energy prices, we react if we see risks of higher energy prices becoming embedded in inflation," Christine Lagarde told the European Parliament's economic committee on 28 September [3]. Her evidence that it is not yet embedded: compensation per employee grew 3.3% in the second quarter, down from 3.6% in the first, and most measures of longer-term inflation expectations stand at around 2% [3]. And a quieter argument: long-term interest rates "have risen notably", which she said will slow growth and reduce pass-through by more than the September projections assumed [3]. The bond market is doing some of the committee's work for it.

That is a coherent position, and also a bet. It holds as long as two dull prints stay dull: wages and core. Core edged up from 2.4% to 2.5% in September [1]. Not a break; a lean. The next decision is on 29 October [4]. The desk will be reading the wage line before the press conference, not after.
