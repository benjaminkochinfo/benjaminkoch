EU leaders met in Brussels on Thursday and Friday. On the first day they adopted conclusions on competitiveness, the next long-term budget and enlargement [1]. Podium coverage will lead with "competitiveness". The print has three different kinds of sentence, and they are worth sorting.

**Dates.** On the 2028–2034 budget, leaders invited President António Costa to take talks forward and stressed that an agreement before the end of 2026 would allow the legal acts to be adopted in 2027, so EU funding reaches beneficiaries without interruption from January 2028 [1][2]. That is a deadline with a reason attached, which is the strongest kind. Leaders also asked lawmakers to close the automotive package and two other files by the end of 2026 [1].

**Invitations.** On energy, leaders invited the Commission to examine "and, where necessary, propose" further short-term measures against "soaring energy prices", taking member states' different circumstances into account [1]. They welcomed a plan to prolong the temporary state-aid framework for the most exposed industries and a proposal to postpone some obligations in the methane regulation [1]. Welcoming and inviting are useful verbs. They are not yet law.

**Principles.** Enlargement is "a geostrategic investment", candidates must keep reforming, and the EU must prepare itself [1]. True, and undated, apart from ongoing work on Montenegro's accession treaty [1].

Institutions beat influencers on any horizon longer than a week, but only if you read the institution in its own register. If you run a business or a household budget exposed to EU energy policy, the date-bearing sentences are the ones to plan around. The rest is direction, not timetable.
