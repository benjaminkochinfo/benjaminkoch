While French politicians argued on Monday about blocking social networks, the European Commission said something more modest and more useful.

Its spokesman Markus Lammert called the arson, looting and attacks on people and public buildings "inacceptables", and in the same breath asked France to protect the right to demonstrate peacefully: those who march, he said, must not be lumped in with those who commit violence [1]. France 24 summarised the line the same way [2].

The digital half was drier still. The Commission said it was in "close contact" with Arcom, the French regulator, and ready to help make sure platforms meet their obligations, notably the ban on spreading incitement to violence [1]. Digital spokesman Thomas Regnier recalled that when platforms suspect criminal acts may be committed, they are obliged to inform the authorities proactively, and said a further meeting with Arcom was planned for Monday [1].

Gabriel Attal had floated a "blocage temporaire, ciblé" of some platforms on the same day [1]. Brussels did not take up that idea; it pointed to duties already on the books.

That is the institutional answer in miniature: no new emergency, just the existing rulebook, a regulator and a meeting. Unexciting. Enforceable.
