On Monday, Merck KGaA of Darmstadt agreed to buy Silence Therapeutics, a biotech company based in London, for $20 per ADS in cash. The deal gives Silence an equity value of about $1.3bn and an enterprise value of $1.1bn [1]. The price is an 82% premium to Silence's 30-day average share price [1].

The asset is a treatment for patients. Divesiran is an siRNA drug for polycythemia vera, a rare blood cancer in which the body makes too many red blood cells [1]. Many patients today are treated by regular phlebotomy, which means having blood drawn. In the Phase 2 SANRECO study, 88% of patients on divesiran responded, against 19% on placebo. Over weeks 0 to 36, patients on the drug needed an average of 0.2 phlebotomies, against 2.1 on placebo [1]. It is injected under the skin once every three months, and the company reports no new safety findings [1]. The next step is a Phase 3 trial.

The deal is due to close in the first quarter of 2027. It still needs approval from Silence's shareholders, regulators and a UK court [1]. Merck says it will pay for it with new debt [1]. Silence has about 80 staff in the UK, the US and Germany [1].

Phase 2 is a promise, not a cure. But a European buyer paying for a European lab's trial data is how dull pipeline work reaches the pharmacy shelf.
