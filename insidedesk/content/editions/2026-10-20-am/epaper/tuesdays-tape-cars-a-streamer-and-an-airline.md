This week, 70 S&P 500 companies report. Of the roughly 45 that have reported so far this season, 85% beat earnings estimates, according to FactSet data cited by CNBC [1]. Tuesday comes before the big AI names.

General Motors reports before the US open. Analysts polled by LSEG expect earnings per share up nearly 30% on flat revenue, after vehicle sales fell 5.5% last quarter [1]. Netflix reports after the close. It is expected to show about 40% earnings growth, and last week it announced plans to cut about 1,000 jobs, roughly 6% of its staff, and replaced its chief content officer [1]. CNBC notes that Netflix shares have struggled after five straight reports [1]. United Airlines also reports after the bell. Analysts expect revenue growth of more than 15% but lower earnings per share, after fuel costs tied to the US-Iran war hit last quarter's results [1].

Then comes Wednesday. LSEG consensus has Meta's earnings per share up about 460% and Tesla's down 14.5%, according to CNBC [1]. Before then, keep the order in mind: tonight's reports show how costs and staffing are going, and Wednesday's will show whether the AI spending is paying off. For both, the outlook matters more than the results. Read the guidance verbs.
