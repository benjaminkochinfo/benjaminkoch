The clip looked like a confession. Two police officers at a looted Lacoste shop in Limoges pick up white trainers and stow them in their motorbike cases, glancing around. On Facebook it was viewed nearly three million times; one online outlet that relayed it under a "police looting" headline has since changed the title [1].

On Monday the paperwork arrived. The national police account said the officers acted on the instruction of a judicial police officer to secure items in a flagrante delicto investigation [1]. The Limoges prosecutor "fermement démenti" the allegations: the officers had been ordered to take items immediately reachable from the window as evidence in an aggravated-theft inquiry, and those items have since been returned to the manager [1]. The manager told franceinfo he went to the police station on Sunday and got some trainers back, including the white pair seen in the video [1]. The prosecutor says an investigation for false accusation ("dénonciation calomnieuse") will be opened against the video's authors [2].

A penal-law professor told franceinfo that seizing exhibits is a normal act in such an inquiry, provided it is recorded [1].

The video was quick. The correction took a day and needed a prosecutor, a police account and a shopkeeper. That ratio is the problem to work on — and the shopkeeper's word is the detail to remember.
