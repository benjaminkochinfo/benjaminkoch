South Korea's stock market fell hard again on Tuesday morning. The Kospi was down 3.51% and the smaller Kosdaq 4.14% in early trade [2]. SK Hynix lost 5.56% and Samsung Electronics 2.64% [2]. That happened on the morning after the Philadelphia semiconductor index in New York rose 1.6% and ended an eight-day losing streak [2].

The reason given is ordinary commerce. Newsquawk reported that large US technology buyers, including Apple and Tesla, are pushing for lower memory-chip prices and naming the Chinese suppliers CXMT and YMTC as alternatives [1]. On that account, the index has fallen more than 17% in the first half of October, the steepest drop among major global markets [1].

A buyer asking for a discount is not the same thing as a buyer walking away. The contract price is the document that settles it. Until memory contracts are actually repriced, today's selling is a forecast of a negotiation. You can check the outcome later in quarterly reports from Seoul, which carry both volumes and prices.
