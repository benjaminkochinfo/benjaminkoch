Germany's pension argument has moved from slogans to what counts as a contribution year. According to Deutschlandfunk, citing a Bild report, the CDU/CSU parliamentary group is discussing a compromise on retirement without deductions for people with very long insurance records [1].

The reported idea would keep retirement without deductions after 45 contribution years in substance. However, time spent raising children or caring for relatives might count only partly, or not at all [1]. That could mean many workers need noticeably more years before they can retire early without losing money [1]. Bild quotes an unnamed Union MP who called it a serious concession to the Social Democrats. The labour ministry, run by the SPD under its new minister Schweitzer, would not comment [1].

So the headline promise survives, but some of the years it relied on may not. For you, the question is not "45 years, yes or no?" It is which entries on your Rentenversicherung account would still count. That account statement is already in your drawer. It is worth reading before the bill is written.

None of this is law, or even a draft. It is a reported discussion inside one coalition group. Nothing here is advice. Wait for the draft and its definitions.
