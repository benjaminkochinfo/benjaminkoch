Iran has attacked at least 15 container ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz in the past ten days, according to the Guardian's count [1]. Some were hit by drones and others by mines. On Sunday one ship was struck by a rocket-propelled grenade fired from a small boat north-east of the Saudi port of Jubail [1]. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre (UKMTO) recorded 12 attacks in the seven days to Friday 16 October [1].

The more useful figure is how many ships still got through. UKMTO's weekly overview counted 254 transits, 125 outbound and 129 inbound, in the week to 14 October [1]. That is roughly 70% below pre-conflict levels [1]. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say they may attack any ship that crosses without their permission or with its transponder switched off [1]. Tehran has also offered to reopen the strait within six days in return for sanctions relief [1].

On the diplomatic side, Russian foreign minister Sergei Lavrov visited Tehran. He offered to mediate and proposed a China–Russia–Iran summit on security in the strait [1]. That is a proposal for a meeting, not an agreement. The weekly UKMTO count will show whether anything changes at sea. You should trust it more than any statement from the region's capitals.
