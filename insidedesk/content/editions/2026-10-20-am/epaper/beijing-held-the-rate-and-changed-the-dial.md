China kept its one-year loan prime rate at 3.0% and its five-year rate at 3.5% on Tuesday, as expected [1]. It is the 17th month in a row without a change; both were last cut, by 10 basis points, in May 2025 [1]. The seven-day reverse repo rate, at 1.40%, remains the main policy rate [1].

The governor's comments on Monday mattered more than the hold. Speaking at the Financial Street Forum in Beijing, People's Bank of China (PBoC) Governor Pan Gongsheng said the bank would study moving its main operations to an overnight tenor when the time is right. That would make the overnight reverse repo rate the main policy rate [1]. Financially strong companies would be able to price floating-rate loans off the overnight rate or the LPR. Household and small-business lending would stay mainly tied to the LPR [1].

Separately, the China Securities Regulatory Commission approved 18 actively managed ETFs. Newsquawk said they could channel tens of billions of yuan into A-shares [2].

This is a change to the mechanics of policy rather than a stimulus. For a mortgage holder in China, the five-year LPR is still the rate that sets the payment, and it has not moved. The PBoC's next real signal will be the level at which it sets the new overnight rate [1].
