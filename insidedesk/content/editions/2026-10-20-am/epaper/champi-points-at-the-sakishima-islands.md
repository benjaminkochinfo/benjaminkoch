Typhoon Champi, No. 30 of the season in Japan's count, was east of the Philippines at 09:00 Japan time on Tuesday (02:00 Berlin). It was moving west-north-west at 20 km/h with a central pressure of 945 hPa and maximum winds near the centre of 50 m/s, gusting to 70 m/s [1]. It is forecast to reach the sea south of Okinawa on Thursday and Friday [1].

The useful numbers are the Japan Meteorological Agency's probabilities of entering the storm-wind zone within five days: Ishigaki 94%, Yonaguni 89%, Miyako 74%, and only 4% for the northern and central parts of Okinawa's main island [1]. Swells have already reached Okinawa. Seas of 5 metres or more are expected on Wednesday, and gusts above 40 m/s are possible around Friday [1]. Beyond the weekend the forecast spread widens: some model runs head for Taiwan and mainland China, others turn north towards western Japan [1].

The season itself is a statistic. Champi is the 30th typhoon of the year. That total was last reached in 2013, and 2026 is only the third year since 1951 with a typhoon in every month from January to October [1]. You cannot move a typhoon. A 94% figure two days ahead is enough to move boats, shutters and ferry timetables.
