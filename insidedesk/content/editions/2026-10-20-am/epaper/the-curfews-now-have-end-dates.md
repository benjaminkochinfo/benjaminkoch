Outside Paris, Monday's orders came with something reassuring: an expiry.

In Bordeaux, prefect Sophie Brocas imposed a curfew on minors not accompanied by a legal guardian, from 22:00 to 07:00, starting Tuesday 20 October and running until the morning of Monday 26 October, on rue Sainte-Catherine, rue de la Porte-Dijeaux, cours de l'Intendance and three more streets and squares hit by Saturday's looting [1].

In Nice, the Alpes-Maritimes prefecture set a 21:00–06:00 curfew for minors in the city centre, which mayor Éric Ciotti said would last only until Sunday 25 October [1]. A second Nice order bans carrying firework mortars, pyrotechnics, fuel in small containers and flammable or corrosive liquids; ski goggles, gas masks and helmets are prohibited too [1].

In the Nord, the prefect's order covers parts of Lille, Douai, Wattignies and Denain from 20:00 to 06:00 for two nights — Monday into Tuesday and Tuesday into Wednesday — and is aimed at minors, according to actu.fr; any extension depends on how things go [2]. Lyon's Presqu'île curfew ran 21:00 to 06:00 for all persons on the street, with bars, cinemas and public transport left open [1].

Desk arithmetic: Bordeaux's order covers six nights, Tuesday through Sunday. An emergency measure that names its last night is easier to accept — and easier to hold to account.
