A shut market is a strange tool for public health, but on Monday North Kivu used one.

**The day.** Radio Okapi reported that economic activity and schools stopped in Beni, Butembo, Oïcha, Lubero-centre and Kasindi-Lubiriha on Monday 19 October [1]. Schools, markets, small shops and fuel stations closed. Few motorbike taxis ran, and police set up checkpoints to enforce the measure [1]. A provincial notice signed on 9 October set the rules. Health facilities, pharmacies, Ebola response teams and security services kept working [2]. The day was agreed with civil society, religious groups and business, and was meant both to honour the dead and to win support for the response [2].

**The numbers behind it.** Governor Évariste Somo Kakule cited the rise in cases. "As of 17 October 2026, we have 1,252 deaths. The case fatality rate is estimated at 60%," he said (our translation) [1]. The provincial count on 8 October was 1,859 confirmed cases, 1,110 deaths and 496 active cases [2]. ECDC's page, updated on 19 October, lists North Kivu at 2,036 confirmed cases and 1,156 deaths from 17 of the province's 34 health zones, using data up to 17 October [3].

**Desk arithmetic (labelled).** ECDC's figures give a fatality share of about 57% among confirmed cases in North Kivu. The governor's 1,252 deaths are higher than ECDC's 1,156 confirmed deaths. The provincial figure is probably counted on a wider basis, so do not treat the two as one series.

**What it is for.** Radio Okapi says some health zones still resist the response [1]. That is why the day matters more than its closures. ECDC says 80% of identified contacts across the affected provinces are under follow-up [3]. The remaining fifth is where outbreaks grow. A vaccination campaign began in Beni on Friday 16 October [1]. Trust brings people to the door when the tracing team knocks, and a day of shared mourning helps build that trust.
