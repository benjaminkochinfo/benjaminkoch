Monday's settlements went in opposite directions. WTI for November delivery rose $2.25, or 2.22%, to $103.66 a barrel on Nymex. Brent for December fell 31 cents to $109.55 on ICE [1]. Brent had traded as high as $110.70 during the session, its highest level since 20 May [2].

The US number needs care. The November WTI contract expires today [2]. At 02:10 Berlin time it was quoted at $103.32, while the more active December contract was at $98.08 [2]. Desk arithmetic: that is a gap of about $5.24 between two consecutive months of the same crude. A gap that wide usually means oil is scarce right now, not in December. By 04:20 Berlin, Brent was 21 cents higher at $109.76 [3].

Two stories are pulling on the price. On one side is the US executive order waiving Russia-related sanctions and tariffs. On the other are new Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi energy sites, which Saudi authorities had not confirmed [3]. Goldman Sachs has set out scenarios with oil at $120 if attacks on shipping escalate and $80 if exports return to normal [3]. Those are scenarios, not forecasts, and the bank presents them that way.

European gas was calmer. The Dutch TTF benchmark ended Monday virtually flat at €87.88 per megawatt-hour, still up about 210% on the year [4]. ICE low-sulphur gasoil, Europe's diesel and heating-oil benchmark, was at $1,470 a tonne at 04:46 Berlin [5]. For your fuel bill, gasoil is the price to watch, because it arrives with a delivery date.

Nothing here is advice; these are published prices with their times.
