Storm Austen, the first autumn storm of the year, crossed north-west France on Friday [1]. By Friday midday the grid operator Enedis counted about 288,600 homes without power: 148,500 in Brittany, 88,800 in Pays de la Loire, 49,600 in Normandy and 7,800 in Hauts-de-France [1]. Météo-France recorded gusts of 161 km/h at the Pointe du Raz in Finistère [1]. Firefighters in the department had intervened 384 times since the start of the episode, and three people in Brittany suffered electric shocks [1]. No serious injuries had been reported by midday [1].

**The trains and planes.** Rail traffic was halted as a precaution on Normandy's lines and partly in Brittany and the north, with disruption expected on Saturday too; flights at Roissy, Orly and Nantes were delayed or cancelled, and DFDS cancelled Dover–Calais crossings until Saturday morning [1].

**The river.** Several hundred kilometres east, a quieter number moved, and in the right direction. The Rhine gauge at Kaub has stayed at or above 25 cm all day on Friday, between 25 and 29 cm, for a daily mean so far of 27.3 cm by 16:15 [2]. Twenty-five centimetres was the lowest daily reading of the 2010–2020 reference decade, set on 22 October 2018 [2][4]. Desk arithmetic: the thirty daily means before Friday all sat below it, five of them below zero. Discharge stood at 547 cubic metres a second at 16:00 [3]. This morning's edition asked whether the day would hold. By 16:15, it had.

[[chart:kaub-above-2018-23pm]]

**What it is not.** It is not a recovery. The gauge is still 51 cm short of the 77 cm reference at which the shipping channel depth is guaranteed, the equivalent water level GlW, with a mean water level of 208 cm [4]. One day above a line is a data point, not a trend.

**The constructive reading.** Both stories run on public measurement. Enedis counts homes off the grid by region; Météo-France publishes gusts by station; the federal waterways office publishes the Rhine every fifteen minutes, free to use [1][2]. Repair crews, barge operators and insurers plan on exactly these numbers. A storm is frightening. A storm that is measured is a job list.
