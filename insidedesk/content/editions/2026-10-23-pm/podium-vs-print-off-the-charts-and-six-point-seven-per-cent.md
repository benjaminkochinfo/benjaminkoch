**The podium.** Speaking to CNBC in Berlin on Thursday, Jensen Huang said: "If you look at the fundamentals, the demand for the neo clouds is off the charts" [1]. Neoclouds are the specialised firms that rent out AI computing power. He went on: "What is missing in the middle is the working capital to fuel that growth … because there's just so much demand across so many sectors of AI, there's a scarcity of capital" [1].

**The print.** By the close the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had fallen 6.72% to 10,731.98 [2]. Lam Research lost 11.76% after its results, AMD 8.1%, Nvidia 4.4% and Broadcom 4.31% [2]. Meta fell 7% after reporting negative free cash flow, and the Nasdaq lost 2.6%, its worst day since early June [2]. CoreWeave, which CNBC calls the poster child for borrowing to build data centres, dropped 10% [3].

**The gap.** Mr Huang made two statements, and investors weighed the second. Demand that has to be financed by borrowing is demand with a credit risk attached. Dario Perkins of TS Lombard put it plainly: until recently hyperscalers invested out of cash flow, but now "debt is now the marginal source of funding" [1]. He expects AI companies to issue around $700 billion of new bonds in 2027 [1]. If credit markets fund the margin, he warned, someone outside the AI ecosystem can "turn off the credit taps" [1].

**The other side.** The businesses are not failing. Meta's revenue grew 26% in the September quarter, and FactSet expects the "Magnificent Seven" to report earnings growth of about 33% [1]. On Friday chip stocks such as Micron and AMD were up more than 1% in early trading [3]. Ed Yardeni called the trade a bubble but said "taking some air out" is "a good thing" [3].

**Verdict.** "Off the charts" describes an order book. Minus 6.72% describes what the market thinks the order book will cost to fill. Both can be true. The dull number that settles it will not be a keynote; it will be interest rates on the next bond issue. Nothing here is advice.
