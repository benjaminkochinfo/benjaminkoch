At the initiative of Germany and Turkey, the foreign ministers of Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Italy met in Istanbul on Friday for the first time with Turkey, Egypt, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia [1]. The subject was freedom of navigation [1].

The Foreign Office spelt out the stakes in numbers. Around 12% of global maritime trade passes through the Bab al-Mandab strait, where the Houthis have stepped up attacks since July; about a fifth of the oil consumed worldwide passes through Hormuz [1]. In the Black Sea, Russian attacks have brought Ukrainian grain exports to a virtual standstill since July, on a route that once carried around 30% of global wheat trade [1].

"We do not accept that Iran and the Houthi militia are blackmailing world trade," Johann Wadephul said [1]. The release also points to a precedent: the UN-mediated Black Sea Grain Initiative of 2022 showed that agreements can improve access to food [1].

A first meeting in a new format produces a photo, not a corridor. But the format itself is the news: the buyers and the regional powers in one room, about routes rather than slogans. That is how the 2022 grain deal started too.
