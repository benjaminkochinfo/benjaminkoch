Meta fell 7% on Thursday after reporting negative free cash flow and announcing non-voting shares that could help fund AI investment, Reuters reported [1].

That second detail is the dull one that matters. A company that pays for its data centres out of profits asks nothing of anyone. A company that issues new shares, or borrows, is asking investors to fund the build-out directly [1]. Shares without votes let it raise that money without giving the new owners a say.

"The big question" is "the (return on investment) on all the spending," Matthew Maley of Miller Tabak told Reuters [1]. For chipmakers it matters because their growth depends on such customers spending at record levels [1].

Nothing about this is a scandal. Raising money is what capital markets are for. It does mark a change of regime: the AI build-out is moving from cash to capital. Nothing here is advice.
