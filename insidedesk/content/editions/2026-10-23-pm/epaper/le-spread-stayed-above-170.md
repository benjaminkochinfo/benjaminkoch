French government bonds had a calmer Friday. The ten-year yield fell 4 basis points to 5.05%, Reuters reported [1]. That still leaves it up nearly 27 basis points this week, the most of any major euro-area country and the most in a week since late May [1].

The premium investors demand to hold French debt rather than German Bunds, known in the market as "le spread", stayed above 170 basis points after reaching its widest since the euro-zone debt crisis [1]. Reuters cited concerns over France's finances and political uncertainty [1]. Desk arithmetic: 170 basis points is 1.7 percentage points a year, on every euro the French state borrows for ten years.

Elsewhere the week ran the other way. Markets stripped almost a full quarter-point of US rate rises for next year, and the chance of a December rise by the European Central Bank fell to 69% from 90% earlier in the week, according to Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid [1].

A spread is the bond market's opinion, paid in interest. It falls when a budget is passed and kept. No speech has ever done the job of a dull, adopted budget law. Nothing here is advice.
