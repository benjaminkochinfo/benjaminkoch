The US Department of Energy designated helium a critical material on Friday, under the Energy Act of 2020 [1]. Its notice calls the gas "non-substitutable" for chipmaking, for leak-testing gas pipelines and for quantum computing; the last addition to the list was steelmaking coal, in May 2025 [2]. The designation brings closer federal monitoring and opens more federal financing to helium producers [3].

Europe reads it from the buying side. Qatar's exports have been disrupted by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, Russia restricted exports in April, and China, a transit route for Russian helium, imposed a temporary export ban in July [4]. The EU has banned direct imports of Russian helium since 2024 [4].

The department's own release names the real bottleneck at home: limited refining capacity and lengthy permitting, not a lack of gas in the ground [1]. That is the useful part. A list does not refine anything; a permit desk that clears its pile does. Watch the dull numbers that follow, the plants approved and the cylinders delivered, rather than the statements. Nothing here is advice.
