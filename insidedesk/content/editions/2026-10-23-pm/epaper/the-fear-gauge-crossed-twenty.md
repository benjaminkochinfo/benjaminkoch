The Cboe Volatility Index, the VIX, rose another 6% on Friday to 20.24, after jumping 14% on Thursday to 19.03 [1]. It measures what traders expect from the S&P 500 over the next 30 days, as priced into options [1]. Last week it rose 34.7% to 19.99, its largest one-week gain since early June [1]. This week it is on track to close above 20 for the first time since early June [1].

Twenty is the number headlines like, because it is round. It is not a threshold where anything breaks. It says that buying protection costs more than it did a month ago, which, after a 6.72% fall in chip stocks and Brent near $115, is not surprising.

The more useful reading is what moved with it. On Friday morning the Dow was down 805 points, or 1.5%, while the Nasdaq was off only 0.3% and chipmakers such as Micron and AMD were up more than 1% [1]. Selling had spread from technology to the rest of the market, driven by oil [1]. On the week, the S&P 500 was still heading for a gain of less than 0.1% [1].

A fear gauge that rises when fear rises is working. Nothing here is advice.
