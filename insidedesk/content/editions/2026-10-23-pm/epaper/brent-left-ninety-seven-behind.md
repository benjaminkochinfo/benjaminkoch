Brent crude rose 2.7% to $116.24 a barrel on Friday morning in New York, according to the Associated Press, up from less than $97 at the start of October and at its highest since March [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is a rise of about a fifth in three weeks.

Reuters tied the move to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and to reports that the US and Israel had drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections [2]. European natural gas prices rose another 4% on Friday, leaving them almost 30% higher so far this month [2].

The two moves reach you differently. Oil reaches a German household through diesel, petrol and heating oil, within days. Gas reaches most homes through tariffs fixed for months, which is why a 30% move on the wholesale market is a warning for next year's contract rather than this month's bill.

Markets price fear quickly and peace slowly. When either moves, the dull defence is the same: know when your contract ends, and what it costs per unit.
