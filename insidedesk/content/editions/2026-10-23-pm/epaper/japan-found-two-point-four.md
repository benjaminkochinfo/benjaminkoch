Japan's core inflation, excluding fresh food, rose to 2.1% in September from 1.7% in August, its highest this year and in line with a Reuters poll [1]. The "core-core" measure, which also excludes energy and is the Bank of Japan's preferred gauge, was 2.4% [1]. Rice prices fell 20.6% from a year earlier [1]. For a country that spent decades trying to get prices to rise at all, the boring news is that the gauge is now doing what it was built to show.
