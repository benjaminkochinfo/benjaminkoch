The Kuwaiti-flagged tanker Al Dasma was struck by a projectile of unknown type on Wednesday night, about 11 nautical miles west of Bukha in Oman, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre (UKMTO), reported by the shipping journal Hansa [1]. A fire broke out and was put out; the ship then anchored [1].

India's shipping minister, Sarbananda Sonowal, said the 21 Indian seafarers on board are unharmed [1]. The Aframax tanker, built in 2014 and owned by the Kuwait Oil Tanker Company, had already been attacked and damaged on 7 September south-east of the strait, according to the International Maritime Organization [1]. No pollution has been reported so far [1].

The IMO count is the number to keep. By 21 October it had confirmed 119 security-related incidents in the Middle East, in which 24 seafarers lost their lives [1].

Every tanker headline is also a crew of real people. The machinery that protects them is dull: UKMTO alerts, IMO counts, a minister on the phone to a ship manager. When you read "ship hit" today, read the second line: 21 people, unharmed.
