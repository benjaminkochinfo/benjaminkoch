Chancellor Friedrich Merz will travel to Singapore, Japan and South Korea next week for talks on economic and security policy, the deputy government spokesman, Steffen Meyer, said on Friday [1]. He leaves Berlin on Monday and returns on Friday, with a business delegation [1].

The order is set. Singapore on Tuesday, with the prime minister, the president and a speech; Tokyo on Wednesday, for the prime minister and a German-Japanese economic summit; then Seoul, where he will meet the president and open the 19th Asia-Pacific Conference of German Business [1].

"We share with all three countries a commitment to open, rules-based trade and a desire to strengthen economic resilience and security, particularly with regard to global supply chains," Mr Meyer said [1]. He named AI as one area for closer work [1].

The trip comes as DW describes plunging national ratings for the chancellor's CDU, with his Bavarian allies meeting in Munich this weekend [1]. Supply chains are not glamorous. They are, however, what a week of fuel prices and chip sell-offs has been about. A chancellor who spends five days on them is spending them on the right thing.
