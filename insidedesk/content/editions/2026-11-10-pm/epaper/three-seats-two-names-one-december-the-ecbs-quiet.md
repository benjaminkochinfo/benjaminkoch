Euro-area finance ministers spent Monday evening on a personnel question that will shape your mortgage rate for years. Eurogroup President Kyriakos Pierrakakis said two candidates have been nominated to succeed Christine Lagarde as president of the European Central Bank: Klaas Knot, former head of the Dutch central bank, and Pablo Hernández de Cos, head of the Bank for International Settlements and former governor of the Bank of Spain [1]. Lagarde announced on 29 October that she will step down in February, nine months before her term ends [1].

The presidency is not the only vacancy. Isabel Schnabel, who oversees market operations, is leaving for the International Monetary Fund, and Chief Economist Philip Lane's term ends in May 2027 [1]. "It is possible that all three appointments will be considered together at the level of European leaders in order to facilitate consensus," Pierrakakis said, adding he expects the process to finish by the end of the year [1].

Under EU rules, the European Council appoints board members by reinforced qualified majority, on a recommendation from the Council of the EU, after consulting the European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council [1]. It is slow and it is meant to be. The backdrop is not calm: Eurostat's flash estimate put euro-area inflation at 4% in October, up from 3.8% in September [1].
