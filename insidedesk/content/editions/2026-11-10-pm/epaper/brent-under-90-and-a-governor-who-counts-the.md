Brent crude futures fell more than 3.2% on Tuesday and dipped below $90 a barrel for the first time since late August, Reuters reported, as investors pinned their hopes on possible peace talks between the US and Iran [1]. The trigger was President Donald Trump saying he plans to hold off on new military strikes [1]. Lower oil helped carry the STOXX 600 to within 1% of its all-time high before the US inflation figures trimmed the gains [1].

The more useful line of the day came from a central banker, not a trader. In London, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said that even with more tankers passing through the Strait of Hormuz, the traffic "is moving at great expense": insurance and the ship-to-ship transfers used to guard against attacks are reportedly adding $20 to $30 a barrel to oil prices [2]. He also said diesel, constrained by limited refining capacity, is not expected to ease as readily as crude [2].

Desk arithmetic: if the reported $20–30 premium is right, roughly a fifth to a third of a barrel priced just under $90 is paperwork, cover and handling rather than oil. That is the part a ceasefire headline does not remove on its own; underwriters and shipowners remove it, slowly, one voyage at a time. Waller added that he was "pleased" to see energy prices off their recent highs, but said they remain "quite elevated" relative to the start of the year [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
