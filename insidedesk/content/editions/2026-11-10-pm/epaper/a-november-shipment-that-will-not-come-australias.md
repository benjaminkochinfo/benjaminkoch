Benzathine benzylpenicillin is an old, cheap antibiotic that treats syphilis and strep infections and prevents rheumatic heart disease [1]. In Australia it is running short again. Pfizer has advised that the 1.2 million-unit Bicillin L-A order expected for November is unlikely to be available; that strength stays in shortage until 31 March 2027, and the 600,000-unit syringe until 30 November 2027 [1][2].

The regulator's answer is unglamorous and sensible: reserve remaining local stock for children, use the approved imports from Portugal, Spain and the UK, which are subsidised but need mixing before injection (the UK one is currently unavailable), and order only for immediate need rather than stockpile [1]. That is what shortage management looks like: a list, a priority order and a needle-gauge table.
