Caixin's summary of Tuesday's trade data said much of China's export growth came from rising prices rather than volumes [2]. The customs product table shows it line by line. From January to October, China shipped 48,097 tonnes of rare-earth ores, metals and compounds, 8.7% less than a year earlier, for $584.3 million, 46.4% more [1]. Integrated circuits: 3.2% more pieces, 111.1% more value [1]. Fertiliser exports fell 18.8% by weight and 32.5% by value [1].

Desk arithmetic: rare earths averaged about $12,150 a tonne this year against about $7,570 last year; a chip averaged about $1.13 against $0.55. Fewer tonnes at higher prices is how scarcity shows up in an invoice, long before it shows up in a speech.
