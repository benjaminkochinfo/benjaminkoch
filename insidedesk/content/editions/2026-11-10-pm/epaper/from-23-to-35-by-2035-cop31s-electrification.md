Tuesday at COP31 was Energy and Transport Day, and it began with traffic jams: more than 68,500 registered participants and another 24,500 workers trying to reach a venue about 25 kilometres from central Antalya [1].

Once inside, the Turkish presidency pushed its Global Electrification Pledge: raise electricity's share of final energy use from about 23% today to 35% by 2035 [1]. "Electrifying the global economy is now essential," said COP31 president Murat Kurum, citing this year's energy crisis [1]. The International Energy Agency will run a tracking mechanism and says 35x35 is within reach with strong political backing [1].

Then the second number. A recent Climate Action Tracker analysis finds current policies lead to only 28% by 2035 [1]. It is also unclear who has signed: Climate Home News understands no list of backers is expected this week, and some fossil-fuel producers are expected to oppose any reference to clean sources in the final decision [1].

Smaller, concrete things also happened. An Australia-led Global Rooftop Solar Declaration aims to double solar-powered households to 300 million by 2030 [1]. A maritime group including the ports of Esbjerg and Long Beach set a goal of 20% electricity in shipping's energy demand by 2035 [1]. The Powering Past Coal Alliance launched partnerships to unlock more than $23 billion for countries drafting their first coal phase-out plans [1].

For you, the honest reading is the gap: 35% on the podium, 28% on current policy. A pledge with a tracker attached can be checked. That is worth more than one without.
