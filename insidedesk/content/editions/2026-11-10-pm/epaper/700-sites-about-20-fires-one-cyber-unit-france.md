France's foreign minister Jean-Noël Barrot says Iran's Revolutionary Guards were behind last month's cyber-attacks on refrigeration systems in major supermarket chains, POLITICO reports [1]. His ministry named the unit as Shahid Kaveh; 700 food distribution sites in France were affected [1]. Paris prosecutors say about 20 fires were started [1].

**How it worked.** "The attackers took remote control of the equipment via web interfaces to trigger abnormal defrost cycles of unlimited duration, while disabling the security alerts," the prosecutor's office said [1]. In plain words: fridges were told to warm up, and the alarms were told to stay quiet.

**The replies.** Barrot said: "We will impose sanctions at European level against those responsible" [1]. Iran's foreign ministry "categorically rejects" what it called baseless accusations, AFP reports [2]. A European Commission spokesperson acknowledged the attribution without endorsing it and said the EU stands ready "to come with a swift and united response" [2]. Belgium, investigating similar incidents in early November, says it has no evidence yet to attribute them to anyone [2].

For you, the defence is not dramatic. It is a password changed on a web interface, an alarm wired so it cannot be switched off remotely, and someone who checks the cold room at closing time.
