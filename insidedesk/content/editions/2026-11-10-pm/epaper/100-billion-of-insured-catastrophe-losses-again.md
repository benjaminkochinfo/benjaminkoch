Global insured catastrophe losses have passed $100 billion in 2026, says Steve Bowen, chief science officer at the reinsurance broker Gallagher Re: the tenth year in a row on an inflation-adjusted basis and the seventh in nominal terms [1]. The year had been comparatively quiet: $92 billion from January to September, the lowest nine-month total since 2019 and 29% below the ten-year average of $128 billion [1].

"A decade ago, this was a huge deal. Today, it is notable, but no longer enough on its own to move the needle," Bowen said, crediting better risk management, stronger capital, improved modelling and more disciplined pricing [1]. With the five-year nominal average around $150 billion, he points to $200 billion as the threshold to watch [1]. His caution is the useful part: the communities most exposed are often the least insured [1].
