Two days before the budget committee's clean-up session, the electricity tax is still the main sticking point between CDU/CSU and SPD [1][2]. A cut for all households would cost the state around €5 billion in lost revenue, the Tagesspiegel reports, and no plan to cover it has been presented [1].

**The pressure.** The state parliamentary group leaders want the tax lowered to the European minimum for everyone. Their chair, Sebastian Lechner, spoke of "noticeable relief for families, crafts, retail and small firms", Merkur reports, citing Table.Briefings [2]. CDU state premiers, led by North Rhine-Westphalia's Hendrik Wüst, are pushing in the same direction [1].

**The answer.** The SPD and the finance minister say they are open to a cut if it is properly financed [1]. "Part of honesty is that not everything in the coalition agreement is financed," the SPD's deputy parliamentary leader Wiebke Esdar told ZDF [1]. She also called a general cut untargeted, spreading relief thinly over everyone [1].

**Elsewhere on the list.** The CDU's Thorsten Frei has ruled out the planned plastic-packaging tax, which was meant to raise €670 million in 2027 [1]. The CSU wants the sugar tax delayed by a year [1]. The coalition expects the income-tax relief to come next year without fully offsetting bracket creep [1].

A tax cut is a sentence. Its financing is a page. This week, ask to see the page.
