Single record days attract attention; monthly averages pay the suppliers. Citing Energy-Charts data, top agrar reports that the German day-ahead power price averaged €70 per megawatt hour in April, €168 in October and €277 across the first ten days of November [1]. Desk arithmetic: November so far is about four times April and about 65% above October.

The magazine's explanation is the season's oldest: when wind and sun are weak, more gas and coal plants run, and they set a higher price [1]. Gas itself has eased. top agrar puts the German trading point THE at €88 per megawatt hour on 9 November, down from a peak of €105 at the end of October, and cites traders who link the fall to the chance of new talks between the US and Iran [1].

Storage is the slower worry. European gas stores are about 73% full, according to Gas Infrastructure Europe, where the five-year average for this time of year is closer to 90% [1]. German storage stands at about 59% per the Federal Network Agency, after a peak of almost 62% at the end of October; cooler weather and weak renewable output mean gas is already being withdrawn, and traders report lower Norwegian deliveries because of unplanned outages [1].

For you: wholesale prices reach household tariffs with a lag and through contracts. That is the point of contracts. If yours renews this winter, read the offer slowly and compare it with the basic-supply price before you sign. Nothing here is investment advice.
