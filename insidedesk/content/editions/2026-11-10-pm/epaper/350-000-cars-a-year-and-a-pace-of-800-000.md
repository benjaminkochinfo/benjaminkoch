The EU agreed with Beijing last month to limit imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles over the next few years, and the curbs take effect on 1 December [1]. Tuesday's trade figures show what they are up against. China's shipments to the bloc rose 24.4% in October while its imports from the EU fell more than 4%, widening the monthly surplus to $33.4 billion [1].

Brad Setser of the Council on Foreign Relations, a former US Treasury official, put the cap in numbers: 350,000 imported cars a year, above the 300,000 hybrids China exported to Europe last year but well below the roughly 800,000 annualised pace of the last three months [1]. "Europe still is a long way from a coherent trade and industrial policy with respect to autos," he said [1].

China's customs table shows the wider scale: 9.736 million motor vehicles exported from January to October, up 49.6% in units, worth $169.5 billion, up 52.2% [2]. In October alone, 1.108 million vehicles left the country [2].

Desk arithmetic: at the recent pace, the 350,000 cap is less than half of what is arriving. That does not make the agreement worthless. A cap is a measurable promise, checked at a port by customs officers, and it can be tightened by the same procedure that set it. The test is not the press release but the monthly count from December. Elsewhere, Washington and Beijing extended their broader trade truce to January, and Presidents Xi Jinping and Donald Trump are due to meet at the APEC summit in Shenzhen next week [1].
