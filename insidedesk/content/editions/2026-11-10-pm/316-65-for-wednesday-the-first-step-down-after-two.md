The day-ahead auction for Wednesday 11 November cleared at a German-Luxembourg daily average of €316.65 per megawatt hour, the SMARD series shows [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €34.83, or about 9.9%, below Tuesday's €351.48, the first fall after two record delivery days in a row [1]. It is also, on the same series, the third-highest daily average of 2026, just above the €314.85 of Thursday 29 October [1]. On 11 November 2025 the series stood at €96.89; Wednesday's price is about 3.3 times that (Desk arithmetic) [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-10pm]]

**The shape went back to evening.** Tuesday's dearest hour was 08:00. Wednesday's is 17:00 at €447.65, with 18:00 close behind at €433.59 [1]. In quarter-hours the peak is €457.77 at 17:45 [1]. The cheapest hour is 03:00 at €200.39 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the gap between dearest and cheapest hour is €247.26, narrower than Tuesday's €274.39 and far narrower than Monday's €420.91. Every hourly price for Wednesday is above €200 [1].

**Why it stays high.** The explanation has not changed since the weekend. top agrar, reading the same week's prices, calls it a "Dunkelflaute": little wind and weak sun, so more gas and coal plants run and set the price [2]. SMARD's forecast puts onshore and offshore wind together at about 99.1 gigawatt hours for Tuesday (Desk arithmetic, from [1]); a figure for Wednesday was not yet published when we checked.

**For you.** Most households pay a fixed tariff and will not see Wednesday on any bill this month; the auction sets the wholesale leg, and grid fees, taxes and levies sit on top. On a dynamic tariff, the habit flips back: the night until about 06:00 is the cheap block, and late afternoon into early evening is the one to avoid for the washing machine or the car charger [1]. A fall of €34.83 is welcome. It is not yet a trend. Nothing here is investment advice.
