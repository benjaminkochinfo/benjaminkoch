The first trilateral session between Ukraine, Russia and the United States in Abu Dhabi ended on Tuesday afternoon. Russian state media put it at about four and a half hours, with a 40-minute break roughly two hours in, Anadolu reports [1]. Talks began at around 09:15 CET, and Ukraine's Foreign Intelligence Service says they resume on Wednesday morning [1][3].

**The American reading.** Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking after the G7 in France, called the talks "very productive" but said there was still a lot of work to do [3]. "We think the trilateral (format) should eventually include our European partners at the table as well," he said [2]. Jared Kushner said at the start that Washington had "narrowed the gaps", without details [2].

**The Russian reading.** Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said in Moscow that defining "clear parameters for a future security architecture, and only then moving on to resolving the actual Russia-Ukraine conflict, is absolutely reasonable" [2]. That is the same order of business US officials proposed before the talks. Agreement on sequence is not agreement on substance: RFE/RL notes Moscow has given no sign of dropping its demand for all of Donbas, including land it does not hold [2].

**The Ukrainian reading.** President Zelenskyy, in Copenhagen, said humanitarian issues such as prisoner-of-war exchanges and bringing back children were discussed, with security guarantees on the agenda [3]. Before the session, Steve Witkoff wrote after meeting European and Ukrainian officials that any arrangement must "provide credible security guarantees for Ukraine" [3]. The E3, Italy, NATO and the EU consult the US separately from the trilateral table [2].

**The night before.** Russia attacked 10 regions overnight, hitting five major substations that move electricity between regions, Ukraine's prime minister said [3].

For you: a first day without a communiqué is not a failure. Prisoner exchanges and returned children are small, countable things. If day two produces one of them, that is more than a speech would.
