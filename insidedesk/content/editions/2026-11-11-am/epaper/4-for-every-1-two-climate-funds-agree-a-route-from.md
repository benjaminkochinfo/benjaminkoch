Most summit news is a number someone hopes for. This one is a process someone will use. In Antalya on Tuesday the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and the Adaptation Fund (AF) launched an "AF-GCF Scaling-Up Framework Operational Guidance" [1]. In plain words: the two funds will jointly pick completed or advanced Adaptation Fund projects with strong results and work them up into larger investments that the GCF may finance [1]. Each project still needs the country's priority and each fund's normal approval [1].

Why it matters is in one sentence from Mikko Ollikainen, head of the Adaptation Fund: projects that have gone on to receive GCF money "have attracted an average of $4 for every $1 invested", and in half of those cases the same partner did the scaling-up [1]. GCF chief Mafalda Duarte says the point is that countries do not have "to start from scratch" [1].

Keep the scale honest. The GCF says it has $22.3 billion committed across more than 130 countries; the Adaptation Fund is a $2 billion-plus fund that gives only grants [1]. UNEP's Adaptation Gap Report 2026, cited by Down To Earth, puts international public adaptation finance at $28.2–31.7 billion in 2024 against needs of $305 billion a year by 2035 [2]. And the proposal for a separate COP31 agenda item on tripling adaptation finance was dropped [2].

So this does not close the gap. It does something smaller and more reliable: it stops good projects dying at the pilot stage because the next application form starts on page one. Adaptation is mostly drainage, seed banks and early-warning sirens. It scales through paperwork that works.
