There is no trading in cash Treasuries on Wednesday because the US is closed for a holiday [1]. So the first government-bond reply to Tuesday's hot price figures came from Asia. Japan's 10-year yield rose 8.5 basis points to 2.765% and Australia's 10-year six basis points to 5.05%, tracking Tuesday's Treasury sell-off [1].

The yen was little changed at 158.86 per dollar and the euro at $1.1167 [1]. Money markets now price a quarter-point Fed rise in December, and roughly three quarter-point rises by June, Investing.com reports [2].

A holiday is a pause in the paperwork, not in the argument. For you: loan and deposit rates follow government-bond yields with a lag, so the useful moment this week is Thursday's US reopening, not Wednesday's quiet. Nothing here is investment advice.
