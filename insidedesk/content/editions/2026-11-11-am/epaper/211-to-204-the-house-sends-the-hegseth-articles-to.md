The US House of Representatives voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday to refer articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, NPR reports [1][2]. In practice, that ends the effort for now. The roll call, taken at 00:02 CET, split almost exactly on party lines: Republicans 210 to 1 in favour of referral, Democrats 203 against, one independent in favour [2]. Referral rules out a floor vote and makes it very unlikely the measure will be taken up before this session of Congress expires [1].

The articles came from a Republican, Thomas Massie of Kentucky. He reintroduced them on Monday, accusing Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanours over the war with Iran, strikes on alleged drug boats, the capture of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro without congressional authorisation, and the effort to punish Senator Mark Kelly [1]. It was his second attempt. The first expired when Speaker Mike Johnson called an early recess before the midterms [1].

Hegseth called Massie a "disgruntled and defeated congressman" at a Pentagon press conference: "I stand behind every one of these operations." [1] President Trump said on Monday: "I'll stand by him." [1]

The calendar matters more than the rhetoric. Democrats take the House majority in January and have promised investigations into the Pentagon. Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become Speaker, told NPR of Hegseth: "He's got to go." [1] A referral to committee is the quietest way a legislature can say "not now". From January, the committees will belong to the other side.
