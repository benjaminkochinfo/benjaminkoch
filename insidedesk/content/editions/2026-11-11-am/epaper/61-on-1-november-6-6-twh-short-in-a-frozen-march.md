Germany's gas storage sites entered the heating season only 61% full on 1 November, nine percentage points short of the 70% legal target, the storage operators' association INES said on Tuesday [1]. By Tuesday morning the level had slipped to 58.95%, against a European average of 72.38%, according to figures compiled by the Allgäuer Zeitung [2].

INES's new November scenarios are calm about most winters and blunt about one. With normal or mild temperatures, supply is secure [1]. In an extremely cold winter like the reference year 2010, demand could not be fully met in January, February and March 2027: the model shows shortfalls of up to 6.6 terawatt hours in a single month, and up to 1.7 TWh on individual January days [1]. The LNG terminals, with at most 0.8 TWh a day of technical send-out capacity, are already counted at full use in the model [1].

The association's diagnosis is about incentives, not geology. Refilling in 2026 was the lowest on record for the time of year, it says, after heavy withdrawals at the end of last winter and weak economic reasons to fill; in an earlier update it had called 77% technically reachable [1]. "Low storage levels represent a considerable risk," said INES managing director Sebastian Heinermann (our translation) [1]. INES suggests paying the gap between market value and storage cost, or obliging traders to use storage, alongside the state gas reserve the economy ministry has announced [1].

For you, this is a scenario, not a warning to hoard. The next INES update is due on 12 January 2027 [1].
