Japan's current account surplus fell to ¥2.3723 trillion in September, the Ministry of Finance said on Tuesday, 45.8% less than a year earlier and well below the ¥3.3544 trillion markets expected; August had been ¥4.062 trillion [1]. Exports rose 12.8% to ¥10.7283 trillion, but imports rose faster, 17.8% to ¥10.9159 trillion, so the trade balance turned to a deficit of ¥187.5 billion [1].

The overall account stays in surplus because it also counts primary income, the returns on investment abroad, and services such as travel; the goods line is the part that swung [1]. The yen barely moved, at about 158.07 per dollar shortly after the release [1]. A country's bill for imports is the least glamorous statistic it publishes, and often the most honest.
