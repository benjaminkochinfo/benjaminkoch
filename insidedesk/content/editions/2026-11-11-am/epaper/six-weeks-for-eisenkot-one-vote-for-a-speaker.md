Israel's new Knesset was sworn in on Tuesday, and the opposition used its majority straight away to request a vote on a new speaker, the Guardian reports [1]. Replacing Netanyahu loyalist Amir Ohana would let the six opposition parties set the agenda even if coalition talks fail [1]. Ohana has scheduled the vote for next Tuesday, the latest date possible [1].

Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot holds the presidential mandate and has six weeks to form a government; if he fails, a second candidate, probably Netanyahu, gets a chance [1]. His Yashar party tabled bills moments after the ceremony: term limits for prime ministers, a state commission of inquiry into 7 October 2023, and measures to revoke the judicial overhaul [1]. President Isaac Herzog urged lawmakers to avoid a second election: "Reach out a hand for compromise, even between rivals." [1]

Coalitions take weeks. Control of a chamber's calendar can take a single vote.
