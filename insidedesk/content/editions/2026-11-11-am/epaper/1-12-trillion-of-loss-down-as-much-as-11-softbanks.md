SoftBank Group swung to a loss of ¥1.12 trillion, about $7.1 billion, in its fiscal second quarter, after writing down non-refundable advance payments for its US data-centre projects [2]. Investing.com puts the write-down on those projects at $3.7 billion, "amid falling AI valuations" [1]. On Wednesday the shares fell as much as 11% in Tokyo [1]; CNBC TV18 reported an 8% drop earlier in the session [2].

**The contrast in the same room.** The rest of Tokyo's technology board went the other way. TDK gained 3.43%, Kioxia 2.99% and Murata Manufacturing 2.65% [1]. In Seoul, SK Hynix rose 3.6% [1]. The suppliers that ship components and invoice for them were bought; the investor that paid in advance for capacity was sold.

**Why it matters beyond one company.** A write-down is an accountant's way of saying a prepayment will not come back as planned. It is unglamorous and it is honest: the number is now in the books, not in a presentation. Desk arithmetic: SoftBank's $3.7 billion write-down is a little over half of its $7.1 billion quarterly loss.

**For you.** If you hold a fund with Japanese technology exposure, the lesson is about spread: the same theme produced both an 11% intraday fall and 3% gains on one morning [1]. Look at what your fund actually owns, not the theme on its label. Nothing here is investment advice.
