While negotiators slept in Abu Dhabi, a drone attack set fire to the port area of Ust-Luga, one of Russia's most important western oil-export hubs. "A UAV attack hit the port area of Ust-Luga, causing a fire," Leningrad regional governor Aleksandr Drozdenko wrote on Telegram; crews were working to put it out [1]. The port has been hit repeatedly before [1].

The same night, Lithuania issued red air alerts for Kaunas, Panevėžys and Druskininkai and the districts of Prienai, Lazdijai, Kėdainiai, Pasvalys and Biržai over a possible drone, Daily Baltic reports, citing LRT [2]. Latvia also warned people in several regions to take shelter [1]. The previous red alert over an object entering Lithuanian airspace was on 18 October and was lifted after about an hour [2].

Who sent the drones is disputed. Moscow accuses Kyiv of using NATO airspace as cover; Kyiv says Russian jamming pushes some of its drones off course; NATO governments blame Russia for starting the war that causes these incidents [1].

What stands out is the routine. Lithuanian authorities told people to move to a marked shelter or a room with at least two walls between them and the outside, and to keep away from windows, lifts and top floors [2]. A country with yellow "Shelter" signs and a text-alert system takes the night seriously without panicking. That kind of preparation is dull, and that is why it works.
