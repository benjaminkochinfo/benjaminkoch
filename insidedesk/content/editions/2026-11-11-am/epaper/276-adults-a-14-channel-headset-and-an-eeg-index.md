Researchers at the University of Tsukuba recorded resting brainwaves from 276 untreated adults aged 18 to 59 with a portable 14-channel headset, and compared them with a standard 20-item self-rating depression questionnaire, according to a report on their Nature Mental Health paper [1]. A combined index of how rhythm "resets" at one scalp site follow those at another correlated with questionnaire scores at more than 0.6 [1]. It separated 116 people with moderate-to-severe symptoms from 114 with low symptoms with about 80% accuracy, after excluding those with mild symptoms [1]. The team's own caveat: the findings concern self-reported symptoms, not diagnosis, and need validating in independent populations before clinical use [1]. A cheap headset that helps someone ask for help sooner would be worth having. Not yet, but worth watching.
