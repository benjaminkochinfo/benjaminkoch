Some companies wait three months to tell you how they are doing. TSMC tells you every month. On Tuesday it reported October revenue of NT$586.89 billion, about $17.88 billion, up 59.7% on a year earlier and 14.7% on September [1]. CNBC attributes it to strong demand for advanced chips as big technology companies keep investing in AI [1].

The monthly figure is a sales total, not a profit, but it is hard to fake and arrives fast. It also squares with the customs ledger: Taiwan's integrated circuit exports rose 63.0% in October to $31.17 billion, the finance ministry's figures show [2]. Machinery exports, driven by chipmaking equipment as manufacturers expand capacity, rose 35.5% [2].

The context is a jumpy market. On Tuesday TSMC shares closed 2.24% higher amid broader volatility in Taiwan, and Taiwan's regulator has just tightened short-selling rules, cutting the daily limit on intraday short-selling orders from 30% to 3% [1]. TSMC has said it expects capital spending from 2026 to 2028 to be significantly higher than in the previous three years [1].

Desk arithmetic: at $17.88 billion, TSMC's October revenue alone equals more than half of Taiwan's entire IC exports for the month, though the two figures measure different things and should not be added up. The point for you is simpler. Share prices argue about tomorrow; monthly sales report what customers already paid for. On that measure, October was not a bubble bursting. Nothing here is investment advice.
