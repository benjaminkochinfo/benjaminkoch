On Tuesday the US Food and Drug Administration approved pimicotinib (brand name Pimjetmi, from EMD Serono), a kinase inhibitor, for adults with symptomatic tenosynovial giant cell tumour, a growth in the lining of joints and tendons, when surgery could worsen function or cause severe harm [1].

The evidence is one randomised, double-blind trial, MANEUVER, which assigned patients two to one to 50 mg of pimicotinib or placebo once a day [1]. At week 25, an independent blinded review found the tumour had shrunk enough to count as a response in 56% of the pimicotinib group against 3.2% on placebo [1]. Median duration of response was not reached; 91% of responders had lasted at least six months and 77% at least 12 [1]. Patients on the drug also gained range of motion in the affected joint, reported less worst pain and better physical function [1].

The price of that is listed plainly. Side effects in more than 20% of patients included itching, rash, swelling of the face and around the eyes, fatigue, nausea and headache; the label carries warnings on liver toxicity, harm to an unborn child and a rise in serum creatinine without effect on kidney function [1].

The quiet part is how it was reviewed. The FDA used Project Orbis, sharing the review with Australia, Canada, Israel, Switzerland and the UK, with the European Medicines Agency as an official observer [1]. Regulators reading the same file at the same time is not glamorous. It is how a good medicine reaches more than one country's patients without five separate queues.
