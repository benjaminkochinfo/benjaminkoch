Taiwan's exports rose 58.9% on the year in October to a record $96.76 billion, and imports rose 75.1% to $68.89 billion, leaving a surplus of $27.88 billion, the finance ministry said [1]. The previous record, $87.22 billion, was set only in September; October was the 36th month in a row of year-on-year growth [2].

The goods are what you would expect. Information, communications and audiovisual products jumped 72.9% to $47.60 billion, and electronic components reached $32.68 billion; together they made up 83% of exports [2]. Machinery exports rose 35.5%, driven by chipmaking equipment [2].

The destinations are the more durable story. The US took a record $32.93 billion, 34% of the month's exports [2]. Over January to October, the ministry's statistics chief Beatrice Tsai said, the US share was 31.7%, its highest in 36 years, while China and Hong Kong fell to 23.8%, their lowest in 27 years [2]. She put it down to supply chains shifting amid US–China tensions and to AI demand [2].

Desk arithmetic: in ten months Taiwan sold $758.29 billion abroad, so the ministry's expectation of more than $900 billion for 2026 needs roughly $142 billion from November and December; the ministry's November forecast of $94.2–96.7 billion alone covers about two-thirds of that [2]. Supply chains do not move by speech; they move by invoice, one container at a time, and this is the ledger that records it.
