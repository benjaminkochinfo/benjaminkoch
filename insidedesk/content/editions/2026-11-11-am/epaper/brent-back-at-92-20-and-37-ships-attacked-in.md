Brent crude futures rose 71 cents, or 0.78%, to $92.20 a barrel by 02:19 CET on Wednesday, after two-month lows in the previous session; US WTI gained 49 cents to $86.53 [1]. The reason sits in a ledger, not a speech. The Washington Institute for Near East Policy counted 37 ships attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz in October, the most in any month since the war began, and 10 so far in November [1].

LSEG analysts put the dull point plainly: even with a deal, more flows "would still depend on mine clearance, insurance costs" and shipowners' willingness to take the risk [1]. Also on the screen: US crude stocks up 9 million barrels in the week to 6 November, according to market sources citing the American Petroleum Institute [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
