Today is St Martin's Day, and the goose on the table costs more. Many direct sellers charged about €20 a kilogram for a goose last year; this year prices are often between €20 and €24, WDR reports, so a whole bird can easily cost more than €100 [1].

**Why.** "Almost 60 percent of the entire breeding-goose stock in Germany" fell victim to bird flu last year, Charlotte Wilmes of the North Rhine-Westphalia poultry association told WDR, so fewer young geese could be raised this year [1]. Feed, energy and staff also cost more [1]. In North Rhine-Westphalia the number of geese slaughtered in 2025 fell to its lowest in ten years: the state's 18 slaughterhouses processed around 16,000 birds, averaging about 4.5 kilos each, according to the state statistics office IT.NRW [1].

**The import ledger.** Only about 15% of the geese eaten in Germany come from German farms; most come from Poland, where some are offered for €11 or €12 a kilo [1]. Desk arithmetic: for a 4.5-kilo bird, €20–24 a kilo means €90–108; €11–12 a kilo means €49.50–54.

**The quiet substitute.** Wilmes sees a trend towards duck, a smaller bird that still feeds three, four or six people depending on size [1].

**For you.** If you want a German goose for Christmas, order early: WDR says German geese could run short before Christmas [1]. If the price matters more than the postcode, the label will tell you where the bird was raised; read it.
