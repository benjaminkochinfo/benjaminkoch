The trade truce between the world's two largest economies was renewed this week the way most useful things are: in a notice. The US Trade Representative extended all 178 exclusions from Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods to 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on 9 January 2027, covering goods entering from 10 November [1]. That is 164 product categories carried over from earlier relief, plus 14 types of equipment used to make solar products [1]. "In light of ongoing discussions with China," the notice says, the extension is "appropriate" [1].

**Beijing matched it, line by line.** China's State Council Tariff Commission kept its extra 24% tariff on US goods suspended for two more months, to 10 January 2027, Forwarder News reports, citing the finance ministry notice and Seoul Economic Daily; the separate 10% extra duty stays [2]. The commerce ministry also kept on hold its export-control measures against 16 US entities, and tighter controls on heavy rare-earth metals, battery lithium and graphite and rare-earth technology were postponed to the same date [2]. Desk arithmetic: if the pause lapses, a US exporter faces a 34% extra Chinese duty instead of 10%.

**The small print is the news.** China's text, in Seoul Economic Daily's machine-assisted translation, says Beijing will "closely monitor follow-up U.S. actions and adjust related retaliatory tariff measures at an appropriate time", a reference to US plans to replace court-voided emergency tariffs with Section 301 duties [2]. Washington's tone is no warmer: on 6 November, Jamieson Greer pressed Vice Premier He Lifeng on farm purchases, rare earths and critical minerals [1].

**For you.** Two months is short for diplomacy and long for a logistics planner. It covers December and ends less than four weeks before Lunar New Year on 6 February, as Forwarder News notes [2]. If you buy anything with Chinese graphite, magnets or solar parts inside it, the price you are quoted today assumes the truce holds to 10 January. That is a date to put in the diary, not a reason to panic. Nothing here is investment advice.
