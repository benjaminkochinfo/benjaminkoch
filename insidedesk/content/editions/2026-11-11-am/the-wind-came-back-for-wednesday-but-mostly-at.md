You already know the headline number: Wednesday's power was auctioned on Tuesday at a German-Luxembourg daily average of €316.65 per megawatt hour, a step down from two records [1]. This morning the more useful number sits one table over. SMARD's generation forecast for Wednesday, which had not yet appeared when we checked on Tuesday afternoon, puts onshore and offshore wind together at about 148.1 gigawatt hours [2]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 49% more than the 99.1 GWh forecast for Tuesday and roughly double Monday's 71.6 GWh [2].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-11nov]]

**The wind keeps night hours.** The forecast has wind at about 8,728 megawatts at 03:00 but only about 4,433 MW at 17:00 [2]. Desk arithmetic: the night hour gets nearly twice the wind of the teatime hour. The price follows faithfully. 03:00 is Wednesday's cheapest hour at €200.39, and 17:00 its dearest at €447.65 [1]. Solar does not help in the evening either, and on Wednesday it barely helps at noon: SMARD forecasts about 35.5 GWh of solar for the whole day, roughly half Tuesday's 66.9 GWh [2].

**Why a 49% gain buys only 10%.** Wind and sun are still a small slice of what the country will use. SMARD forecasts consumption of about 1,418 GWh for Wednesday [2]. Desk arithmetic: forecast wind and solar together cover about 12.9% of it, up from 11.6% on Tuesday and 10.8% on Monday. The rest comes from plants that burn something: on Monday, the last full day of realised data, gas plants delivered about 411.6 GWh, lignite 311.8 GWh and hard coal 122.0 GWh, against 77.8 GWh from wind [2]. That is why the daily average fell by €34.83, or about 9.9%, and not by half [1].

**For you.** On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches your bill this month. On a dynamic tariff, Wednesday is a night-owl's day: every hour from 00:00 to 05:00 clears between €200.39 and €215.85, and 16:00 to 19:00 all clear above €390 [1]. Run the dishwasher before bed, charge the car overnight, and treat the forecast as a forecast. The grid operators plan with it. So can you. Nothing here is investment advice.
