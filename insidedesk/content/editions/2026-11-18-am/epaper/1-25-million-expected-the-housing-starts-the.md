Today's most useful American number will not come from a podium. At 14:30 CET the Commerce Department publishes October housing starts and building permits; the consensus compiled by Dow Jones is 1.25 million starts and 1.38 million permits at an annual rate, MarketScreener reports [1]. Initial jobless claims for the week to 14 November follow on Thursday, with 197,000 expected [1].

Starts are a peculiarly honest statistic. A builder who pours a foundation has borrowed money, hired a crew and bet on a buyer. A permit is a promise to do so. With mortgage rates driven by a Fed that markets expect to raise rates in December [2], the gap between the two is a reading on how many builders are still willing to bet.

Tuesday's retail figures showed building-material store sales up 0.5% in October [2]. That is a small sign that people are still fixing and finishing houses, even if fewer are starting them.

Treat today's release as you would a delivery note: a count of things that happened in October, revised later, not a forecast. If it surprises, it will move bond yields within seconds. If it does not, it will still tell you more about the American economy than most of what is said about it this week.
