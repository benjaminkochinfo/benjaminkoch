Franziska Giffey, the Berlin SPD politician, says she will not stand for the chair of the federal party. She told dpa she values Hubertus Heil highly and "expressly" supports his candidacy, Süddeutsche Zeitung reports [1]. "That does not mean I am keeping my own candidacy open. I will not run for the chair of the federal SPD," she said [1].

The contest has a paper trail. Der Spiegel reported that a broad alliance of the party's working groups has filed two motions for the SPD conference in January aimed at preventing a renewed candidacy by Lars Klingbeil, according to SZ [1]. One motion argues the party chair should not, "in the current situation", be held together with a federal ministry; it is supported by groups including the AfA labour wing, SPD Women and AG 60+ [1]. The Jusos filed a similar motion and favour a woman as sole chair [1].

Parties settle leadership questions on conference floors, by vote, after motions are printed and debated. That is slower than a television interview and much more binding. Klingbeil is finance minister and vice-chancellor; he was in the chancellery until about 2 a.m. negotiating over the electricity tax (see the lead desk note) [1]. The January conference will decide whether one person can carry both briefcases.
