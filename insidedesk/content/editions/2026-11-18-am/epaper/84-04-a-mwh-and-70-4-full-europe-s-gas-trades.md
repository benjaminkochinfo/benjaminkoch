The Dutch TTF benchmark, the reference for European wholesale gas, fell 7.59% to €84.04 a megawatt hour at the start of this week, closing at the day's low, GasRadar's daily briefing reports [1]. It had touched €93.70 on 12 November [1]. The proximate cause is weather: a mild pattern for mid-November, with Berlin and Amsterdam around −0.1 °C and Stockholm −3.3 °C on the briefing's figures [1].

The tanks tell a different story. EU storage was 70.39% full, about 18.8 percentage points below the five-year average of 89.2% for the date, GasRadar calculates [1]. Germany, the largest consumer, sat at 57.3%; the Netherlands at 61.1% and Austria at 63.0%, while Poland and Portugal were above 95% [1].

Both readings are honest. Price reflects the next few weeks of demand; storage reflects whether the winter can absorb a cold snap. A mild November lets countries draw less and keep more for February. That is the constructive part: every warm week now is gas left in the ground for later.

For households, the wholesale price reaches the bill with a lag of months, through supplier contracts. A falling TTF in November is not a lower bill in December. It is a slightly better starting point for next year's tariffs. Nothing here is investment advice.
