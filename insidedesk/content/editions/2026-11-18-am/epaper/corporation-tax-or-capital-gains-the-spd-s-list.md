The question the coalition argued over until 2 a.m. was not whether to cut the electricity tax for everyone, but who pays the €5.4 billion a year that the finance ministry estimates it would cost [1][2]. The ideas on the table, as Süddeutsche Zeitung lists them, each have a different payer [1].

The CDU and CSU proposed using surpluses in the account that funds renewable-energy support; the SPD regarded that as constitutionally risky [1]. Cuts to programmes in the Climate and Transformation Fund were also rejected [1]. From the SPD came the idea of raising corporation tax or the flat 25% tax on capital income (Abgeltungsteuer) [1]. Merz also wanted to address compensation for "cold progression", the hidden tax rise when inflation pushes wages into higher brackets [1].

Read these as transfers. Using an energy-account surplus spends money already raised from electricity users; a corporation-tax rise asks companies; a capital-income tax rise asks savers and investors. None is free, and none is wrong in principle. The test of this morning's announcement is whether it names one, with a number.

For a household the cut is concrete: StromAuskunft calculates about €58 a year for two people using 2,500 kWh and about €104 for a family of four at 4,500 kWh, if suppliers pass it on in full [2]. Nothing here is tax or investment advice.
