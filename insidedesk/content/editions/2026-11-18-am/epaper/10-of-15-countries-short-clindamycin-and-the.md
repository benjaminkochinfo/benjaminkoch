Clindamycin, an antibiotic used for skin, bone and dental infections among others, was reported short in 10 of the 15 countries tracked by ShortageWatch as of 17 November, covering 16 products from 6 companies [1]. Germany, Ireland and Austria were added in the past month; Germany's listing, a 600 mg tablet from ratiopharm, dates from 5 November with an expected return of 15 January 2027, citing increased demand [1].

The reasons recorded with national regulators are unglamorous: "increased demand", "manufacturing problem", "delay in production", "distribution" [1]. Seven of the 16 affected products carry no return date [1]. Clindamycin is on the EU's list of critical medicines, and its active ingredient comes mainly from a handful of certified makers, nine of the twelve in China [1]. Denmark separately reports a supply difficulty for a 2,000 mg ceftazidime hospital antibiotic from Fresenius Kabi [2].

This is the textbook case of a medicine that is old, cheap and made by many companies competing on price – exactly the profile that runs short. The fix is not a speech about sovereignty. It is contracts that pay a little more for a second source and a buffer stock, and the patience to keep paying for them in years when nothing goes wrong.

If you have a prescription, ask your pharmacist about alternatives; do not stop or change treatment on your own. This is not medical advice.
