On Tuesday's earnings call, Nvidia's interim chief executive Colette Kress reaffirmed the company's commitment to open-source AI, TechCrunch reports [1]. "At Nvidia, we are committed to fostering a vibrant open model ecosystem, so every country, company, and industry can build its own intelligence," she said [1].

That matters for one deal in particular. In September Nvidia agreed to acquire Hugging Face, a platform for open AI models serving more than 18 million developers and 200,000 companies, for $12.9 billion – what would be the second-largest acquisition in Nvidia's history [1]. The release lists the agreement among the quarter's highlights [2]. Hugging Face's chief executive had called Jensen Huang's departure "a sad day" [1]. The acquisition was not directly addressed in the analysts' questions, TechCrunch notes [1].

Kress cited a 35-fold year-on-year rise in weekly token volume on OpenRouter, a marketplace that routes requests to many models [1]. She described Nvidia's Nemotron family as the "most widely used U.S. open model" there [1].

Strategy statements are cheap; closing a deal is not. The test is the completion of the purchase and the terms on which Hugging Face's platform stays open to models from Nvidia's rivals. That will be written in contracts, not on calls.
