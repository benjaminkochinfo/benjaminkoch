After a trilateral meeting in Abu Dhabi, President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday that Ukraine has proposed a humanitarian operation like the one in Oleshky in 10 more Russian-occupied settlements in Kherson and Zaporizhzhia oblasts, the Kyiv Independent reports [1]. The Oleshky model was a local ceasefire of a few days that allowed civilians to leave the occupied town [1]. Discussions would follow with American mediators [1].

Zelensky also said Kyiv was preparing a prisoner-of-war exchange, which he called one of the most important agreements of the Abu Dhabi talks on 10–11 November. "We are already communicating through intelligence channels. Ukraine is ready to deliver results in the coming weeks," he said [1]. Both sides had confirmed a new "large-scale" exchange when those talks ended [1].

On Thursday, US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner plan to meet Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin on security guarantees and a post-war recovery plan, a US official told Axios [2]. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said on Monday that no agreement had been reached on Europe having a seat at the table [2].

Corridors and exchanges are small, specific and verifiable: buses leave or they do not, names are on a list or they are not. That is precisely why they matter more than declarations.
