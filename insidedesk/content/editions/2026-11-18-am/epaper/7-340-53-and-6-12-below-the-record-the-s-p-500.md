The S&P 500 fell 1.03% to 7,340.53 on Tuesday, its fourth loss in a row and its lowest close since 29 July, Portfolio Terminal's close table shows [1]. That leaves it 6.12% below the record of 7,818.93 set on 6 October [1]. The Russell 2000 of smaller companies fell 1.50% to 2,701.87 [1].

The day's swing was wider than the close. The index fell as much as 1.73% during the session, and the Dow was down more than 1,000 points at its low before ending 848.75 lower, CNBC reports [2]. Microsoft took about 10.1 points off the S&P 500 on its own, falling 2.27%, while Broadcom added 4.5 points [1]. The VIX, a gauge of expected volatility, rose 11.13% to 25.46 [3].

Two things worth keeping in proportion. A 6% fall from a record is a pullback, not a crash. And Tuesday's close came hours before Nvidia's report, so today's session is the first to trade on it (see the Tech lead).

The dull figure to watch is not the index but its breadth: how many shares rise when it rises. On Tuesday the losses were wide, from software to banks [1]. A recovery led by a handful of chip names alone would be thinner than it looks. Nothing here is investment advice.
