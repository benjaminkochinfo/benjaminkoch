The ZEW's survey of financial-market analysts, published on Tuesday, rose 7.5 points in November to plus 35.6 [1]. The assessment of the current situation in Germany improved by 2.7 points – to minus 33.2 [1]. For the euro area, expectations rose 8.0 points to plus 26.5 while the current situation stayed at minus 14.7 [1].

ZEW President Achim Wambach named the supports: "Increasing manufacturing orders and falling oil prices are likely to underpin the upturn" [1]. Then the caveat in the same breath: "The mainstay of the recovery has been government expenditure so far, but this leads to rising concerns about the sustainability of public debt" [1]. Chemicals, pharma and the electrical industry improved; metal manufacturing worsened; construction moved deeper into negative territory [1].

That caveat has a number attached. Last week the Bundestag's budget committee approved net borrowing of almost €123 billion in the core federal budget for 2027, about €4 billion more than the government's first draft [2].

A gap of nearly 70 points between hope and the present is not a contradiction. It is a survey of people who expect public money and cheaper energy to show up in private order books. Whether they do is a question for the ifo survey of companies next week, and for the payroll data after that.
