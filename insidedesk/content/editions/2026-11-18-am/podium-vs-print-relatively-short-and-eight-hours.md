**The podium.** On Tuesday afternoon Chancellor Friedrich Merz went into the coalition committee expecting, in his words, that it would be "relatively short" [1]. He had renewed his promise to cut the electricity tax for all consumers after the CDU's defeats in September's state elections, calling it a gap in credibility to close [1].

**The print.** The meeting ran for more than eight hours. Around 2 a.m. Merz, Vice-Chancellor Lars Klingbeil, CSU leader Markus Söder and the other leaders left the chancellery; results were to be announced only in the early morning [1]. The central question was arithmetic: how to raise the €5.4 billion a year that, by the finance ministry's estimate, a general cut would cost [1][2]. The CDU and CSU proposed using surpluses in the renewable-energy account; the SPD considered that constitutionally risky and rejected cuts to climate-fund programmes; from the SPD came ideas such as raising corporation tax or the flat tax on capital income [1]. Pensions, mini-jobs and a fuel-price cap were also on the table [1].

**The gap.** The electricity tax is 2.05 cents per kilowatt hour; the EU minimum for households is 0.1 cents [2]. Today the cut applies only to certain companies [1][2]. Last week's budget committee approved spending of €560.9 billion and net borrowing of almost €123 billion in the core budget for 2027, but left the tax cut's funding open [3].

**What it means.** Eight hours is not failure. It is what deciding who pays looks like. The podium says "everyone"; the print will have to say "from where". Check this morning's statement for a line with a number in it.
