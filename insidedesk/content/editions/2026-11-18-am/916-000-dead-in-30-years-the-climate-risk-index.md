On the day ministers in Antalya picked over an 18-page draft of a possible COP31 outcome, Germanwatch released its Climate Risk Index 2027, UN News reports [1]. Over the three decades to 2025, Dominica, Myanmar and Honduras were hardest hit by extreme weather [1]. The index looks at deaths, people affected and economic losses across 178 countries [1].

The totals are stark: extreme weather claimed more than 916,000 lives and affected over 5.5 billion people worldwide between 1996 and 2025; heatwaves accounted for 40% of deaths, floods for the largest number of people affected [1]. For 2025 alone, Jamaica topped the ranking after Hurricane Melissa, ahead of Sri Lanka and Spain [1]. "Behind these figures are families who lost their homes, children unable to attend school, farmers who lost their crops," said Honduras's Vice Minister of Finance Roberto Chang López [1].

Spain's place in the single-year list is a reminder that the index is not only about the tropics. Europe's floods and heat are in the same ledger.

Meanwhile, the draft decision proposes a pathway to bring forward the tripling of adaptation finance by 2035, Australia's Chris Bowen said [2]; it also includes the contested call to "immediately transition away from fossil fuels", The Guardian reports [3]. Bowen expects at least one more draft before the week ends [2]. The index is a reason to read the adaptation paragraphs first.
