Tuesday's New York session closed before Nvidia reported, and it closed badly. The Dow fell 848.75 points, or 1.64%, to 50,759.42; the S&P 500 lost 1.03% to 7,340.53, its lowest close since late July; the Nasdaq fell 0.74% to 24,328.38 after touching its lowest level since April [4]. It was the S&P 500's fourth loss in a row, and the index ended 6.12% below its 6 October record of 7,818.93 [5].

Asia opened with Nvidia's after-hours gain in hand and gave most of it back. RTHK reports the Nikkei rose as much as 1.3% early before turning lower [6]. At about 03:30 CET AFP had Tokyo down 0.3% at the lunch break, Seoul down 0.2%, Hong Kong down 0.5% and Shanghai down 0.3% [1].

[[chart:asia-wednesday-18nov]]

The numbers moved all morning. An earlier snapshot by Kaohoon had the Kospi up 0.27% and the Nikkei down 0.79% [2]; AP had Seoul up 0.2% at 4,978.16 and Tokyo down 0.6% [3]. A market in two minds prints a different figure every quarter of an hour. That is why the chart above carries a clock.

Tokyo Electron and Kioxia were among the heavier chip losers, AP and AFP report [1][3]. Wall Street's fear index, the VIX, rose 11.13% to 25.46 on Tuesday, Kaohoon notes [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
