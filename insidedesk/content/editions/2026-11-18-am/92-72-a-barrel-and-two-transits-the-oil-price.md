At about 03:30 CET Brent was down 0.5% at $92.72 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate down 1.0% at $85.18, AFP's table shows [1]. Brent has lost about a fifth of its value this month, falling from a six-month high in late October as Middle East supplies recovered and the International Energy Agency flagged a shift to a global surplus, CNBC TV18 reports [2]. Reuters, via CNBC, cites higher US crude stocks [4].

The physical news points the other way. US warplanes struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday, a US official told Bloomberg, according to The Economic Times; Iran's Mehr agency reported no casualties [3]. India's foreign ministry said 21 of 24 Indian crew from the LPG tanker Tara Gas, attacked in Hormuz on 16 November, reached Khasab in Oman; two were in hospital and one was missing [2]. Straits.live's brief, citing IMF PortWatch, counts 2 transits through the strait on 15 November against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 a day [5].

The Gulf's own crude moved most: Murban fell 4.22% to $91.39 by 10:35 Singapore time, Gulf News reports, about $1.22 below Brent [6].

So the price says "enough oil"; the strait says "not by this road". Both can be true, because oil is fungible and routes are not. For European buyers the difference shows up in freight, insurance and the delivery date on the contract, not on the screen. Nothing here is investment advice.
