US indices hit their session lows on Tuesday after CNN reported that a classified network operated by the National Security Agency had suffered an outage, citing six people familiar with the matter, Sharecast reports; the sources indicated no evidence of a cyberattack [1]. The NSA then said it had "experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and that "mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational" [1].

The Dow had been down more than 1,000 points at its low before closing 848.75 points lower [2]. CNBC reports traders citing the outage as adding to jitters about the stability of US technology infrastructure [2]. Sharecast lists the string: a Federal Aviation Administration ground stop at the weekend, a Microsoft Outlook outage last week and glitches across several banking apps [1].

Take the agency at its word: maintenance, not attack. The lesson survives either way. Systems that matter go down for ordinary reasons more often than extraordinary ones, and the public learns of it from leaks rather than notices. A short, timely, factual statement – what failed, for how long, what was affected – is a security measure in itself, because it starves rumour of oxygen. Markets react to silence more than to news.
