On 17 November the European Medicines Agency updated its 2026/27 seasonal flu recommendations to include mRNA, or nucleic-acid, vaccines for the first time, Europe Pharma Today reports, citing the agency's news page [1][2]. Earlier steps, on 7 and 26 May, had set strains for egg-based, cell-based and live-attenuated vaccines [1].

For nucleic-acid vaccines the protein sequence should closely match three cell-derived viruses: A/Missouri/11/2025 for H1N1, A/Darwin/1415/2025 for H3N2 and B/Pennsylvania/14/2025 for the B/Victoria lineage [1]. Egg-based and live vaccines use a different H3N2 (A/Darwin/1454/2025) and a different B virus (B/Tokyo/EIS13-175/2025) [1]. No B/Yamagata strain is recommended; that lineage has not been detected in circulation since March 2020, matching WHO advice [1].

"The advice is a manufacturing target," the outlet notes, "not a new marketing authorisation, and it does not rank one platform over another" [1].

Why it matters at home: flu vaccine supply depends on months of production planned around these lists. A platform that can be retargeted quickly is useful only if the regulator has said what to target. That sentence now exists for mRNA in the EU. This is not medical advice; ask your doctor or pharmacist about this season's vaccine.
