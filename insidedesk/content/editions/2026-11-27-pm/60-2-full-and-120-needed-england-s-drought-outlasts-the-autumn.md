England's reservoirs held 60.2% of their capacity on 24 November, up on the week, the Environment Agency's weekly drought report says [1]. Fourteen reservoirs or groups remain less than 50% full, eight of them classed as exceptionally low [1]. Severe drought persists in parts of Cornwall, Devon, Dorset, Somerset and East and West Sussex, areas that rely on Ardingly and Wimbleball reservoirs [1].

The sentence that matters is a requirement, not a forecast: 'We need at least 120% of average rainfall over the next 5 months for the entire country to be out of drought by the spring' [1]. Last week rain totals ranged from 49mm in the south-west to 11mm in the east, and river flows fell at more than three-quarters of reported sites [1]. Groundwater fell at most sites because dry soils delay recharge [1].

Farmers feel it first. Many irrigation reservoirs began the refill season at historic lows, and there are 386 restrictions on abstraction licences, down from 431 the week before [1]. The agency is advising the sector to act now in case drought runs into spring [1]. It is unglamorous work: permits, licences, lock closures on canals. It is also how a country keeps the taps on without anyone noticing.
