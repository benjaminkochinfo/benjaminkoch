Flu hospital admissions across England rose by almost 80% in a week, and in the South East the rate almost tripled, according to the UK Health Security Agency [1]. The South West saw a rise of about a quarter [1]. 'A lot of people think it's not serious. It absolutely is,' said Dr Suzanna McDonald of UKHSA [1]. Flu arrived 'a few weeks ahead' of last year, she said, but remains 'very, very unpredictable': last season also started early and peaked at moderate levels [1].

The hospitals feel it. More than 570 patients passed through the Royal Berkshire Hospital's emergency department in a single 24-hour period last week, which its chief operating officer called 'probably' the second busiest day it had ever had [1]. RSV kept the children's department busy too [1]. Some hospitals, including Queen Alexandra in Portsmouth, are encouraging masks in certain areas [1].

The weak line is uptake. Just over a third of two- and three-year-olds have had a flu vaccine, and uptake among young children and over-65s is lower than at the same point last year [1]. A GP in Oxfordshire noted that the vaccine needs a couple of weeks to take effect, so a jab now covers Christmas [1]. Health systems are built for winter. They work best when the appointments are kept.
