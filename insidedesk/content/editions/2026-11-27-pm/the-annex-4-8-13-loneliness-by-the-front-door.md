The English Housing Survey for 2025-26, published on Thursday, asks household reference persons the standard national question: how often do you feel lonely [1]. Among owner occupiers, 4% said often or always [1]. Among private renters it was 8%, and among social renters 13% [1]. Within social housing, 15% of council tenants said so, against 12% of housing association tenants [1]. Owner occupiers were also likelier to say they never feel lonely: 38%, against 29% of private renters [1].

The survey is careful about what it does not claim. The link between well-being and tenure 'could be mediated by related factors, such as income, age and working status' [1]. Owning a home does not make you less lonely. People who own homes tend to be older, richer and more settled, and those things travel together.

Two other lines deserve your attention. Across all tenures, mean anxiety stood at 3.0 out of 10 in 2025-26, against 2.7 before the pandemic [1]. And the share in each tenure who feel lonely often or always has stayed broadly the same since 2021-22 [1]. No surge, no recovery. A dull, steady number is still a number someone can work on, street by street.
