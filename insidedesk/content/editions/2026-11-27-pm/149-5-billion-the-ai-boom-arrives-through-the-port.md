The US goods trade deficit jumped 10.7%, or $14.4 billion, to $149.5 billion in October, the Census Bureau said on Friday [1]. Economists polled by Reuters had expected $133 billion [1]. Goods imports rose 6.1% to $365.3 billion, the highest since the series began in 1992 [1]. Capital goods led, up 8.5% on the month and 80.0% on the year [1].

The reason is not mysterious. Businesses are spending heavily on AI, and the buildout relies on imported equipment [1]. 'Capital goods imports are surging, showing that AI doesn't always lead to greater US economic growth,' said Christopher Rupkey of FWDBONDS [1]. Goldman Sachs economists wrote that the import surge is one reason AI adds less to GDP than corporate spending suggests [1].

This week the administration also proposed new maximum rates of 20% to 40% on 14 major partners, including China, Japan and the EU [1]. Friday's figures are the context the speeches leave out. The gap the tariffs are meant to close widened, because American firms chose to buy what they could not yet make. Invoices follow demand, not slogans.
