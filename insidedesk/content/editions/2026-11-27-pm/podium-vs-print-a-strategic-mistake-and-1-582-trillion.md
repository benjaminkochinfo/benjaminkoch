**The podium.** France's president said it would be 'a strategic mistake' to lower the EU's long-term spending [1]. Countries such as France and Italy want a higher-spending budget; Germany leads those who want restraint [1].

**The print.** The Commission's original proposal was €2tn [1]. The paper set up to break the impasse proposes cutting roughly €220bn to €230bn, and the budget under the current proposal totals €1.582tn, including €140bn to repay pandemic-era borrowing [1]. Four countries that want it smaller, among them Denmark and Austria, walked out after several hours [1]. The talks ended after 12 hours on Thursday without a deal [1]. Friedrich Merz spoke of 'new movement' but said 'we are not there yet' [1].

**The gap.** The podium argues about direction. The print has already moved: cuts of roughly €220bn to €230bn are on the table before a single vote [1]. Seven-year budgets need all 27 signatures, so the size that survives will be the size the most reluctant government can sign. Bulgaria, which had opposed the Ukraine loan, now says it will fund its non-military part [1]. That is what progress looks like in Brussels: one country, one half of one line.
