The Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore released the findings of its AI Market Study on Friday [1]. Its main finding is reassuring: businesses generally have a good range of cloud providers and AI models and can switch between them [1]. The risks it names are specific. Dominant firms could favour their own products or restrict access to essential technologies; dominant AI agent providers could stop rival agents from working with other operating systems; and AI pricing tools could learn to coordinate prices 'even without being explicitly instructed to do so' [1].

The consumer section is the more concrete. CCS says that in 2026 it acted against a fake review provider that used generative AI to write reviews for about 100 businesses [1]. AI can also personalise dark patterns, such as false scarcity messages, to an individual's online behaviour [1]. And as AI enters appliances and cars, the study asks for manual overrides and components 'that cannot be overridden by AI' [1].

Out of this come eight guiding principles, from pro-competitive algorithms to accuracy [1]. They are not a law. They are a regulator telling a market, in plain words, what it will look for. That is how most good rules begin.
