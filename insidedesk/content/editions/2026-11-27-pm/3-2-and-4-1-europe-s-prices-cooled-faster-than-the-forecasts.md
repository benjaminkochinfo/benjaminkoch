On Friday morning two statistics offices did more for the inflation debate than a week of speeches. France's harmonised inflation rate was 3.2% in November, down from 3.7% in October, INSEE's preliminary data showed [1]. A Reuters poll of 15 analysts had expected 3.6% [1]. The national index rose 2.9%, against 3.4% in October [1].

[[chart:hicp-flash-27pm]]

Spain's INE put its flash HICP at 4.1%, eight tenths below October's 4.9% [2]. The office names the cause plainly: fuel prices fell, where they had risen in November 2025, and clothing and footwear rose less than a year earlier [2]. Consumer prices fell 0.6% from October [2]. The detail that matters most is core inflation, which strips out unprocessed food and energy: it dropped five tenths to 2.6% [2]. FinanzaOnline notes that analysts had expected 3.2% for Spanish core and 4.7% for the headline [3]. The slowdown therefore did not stop at the petrol pump. French services inflation eased too, from 2.6% to 2.3% [3].

Traders, the Italian outlet reports, trimmed their bets on a December rate rise only slightly; a hike remains by far the most likely scenario priced in swaps [3]. That is the right temperament. Two countries are not the euro area, and a flash is an estimate, revised next month. Germany and Italy report on Monday, the euro area on Tuesday [3][4].

What you can take from Friday is smaller and sturdier than a forecast. The energy shock is passing through the price indexes in the order textbooks describe: fuel first, then the things that carry fuel. Nobody announced it. Two spreadsheets showed it.

This is reporting, not investment advice.
