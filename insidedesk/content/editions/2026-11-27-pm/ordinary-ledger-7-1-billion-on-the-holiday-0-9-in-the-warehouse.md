Three entries from the back office of the holiday season.

**$7.1 billion.** US consumers spent about that much online on Thanksgiving Day, 10.4% more than last year, according to Adobe Analytics [1]. Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion for Black Friday itself, up 9.2% [1]. Reuters notes that most of the season's projected gains will come from inflation, which at 3.8% runs about a percentage point above each of the last two years [1].

**82%.** That is the share of consumers in a November survey by the National Retail Federation who said they were taking steps to manage holiday costs, such as buying cheaper gifts or buying for fewer people [2]. The NRF expects 182.7 million people to shop over the five days to Cyber Monday, against 186.9 million who did last year [2].

**0.9%.** Wholesale inventories rose that much in October, after 0.8% in September; retail inventories rose 0.8% [3]. It is the least glamorous line of the day. It is also the one that decides whether January brings discounts or empty shelves.

One shopper in Raleigh told Reuters he would only buy items discounted by at least 40% [1]. That is not a mood. It is a rule, and rules are what budgets are made of.
