At 16:04 Paris time on Monday, Météo-France's accessible vigilance listed Gard and Hérault in orange for rain-flood, storms and river rises, and Lozère in orange for rain-flood; thirteen departments were yellow [1]. For Tuesday the orange list lengthens to eight: Ardèche, Bouches-du-Rhône, Haute-Corse, Gard, Hérault, Lozère, Var and Vaucluse [1].

The agency's episode note expects intense thunderstorm rains from Monday into Tuesday, with possible hourly totals of 60–80 mm, gusts to 80 km/h, hail and strong lightning, and episode cumuls of order 200–250 mm, locally 300 mm, on eastern Hérault and the Gard [2]. On Tuesday the rains shift east toward Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur and Corsica [2].

This is not a metaphor for climate politics. It is a public timetable: stay off submerged roads, avoid underpasses, follow prefecture instructions [2]. The constructive act tonight is local and dull — check the vigilance map before a journey, not after.
