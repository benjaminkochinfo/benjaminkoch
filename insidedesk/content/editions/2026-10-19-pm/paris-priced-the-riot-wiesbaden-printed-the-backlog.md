The screen that moved Europe on Monday was not a factory floor. Paris blue chips fell about 1.4%; BNP Paribas, Société Générale and Crédit Agricole dropped around 2.5%; LVMH lost 2.2% after stores were looted; Carrefour fell 1.8% after its chief executive said fifty of its shops had been ransacked [1]. The pan-European STOXX 600 was down about 0.8% by mid-morning [2]. That is how markets price a weekend of burned town halls: through banks, landlords and retailers with glass on the pavement.

Across the Atlantic the story was the opposite of Friday's AI scare. Chip and AI names led a rebound; the S&P 500 was up about half a percent in early US trade, with Brent off about 1.6% to around $108, which helped keep yields in check [4]. Earnings from Meta, Tesla and several chipmakers land later this week [2][4]. Investors will want free cash flow, not another capital-expenditure slide.

The quiet German print cut across both moods. Destatis reported that the real stock of manufacturing orders rose 0.5% in August on July, seasonally and calendar adjusted, and stood 10.7% above a year earlier [3]. The range of the backlog was unchanged at 9.0 months [3]. Machinery, other transport equipment and computers/electronics lifted the total; cars subtracted a little [3]. A backlog is not a boom. It is work already sold and not yet shipped — the opposite of a riot video, and closer to how a winter is paid for.

When the riot and the backlog share a Monday, favour the one with a delivery date.
