Emmanuel Macron opened a three-day Gulf tour on Monday in Kuwait, then Abu Dhabi, with Bahrain and Qatar still to come [1]. The Élysée's own framing was blunt: promote stability and freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, and "securing and diversifying our energy supplies" to limit the crisis's impact on daily life in France [1]. TotalEnergies and Engie chiefs travelled with him; Kuwait saw a defence-and-AI agreement including a Kuwait Airways fleet renewal with Airbus [1].

The optics are harsh because the domestic print is harsher. He left hours after promising the trip would help secure fuel, and after a weekend that left about 3,000 businesses looted or vandalized and nearly 2,400 people arrested [2][1]. Critics called the schedule tone-deaf [2]. That criticism does not cancel the logistics. France's fuel and gas bill is partly written in Gulf barrels and LNG cargoes; a president who refuses to talk about routes and volumes leaves the invoice to the tanker market alone.

The constructive test is narrow. Did any stop produce a dated commitment on volumes, routes or insurance that a household might eventually feel at the pump? A reception line is not a cargo. A cargo with a date is.
