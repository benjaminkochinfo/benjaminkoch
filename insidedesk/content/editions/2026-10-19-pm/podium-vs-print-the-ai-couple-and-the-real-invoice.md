**The podium.** Amid real fires and real arrests, an image of an elderly couple supposedly left in underwear after a robbery near Place de la République spread as proof of the night [1]. It looked like the weekend. That was the point.

**The print.** Fact-checkers at Libération's CheckNews, and detection tools including Google's SynthID, flagged the image as AI-generated or heavily edited — missing fingers, merged figures, watermarks [1]. Separate from that fiction, Carrefour's chief executive said fifty of the chain's stores had been looted, with repairs expected to cost more than €10 million [2]. Nearly 2,400 people were arrested; about 3,000 businesses were looted or vandalized [3]. Those figures do not need a generator.

**The gap.** A synthetic image borrows the authority of a photograph and spends it before the receipt arrives. The constructive habit is small and stubborn: when a picture arrives hotter than the official count, ask who measured the glass. The couple image failed that test. The Carrefour invoice did not.

**Verdict.** The weekend was violent enough without inventions. Prefer the store tally, the arrest sheet and the curfew notice to any image that cannot survive a finger count.
