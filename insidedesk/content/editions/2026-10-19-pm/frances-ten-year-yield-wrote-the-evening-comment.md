French ten-year yields rose more than ten basis points on Monday, trading close to 4.89%, while the spread over German Bunds widened to about 145 basis points — the highest in two weeks on that report [1]. Other desks saw the French ten-year near 4.875% with Bunds around 3.43% [3]. The euro slipped toward $1.122 [1][3]. None of those figures require a press conference.

The economic backdrop was already tight: fiscal strain, a 2027 presidential race, and a minority government that still has to pass a budget and a deficit path [2]. Weekend unrest layered political risk on top. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid called it "another layer of political risk for markets" [1]. French bank shares, which feel sovereign stress quickly, fell harder than the index [1][3].

The constructive reading is institutional, not fatalistic. Spreads widen when buyers demand compensation for uncertainty; they narrow when the uncertainty is reduced by dated plans — curfew hours, operational reviews, budget arithmetic. The screen is already doing the homework. The government still has to hand in its own.
