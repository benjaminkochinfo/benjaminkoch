The DAX closed Monday's Xetra session at 25,363.70, up 2.26%, after a six-day losing run that had taken it more than 8% below its record of just over 27,000 in mid-November [1][3]. The MDax rose 2.21% to 29,374.32, the Euro Stoxx 50 2.27% to 6,077.06 and the Stoxx Europe 600 1.95% to 637.84 [1][2]. Paris's CAC 40 gained 1.48% to 8,044.84, London's FTSE 100 1.35% to 10,486.35, Madrid's IBEX 35 1.71% and Milan's FTSE MIB only 0.58% [1][2].

[[chart:europe-monday-23nov-pm]]

The trigger arrived before New York opened: Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang, and Nvidia was quoted up 32.87% in the afternoon [2]. In Europe the biggest winners were the companies that build chipmaking machines: ASML, ASM International, BE Semiconductor and STMicroelectronics each rose more than 10%, ADVFN reported [4]. The rally was broad, with gains in nearly every sector; the exceptions were defensive staples such as Unilever and Nestlé, as money rotated back into risk [4].

The stronger German PMI helped too, Andreas Lipkow of CMC Markets wrote, while noting that the turmoil in US tech and the return of rate fears had dampened the appetite to buy [5]. Consorsbank's Jochen Stanzl called Monday's move an "Erholung auf Bewährung", a recovery on probation [3].

Desk arithmetic: the DAX closed at 24,802.78 on Friday, per Boursorama's previous close, and needs another 6.5% or so to get back to 27,000 [1]. The Stoxx 600 had lost 5.8% last week, its worst week since March 2025; Monday's 1.95% recovers about a third of that [4]. Relief is real. It is also partial, and Milan's small gain shows that not every market joined in. Nothing here is investment advice.
