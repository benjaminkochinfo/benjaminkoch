The three objects that came down in south-eastern Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, according to Polish prosecutors; they fell at Nowy Lipowiec in the Lublin region and at Rudnik and Mołodycz in Podkarpackie during one of Russia's larger attacks on western Ukraine, and no one was injured [1][2][3].

On Monday the North Atlantic Council, NATO's top decision-making body, met at Poland's request in Brussels [1]. Deputy Defence Minister Paweł Zalewski said Warsaw would propose better "situational awareness" on the eastern flank and more air cover from allied aircraft, including an additional AWACS early-warning plane, and that there were "grounds to assume" the council would back the proposals [1]. He called the incursion "an obvious Russian provocation" and noted that one missile came down 35 to 40 km from the Nowa Dęba training ground, where allied exercises took place that day [1]. The meeting is not an Article 4 consultation, Zalewski said [1].

In Berlin, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte told the alliance's parliamentary assembly that "it is clear that this is a dangerous incident", but that whether it was intentional was for the authorities to establish: "I will never speculate" [3]. Poland intends to summon the Russian ambassador once its investigations are done [3].

The useful thing about this week is the procedure. A council meeting, named missiles, a list of requested aircraft: none of it is dramatic, and all of it can be checked later. Zalewski raised one harder question for the record, whether NATO procedures and pilots' decisions on shooting down incoming objects could be made faster [2]. That one will take longer than a Monday.
