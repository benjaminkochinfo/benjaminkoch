Tuesday's power was auctioned at noon, and the bill is in: the German-Luxembourg day-ahead price averages €254.15 a megawatt hour for delivery on Tuesday 24 November, up from €240.42 for Monday, according to the quarter-hourly results on Energy-Charts [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is 5.7% more.

The average hides the shape. Tuesday's cheapest quarter-hour is €199.45 at 03:30; the dearest is €356.69 at 17:00, when the light has gone, the heating is on and people are cooking [1]. Monday's peak was €331.13 at 17:45 [1]. The difference between night and early evening is now about €157 a megawatt hour, which is the clearest price signal on the system: shift what you can away from five o'clock, and somebody's power station does not need to start.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-23nov-pm]]

The weather explains the direction. The Deutscher Wetterdienst calls this "a foretaste of the coming winter": highs only around freezing, moderate night frosts, and down to −5°C to −8°C in the south if skies stay clear tonight, with permanent frost above 300 to 500 metres by Thursday [2]. Fog and low cloud also mean less sun on the panels in the lowlands, and cold, still air rarely brings much wind.

Oil went the other way. WTI, the US benchmark, was down 3.5% at $80.55 a barrel in New York's morning, its lowest since August, Investopedia reported [3]. A cheaper barrel and a dearer megawatt hour on the same day is a reminder that "energy prices" is two markets, with two weathers.
