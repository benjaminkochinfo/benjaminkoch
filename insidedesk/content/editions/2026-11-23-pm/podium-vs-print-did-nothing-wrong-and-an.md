**The podium.** President Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had spoken to Nvidia's board "directly and indirectly" and that Huang "did nothing wrong"; in a post he called the reinstatement "the correct and proper decision" [1]. Investopedia reported that he said he had convinced the board [4]. Nvidia shares rose about 33% [1].

**The print.** The company's own words are more careful. The board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president, chief executive and director, "effective immediately", after it was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor", and says it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" before concluding his return "poses no national security risk" [2]. The 8-K adds one dull line that matters: Colette Kress, interim chief executive since Huang resigned on 13 November, goes back to being chief financial officer [3][1].

Then come the open files. The independent directors' investigation into reporting that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort "will proceed to conclusion" [2]. A new National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors is hiring counsel, and the company is searching for a Chief Compliance Officer, a post it did not have [2]. Congress has its own dates: a transcribed interview for Huang on 1 December and for the general counsel on 2 December, set before Monday [1].

**The gap.** A politician can declare innocence; a board can only declare confidence and keep investigating. Even after the jump, the stock at about $168 was well below its record close of $239 on 6 October, Investopedia noted [4]. The market has priced the return of a founder. It has not priced the report.
