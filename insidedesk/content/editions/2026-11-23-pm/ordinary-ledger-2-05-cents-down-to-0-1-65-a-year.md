**The line.** From 1 January 2027 the electricity tax for households and businesses falls from 2.05 cents to 0.1 cents a kilowatt hour, the European minimum, for one year [1]. The coalition wants to extend it but has no money for 2028 yet [1].

**What it is worth.** Verivox, the comparison site, puts the saving at about €93 a year, or 7%, for a three-person household using 4,000 kWh and paying about €1,375 a year; about €65 for two people using 2,800 kWh; and about €35 for a single person using 1,500 kWh [1]. Most tariffs oblige suppliers to pass the change on, Verivox says, so power should indeed get cheaper [1]. Desk arithmetic: 1.95 cents a kilowatt hour is the tax difference; on 2,800 kWh that is €54.60 before VAT, and with 19% VAT about €65, which matches the Verivox figure.

**What it is not.** It is not a change in the wholesale price. On Monday's auction, Tuesday's power costs €254.15 a megawatt hour on average, or about 25 cents a kilowatt hour before grid fees, taxes and margins, and €356.69 at 17:00 [2]. Desk arithmetic: the wholesale spread between Tuesday's cheapest and dearest quarter-hour is about 15.7 cents a kilowatt hour, eight times the tax cut. If you have a dynamic tariff, timing matters more than the tax; if you have a fixed tariff, as most households do, the cut is the part you will actually see.

**Who pays.** €5.4 billion, from unneeded money on the renewables account; if that runs short, the coalition says it will agree a package of savings or other taxes [1]. A good ledger has two columns. This one has a second column marked "to be decided".
