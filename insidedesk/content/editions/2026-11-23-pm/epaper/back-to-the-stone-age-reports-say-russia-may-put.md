Western military intelligence services believe Russia plans to station a nuclear weapon in space by the end of this year, NDR, WDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung reported on Monday, as summarised by DW [1]. The intelligence was reportedly shared among NATO members recently, and Defence Minister Boris Pistorius was said to have been briefed by Bundeswehr experts [1].

A Bundeswehr spokesman reportedly declined to comment on the reports, acknowledging only that Russia has such capabilities and warning that a nuclear explosion in space would devastate satellite communications: "It would effectively mean: back to the stone age" [1]. The defence ministry said it continuously observes developments in space; the Russian embassy said it had no information about such plans [1].

Context matters here. The US Defense Intelligence Agency warned in October that Russia was developing a satellite able to carry a nuclear weapon; stationing one in orbit would breach the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which Russia backed again at the UN General Assembly in December 2024 [1]. These are reports of an assessment, not an event. The calm response is the institutional one: track, attribute, and keep the treaty in the conversation.
