Black Friday falls on 27 November this year, the same day as the budget vote, and Cyber Monday on 30 November [1]. The retail association HDE expects €6.2 billion in sales across the two days, a new high and almost 6% more than last year, after sales fell for the first time in 2025 [1].

The survey behind the forecast was carried out for the HDE by IFH Köln in October among about 1,000 online shoppers [1]. Of them, 51% plan to hunt for bargains on Black Friday and more than a third on Cyber Monday [1]. About €1.9 billion of the spending is expected to go on Christmas presents; 58% say they use Black Friday for Christmas shopping and 48% Cyber Monday [1]. Among online shoppers aged 30 to 39, planned use of Cyber Monday is 47% above the average [1].

"Despite subdued consumer sentiment", spending intentions are at a new high, said Stephan Tromp of the HDE [1]. That sounds like a contradiction, and it is not: when money is tight, people do not stop buying presents; they move the buying to the days with discounts. A forecast of record sales is also a forecast of careful households.
