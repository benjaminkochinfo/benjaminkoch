Among 15- to 22-year-olds surveyed in 16 countries this year, a median of 93% have a close friend in person and 37% a close online-only friend, Gallup reports with Boston Children's Digital Wellness Lab [1]. In-person friendships are more often rated high-quality, 41% against 26% [1].

The factors most strongly associated with how young people rate their lives are all offline: how comfortably the household gets by on its income, feeling safe at home, and the quality of the relationship with the main caregiver [1]. A young person with all three at their best rates life 3.4 points higher on a 0-to-10 scale, on average, than one with all three at their worst [1]. A medium- or high-quality in-person friendship goes with higher wellbeing; an online-only friendship does not, and having one goes with more loneliness and more moderate-to-severe anxiety and depression, whatever its quality [1].

Two cautions. The surveys in 14 of the 16 countries used non-probability web samples, and these are associations, not causes [1]. Still, the calm reading is encouraging: the things that matter most are ordinary and fixable, and few of them live on a screen.
