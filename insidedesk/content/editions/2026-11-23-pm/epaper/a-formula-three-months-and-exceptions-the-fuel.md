The budget the Bundestag votes on this Friday comes with a second energy measure that gets less attention than the electricity tax. From 1 January 2027 the government wants a temporary cap on fuel prices, designed to stop abusive mark-ups at filling stations [1]. Maximum permitted prices are to be calculated from a transparent formula based on international market prices, which tightens a draft by Economy Minister Katherina Reiche that the SPD had called "toothless" [1].

The cap is meant purely as a crisis measure and is strictly limited to three months; security of supply must not be put at risk, and exceptions are planned for regional differences [1]. It is to be implemented with the Länder and the oil industry [1]. How much drivers would save is still open, tagesschau notes [1].

A formula tied to world prices cuts both ways. On Monday WTI fell 3.5% to $80.55 a barrel, its lowest since August [2]; a cap that follows the market would follow that down too, which is the point. The test of the measure will not be the headline but the formula's details: which reference price, which lag, which margin. Those are the lines to read when the text is published.
