The emergency-care reform has cleared the Bundesrat, and with it comes a right for doctors in the emergency service to dispense medicines themselves, the Pharmazeutische Zeitung reports [1]. Pharmacists had sharply criticised the plan and warned against weakening the rule that medicines are dispensed by pharmacies [1]. The Länder want medicines-law questions clarified too, and asked the federal government in a resolution to make improvements afterwards; such a resolution is not binding [1].

In the same session the Bundesrat took a first position on pharmacies making preparations in advance, without a patient-specific prescription. The Federal Administrative Court recently ruled that practice inadmissible [1]. The Länder warn that care would become much harder without it, a concern a hospital pharmacist had also raised, and recommend that the law make clear that, as a rule, no special licence is needed for such stock preparations for practice use [1].

None of this is dramatic. It is the plumbing that decides whether a patient seen at night leaves with the first dose in hand, and whether a hospital pharmacy can prepare a common mixture before the ward asks for it.
