Flooding after heavy rain on Saturday inundated 33 villages in four sub-districts of Kapuas Hulu, in Indonesia's West Kalimantan province, affecting 11,113 families or 28,875 people, the national disaster agency BNPB said on Monday [1]. The provincial government has declared an emergency alert for floods, whirlwinds and landslides, formalised by a governor's decree and valid until 31 January 2027, with a disaster response command post [1].

By Monday the water was gradually receding and traffic had resumed on major roads, BNPB said [1]. It had warned the province on Thursday 19 November of heavy to extreme rainfall [1]. High rainfall together with increasingly degraded water catchment areas makes flooding recur in West Kalimantan every November; from 2021 to 2025 BNPB recorded three to four flood events a year in the province [1].

That last sentence is the story. A flood that comes every November is not a surprise; it is a season. The useful responses are the dull ones the agency lists: early warnings two days ahead, a command post, an alert that lasts as long as the rains, and, slower and harder, the catchments upstream.
