Last week investors ran to German government bonds: the ten-year Bund yield fell 10 basis points on Friday alone [1]. On Monday, with Nvidia's chief executive back and shares rallying, you would expect that trade to reverse. In Frankfurt it did not.

By Monday afternoon the German ten-year yield was down 2 basis points on the day at 3.285%, having been higher earlier, Reuters reported; US ten-year yields moved the other way, up 5 basis points to 4.96%, as investors piled into shares [1]. Investopedia had the US ten-year at 4.98% in New York's morning, from 4.94% at Friday's close [2].

The bigger moves were in the spreads, the extra yield governments pay over Germany. France's ten-year fell 8 basis points to 4.8%, narrowing its premium to 150 basis points; Italy's fell 4 basis points to 4.365%, cutting its premium to 106.5 basis points, about 5 less than on Friday [1]. Dan Coatsworth of AJ Bell called the mood "an immense sigh of relief" [1].

Why would Bund yields fall on a risk-on day? One plausible reading: when fear eases, money leaves the safest bond and moves to the next-safest ones, which is what narrowing French and Italian spreads look like. A falling spread is a small, real saving for a treasury that refinances billions every month. It is not a verdict on France's economy, which Monday's survey showed shrinking (see Geo-economics).
