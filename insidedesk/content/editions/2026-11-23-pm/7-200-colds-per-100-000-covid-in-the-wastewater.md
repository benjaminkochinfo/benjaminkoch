The Robert Koch Institute's weekly numbers, as reported by MDR on Monday, describe a normal November [1]. In the second week of the month, 9 to 15 November, about 7.2% of people in Germany caught a new respiratory infection, about 7,200 per 100,000, roughly six million people, of whom about 800,000 had a fever [1]. For the time of year the RKI calls that moderate [1].

What is circulating is the ordinary cold: rhinoviruses and parainfluenza viruses, which mostly bring sore throats, runny noses and coughs, rarely fever [1]. Covid runs at about 3.5 infections per 100,000 and influenza at about 1.6 [1]. In wastewater, the RKI measures less Sars-CoV-2 than at this time of year in any year since 2020 [1].

The exception is age. Among people over 80 the Covid rate is 15.2 per 100,000, far higher than in other groups, and the very old still carry a high risk of dying from an infection; the RKI recommends a prompt booster for them [1]. Saxony, which runs its own sampling, reports most cases among older adults and the elderly [1].

The numbers come from GrippeWeb, a weekly survey of about 12,000 people, and from a sentinel of doctors' practices [1]. That dull machinery is why you can know, in the third week of November, that this is a cold season and not yet a flu season. The calm conclusion is the old one: the window for a flu jab in risk groups is still open until December, and the cold week ahead is when people crowd indoors [1].
