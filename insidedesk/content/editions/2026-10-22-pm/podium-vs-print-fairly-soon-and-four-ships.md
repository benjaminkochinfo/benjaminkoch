**The podium.** Flying back from a rally in South Carolina, President Trump told reporters: "No, I think we're not negotiating." Then: "We are going to have something one way or the other that'll happen fairly soon" [1]. At the rally itself he said: "It'll end very soon. We could leave now, but we want to make sure it lasts for a long time" [1]. He added that "most people seem to think it's after the election" [1]. That puts the timing after the 3 November midterms, without saying what "it" is.

**The print.** The waterway keeps its own minutes. By 14:25 UTC on Thursday, the hormuz.now counter had logged four AIS-visible commercial crossings of the Strait of Hormuz: three into the Gulf and one out [2]. One was a tanker, the 182-metre *Yoko 1*, bound for Iraq; the other three were a container ship and two bulk carriers [2]. The seven-day average is 3.1 crossings a day, against a pre-crisis baseline of 84.8 [2]. Desk arithmetic: that average is under 4% of a normal day. Wednesday's full day closed at ten.

**The noise between them.** Overnight, Iranian state television said four vessels involved in oil transfers in the northern Gulf had been struck and set on fire, and three more had hit mines west of the strait [3]. The Associated Press could not confirm the claims, and the British monitor UKMTO had not reported them [3]. UKMTO does say October has had the most reported attacks on shipping since March, and it notes that some shippers are now reluctant to report incidents [3]. The Revolutionary Guards again threatened US bases if Washington keeps guiding ships through [3].

**The gap.** "Fairly soon" has no date and no object. It could mean a deal or more bombing, and its own speaker says it may come after a vote [1]. The counter has a date, a time and a list of ships with names and flags [2]. Neither tells you the future. Only one of them can be checked tomorrow morning.

**Verdict.** The podium promises an ending. The print shows a strait running at a few per cent of normal, on a day when Iran claims seven ships were hit. If you want to know when "soon" arrives, don't listen for an announcement. Watch for the daily count to rise for a week in a row. Nothing here is advice.
