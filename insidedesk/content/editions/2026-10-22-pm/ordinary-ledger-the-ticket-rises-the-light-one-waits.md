The transport ministers of the Länder and the federal government closed their autumn conference in Hohenschwangau, in Bavaria, on Thursday [2]. For your wallet, three lines matter.

**The price.** From January the Deutschlandticket costs €66.80 a month instead of €63 [1]. Desk arithmetic: €3.80 more a month, or 6.0%; €45.60 more over a year, which brings twelve months to €801.60.

**The version that did not come.** North Rhine-Westphalia and Hamburg wanted a working group on a nationwide "Deutschlandticket light" for occasional travellers: you tap in and out on your phone, and at the end of the month you pay the cheapest fare for the rides you took, capped at the Deutschlandticket price [1]. NRW already runs such a system, eezy.nrw [1]. On Wednesday a majority took the motion off the agenda, saying it had been submitted too late [1]. Bavaria's minister, Christian Bernreiter, had said beforehand there was no money for it, and the transport operators' association also opposed it [1]. NRW's minister, Oliver Krischer, called it a missed chance and wants it on the next agenda [2][3]: "Wer nur gelegentlich Bus und Bahn fährt, muss genauso unkompliziert und zu fairen Konditionen unterwegs sein können wie die Vielfahrer" [2].

**The money behind the trains.** The ministers' resolution asks the federal government to compensate "vollständig", in full, the extra costs from higher track-access charges that follow an EU Court of Justice ruling and a Cologne administrative court ruling [4]. And it says that keeping today's regional rail service alone needs €14 billion more in regionalisation funds by 2031 [4]. Bernreiter's warning is plain: without it, "höhere Ticketpreise und Abbestellung von Verkehren", higher fares and cancelled services [5]. Federal transport minister Steffen Bilger came, in dpa's words, without fresh money [5].

**The constructive reading.** Demand is not the problem: Bavaria says regional rail use in 2024 was 23.8% above 2019 [5]. The problem is the invoice for the track, and that is a solvable kind of problem, settled in a budget line rather than a slogan. If you ride rarely, January is a good moment to count your trips against €66.80. Nothing here is advice.
