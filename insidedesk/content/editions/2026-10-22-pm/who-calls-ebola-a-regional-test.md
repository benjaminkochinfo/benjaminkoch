The World Health Organization's Africa office said on Thursday that the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak has become a regional public health concern [1]. "Ebola does not wait. It follows movement, exploits gaps, and tests the strength of our health systems," regional director Mohamed Yakub Janabi told an online briefing [1]. "Today, the test is no longer confined to one place." [1]

**The count.** Congo's health authorities reported 9,704 confirmed cases and 4,687 deaths as of 20 October, a case fatality rate of 48.3% [1]. The WHO's weekly report, with data to 19 October, had 9,637 cases and 4,646 deaths [2]. Desk arithmetic: 67 more confirmed cases and 41 more deaths in one reporting day. The outbreak now covers eight of Congo's 26 provinces, and 50 of the 66 health zones ever affected reported a new case in the last 21 days [1][2]. The WHO says its seven-day moving average shows "a modest upward trend" [2].

**The line that matters.** Janabi said about six in every ten recent deaths in Congo occurred outside treatment centres [1]. That is not a medical fact so much as a logistical one. It means people fall ill and die at home or in ordinary clinics, where nobody can isolate them and where their care exposes relatives and staff.

**Beyond Congo.** Kenya has nine cases, including one death, and Nigeria one imported case [1]. The WHO report cites insecurity, displacement and restricted humanitarian access as threats to surveillance [2]. The Africa CDC will convene health ministers virtually on 28 October [1][2].

**The constructive reading.** The figures are bad, but they are published, daily and by province, which is the precondition for any response. Ministers have a date in the diary, and the WHO has named the specific gap: getting sick people into a bed that is built for this. That is a problem of ambulances, trust and rosters. It is solvable, and it is now on the table in plain numbers.

Nothing here is a reason for readers in Europe to change plans; the figures describe a response, not a risk to you.
