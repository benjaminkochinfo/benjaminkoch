The lowest daily water level the Rhine gauge at Kaub recorded in its 2010–2020 reference period was 25 cm, on 22 October 2018 [2]. That was eight years ago today. At 16:15 this afternoon the gauge read 17 cm [1][2].

[[chart:kaub-month-below-2018-22pm]]

**The month.** The federal waterways administration publishes the gauge every 15 minutes [1]. Desk arithmetic on those readings: all 31 daily means from 22 September to 22 October sat below 25 cm. Five were below zero, the lowest a mean of −4.8 cm on 1 October, when the gauge touched −6 cm in the early morning [1]. The best day was 13 October, at 19.7 cm [1]. Thursday's mean so far is 18.2 cm [1]. The administration flags the readings as raw data, so they can still be revised [1].

**What the number is.** A gauge reading is not the depth of the river. It is measured from a fixed zero, 67.669 m above sea level at Kaub [2]. The shipping channel is guaranteed to be 1.90 m deep when the gauge reads 77 cm, the "equivalent water level" [2]. Desk arithmetic: today's 17 cm is 60 cm short of that reference. Barges load lighter, and more of them are needed for the same tonnage.

**The weather.** Storm Austen is bringing heavy rain this week, but to Brittany and Normandy, and the winds then shift towards northern France [3]. That is not the Rhine's catchment. A river at Kaub is fed by Switzerland, the Black Forest, the Main and the Neckar, and it answers to their rain, not the Channel's.

**The constructive reading.** Nothing about this month was hidden. Every reading was public, every quarter of an hour, free to use. Shippers, power stations and chemical plants along the river plan on exactly this data. A low that arrives slowly is one that logistics can price and route around, and the published numbers are what make that possible.
