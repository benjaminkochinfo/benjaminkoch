Kenya's director-general for health, Patrick Amoth, gave the country's numbers at the WHO Africa briefing on Thursday: nine Ebola cases, one death, eight patients in treatment and more than 120 people in quarantine [1]. AFP counts the nine cases since 6 October [2].

The request is the interesting part. Kenya has asked the WHO and the Africa CDC for 10,000 vaccine doses to protect frontline health workers [1]. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain, for which, AFP reports, there is still neither a vaccine nor a specific treatment [2]. The briefing account does not say which product Kenya has in mind [1]. Read it as a request to be first in line, should a candidate be made available, for the people most exposed: nurses, cleaners, ambulance crews.

Desk arithmetic: more than 120 people in quarantine for nine cases is about 13 per case. Every one of those people has to be fed, checked twice a day and kept informed for 21 days. That is the unshowy part of containment, and it is where Kenya's response will be judged: the next bulletins will show whether the circle around each case holds.
