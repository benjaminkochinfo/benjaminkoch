A George Mason University study of 221 young adults aged 18 to 21, in the Journal of Child & Adolescent Substance Use, sorted adverse childhood experiences into three patterns rather than one score [1]. Participants reported 2.7 adversities on average; about 23% reported none [1]. Those whose pattern centred on emotional abuse reported more hopelessness; those exposed to many kinds of adversity reported stronger cravings for alcohol or drugs when upset, but not when happy [1]. The study measured risk factors, not substance use itself, and the authors ask for larger, longer studies [1]. Two people with the same total can need different help.
