On Wednesday, a day before Berlin's tax estimate, the European Parliament did its own budget arithmetic. It set commitments for the EU's 2027 budget, the legal promises to pay, at €194 billion, and payments actually made at €203.3 billion [1]. That is €1.17 billion more in commitments than the Commission proposed [1].

The additions are small lines with names: €260 million for transport and energy networks, €96 million for Erasmus+, €85 million each for farm guarantees and the LIFE environment programme [1]. The bigger argument is about debt. MEPs want the repayment costs of the NextGenerationEU recovery fund and the Ukraine Support Loan covered by dedicated instruments, not taken out of those programmes [1].

"In this exceptionally difficult moment for Europe, simply having a budget is an achievement in itself," said the general rapporteur, Nils Ušakovs [1]. Desk arithmetic: the extra is about 0.6% of the total. Now comes the dull part that decides it: three weeks of talks with the Council, which set its position in September, starting on 3 November, for a deal before the year ends [1].
