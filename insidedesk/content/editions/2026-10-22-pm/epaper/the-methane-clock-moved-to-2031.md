The European Parliament voted on Thursday, by 341 to 225 with 6 abstentions, to delay the import rules of the EU Methane Regulation by three years [1]. The Commission had proposed one [1].

Under Parliament's text, importers of crude oil, gas and coal would have until 31 May 2031, instead of 2029, to show that contracts signed or renewed since 4 August 2024 cover only fuel monitored, reported and verified to standards equivalent to the EU's [1]. The first reports on the methane intensity of fuel sold in the EU would move from 5 August 2029 to 5 August 2031 [1]. Importers had warned they might not find enough compliant supply while markets are strained by the conflict in the Middle East, the press release says [1]. Reuters notes the vote followed pressure from governments including France and the US [2].

Two clauses are worth more than the headline. The longer timeline does not apply to oil, gas and coal from countries under EU import sanctions, and those volumes, including in mixed cargoes, must be reported separately [1]. And the Commission must report by 1 July 2029 on implementation and supply security [1].

It is not law yet: Parliament now negotiates with the Council [1]. A deadline moved is not a rule dropped. The measuring still has to be done; it now has a later invoice date.
