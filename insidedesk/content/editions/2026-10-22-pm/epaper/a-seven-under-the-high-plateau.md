A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck Baqen county in Nagqu, northern Tibet, at 22:33 local time on Thursday, which was 16:33 in Berlin [2][3]. The US Geological Survey puts the epicentre about 191 km east-north-east of Nagqu and has issued an orange alert on its loss model [1]. China's earthquake centre called it the strongest to hit the country since January 2024 [2].

The place matters as much as the size. The epicentre lies in a sparsely populated county where the ground within 5 km averages about 4,864 metres above sea level, some 400 km from Lhasa [2]. In the township nearest the epicentre, Xinhua reported cracked walls in the government office and collapsed houses; power was cut and phone signals were weak in the county seat [3].

The early counts do not yet agree. Reuters, citing the state broadcaster, reported at least two people injured in a village near the epicentre [2]; Xinhua's report, filed at 00:36 Beijing time, said no casualties had yet been reported and that villages were still being checked [3]. Read that as a process rather than a contradiction: rescue teams are travelling, and lists are made village by village. The figure worth waiting for is the one that comes after the checks, not the first one.
