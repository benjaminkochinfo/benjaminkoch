On Thursday the Court of Justice in Luxembourg upheld the Commission's cases against Hungary and the Czech Republic "in their entirety" [1]. Hungarian law let police question a suspect without a lawyer if the lawyer had not arrived two hours after being told of the custody; Czech law set 48 hours, or none if the lawyer could not be reached [1]. The court said the right to a lawyer means, in principle, a lawyer physically present, and the EU directive lists the only exceptions exhaustively [1]. Hungary must also write proper rules for waiving the right [1]. Both states must comply without delay [1]. A clock is not a ground for exception.
