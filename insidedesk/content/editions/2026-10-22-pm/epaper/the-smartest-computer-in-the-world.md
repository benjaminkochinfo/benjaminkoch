Researchers at the Technion in Israel put 15 first graders, average age 6.4, and 16 adults into an fMRI scanner for a five-minute spoken conversation with ChatGPT about opening a shop [1]. The study is published in Cerebral Cortex [1].

On a creativity test outside the scanner, both groups scored alike for their age [1]. Inside, adults showed stronger synchronisation in the frontoparietal control network, 0.201 against 0.113, and stronger links between that network and the attention network, 0.126 against 0.039 [1]. Only in adults did higher creativity go with stronger coordination between control and salience networks [1]. The default mode network, central to most models of creativity, did not differ [1].

The caveats are the useful part. There was no condition with a human partner or with no AI, so age and tool cannot be separated [1]. "This study does not show that ChatGPT reduces cognitive control in children or harms their brains," said the lead author, Tzipi Horowitz-Kraus [1]. The sample was small and the sexes unevenly split [1]. And the children, unlike the adults, were told they would talk to "the smartest computer in the world", which may itself invite less questioning [1].

Her practical line is modest: sit with the child and ask questions about the answers [1]. Introductions matter, too. Nothing here is advice.
