Saudi Aramco has ordered the evacuation of its headquarters in Dhahran and told employees to work remotely where possible, six people with knowledge of the matter told Reuters on Thursday [1]. Aramco did not comment; the headquarters has been damaged in recent attacks [1].

On the same day, the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted drones over Riyadh and over the holy city of Medina [1]. The Houthis in Yemen said they had attacked the Defence Ministry in Riyadh, Aramco facilities in Yanbu and King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, where Saudi authorities said two people were killed [1]. There was no immediate Saudi confirmation of attacks in Riyadh or Yanbu [1].

The US State Department has already ordered "non-emergency U.S. government employees and their family members in Saudi Arabia only" to leave [1]. US officials told Reuters it also plans to withdraw family members from several other US missions in the region [1].

Read these as two administrative acts from two very different organisations, a foreign ministry and an oil company, pointing the same way: fewer people at fixed addresses that have become targets. Nobody gave a speech about it. Moving staff out of a building is not a retreat from the oil business; it is how the business keeps running when the building cannot be guaranteed. Nothing here is advice.
