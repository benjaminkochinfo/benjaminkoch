Barclays reported third-quarter profit before tax up 12% at £2.3 billion on Thursday, roughly in line with analysts' estimates, and announced a new share buyback of up to £500 million [1]. It also said it expects to cut about 5,000 jobs, roughly 5% of its workforce, through "technology-enabled" reductions, and that artificial intelligence is beginning to deliver efficiencies [1].

The cost line is where the plan shows. Barclays booked £201 million of structural cost actions in the quarter, against £93 million a year earlier, and plans about £300 million more in the fourth [1]. Costs rose 1% to £4.6 billion while income rose 3% to £7.4 billion, so the cost-to-income ratio improved to 62% from 63% [1].

Inside the investment bank the quarter split in two: equities income rose 47%, while fixed income, currencies and commodities fell 19% [1]. The shares were down 1.5% in early London trading and little changed by midday [1][2]. The new buyback takes distributions announced this year to £2.8 billion, and the bank reaffirmed its 2028 targets [1].

A job cut written into a results release is not a forecast; it is a plan with a cost attached, and the cost is already on the invoice. Nothing here is advice.
