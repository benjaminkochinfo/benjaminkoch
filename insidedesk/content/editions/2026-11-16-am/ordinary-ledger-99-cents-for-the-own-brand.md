The ledger has a rare entry this week: a festive price that fell. The 110-gram own-brand chocolate Santa at Germany's big grocers costs €0.99 this year, down from €1.69, according to a survey by the shopping app MarkenDetektive for dpa [1]. Desk arithmetic: 70 cents, or about 41% off.

It is not alone. All five own-brand products in the survey cost less than last year, by 21.4% on average and by up to 41% in single cases [1]. Dominosteine, filled gingerbread hearts and chocolate Lebkuchen were all cheaper [1]. The survey used the dominant shelf prices of 16 Christmas items at Rewe, Edeka, Aldi and Lidl, without special offers [1]. Experts cited by dpa put it down to the cocoa market easing, with larger volumes exported again [1].

**The brands are another shelf.** On average, branded Christmas sweets cost about the same as last year, with some up and some down [1]. Milka's 90-gram Santa went from €2.99 to €3.29; the Kinder Santa rose 30 cents and Duplo's 10 cents [1]. Lindt's 70-gram Santa went the other way: €3.49, 50 cents cheaper [1].

**Read the small label.** "Per kilo, a branded Santa costs up to five and a half times as much as the own brand," says Patrick Sieber of MarkenDetektive [1]. Desk arithmetic: €0.99 for 110 grams is €9.00 a kilo; Milka at €3.29 for 90 grams is about €36.56; Lindt at €3.49 for 70 grams is about €49.86, which is where the five and a half comes from. The shelf label's unit price, required by law, does this sum for you.

**And keep the year in view.** Cheaper is not cheap. The own-brand chocolate lolly falls from €2.49 to €1.69, about a third less, and still costs 42% more than in 2022 [1]. A price that comes down a step after climbing a flight is still on the second floor. Buy the Santa you like; just let the kilo price, not the wrapper, make the first offer.
