Japan's Cabinet Office published its first estimate of the third quarter on Monday. Real GDP was flat to the first decimal, -0.0% on the quarter, or -0.1% annualised [1]. Headlines say "shrinks". The more telling line sits further down the same table: real gross domestic income fell 0.4% on the quarter, -1.6% annualised [1].

**Why income fell faster than output.** Imports of goods and services rose 2.5% in real terms in the quarter, and net exports took 0.6 percentage points off growth [1]. Reuters reports that imports rose sharply as oil and other energy purchases recovered after Japan diversified its suppliers following the disruption linked to the war in the Middle East [2]. Income can fall faster than output when what a country buys abroad gets dearer relative to what it sells. Desk arithmetic: the gap between -0.0% for output and -0.4% for income is 0.4 points in one quarter. The table does not label the cause; with energy imports as costly as Reuters describes, the oil bill is the obvious place to look.

**The till noticed.** Household consumption fell 0.3% on the quarter, -1.3% annualised [1]. Reuters puts it down to persistent price pressures and poor weather, with typhoons and heavy rain hurting spending, and to demand for cars and air conditioners fading after a temporary boost in the previous quarter [2]. "Higher prices linked to Middle East tensions will continue to squeeze household purchasing power," said Masato Koike of Sompo Institute Plus [2].

**Firms are not waiting.** Business investment rose 1.6%, backed by labour-saving technology and AI projects, according to Reuters [1][2]. The Bank of Japan raised rates in June and September, and many analysts expect another rise as soon as December [2]. The yen traded at about 159.67 per dollar on Monday morning [3].

**For you.** The lesson travels well. If your wages rise but your energy bill rises faster, your own little GDP can be flat while your income falls. The statistic that matters at home is not what you earn, but what it buys. Nothing here is investment advice.
