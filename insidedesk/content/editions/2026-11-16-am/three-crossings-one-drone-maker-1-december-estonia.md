Shortly after 3am on Saturday, security staff at Threod Systems in Viimsi, just north of Tallinn, found intruders trying to break into the building. The aim, according to Estonia's prosecutor general Astrid Asi, was to set it on fire and cause an explosion [2]. The company makes long-range reconnaissance and attack drones [1][2]. Eight people, all residents of neighbouring Latvia, were detained; five have been remanded in custody [2]. Estonia's Internal Security Service, KAPO, says its intelligence shows the job was commissioned by Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU [1][2].

By Sunday afternoon there was a policy attached. "Estonia will close all Estonian-Russian border crossing points as of 1 December," foreign minister Margus Tsahkna wrote, citing "clear evidence" of GRU involvement [2][4]. The foreign ministry's own statement is narrower and more precise: the government "plans to close its road border crossings with Russia from 1 December" [3]. Moscow's first answer came via TASS, from foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova: "We will verify all this to see if it's true", and if it is, it will be seen as "yet another bout of aggressive and unbridled Russophobia" [4].

**What actually closes.** Estonia has three road crossings with Russia, at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, open twelve hours a day from 7am to 7pm for pedestrians and freight [1]. Russia itself suspended vehicle traffic through Narva in February 2024, though people could still walk across [4]. These are among the last overland links between the EU and Russia still operating since 2022 [4]. Railway crossings at Narva and Koidula also exist; the ministry's wording covers roads [1][3]. Interior minister Igor Taro says the government will discuss in the coming days whether the closure is temporary or indefinite, and that Estonian residents and lorry drivers now in Russia have about two weeks to come home. "After that, the journey home could become long and complicated," he said [1].

**Why a border, for an arson.** The government's stated logic is not punishment alone. Prime minister Kristen Michal said Russian intelligence services "exploit border crossings to exert influence over people" [1]. Tsahkna says the closure will "help mitigate risks related to sanctions circumvention" [3]. Both are claims about mechanics: who crosses, what they carry, and who recruits them on the way. They are the kind of argument a border guard can test, which is more than can be said for most statements made on a Sunday.

**A pattern, not a one-off.** This is the second attempted arson on an Estonian defence company since August, when Tallinn-based Milrem Robotics, which supplies unmanned ground vehicles to Ukraine, was targeted; Estonia formally blamed Russian intelligence for that in September [1][2]. In August, too, Latvian nationals were arrested [4]. KAPO has warned that Russia is increasingly targeting European defence manufacturers to disrupt production and deliveries to Ukraine [2]. Michal called Saturday's attempt "part of Russia's broader hybrid war against Europe, with the aim of decreasing support to Ukraine and pressure on Russia" [2].

**What held.** The story you can take from this weekend is less about escalation than about a guard on a night shift. "Extensive damage was averted thanks to heightened security measures and a swift reaction by law enforcement," Michal wrote [4]. The suspects were detained, a prosecutor briefed journalists on Sunday, and an investigation is under way [2]. That is the dull machinery working: fences, alarms, shift rotas, a duty prosecutor, a court that remands. None of it makes a speech, and all of it is why the factory still stands.

**The next documents.** Tsahkna says he has spoken to his Latvian, Lithuanian and Finnish counterparts and informed EU colleagues, and that he will argue at Monday's meeting of EU foreign ministers in Brussels for "tougher sanctions, a stricter visa policy, and additional measures" [3]. Watch for three pieces of paper: the cabinet decision that turns a post on X into a regulation, any text out of Brussels on Monday, and the charging documents for the eight suspects. Desk arithmetic: from the attempted break-in on 14 November to the announced closure date of 1 December is 17 days.

**For you.** If you, your family or your freight route rely on the Narva, Luhamaa or Koidula crossings, plan as though roads shut on 1 December and check the Estonian government's formal decision before booking anything [1][3]. If you work at a defence supplier anywhere in Europe, the lesson from Viimsi is unglamorous: the night shift and the alarm contract are part of the defence budget too.
