**The podium.** Ahead of Tuesday's coalition committee, SPD parliamentary leader Matthias Miersch has set out a different priority from the chancellor's. An income-tax cut, he argues, would relieve a family of four by around €600 a year, far more than a lower electricity tax, which would bring "only around €100 a year", as ZDF reports his case [1]. Chancellor Friedrich Merz has promised again to cut the tax to the EU minimum of 0.1 cents per kilowatt-hour from the start of next year [1].

**The print.** The price-comparison site Verivox did the sum in October. Household electricity averaged about 34.36 cents per kWh; the tax for households is 2.05 cents; cutting it to 0.1 cents, with the VAT that falls away too, would bring the price to about 32.04 cents [2]. For a three-person household using 4,000 kWh, the annual bill would fall from about €1,375 to €1,282, a saving of €93 [2]. A couple using 2,800 kWh would save €65 and a single person on 1,500 kWh €35; across all households, about €3 billion [2]. The federal government would forgo about €5.4 billion a year in tax, a figure not yet in the 2027 budget [3].

**The gap.** On the household bill, there is hardly one: Miersch's €100 is a fair rounding of Verivox's €93, and Verivox counts three people, not four [1][2]. The gap is elsewhere. ZDF points out that the SPD's comparison leaves out the lower power costs for shops, services and restaurants [1]. Desk arithmetic: Verivox's €3 billion for households, which includes the saved VAT, against €5.4 billion in forgone tax shows that a large share of the cut lands outside the family budget, though the two figures are not measured the same way [2][3]. Finance minister Lars Klingbeil says he is open to the cut if it is paid for: "We can't just widen the gaps for the coming years by five billion" [3].

**For you.** If you use about 4,000 kWh a year, the cut is worth roughly €93 to you, or about 7% of your bill, on October prices [2]. Read your annual statement for your own consumption and multiply. Whatever the coalition decides on Tuesday, it only reaches your bill if an amendment passes in budget week [1].
