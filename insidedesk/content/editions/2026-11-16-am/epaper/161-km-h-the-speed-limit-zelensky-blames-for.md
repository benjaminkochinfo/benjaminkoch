Russia launched 132 long-range attack drones at Ukraine overnight into Sunday, 78 of them jet-powered, Ukraine's Air Force said; 106 were intercepted, including 61 of the jet-powered ones, and impacts were recorded at 20 locations [1]. One strike hit a DTEK energy facility in Odesa Oblast, leaving about 41,000 families temporarily without power, according to governor Oleh Kiper [1][2]. Desk arithmetic: 26 drones got through, about one in five.

President Volodymyr Zelensky says part of the reason is a setting, not a missile. Speed restrictions on Starshield, the military version of Starlink, cap terminals at 161 kilometres per hour (100 mph) along the front and over occupied territory, the Kyiv Independent reports, which hampers the interceptor drones that usually meet jet-powered Shaheds before they reach government-controlled areas [1]. "Many of these strikes get through precisely because speed restrictions remain in place," he wrote [1]. Starlink has denied restricting speeds, and Elon Musk said last month that the US government controls Starshield, Al Jazeera notes [2]. Zelensky has told his foreign and defence ministries to work with US officials and SpaceX on a fix [1].

Zelensky's weekly count: more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 glide bombs and 15 missiles [1]. The front line now runs through a terms-of-service page as well as a field. Who signs that page, and when it changes, is the question for this week.
