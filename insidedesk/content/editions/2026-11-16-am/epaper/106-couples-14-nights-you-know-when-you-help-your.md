For 14 nights, 106 cohabiting US couples separately rated whether they had helped or hindered each other's goals that day, giving 2,377 daily reports, according to a study in the Journal of Social and Personal Relationships [1]. People tracked their impact reasonably well, and were on average accurate about how helpful they were [1]. But they tended to underestimate how much they obstructed their partner [1]. That optimism was not harmless: when people underestimated their obstruction, both partners felt less close, and the partner was less satisfied [1]. The sample was predominantly White and recruited online, and without observers it is hard to say who misjudged [1]. The cheap fix is old: ask, tonight, what made your partner's day harder.
