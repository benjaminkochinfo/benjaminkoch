Spot gold was up 0.3% at $3,892.42 an ounce at 01:50 CET on Monday, and December futures were up 0.2% at $3,895.60, Reuters reported [1]. That came after a 4.5% fall last week that took bullion to its lowest in more than a year [1].

The cause is plain arithmetic about interest. US producer prices posted their biggest annual rise in four years in October, and markets put a 94% chance on a quarter-point Federal Reserve rise in December, according to CME's FedWatch tool [1]. Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid said on Sunday that inflation was still "running too hot" [1]. Gold pays no interest, so every rise in rates makes holding it a little dearer in lost income [1].

Silver was up 0.2% at $48.80, platinum at $1,497.12 and palladium at $1,001.40 [1]. Investors now wait for remarks from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh later on Monday and for the minutes of the October meeting on Wednesday [1].

Desk arithmetic: backing out Monday's 0.3% rise puts the starting point near $3,881; a 4.5% weekly fall to there means a drop of roughly $183 an ounce from about $4,064. An approximation, not a quoted price.

For you: gold is often sold as protection against inflation. This week it showed the other half of the bargain: when central banks fight inflation with rates, the metal can fall while prices rise. Nothing here is investment advice.
