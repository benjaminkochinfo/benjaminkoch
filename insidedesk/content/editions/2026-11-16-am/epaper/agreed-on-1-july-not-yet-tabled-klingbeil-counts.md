SPD leader and finance minister Lars Klingbeil used a members' dialogue with around 120 Brandenburg Social Democrats in Potsdam on Saturday to put a date on his impatience [1]. "When I look at 1 July and ask what we actually decided there, we could use a bit more speed," he told journalists afterwards [1].

He named two items: the Healthy Heart Act, for more prevention of cardiovascular disease, and an appointment guarantee for specialists for patients with statutory insurance [1]. Both, he said, were agreed in the coalition committee on 1 July, "and the health minister has not yet presented anything" [1]. The health minister is Carsten Linnemann of the CDU, in office since 29 July, when he succeeded Nina Warken; the Healthy Heart Act had already been approved by the previous coalition's cabinet in 2024, dpa notes [1]. Klingbeil added that he does not blame Linnemann, but wants a "high pace of reform" [1].

On the electricity tax he kept the door ajar and the wallet shut: "We said that if it is financially possible, we want to do it," he said, adding that he was keen to see the Union's funding proposals and that there was no infighting in the coalition [1].

Desk arithmetic: from the 1 July coalition committee to Tuesday's is 139 days. Promises in a coalition paper are cheap; the useful test is a bill number. Watch whether Tuesday ends with drafts and dates for these two, or only with a statement.
