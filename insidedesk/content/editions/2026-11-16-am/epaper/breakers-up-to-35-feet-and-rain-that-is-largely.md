A series of Pacific storms, fed by an atmospheric river, is moving through the American West and will keep doing so well into this week, The Weather Channel reports [1]. Its forecasters expect 3 to 6 inches of rain in southern Oregon, far northern California, the northern Sierra Nevada foothills and Washington's Olympic Peninsula, and more than an inch from the Bay Area to western Washington and Oregon [1].

The headline hazard is at the coast. Waves of 15 to 25 feet are expected, breaking up to 35 feet at favoured spots, highest from northern California to Oregon [1]. The National Weather Service in Portland warns that logs carried by cresting waves have killed and seriously injured people on beaches before [1]. High tides on top of the swell could bring several feet of coastal flooding during each storm [1].

Inland, the southern Sierra Nevada could see 1 to 2 feet of snow by the end of Monday, and more than 2 feet by the end of the week, with very difficult travel through the passes [1].

And then the sentence worth keeping: because this is the first stronger system of the season, flooding is not expected and the rain will "largely be beneficial" [1]. The forecasters add the caveat themselves. Later storms, falling on softened soils and on wildfire burn scars, raise the risk of mudslides and debris flows [1].

The first storm refills. The third one tests the drains. The work in between is clearing them.
