Brent crude was down 47 cents, or 0.49%, at $95.37 a barrel by 02:45 CET on Monday, and US WTI was 23 cents lower at $88.33, Reuters reported [1].

The reason is a word the market had not used since the war with Iran began. "For the first time since the US-Iran conflict began, traders are actively contemplating the possibility of excess supply rather than persistent tightness," said Tim Waterer of KCM Trade [1]. The International Energy Agency said on Friday that supply will average 105 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, 1.7 million more than forecast demand; it had previously expected a deficit [1]. The agency added that 71 million barrels from members' emergency stocks, including 15 million barrels of diesel, will be released by the end of February [1].

Washington helped at the margin: President Trump extended a waiver of the Jones Act for 90 days, letting foreign-flagged tankers carry fuel between US ports [1].

The floor is freight. Waterer noted shipping rates remain high because of the risks in the Strait of Hormuz [1]. On Sunday night Iran's navy said a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the Strait, a report Reuters could not independently verify [1]. CNBC TV18 noted "fresh attacks in West Asia" as a reason the physical market may stay volatile [2].

For you: a surplus forecast is not yet a cheaper tank. Watch the barrels arrive, not the adjectives. Nothing here is investment advice.
