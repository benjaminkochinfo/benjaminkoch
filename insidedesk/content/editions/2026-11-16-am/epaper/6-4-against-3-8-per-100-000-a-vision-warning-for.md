The US Food and Drug Administration has told makers of Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound and every other GLP-1 drug sold in the country to add language on a possible increased risk of non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy, NAION, Complex reports, citing CNN [1]. The rare condition cuts blood flow to the optic nerve, can strike without pain and can leave permanent loss of sight; the warning goes into the labels' "Warnings and Precautions" section [1].

The numbers are small, and the FDA says so. An analysis of 126 controlled trials found 10 probable NAION cases among GLP-1 users and five among those on placebo: 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years against 3.8 [1]. Cases have been reported with every GLP-1 on the US market, but the agency found no biological mechanism [1]. Novo Nordisk says the data "do not establish a cause-and-effect relationship"; Eli Lilly says it is evaluating the update [1].

Europe got there first: in 2025 the European Medicines Agency listed NAION as a very rare side effect of semaglutide, affecting up to one in 10,000 patients taking it for at least a year [1]. The FDA's advice to patients is plain: sudden loss of sight, blind spots or blurred areas mean calling a clinician at once [1].

A rare risk on a widely used drug is still a rare risk. It is also, at this scale, a number of people, which is why the leaflet matters.
