Japan's real GDP fell an annualised 0.1% in July to September, the Cabinet Office said on Monday, a quarter-on-quarter change it rounds to minus 0.0% [1]. It is the first contraction in four quarters [3]. A Reuters poll had expected annualised growth of 0.3%, and Jiji's survey of 21 think tanks had the same median [2][3]. In nominal terms GDP rose 0.4%, or 1.6% annualised [3].

The interesting line is trade. Imports of goods and services rose 2.5% on the quarter while exports slipped 0.2%, and net exports took 0.6 percentage points off growth [1]. Reuters says imports rose sharply as oil and other energy purchases recovered after Japan diversified its procurement following disruptions linked to the Middle East war [2]. Private consumption fell 0.3%, hit by prices and by heavy rain and typhoons; business investment rose 1.6%, helped by labour-saving and AI projects [2].

Economy minister Minoru Kiuchi said it will be necessary to keep "closely monitoring the impact of the Middle East situation" [2]. Many analysts expect the Bank of Japan's next rate rise as soon as December, Reuters reports [2].
