For the first time since 2010 the American Cancer Society has updated its prostate cancer guideline, and it now makes a direct recommendation: a PSA-based test every two years for average-risk men aged 50 to 69 [1]. Black men and men with a strong family history should be tested every year from 45 [1]. The old guideline asked mostly for shared decision-making [1]. The safeguards are part of the package: an elevated PSA should be repeated within two weeks to three months, then followed by MRI or a validated biomarker before any biopsy, and low-risk cancers should be offered active surveillance first [1]. No screening is recommended from 80, or for men not expected to live another ten years [1]. The society estimates 333,830 US diagnoses and 36,320 deaths this year [1].

A blood test is only the first form in a longer file. The guideline's real work is in the follow-up steps.
