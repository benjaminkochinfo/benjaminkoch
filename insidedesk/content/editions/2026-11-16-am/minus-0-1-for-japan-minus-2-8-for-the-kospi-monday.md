Two statistics offices spoke before breakfast, and neither said what the forecasters had pencilled in. Japan's gross domestic product fell an annualised 0.1% in July to September, against a median forecast of 0.3% growth in a Reuters poll; it is the first decline in four quarters [2]. China's industrial output grew 4.8% on the year in October, against an expected 5.3%, and retail sales rose just 0.4%, against 0.8% expected [3].

[[chart:asia-monday-16nov]]

**The tape, at about 04:49 CET.** The Kospi was down 2.8%, pressed by its tech heavyweights; the Nikkei 225 was down 1.6% after wiping out early gains, with tech leading the fall; the Shanghai Composite was off 0.4% and Australia's ASX 200 0.1% [1]. Hong Kong went the other way: the Hang Seng was up 0.9%, led by consumer and electric-vehicle stocks [1]. These are intraday snapshots, not closes.

**Read the split, not the headline.** On a day of weak consumer data, Hong Kong's consumer names rose and Seoul's chip names fell [1]. That is not the data talking. It is the calendar: Nvidia reports on Tuesday, and Newsquawk notes reports over the weekend that co-founder Jensen Huang was considering a return as chief executive, while Treasury Secretary Bessent said the company needs a "shake-up" with an external CEO [1]. A market pricing a boardroom is not pricing a GDP print.

**The quarter itself was less dramatic than "shrinks".** In quarterly terms the fall was 0.02%, Reuters reports; consumption fell 0.3% and capital spending rose 1.6% [2]. "There is no need to be overly pessimistic about the result," Kento Minami of Daiwa Securities told Reuters [2]. In Europe, Euro Stoxx 50 futures were up 0.2% before dawn [1].

**For you.** A red Monday in Asia is a price, not a verdict. The verdicts this week come on paper: an earnings report on Tuesday, US retail sales and the Fed's minutes [4]. Read those, then the mood. Nothing here is investment advice.
