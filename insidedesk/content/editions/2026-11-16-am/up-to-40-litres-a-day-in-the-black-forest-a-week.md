The weather service's weekly hazard outlook, issued at 05:00 CET, starts with a sentence that will not trend: from Tuesday, a spell of continuous rain sets in on several west-facing upland slopes, with the southern half of the country at times stormy [1]. In the Black Forest the Deutscher Wetterdienst expects "unwetterartige" amounts, severe-weather levels of rain [1].

The figures are specific. On Wednesday and Thursday, 25 to 35 litres per square metre in 24 hours are possible, though rated unlikely, on some western upland slopes; in the Black Forest severe weather is rated likely, with 30 to 40 litres in 24 hours possible [1]. On the ridges of the uplands and the Alps, gusts of force 9 to 10 are likely from the south-west, and isolated hurricane-force gusts of force 11 are not ruled out [1]. The rain eases only slowly from the north towards Saturday [1].

The model run behind the forecast puts numbers on the week. At a grid point near the Feldberg, at 1,400 metres, the DWD's ICON model via Open-Meteo shows 29.4 mm on Tuesday alone and 123.1 mm in total from Monday to Sunday; at a grid point near Freudenstadt, at 758 metres, 33.4 mm on Tuesday and 114.0 mm over the seven days [3]. Desk arithmetic: that is roughly 110 to 125 litres on every square metre of slope in a week. These are forecasts, not measurements, and grid points are not rain gauges.

Monday itself is the warm-up: mostly cloudy, showery rain from the west, highs of 8 to 14 degrees, and strong to stormy gusts, with severe gales in the uplands [2]. Tuesday brings a sharp divide, with only 4 to 8 degrees in the north and 15 in the south-west, locally up to 18 on the Upper Rhine [2].

None of this is dramatic, which is the point. Long rain on saturated slopes is the kind of weather that fills small rivers, cellars and loss adjusters' diaries, not front pages. The useful machinery is local: gauges, gutters, and the municipal crews who clear the drains before Wednesday rather than after it.
