Monday's electricity was bought on Sunday, and the auction has set the price: delivery in Germany and Luxembourg will average €194.49 per megawatt hour [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €54.79 dearer than Sunday's €139.70, the week's cheapest day [1]. It is also €151.15, or about 44%, cheaper than last Monday, 9 November, at €345.64 [1]. Which of those two comparisons you hear first tells you more about the speaker than about the market.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-16nov]]

**The wind explains the week, not the day.** SMARD's forecast for Monday is about 775.7 GWh of onshore wind and 119.8 GWh offshore [2]. Desk arithmetic: some 895.5 GWh together. Last Monday the meters logged only about 59.7 GWh onshore and 18.1 GWh offshore [2], about 77.8 GWh, so this Monday is forecast to bring roughly eleven times the wind of a week ago (desk arithmetic). That is why the bill is €151 lighter.

**And the weekday explains the day.** Monday is forecast to bring more wind than Sunday actually delivered: the realised figures for Sunday were about 589.8 GWh onshore and 146.1 GWh offshore, about 735.9 GWh together [2]. Desk arithmetic: around a fifth more wind, and still a higher price. The calendar is the obvious suspect: factories and offices are back. We note the pattern rather than claim a demand figure we have not seen published.

**Read the hours.** Monday's cheapest hour is 03:00 to 04:00 at €114.10; the dearest is 18:00 to 19:00 at €268.29 [1]. Desk arithmetic: a spread of €154.19 within one day, nearly three times the gap between Sunday's and Monday's averages. If you are on a dynamic tariff, the early-evening hour is the one to avoid, and that is printed in the price list, not in a forecast. On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches your bill this week.

**For you.** Wholesale power is not a mood; it is an auction held every day around midday, with its results published for anyone to read [1]. Last week it taught that wind matters more than headlines. This Monday it adds a smaller lesson: even a windy weekday costs more than a windy Sunday. Nothing here is investment advice.
