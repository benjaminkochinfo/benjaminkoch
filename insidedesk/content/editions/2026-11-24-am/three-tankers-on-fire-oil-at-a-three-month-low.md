Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Monday that three "violating" oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported, citing Press TV [1]. Iranian state media said one was near the southern coast and two east of the strait, and warned ships against unauthorised routes, Iran International reported [2]. Separately, the United States is investigating whether Iran was involved in cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers, the Wall Street Journal reported, according to Reuters [1].

The price did not jump. Brent posted its lowest close since 31 August on Monday and was down 0.45% at $88.97 a barrel early on Tuesday; US crude slipped to $79.94 after its lowest level since 26 August [1]. In the previous session Brent fell 3.5% to $89.37, the Economic Times reported [3].

The reason is in the inventories. Global observed liquids stocks rose 52 million barrels in October, implying oversupply of 1.7 million barrels a day, Deutsche Bank said, and Libya's Sharara pipeline has reopened [1]. ANZ's Daniel Hynes calls it a move "from acute scarcity towards persistent vulnerability, rather than returning to normal" [1].

That phrase is the useful one. A full tank absorbs a headline; a thin one amplifies it. The market is betting the tanks are fuller now. Bets about tanks can be checked every Wednesday.
