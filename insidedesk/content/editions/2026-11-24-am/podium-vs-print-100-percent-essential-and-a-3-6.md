**The podium.** "The economy is strong and resilient, despite large shocks and elevated uncertainty," John Williams said on Monday. "But getting inflation back to 2 percent is 100 percent essential" [1]. He put inflation at about 3½% and named three causes: tariffs, energy and supply disruptions from the conflicts in the Middle East and elsewhere, and demand for AI-related goods [1]. "Monetary policy cannot move ships or reopen pipelines and refineries," he said, but it must stop those shocks becoming persistent [1].

**The print.** The Fed's preferred gauge rose 0.5% in September, putting the annual rate at 3.6%, the largest monthly gain since March, CNBC notes [2]. Williams himself expects about 3½% this year and inflation "just above 2 percent" next year, reaching 2% in 2028 [1].

**The gap.** The speech and the number agree; that is the news. Williams did not use the word "hike", but futures traders now fully price a quarter-point rise on 9 December, up from 89% on Friday [3]. When the dovish wing of a committee says "100 percent", the market does not wait for the minutes.

**The dull part.** Williams's encouraging lines get fewer headlines: housing services inflation has slowed, wage growth is not adding pressure, and longer-term expectations "remain well anchored" [1]. That is where inflation is defeated: in rent contracts and pay slips, not on podiums.
