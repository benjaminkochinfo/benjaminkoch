Bomb threats reached hundreds of schools across Germany on Monday morning by email, more than 450 of them in Baden-Württemberg alone, according to the Freiburg police headquarters, tagesschau.de reported [1]. Around 260 schools in Saxony-Anhalt were affected, and there were reports from Berlin, Bavaria, Hamburg, North Rhine-Westphalia and other Länder [1]. Police said the wording was the same and there was no indication of a real danger [1].

Some schools were evacuated as a precaution; many held lessons as normal [1]. Investigations for disturbing the public peace by threatening crimes have been opened [1]. A spokesperson for the federal interior ministry said the incidents fit into "a generally high abstract threat situation" but that schools and authorities are prepared [1].

The wave is not only German. Schools in Denmark received threats on Monday; in recent weeks Ireland, the UK and Italy had them too, with 6,000 schools in Italy, and the emails there apparently came from a Taiwanese account, according to findings so far [1].

The useful observation is how much the response has become routine. A police force in Ulm said it took no specific action "because it quickly became clear that these were fake reports" [1]. Practised calm is a security measure in its own right.
