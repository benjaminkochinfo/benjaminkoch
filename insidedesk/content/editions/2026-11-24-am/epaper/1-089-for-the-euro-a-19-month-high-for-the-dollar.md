The dollar hit a more than 19-month high against the euro on Monday, CNBC reported, citing Reuters; the euro fell 0.7% to $1.089 and the dollar index rose 0.62% to 104.45 [1]. The dollar also gained 0.37% to 159.14 yen, while sterling fell 0.45% to $1.3022 [1].

The drivers are named: strong US data, stubborn inflation and hawkish Fed comments have raised bets on rate rises, and capital flowing into US technology has added support [1]. Fed funds futures fully price a 25-basis-point rise in December, from 89% odds on Friday, but have largely priced out a 50-point move [1].

For you, a weaker euro shows up first in anything priced in dollars: fuel, many raw materials, a holiday in the US. Not dramatic, not nothing.
