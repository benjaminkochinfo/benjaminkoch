The two-year Treasury yield rose more than 7 basis points to 4.69% on Monday, the 10-year about 2 basis points to 4.932%, and the 30-year was little changed at 5.299%, CNBC reported [1]. "Assets that had offered a measure of safety last week have retreated today," said Macquarie's Thierry Wizman [1].

Wednesday brings the revised third-quarter GDP figure and October's PCE inflation, for which economists polled by Dow Jones expect a monthly rise of 0.3% [1]. The short end moves first because it listens to the committee most closely.
