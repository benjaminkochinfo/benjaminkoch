More than half of Afghanistan's provinces have faced severe drought in the past year, and aquifer stores have dropped by more than a quarter, PBS News Hour reported from Bamyan province; NGOs have warned the country could run out of groundwater by 2030 [1]. UN documentation says half the residents of Yakawlang district have left this year [1].

The UN says 22 million people need humanitarian assistance, but by mid-2026 only 14% of the $1.7 billion needed this year had been funded; 3.7 million children face acute malnutrition [1]. Some people spend up to 30% of their income on clean drinking water, PBS reports [1].

A rudimentary piped irrigation system would spread the water more evenly, the report notes, but communities cannot afford one [1]. The cheapest adaptation is often a pipe nobody pays for.
