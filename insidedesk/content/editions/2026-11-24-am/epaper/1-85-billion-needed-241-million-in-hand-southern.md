When disaster-risk ministers of the Southern African Development Community met on 12 November, they were told about 41 million people could be at risk in the coming rainfall and cyclone season; costed plans need about $1.85 billion, with about $241 million available, a shortfall of about $1.61 billion, Nature Africa reports [1]. The WMO projected sea-surface temperatures in the El Niño tracking region about 3.8°C above normal from November to January, with warming peaking around December [1].

Seasonal outlooks favour drier conditions across much of southern Africa and heavier rain in parts of East Africa [1]. "These are seasonal outlooks, not predictions of individual events," WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said in October [1]. Lesotho and Eswatini triggered early-action protocols in September [1].

The most forward-looking measures are the smallest: burying pipes deeper, fixing leaks, raising toilets above floodwater [1]. Adaptation mostly looks like maintenance done early.
