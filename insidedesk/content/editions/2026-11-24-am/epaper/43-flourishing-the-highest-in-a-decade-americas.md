More than four in ten US college students are flourishing, the highest share in a decade, according to the 2025–26 Healthy Minds Study of 67,477 students at 107 institutions, Inside Higher Ed reports [1]. On the eight-item Flourishing Scale, 43% had positive mental health, up from 38% the year before [1].

Depression symptoms were reported by 34%, down from 37% but still above about 25% in 2015–16; anxiety by 30%, down from 32% [1]. Stigma eased: 5% said they would think less of someone who had received treatment, down from 7% [1]. One line did not move: 28% reported symptoms of disordered eating in both years [1].

"To have decreases in loneliness, increases in flourishing, to see decreases in perceived stigma—that's all moving in a positive direction," said co-author Justin Heinze [1]. Slow improvement is still improvement; it just does not trend.
