WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus toured a darkened paediatric cardiology centre in Cuba on Monday, on a three-day visit at the invitation of health authorities, AP reports [1]. Hospitals across the island face shortages of syringes, gauze, vaccines and anaesthetics, and lack spare parts for haemodialysis and CT machines [1].

A government report released in June said survival among children with cancer had fallen to 65% from 85% before the energy blockade began in January [1]. Analysts blame fuel shortages they link to tightened US sanctions on the energy sector [1]. "I want to use this visit to better understand the challenges you face," Tedros was quoted as saying by state media [1].

Health systems rest on electricity and spare parts before they rest on doctors. Cuba is showing what happens when the dull layer goes first.
