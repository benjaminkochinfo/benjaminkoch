The European Commission opened a safeguard investigation on Monday into a surge of imports of composite input materials: glass-fibre reinforcements, epoxy and other manufacturing resins, and chemicals used to make them [1]. These go into components for wind energy, electricity infrastructure, aerospace and defence, the Commission says, and the surge is driven by "growing global overcapacity" and trade restrictions elsewhere [1].

Provisional measures could come four or five months after initiation if the conditions are met, and would need a qualified majority of member states [1]. Unlike the existing anti-dumping duties on specific products and origins, a safeguard covers imports from all countries [1]. Euronews reports that Chinese producers had been circumventing earlier duties by shifting production to other countries, and that the EU's trade deficit with China has reached a record €1 billion a day [2].

A safeguard is a blunt instrument, deliberately. It is what you use when the origin keeps moving and the paperwork cannot keep up.
