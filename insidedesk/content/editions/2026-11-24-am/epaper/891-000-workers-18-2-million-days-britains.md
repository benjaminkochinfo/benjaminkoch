An estimated 891,000 workers in Great Britain suffered from work-related stress, depression or anxiety in 2025/26, resulting in 18.2 million working days lost, according to the Health and Safety Executive, which published new research on Monday [1]. Stress, depression or anxiety accounted for 49% of all work-related ill health and 46% of days lost to it [1].

The research names the hazards plainly: excessive demands, low control, poor support, conflict, role uncertainty and organisational change [1]. "Employers are required by law to protect their employees from harm," said the HSE's Mike Calcutt, and that includes psychosocial hazards [1]. A risk assessment is a boring document. It is also how a hazard stops being a personality trait.
