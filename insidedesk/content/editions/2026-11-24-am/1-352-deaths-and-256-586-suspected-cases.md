Nine more children died with measles-like symptoms in Bangladesh in the 24 hours to 08:00 local time on Sunday, bringing confirmed and suspected measles-linked deaths in 2026 to 1,352, according to the Directorate General of Health Services as reported by United News of Bangladesh [1]. Of those, 105 are confirmed and 1,247 suspected [1].

The case count is larger still: 256,586 suspected cases and 26,136 laboratory-confirmed infections this year [1]. Since 15 March, 229,530 patients with suspected measles have been admitted to hospital; 220,705 have recovered [1].

The cause is being argued in politics. Former prime minister Sheikh Hasina alleges that measles-rubella vaccine stocks ran out in March 2026 and that a supplementary campaign was not launched on time; she blames both the former interim administration and the current government [1]. Those are her claims, not established findings.

The general lesson is not in dispute. Measles spreads where coverage falls below the line needed for community protection, and the gap opens long before the first case: in a procurement file, a missed tender, a cold-chain delay. The repair is equally unglamorous: stock, schedule, and a campaign that turns up when it says it will.
