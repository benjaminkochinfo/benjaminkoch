Nearly half of India, 47.2% of its area, is in some category of drought, according to the India Drought Monitor run by IIT Gandhinagar, up from 46.6% a week earlier, Down To Earth reports [1]. IIT Bombay's forecasting system expects agricultural drought to persist across parts of Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Karnataka, western Madhya Pradesh, Telangana and south-western Andhra Pradesh [1].

The cause is the monsoon that ended in September. India received 759.4 mm of rain against a long-period average of 868.6 mm, a deficit of about 13%, making 2026 the fourth-driest monsoon since 2001, according to the India Meteorological Department [1]. Karnataka's deficit of 35% was its worst in 55 years [1].

[[chart:india-monsoon-deficit-24nov]]

The effect is now in the fields. By 13 November farmers had sown 17.83 million hectares of rabi (winter) crops, against 20.10 million a year earlier, a fall of about 11.3% [1]. Six states have declared districts drought-affected, though the monitors show dry conditions well beyond them [1].

That is the forward-looking part. Winter crops in rain-fed areas depend on moisture left from the monsoon; researchers suggest farmers in the driest districts consider less water-hungry crops [1]. A drought that is measured weekly can be managed weekly. Most of the damage is done where nobody counts.
