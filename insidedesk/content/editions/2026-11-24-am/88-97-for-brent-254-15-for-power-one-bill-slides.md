Brent crude was down 0.45% at $88.97 a barrel early on Tuesday, after its lowest close since 31 August on Monday; US crude was at $79.94 [1].

The euro moved the other way. It fell 0.7% to $1.089 on Monday, as the dollar reached a more than 19-month high on bets of a Federal Reserve rate rise in December [2].

Desk arithmetic: at $88.97 and $1.089, a barrel of Brent costs about €81.70. Before Monday's 0.7% fall the euro stood at roughly $1.0967; at that rate the same dollar price would have been about €81.12. The currency took back roughly 58 cents a barrel.

At home, the other bill is not sliding. Germany's day-ahead power price for delivery today averages €254.15 per megawatt hour, up from €240.42 on Monday, with quarter-hours from €199.45 at 03:30 to €356.69 at 17:00, according to Energy-Charts data from the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD platform [3].
