IG Metall says 340,000 employees from 240 companies took part in all-day warning strikes in the metal and electrical industries up to and including Monday; new negotiations are due on Wednesday in Mannheim, junge Welt reports [1]. Mercedes-Benz confirmed to Euronews that its plants were affected; the employers' association Südwestmetall called the 24-hour strikes an "unnecessary escalation" [2].

The union is seeking a 5% rise over one year for 3.7 million workers [2]. Employers offer a one-off payment in July 2027 worth 1% of gross annual pay and a 1.3% rise from July 2028 [2].

Desk arithmetic, on a gross salary of €3,500 a month (€42,000 a year): 5% would be €175 a month, €2,100 a year. The employer offer is a single €420 in July 2027, then €45.50 a month from July 2028. Over the 24 months from January 2027, the union's demand would add about €4,200 gross if it applied from the start; the offer adds €420 plus six months of €45.50, about €693. Before tax and contributions, and only for covered sectors.

The gap is wide, but notice where it sits: in dates more than in rates. Pay deals are usually closed by moving a start month, not a decimal.
