You went to bed on Friday with three American indexes in correction. You wake up on Tuesday to find most of that undone, not by a profit warning withdrawn or an order book revised, but by a personnel decision.

**The decision.** Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, the company said on Monday [4]. The board said it was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor" and had received input "from senior levels of the United States government" [4]. Reuters notes that less than a week earlier the chair had said Nvidia would not rehire him; the reversal came after President Donald Trump urged his return [3]. The board's investigation into a possible foreign intelligence effort targeting Huang continues, and "will proceed to conclusion", the company said [4].

**New York, settled.** The S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq Composite 6.51% to 24,089.56, both their best days since April 2025; the Dow added 1,447.98 points, or 3.01%, to 49,595.31 [1]. Nvidia closed up 31.5%, adding nearly $1 trillion in market value, Reuters reported, though that was not enough to erase all its losses since Huang resigned [3]. Nine of the eleven S&P 500 sectors rose; technology gained 9.03%, while energy fell 1.32% [2].

**The calculation nobody posts.** On Friday the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73 [1]. Desk arithmetic: to get back to Thursday's level, about 23,880, it needed a rise of roughly 5.6%, not 5.28%, because a fall and a rise of the same percentage do not cancel. Monday's 6.51% was enough for that, with a little to spare; it did not undo the rest of last week's selling. A good day always sounds bigger than the hole it fills.

**Asia, unsettled.** By early Tuesday trade, Taiwan's Taiex was up more than 9%, with TSMC and MediaTek up nearly 10%, AP reported [6]. Seoul's Kospi was up 7.19% at 5,047.15 on Tuesday morning after gains triggered a five-minute "sidecar" curb on programme trading, the South China Morning Post said [7]. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 3.9%, with Tokyo Electron up 10.5% [6]. MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan rose 4.6%, Mint reported, citing Reuters [8]. None of these are closing figures yet.

**What did not move.** The dollar rose to a more than 19-month high against the euro on Monday; the euro fell 0.7% to $1.089 [5]. Fed funds futures now fully price a 25-basis-point rate rise at the Federal Reserve's December meeting, up from 89% odds on Friday [5]. The two-year Treasury yield rose more than 7 basis points to 4.69% [9]. In other words: the market bought back the AI story and, in the same session, priced a dearer cost of money. Both cannot be good news for long.

**Why the desk stays calm.** A chief executive matters, and markets are allowed to price that. But an index that loses a tenth of its value in a week and recovers most of it in a day on a board vote is telling you about positioning, not about chips shipped. Nvidia's next revenue figure will be the same whoever announced it; the governance questions Reuters lists, from the board's independence to Huang's Senate hearing, are still open [3]. The useful habit is to separate the price of a share from the invoices behind it, and to notice that Monday changed one of them.
