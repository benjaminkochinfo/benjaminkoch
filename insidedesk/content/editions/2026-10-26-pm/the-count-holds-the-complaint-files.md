Brazil's Superior Electoral Court has Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva at 51.79% and Flávio Bolsonaro at 48.21%, a margin of about 4.4 million votes [3]. AP reports Lula persuaded some 10 million additional voters between the rounds, and is due to take office for a fourth, non-consecutive term on 5 January 2027 [1].

**The complaint.** Bolsonaro said what happened on Sunday "was not normal" and called for the result to be annulled [2]. His objection is a decision by Supreme Court justice Cristiano Zanin to extend voting nationwide by two hours [2]. Lula's campaign had asked for the extension, citing voter transport problems; Bolsonaro's campaign had asked for extra time too, but only in municipalities in five states hit by rain, floods and power cuts [2].

**The rest of the world.** The EU, Mexico, Canada and Russia sent congratulations; Canada's Mark Carney wrote of "new promising commercial prospects" [4]. Donald Trump and Argentina's Javier Milei had not publicly reacted by Monday, BBC Brasil reported [5]; Donald Trump Jr criticised the extension [4].

The sequence is the familiar one: a count, a complaint, a court. Brazil has run it before: Jair Bolsonaro also refused to concede in 2022 [2]. The dull machinery usually wins when it is allowed to finish.
