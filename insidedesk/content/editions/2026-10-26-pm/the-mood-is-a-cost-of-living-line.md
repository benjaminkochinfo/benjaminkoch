The American Psychological Association's Stress in America report for 2026 found that 77% of US adults call the cost of living a significant source of stress, 74% the economy and 71% money [1]. The survey covered 3,132 adults online between 29 July and 24 August 2026 [1]. A health-policy commentator read it on Monday as stress becoming "the new normal" [2].

**The reading.** The three top answers are one answer in three coats. They are about prices, and they come from a year of war-priced oil. The report's title is about uncertainty, which is the right word: the bill you cannot predict is heavier than the bill you can.

**The European contrast.** Monday's ifo survey shows German firms more hopeful about the next six months than about today: expectations at 91.6, the current situation at 88.8 [3]. Firms can see their order books. Households mostly see the receipt. Those two moods meet, eventually, in payrolls.

A survey is not a diagnosis. It is, though, a useful reminder that the remedy for worry about prices is rarely a speech. It is a predictable bill.
