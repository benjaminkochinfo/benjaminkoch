The ifo survey asks about 9,000 firms two questions: how is business now, and how will it be over the next six months [1]. In October the answers moved apart. The expectations index rose to 91.6 from 90.4; the current situation fell to 88.8 from 89.5 [1].

Desk arithmetic: expectations now stand 2.8 points above the present. In April the order was reversed, with expectations at 83.9 below a situation of 85.4 [1]. Firms were then gloomier about the future than about the present; now they are more hopeful.

Psychologically that is the healthier gap. It is also the one that has to be paid for: if orders keep arriving, the present catches up; if they do not, expectations fall back to meet it. Manufacturing, where incoming orders rose, is where the hope has the best collateral [1].
