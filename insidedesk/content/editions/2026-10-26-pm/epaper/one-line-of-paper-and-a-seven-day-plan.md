This evening's edition asked whether anything written would come out of Tehran. Something did. The joint statement calls Monday's talks "the first meeting" of the four countries, and says the participants "agreed to continue the dialogue", including for peace and security in the region [1]. Anadolu reports that the agenda covered regional security, Israel's activities, and Palestine and Lebanon [2]. The published text names no date for a second round [1].

The more practical line came from beside the room. According to Iranian state outlets, Tehran has resubmitted, after technical talks in Doha, a plan to reopen shipping through the Strait of Hormuz in stages over seven days [3]. Its foreign ministry spokesman said Iranian negotiators were studying US counterproposals on lifting the naval blockade [3]. Tehran's condition is that the seven days do not start until $24 billion in foreign reserves is fully accessible and container clearance at Bandar Abbas is freed [3].

A statement that promises more meetings is modest. A plan with a clock, a sum and a port in it is something a clerk can check. Watch for the second meeting's date and for any reply on the sequence, not for adjectives. Nothing here is advice.
