Saudi Arabia will start storing crude oil in Singapore from the second half of 2027, energy ministers from both countries said on Monday, to shore up the resilience of supply in the region [1].

It is a modest sentence with a long logic behind it. The war that began at the end of February has disrupted shipping through both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, and October has been the worst month for attacks on commercial shipping in those waters, according to data compiled by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy and cited by Reuters [2]. A barrel already sitting in Singapore does not need to pass a strait when a refinery calls.

Storage is not glamorous. It costs money every month and earns nothing until the day it is needed. That is exactly why it is a better indicator of what producers expect than any statement: they are paying, in advance, for the next disruption to be survivable.
