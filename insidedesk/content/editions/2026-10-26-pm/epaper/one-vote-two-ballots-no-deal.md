Germany's coalition agreed to change the electoral law so that every candidate who wins a constituency also gets a seat, while keeping the Bundestag at 630 members, down from 736 [1]. So far, the coalition's working group has found no alternative to the system introduced under the previous government, tagesschau reports, and Chancellor Friedrich Merz says the coalition is a year behind [1].

The stakes are concrete. After the last federal election, 23 constituency winners could not take their seats, most of them from the CDU and CSU [1]. The CSU and the Left, unusually, agree on the complaint [1].

The deadline is administrative, which is why it is real. Constituency boundaries have to be redrawn, with the Länder involved, before the next election, and that takes time, as Merz put it: "Das ist ein aufwändiger Prozess" [1]. Electoral law is the most boring law a democracy has. It is also the one everybody else depends on.
