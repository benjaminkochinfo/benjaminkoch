Michael Alfaro, chief investment officer at Gallo Partners, told CNBC on Monday that the risk premium in oil is "about $20 a barrel" [1]. A substantial strike on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline or on refining infrastructure could send prices sharply higher, he said, but further gains would eventually weaken consumption, with more demand destruction setting in above $120 a barrel for Brent [1].

The more durable point in the same report is about refining. Damage to refineries could become a longer structural problem, extending into 2027 and beyond [1]. Crude can be rerouted; a refinery cannot be rebuilt in a month.

Prices are a conversation between producers and buyers. When buyers start driving less, heating less and ordering later, they are answering. That is not a forecast, just how invoices behave. Nothing here is investment advice.
