Urgent applications at Hessen's social courts rose by 76% in 2026 compared with 2025, while ordinary lawsuits rose by 17%, according to projections after the third quarter from the state's justice ministry [1]. Justice Minister Christian Heinz says AI-assisted filings are behind part of the rise: they are often very long, do not always show what the applicant wants, and sometimes cite court decisions that "do not exist" [1]. Because urgent cases take priority, everyone else waits longer [1].

His proposal, taken to the justice ministers' conference in Hamburg, is a €15 contribution for urgent proceedings, explicitly not a classic court fee [1]. The social association VdK rejects it: access to the law "must not depend on the wallet", said president Verena Bentele, adding that one in three lawsuits at social courts is at least partly successful [2]. The VdK wants more staff, better technology and clearer procedures instead [2].

Both sides describe a real cost. One is the judge's time; the other is the claimant's €15. The neglected option is the cheapest: a court that tells AI tools, and their users, what a valid urgent application must contain.
