The St. Johns River near Cocoa, Florida, exceeded its all-time record last week: it reached 18.19 feet on Thursday and 18.68 feet by Friday morning, against the previous record of 17.55 feet set on 11 October 1953, AccuWeather reported [1]. The gauge has 73 years of records [1].

The southern part of the basin had 8 to 18 inches of rain in three days, and the river began flooding near Cocoa on 2 November, reaching major flood stage on 9 November [1]. AccuWeather expects at least minor flooding there into January 2027 [1]. The water management district began controlled releases through the S-96 structure into the C-54 canal, the first such discharges since the tropical storms of 2017 [1].

The reason it lingers is geography. Florida's longest river flows north for 310 miles but drops less than 30 feet, about an inch a mile, crawling through marshes and lakes [1]. Fast floods make the news; slow ones fill the insurance files.
