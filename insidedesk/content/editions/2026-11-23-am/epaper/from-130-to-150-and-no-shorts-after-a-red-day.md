Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission announced on Saturday that, from Monday, short selling is banned in any stock whose price fell in the previous session [1]. It is the first blanket restriction of this kind since 2015, and it replaces a rule from Thursday that applied only to stocks down 3.5% or more [1].

Three more changes came with it. The minimum margin for short sales rises from 130% to 150% [1]. The commission suspended applications from brokers to list new put warrants on the Taiwan Stock Exchange and the Taipei Exchange [1]. And it asked lenders to suspend securities lending and to recall lent shares early or not renew the loans [1].

The context is a big number: the Taiex closed Friday 6.23% lower at 36,033.29, and from its intraday record of 49,968.92 on 6 October it had fallen 28.48% to Friday's intraday low [1]. The NT$500 billion (US$15.18 billion) National Financial Stabilization Fund has been authorised to intervene since 6 November [1].

Rules about who may sell are a brake, not an engine. They slow the speed of a fall; they cannot set the price of a chip order. Nothing here is investment advice.
