A meta-analysis of 138 studies with 35,296 students in the US and Canada, published in JAMA Psychiatry, finds that measured tolerance of uncertainty has declined steadily since the 1990s, according to a Sunday summary [1]. Study author Katherine M. Keyes sees this as a significant risk factor for anxiety disorders [1].

Uncertainty cannot be removed from a life. Tolerance of it, unlike the market, can be trained.
