The Altersvorsorgedepot, the subsidised private pension account that replaces Riester, starts on 1 January [1]. The publicly organised basic fund, however, will "probably only" be available after the start, a finance ministry spokesman told RND; government circles expect it next summer at the earliest, tagesschau reported [1]. Possible operators include the Bundesbank, KfW or the Deutsche Rentenversicherung [1].

The subsidy is already fixed. Up to €360 a year saved, the state adds 50 cents per euro; from €360 to €1,800, it adds 25 cents, for a maximum basic allowance of €540 a year [1]. Families can get a child bonus of €300 per child per year from a monthly contribution of €25 [1].

The consumer federation's Ramona Pop urged the ministry to deliver the regulation quickly, because the public product shows "what a good product may cost" [1].

Desk arithmetic: saving €1,800 a year gets you €180 + €360 = €540 in allowances. A product that waits for the public benchmark loses nothing in allowance if it starts later in the year; what you lose by rushing is the comparison. This is not advice.
