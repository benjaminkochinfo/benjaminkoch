A survey of about 50,000 people in 92 countries, published in Social Indicators Research by more than 260 researchers, finds that mental health has worsened in recent years, especially among the young, the Max Planck Institute in Freiburg reported [1]. Young respondents in particular lacked vitality, emotional stability and resilience [1].

The patterns are familiar but useful. Employment and higher income go with better mental health; lower formal education with lower well-being; living alone with lower well-being than living with others; and hybrid workers did better than fully remote workers [1].

The striking finding is the split. People scored highest on relationships, meaning, competence, engagement and self-esteem, yet rated external factors such as political and economic instability negatively [1]. Experts call this the "optimism gap": a decent life of one's own and a gloomy view of society [1].

On a morning when the screens are red, that gap is worth naming. Your own week is mostly made of the things the survey says you rate well.
