In 2026, up to 30 September, 600 medicinal products were affected by supply shortages, the federal government said in its answer of 29 October to a parliamentary question (Bundestag printed paper 21/8860), as summarised by the industry association Pharma Deutschland [1]. A supply shortage does not necessarily become a care shortage if alternatives or other dosage forms are available, the government noted [1].

Children's medicines improved: first reports of shortages fell from 43 in 2024 to 19 in 2025 and 12 in 2026, though the average duration was 74 days this year after 69 in 2025 [1]. The government says it cannot yet reliably say how much of that is due to the 2023 shortage law [1].

The structural line did not move much. According to the BfArM, 164 of 585 care-relevant active substances have at most three manufacturers [1]. The government is cautious about blanket stockpiling duties and export bans, pointing to storage, capital and expiry costs, and puts its hopes in the EU's Critical Medicines Act [1].

Shortages are solved by boring things: more suppliers per substance, contracts split between them, and prices that make the second supplier worth keeping.
