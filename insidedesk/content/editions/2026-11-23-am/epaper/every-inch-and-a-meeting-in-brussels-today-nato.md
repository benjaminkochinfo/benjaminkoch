NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte pledged in Warsaw on Saturday to defend Poland after Russian missiles landed in the country, AFP reported [1]. Warsaw said two Russian missiles hit its south-east on 19 November and called it an "intentional provocation"; Rutte had earlier condemned "grave violations" [1].

NATO's North Atlantic Council discusses the incident in Brussels today "as part of regular consultations among allies", an alliance official told AFP; Poland's deputy defence minister Paweł Zalewski will represent Warsaw [1]. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Poland would ask NATO to strengthen detection and surveillance over Polish territory [1]. The Institute for the Study of War said it believed Russia fired the missiles intentionally, timed with exercises in Poland [1].

The words are large; the request is technical. Radars, sensor coverage and response times are what an alliance can actually change by Monday evening.
