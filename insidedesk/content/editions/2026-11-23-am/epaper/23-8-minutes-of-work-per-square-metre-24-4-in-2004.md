Measured in working time, renting in Germany has not become more expensive since 2004, according to a new calculation by the ifo Institute's Dresden branch and the Hamburg Institute of International Economics (HWWI), reported by stern [1]. In 2025, employees had to work an average of 23.8 minutes a month for one square metre of rented space; in 2004 it was 24.4 minutes [1].

The averages hide big differences. Between 2004 and 2024 the working time needed for rent fell by more than 5% in 188 of 382 districts, mostly in eastern Germany, and rose by more than 5% in 73, including Berlin and parts of the south and north [1]. In Leipzig rents rose 77.4%, but the net hourly wage rose 102.9% [1].

Buying is another matter. Average purchase prices more than doubled from about €1,435 to €2,989 per square metre; that now takes about 116 working hours per square metre, 11.6% more than in 2004 [1].

"Whoever only looks at rising rents overlooks that incomes have risen markedly too," said ifo Dresden's Marcel Thum [1]. A useful sentence, and a reminder to ask where you live, not what the country does.
