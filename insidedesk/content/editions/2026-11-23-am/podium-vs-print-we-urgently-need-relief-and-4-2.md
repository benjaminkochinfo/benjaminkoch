**The podium.** "We now urgently need an adjustment for cold progression," Markus Söder told Bild am Sonntag; it had to be "a joint effort of federal government and Länder", and Bavaria was ready to carry its part [1]. On ARD's Sunday programme he called it a matter of fairness, BR reported [2].

**The print.** Cold progression is the quiet tax rise that happens when a pay rise only matches inflation but pushes you into a higher rate. Adjusting the tax schedule for it has been routine since 2016 [1]. For 2027 the coalition leaders wrote a cut in electricity tax into the budget plans, but not the adjustment [1]. A full adjustment would cost about €4.2 billion in 2027, roughly €1.8 billion of it falling on the federal government and €2.2 billion on the Länder, according to calculations from coalition and finance circles reported by Merkur [1].

**The gap.** Söder's offer is real, but it covers only Bavaria's share of the Länder's part. The federal government's €1.8 billion is still a line someone in Berlin has to find, in a budget the coalition wants to pass this week [1].

That is the useful way to read any relief promise: not "is it fair?" but "whose column does it come out of?" A fair idea with no column is a speech. A modest idea with a column is a law.
