COP31 in Antalya ended on Sunday without a climate pact, after two weeks of talks and two days of overtime, UN News reported; it is the longest UN climate summit on record [1]. "I understand that there is no agreement on this text. For that reason, these decisions have not been adopted," said COP31 president Murat Kurum [1]. Reuters called it the first collapse of the talks since 2009 [3].

The dividing line was fossil fuels. The EU formally objected to the cover decision because it omitted any reference to fossil fuels or a transition away from them, calling that omission "unconscionable" [1]. Panama and the Marshall Islands also refused [1]. Speaking for the League of Arab States, Saudi Arabia defended the text's references to carbon capture and storage [1]. Ghana, for the African Group, said finance, loss and damage and the just transition had received "a single generic sentence", but would not block consensus [1]. BR reported that a decision to give the most affected states more financial support was postponed to 2028 [2].

Not everything stopped. UN climate chief Simon Stiell pointed to an agreement on a just transition and to initiatives under the Action Agenda on electrification, resilience and investment [1]. António Guterres put it plainly: "There may be no consensus in the negotiating room. There is no ambiguity in the science." [1]

The contrarian reading is not that summits are useless. It is that they were never the engine. The engine is a long list of dull documents: grid connection queues, building codes, insurance terms, municipal heat plans. Those did not need Antalya's signature, and most of them will be written this winter anyway.
