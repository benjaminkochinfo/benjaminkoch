If you have tried to get a flu jab this month and been asked to come back later, you are not imagining it. "Some of the vaccine was delivered far too late, some not in the quantity ordered," said Nicola Buhlinger-Göpfarth, chair of the German GPs' association, tagesschau reported; the problems are biggest with vaccines for people over 60 [1]. Practices without stock phone round pharmacies, and appointments are postponed [1].

The Paul Ehrlich Institute, which tests and releases every batch, confirmed that testing began later than usual this year but sees no nationwide shortage [1]. Its numbers are large: as of 2 November about 19.9 million doses had passed batch testing and been released for the German market, and it had refused no batch [2]. The reason for the delay was technical, not bureaucratic. For the 2026/27 season all three virus strains in the vaccines had to be updated, which took manufacturers longer, and some production materials arrived late [2].

So where is the gap? The GPs point to the order system. Practices must order about nine months ahead, before demand is known; order too little and you cannot vaccinate everyone, order too much and you are left with the cost [1]. The pharmacists' federation says supply is secured in principle, even if a product is sometimes out of stock [1]. The institute notes it is not part of the whole chain from manufacturer to wholesaler to pharmacy, and that short local shortages remain possible [2].

That is a familiar shape: a national figure that is fine, and a last mile that is not. The useful step for you is practical. If your practice is out, ask whether it keeps a waiting list, or whether a nearby pharmacy that vaccinates has stock. A habit, not advice.
