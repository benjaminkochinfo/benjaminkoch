The Amaha Mental Health Report 2026 is based on 13,825 answers given in India before an intervention began, between October 2025 and September 2026, according to a summary published on Sunday [1]. The headline figure is about time: 53% of respondents had lived with their symptoms for more than a year before they consulted a professional, and only 11.3% reached professional help within the first month [1].

The other figures explain why delay matters. 94.9% reported two or more symptoms at once; anxiety was named by 72.1%, stress by 67.5% and low mood by 58.3% [1]. 27.3% said they could no longer do most or all of their everyday tasks [1]. And the hopeful line is in the same report: among psychiatry patients, 56.9% showed a measurable improvement by the fourth session [1].

Read it carefully. This is a sample of people who eventually came to one provider, not a survey of a country; it says nothing about those who never came. But the shape, a long wait followed by fairly quick improvement once care starts, is the kind of finding that should change where effort goes. Not more campaigns that tell people to feel better, but fewer steps between the first bad month and the first appointment.

If there is something in that for you, it is small: the first conversation, with a family doctor, the article notes, is a reasonable first place to go [1]. The rest is paperwork, and paperwork can be done.
