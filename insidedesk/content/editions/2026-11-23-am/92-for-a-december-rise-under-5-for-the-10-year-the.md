Two facts sat side by side this morning. CME FedWatch priced a 92% probability of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate rise in December, and the minutes of the Fed's 27–28 October meeting showed nearly all policymakers expected one, Investing.com reported [1]. Yet the US 10-year Treasury yield slipped below 5%, and the two-year yield had its biggest weekly fall since September 2024 [1].

How can expected rate rises and falling yields coexist? Because a bond yield is not only a forecast of the policy rate. It is also a price that rises when frightened money arrives. On Friday, CNBC noted, investors hid in defensive shares such as Walmart, Costco, Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble [2]. In bonds the same instinct shows up as buying, which pushes yields down.

Gold tells a similar story with a lag. Spot gold rose 1.5% to $3,590.01 by 03:52 CET, after Friday's low of $3,502.49, its weakest since September 2025; it had lost about 8% in the week to 20 November [1]. A haven that fell 8% in a week is a reminder that nothing is safe in the sense of stable. It is only safe in the sense of being someone's obligation, or someone's metal, rather than someone's projection.

For your own savings, the boring conclusion holds: interest-bearing deposits and bonds pay you a known amount for waiting, and that is worth more in a week like this than any view on the Fed. Nothing here is investment advice.
