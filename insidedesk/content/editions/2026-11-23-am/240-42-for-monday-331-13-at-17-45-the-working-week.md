The day-ahead auction for Monday 23 November settled at an average of €240.42 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg, according to the quarter-hourly prices from the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD data, published via Energy-Charts [1]. That is up from €204.08 for Sunday and €187.88 for Saturday [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-23nov]]

The day has a shape. The cheapest quarter-hour is €180.96 at 03:45; the dearest is €331.13 at 17:45, when lights, kitchens and the last factory shifts overlap [1]. Desk arithmetic on the same data: the average from 08:00 to 20:00 is €266.04, and the 1–23 November average is €234.26 [1].

Oil went the other way. Brent futures fell 28 cents to $92.29 a barrel by 03:16 CET, after a 4% fall last week, Reuters reported; attacks on Gulf shipping limited the losses [2]. One analyst told Reuters that empty tankers are building up while laden ones are not, a risk to flows that "will start to price soon" [2]. That is a forecast from one person, not a fact about prices; it is worth noting because it points at the ships, not the screens.

For you, the useful lesson is the old one: a wholesale price for one day does not arrive on your bill tomorrow. Most households pay a tariff set months ahead. What does reach you, eventually, is the pattern: winter evenings priced at a premium, and a grid that pays for whoever can shift a load away from 17:45.
