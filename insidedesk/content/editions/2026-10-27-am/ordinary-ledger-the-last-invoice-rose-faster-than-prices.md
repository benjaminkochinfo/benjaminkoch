Around one million people died in Germany in 2025, 9% more than in 2015, when there were about 925,000 deaths, according to the Statistisches Bundesamt [1]. Each of those deaths ends in an invoice that families rarely compare, sign in a hurry and pay from savings or an estate. Destatis has put a few lines of that invoice in public, just before November, the month of All Saints' and cemetery visits.

**The ledger.** Prices for funeral services and cemetery fees rose 3.2% in 2025 against 2024; coffins, urns, gravestones and other burial goods rose 1.9% [1]. Consumer prices overall rose 2.2% in the same period [1]. Desk arithmetic: the service-and-fees line ran one percentage point ahead of inflation. Goods got cheaper in real terms; the hours of people and the charges of cemeteries did not. On the other side of the same ledger, towns and municipalities outside the city-states took in €951.9 million in fees and charges for cemeteries and burials in 2024, 34.1% more than in 2014 [1].

**When the estate cannot pay.** Sometimes neither the estate nor the relatives can cover the cost. Welfare offices then pay for what is called a Sozialbestattung. In 2025 they spent €51.6 million gross on about 15,400 recipients, such as relatives or named heirs who were obliged to arrange the burial; ten years earlier it was €61.7 million for almost 23,400 [1]. Desk arithmetic: about €3,350 per recipient in 2025, against about €2,640 in 2015. Fewer people needed the help, and each case cost more.

**What you can do.** If you are the one who will one day sign, two dull things help: ask for a written, itemised quote, and ask which lines are goods, such as the coffin, urn or stone, and which are services and cemetery fees, because in 2025 those two groups rose at different speeds [1]. The rules on who must arrange a burial, and where, are set by each Land's burial law [1].

The decent news sits in the same release: there are more people learning to do this work well than ever before. A sector that trains is a sector that intends to stay. Nothing here is advice.
