**The podium.** "We made very clear that we would deliver price stability, and we removed a dose of accommodation about a month ago," Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said in Bangkok, referring to September's rate rise [1]. On the next step he was firm in the other direction: "I don't prejudge meetings" [1].

**The print.** The US Treasury's daily par yield curve shows the two-year yield at 4.68% at Monday's close, against 3.47% on 2 January and a low of 3.38% on 27 February [2]. The ten-year closed at 5.00% [2]. Desk arithmetic: since 2 January the two-year has risen 1.21 percentage points and the ten-year 0.81. The gap between them has narrowed from 0.72 to 0.32 points.

[[chart:ust2y10y-27oct]]

**The gap.** The chair speaks about meetings; the two-year prices them. It is the maturity most sensitive to what a central bank does next, and it has climbed further this year than the long rate. Futures put the chance of a hike on Wednesday at under 10%, but nearly 75% expect at least one by the end of the year [3]. The Fed raised its range to 3.75%–4% in September, its first rise since 2023, with consumer prices up 3.7% on the year [3].

**The verdict.** Both are honest. A chair who refuses to pre-announce keeps his options; a bond market that prices them anyway keeps its discipline. For you the print matters more: credit-card rates track the short end, and mortgages the long one, which has sat near 5% [3]. The podium will speak again on Wednesday. The curve already has.
