Among a stack of announcements on Monday, one document deserves your attention for being dull. Google, Meta, Microsoft and Nvidia published a Grid Resilience Specification that sets expectations for how large AI facilities should respond to disturbances on the utility grid [1]. According to Nvidia, it covers staying connected to the grid, responding automatically and returning to near-full load in less than two seconds [1].

**Why it matters.** A large AI data centre is no longer just a customer of the grid; it is part of its behaviour. If many facilities drop off at once during a fault, or slam back on together, the network operator has a problem. A shared rule is how the engineers turn that into a known quantity.

**The rest of the plumbing.** Nvidia contributed rack and tray designs for its Vera Rubin generation to the Open Compute Project, including a liquid-cooled 5,000-amp busbar and support for racks of up to 250 kW [1]. It joined AMD, Google and Meta in publishing version 0.7 of an Open Data Centre Specification covering power, cooling and layout [1]. A partner, Visionbay, set out an 800-volt direct-current design meant to cut the copper and conversion equipment that lower voltages require [1].

Open standards rarely move a share price. They move the cost of every building after the first. Nothing here is investment advice.
