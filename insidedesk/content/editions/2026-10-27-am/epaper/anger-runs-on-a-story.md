A survey of 791 UK adults aged 19 to 81, published in BMC Psychology, found that more severe symptoms of intermittent explosive disorder went with more frequent violent behaviour (correlation r = .58) [1]. In the statistical model, aggression-supportive thinking, such as beliefs that justify hitting back, carried that link; difficulty regulating emotions did not [1]. It is one survey at one point in time, so it shows association, not cause, and tests no treatment [1]. Still, it points somewhere hopeful: thoughts can be examined and argued with. A temper is hard to retrain; a justification can be talked out of.
