A depression over the Bay of Bengal had intensified into cyclonic storm Arnab by Monday morning, when it lay about 650 km east-south-east of Visakhapatnam [1]. The India Meteorological Department expects it to cross the Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam by the forenoon of Wednesday 28 October, as a very severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 115–125 km/h gusting to 140 km/h [1]. Telangana, southern Odisha, Chhattisgarh and later eastern Uttar Pradesh and Bihar are expected to feel it [1]. MetDesk forecasters cite models showing up to 100 mm of rain in six hours at landfall [2].

The useful part is the paperwork. India's national crisis committee met on Monday: shelters and evacuation arrangements are in place, disaster-response teams are on standby, district control rooms are active, and fishermen have been told to stay off the water from 26 to 29 October. Those already at sea have come back [1]. The Cabinet Secretary set the goal as "zero loss of life" [1].

That goal used to sound like a slogan on India's east coast. Early warnings, cyclone shelters and boats called home in time have made it a plan. The test comes on Wednesday morning.
