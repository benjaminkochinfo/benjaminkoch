There are two lines at the top of the Bank of Korea's release on Tuesday, and the second one is the more remarkable. Real GDP, the volume of what Korea produced, rose 1.0% in the third quarter. Real gross domestic income, what that production could buy, rose 3.4% on the quarter and 18.8% on the year [1].

According to The Asia Business Daily, that annual income gain is the highest since the fourth quarter of 1977, and income has grown at double-digit annual rates for three quarters in a row [2]. Manufacturing output rose 2.0%, led by computers, electronic and optical equipment; services grew only 0.2%, down from 1.0% in the second quarter [2].

**What the gap means.** Income runs ahead of output when a country's exports buy more of the world's goods than before. In an economy whose exports are led by semiconductors [2], the obvious suspect is chip prices: the same factories, the same volume, a better price per wafer. It is good news, and it is also borrowed from a cycle: prices that rise can fall without a single machine stopping.

**The household line.** Private consumption fell 0.2%, with fewer cars and home appliances bought [2]. The national income is up sharply; the shopping trolley is not. Somewhere between the two sits the question of who receives the chip windfall: shareholders, the tax office, or wages.

The Kospi rose about 4% on the morning. Markets priced the income line. Nothing here is investment advice.
