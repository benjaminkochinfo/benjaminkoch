Brent crude settled on Monday at $115.81 a barrel, down 91 cents or 0.8%, and US WTI at $101.76, down $1.51, Reuters reports [1]. During the session Brent had fallen more than $2 on hopes of diplomacy, after Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan held talks with Iranian officials in Tehran [1]. Early on Tuesday, at 01:00 CET, Brent was a further 66 cents lower at $115.15 [2].

Why the fall stayed small: Yemen's Houthis said they had struck pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline over the weekend, with no immediate Saudi confirmation, and the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed three drones aimed at Riyadh and the south [1]. Brent climbed above $117 last week, its highest level since April, and touched $117 again on Monday, according to Reuters [2]. Friday's settlement had capped a weekly gain of 6% [1].

There was one supply note in the other direction. Russian refiners are raising diesel output in October after the government granted export quotas, two industry sources told Reuters [1]. Diesel is the fuel that moves lorries and harvests, so a quiet extra cargo matters more than a loud speech.

Monday's 0.8% fall is small beside last week's 6% gain [1]. A meeting moves the price by cents; an empty tanker lane moves it by dollars. Nothing here is investment advice.
