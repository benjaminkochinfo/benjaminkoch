Two days after Brazil's runoff, the loudest objection has lost its own party. Valdemar Costa Neto, leader of the Liberal Party, said on Monday: "We recognise the presidential election results", adding that "the issues raised need to be properly clarified" [1]. Flávio Bolsonaro himself has not formally conceded and on Sunday promised to seek annulment [1].

The numbers he would have to overturn are not close. Lula won more than 64.25 million votes against 59.83 million [1]. The two-hour extension that Bolsonaro blames produced about 35,000 extra votes, under 0.03% of the total, according to an analysis by Pedro Burgos of Insper [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is less than 1% of Lula's 4.4 million margin.

Folha de S.Paulo reported that legal advisers and allies have persuaded Bolsonaro to drop the annulment bid [1]. He said federal police had asked for his passports, that he would comply, and that he plans "to organise the opposition" [1].

From Washington, Donald Trump told reporters it "looked like another rigged election", offered no evidence, and said he "did not take sides" [1]. Vice-President Geraldo Alckmin called the remarks "totally wrong" and "unfounded" [2].

An opposition that organises instead of annulling is not a defeat for anyone. It is the job description.
