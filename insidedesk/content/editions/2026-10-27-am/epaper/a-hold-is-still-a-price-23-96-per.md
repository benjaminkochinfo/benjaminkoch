The Federal Open Market Committee ends its meeting on Wednesday, 28 October, and is expected to leave its range at 3.75%–4%, CNBC reports [1]. A hold makes few headlines. It does not make borrowing cheap.

**The household ledger.** The average rate on a new US credit-card offer is 23.96%, according to LendingTree data cited by CNBC [1]. The average 30-year fixed mortgage is close to 7.4%, according to Mortgage News Daily, because mortgages follow the ten-year Treasury yield, which has been near 5% [1]. Car buyers are taking larger loans over longer terms, Edmunds says [1].

**The long end hurts more.** "Higher longer-term rates are doing more damage to consumer wallets, as they've risen more than short-term rates," said Mark Zandi, chief economist at Moody's [1]. The Fed sets the overnight rate. The bond market sets what a house costs.

**The quiet upside.** Savers still earn relatively attractive yields on high-yield accounts while rates stay up [1]. The same rate that bites the borrower pays the depositor. "Inflation is still the No. 1 problem in the U.S. economy," said Heather Long of Navy Federal Credit Union [1].

The meeting ends days before the midterm elections [1]. Committees that keep to their calendar whatever the electoral one says are doing their job. Nothing here is investment advice.
