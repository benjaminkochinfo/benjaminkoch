"The American side is not serious about negotiations," Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baqaei, said at his weekly news conference on Monday, as cited by IRNA [1]. He accused Washington of confusing diplomacy with imposing demands [1].

On Europe he was blunter about the paperwork. Tehran, he said, had received no new European proposal for negotiations beyond a political statement, and he criticised Britain, France and Germany for following Washington on the restoration of UN sanctions [1]. He repeated that Iran has never sought nuclear weapons [1]. He also confirmed that a Qatari delegation had recently visited Tehran, describing such visits as normal during regional conflicts [1].

Then the calendar moved on. President Masoud Pezeshkian arrived in Russia for the gas exporters' summit, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said he will meet Vladimir Putin this week [2].

Desk observation: "not serious" is the language of a party that still wants a table, not one that has left it. The same spokesman confirmed the Qatari visit in the same briefing [1]. The useful document, when it comes, will be a dull one: who lifts what, who lets which ship through, and who checks. Until then, read the schedules of mediators rather than the adjectives of spokesmen.
