Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv late on Monday. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said one person was killed and at least 10 injured, "most of whom were students from a university dormitory" [2]; The Guardian's tally, from earlier figures, is one dead and eight wounded [1]. The university was the State University of Trade and Economics, which has more than 15,000 students [2]. Klitschko said a blast wave shattered windows in an intensive care unit, and on Tuesday that a strike hit the Southern Bridge over the Dnipro, damaging metro infrastructure; trains across it were suspended [1].

"The missiles hit very crowded areas, and there is not a single military facility nearby," President Volodymyr Zelensky wrote [1].

**The truce, on paper.** Hours later, at 03:00 local time (02:00 CET), a truce was due to start in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied city in Kherson region where residents have reported months of food, water and medicine shortages [1]. It is meant to let humanitarian aid in. No end date has been given, and neither Russia, Ukraine nor the US has said who will monitor it [1]. Russia's defence ministry said on Friday it would halt fire in the "Kherson sector" for its duration [1].

**The paperwork that matters.** Before the strikes, Zelensky said Ukraine had waited "nearly a month" for US defence secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on missiles for Patriot and NASAMS air-defence systems [1]. RFE/RL reports a bipartisan group of US lawmakers pressing the White House on Ukraine's access to military and intelligence support [2].

You can read the night two ways: as proof that truces mean nothing, or as a small local agreement arriving despite everything. The honest reading is narrower. A truce is a set of checkable facts: trucks in, people out, guns quiet, someone writing it down. Until those facts are reported, it is a date on a calendar.
