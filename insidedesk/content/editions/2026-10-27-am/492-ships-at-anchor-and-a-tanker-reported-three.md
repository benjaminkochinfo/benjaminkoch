The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre, UKMTO, said it had received a "verified, time-late report" of a tanker struck by an unknown projectile while sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz on 23 October at 08:39 UTC [1]. The ship was disabled; the crew are safe [1]. The report surfaced three days after the event. On Monday UKMTO also passed on third-party reports of a vessel on fire about 63 nautical miles southeast of Al-Faw, Iraq, in the northern Gulf, classed as suspicious activity rather than a confirmed attack [1][3].

**The queue.** The Joint Maritime Information Center estimated on 25 October that about 492 vessels were waiting outside the strait: about 194 near Fujairah and Khor Fakkan, 178 off Dubai, 55 near Musandam and Sohar and 32 off Bandar Abbas and Qeshm [2]. It said the backlog was expected to persist [2]. The centre keeps its threat level for Hormuz at "severe", with further deliberate attacks "highly likely", and counts 156 confirmed maritime security incidents in the region since 1 March [1].

**The traffic.** JMIC recorded 101 US-facilitated transits between 22 and 24 October, against a 2025 average of about 138 vessels a day; the two figures are measured differently [1]. Desk arithmetic: about 34 assisted transits a day, roughly a quarter of the old daily average. Outside the US-assisted system, tracking showed single-digit daily transits [1].

**The machinery.** Navigation interference is part of the picture: Windward counted 293 vessels affected by jamming in the Gulf and the Gulf of Oman on 21 October, and UKMTO's weekly overview logged 18,049 anomalous position reports [2]. JMIC also warns that ships sitting still at anchor with their transponders on face higher targeting risk [2].

None of this cancels Monday's talks in Tehran; it tells you what they are up against. Insurers, charterers and port agents will believe a ceasefire when the anchorage count falls, and that is a fair test. A queue of 492 ships is also a queue of crews, cargoes and invoices waiting to move the day the lane is safe.
