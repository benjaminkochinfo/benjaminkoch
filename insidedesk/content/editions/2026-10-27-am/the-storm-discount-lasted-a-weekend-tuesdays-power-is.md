The German-Luxembourg day-ahead auction cleared at an average of €155.11 per megawatt hour for delivery on Tuesday, according to SMARD, the Bundesnetzagentur's data portal [1]. Monday's average was €116.57 and storm Sunday's €13.12 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Tuesday's power costs about 33% more than Monday's and almost twelve times Sunday's.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-27oct]]

**Why it climbed.** The wind dropped. Onshore turbines generated 688.8 gigawatt hours on Monday, against 1,009.2 GWh on Saturday and 1,020.0 GWh on Sunday [1]. Gas-fired plants generated 105.4 GWh on Monday, against 43.5 GWh on Sunday [1]. Desk arithmetic: onshore wind fell by about a third, and gas burn more than doubled. When gas sets the price, the price remembers what gas costs.

**The shape of the day.** Inside Tuesday the spread is wide. The cheapest hour, 12:00 to 13:00 CET, cleared at €43.50, when the sun helps; the dearest, 18:00 to 19:00 CET, at €262.19, after sunset [1]. Desk arithmetic: the evening hour costs about six times the midday one. Customers on a dynamic tariff will feel that directly; everyone else meets it later, averaged into the next tariff letter.

**The month.** From 1 to 27 October the daily price has averaged €154.43 [1], almost exactly Tuesday's level. The windy weekend was a dip, not a new floor. And the Deutscher Wetterdienst expects a strong blocking high over central Europe from Thursday, with stubborn fog and low cloud [2]. Calm, grey air is the weather in which batteries, interconnectors and flexible demand earn their keep.

None of that is a crisis. It is the system doing what it was built to do: price each hour honestly, so that someone has a reason to store, shift or build. Nothing here is investment advice.
