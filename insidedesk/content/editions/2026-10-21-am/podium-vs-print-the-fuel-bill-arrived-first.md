**The podium.** In August, with jet fuel near $4 a gallon, United's chief executive Scott Kirby told reporters: "I think we'll recover 100% in fourth quarter," though he stopped short of a firm commitment [1]. In July the company had said it expected to recover 80% to 90% of higher fuel costs in the third quarter and all of them in the fourth [1].

**The print.** Tuesday's results tell a different story about the same quarter. United now assumes jet fuel will cost $4.80 a gallon on average in the final three months of the year, against $3.90 in the third quarter and $2.43 a year earlier [1][2]. Third-quarter fuel expense rose 61.6% to $4.84 billion, $1.8 billion more than a year before [1]. The company guides to adjusted fourth-quarter earnings of 50 cents to $1.50 a share [1]. With $5.72 earned in the first nine months, even the top of that range gives $7.22 for the year, against July's guidance of $9 to $11 [1]. The shares fell 1.5% after hours [3].

**The gap.** Revenue is doing what the podium promised: it rose 17.4% to $17.9 billion, and United expects fourth-quarter revenue growth above 20% [1]. Fares are rising. The trouble is that fuel rose faster. A recovery measured in revenue is not a recovery measured in profit.

**Verdict.** The podium was not dishonest; it described a plan made at $4 fuel. The filing describes the world at $4.80. When a company talks about passing on costs, check the fuel line in its next report. It shows what actually happened.
