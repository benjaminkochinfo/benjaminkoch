The Copernicus Climate Change Service's hydrology summary for September is a useful backdrop to this week's flood warnings in southern France. Below-average river flows were widespread across much of Europe, and record-low September flows in the EFAS dataset, which runs from 1992, were measured along sections of the Ebro, Garonne-Dordogne, Rhine, Vistula and Danube [1].

The consequences were practical. Low water on the Rhine kept disrupting commercial and passenger shipping in Germany [1]. The Vistula at Warsaw fell to its lowest level in more than 70 years of measurements [1]. Inflows to the Dniester reservoir were around 11% of normal, raising supply worries in Moldova and Ukraine [1]. In England, temporary water-use restrictions covered about 30 million people [1]. EU wildfires of at least 30 hectares had burnt around 689,000 hectares by the end of September [1].

Copernicus also notes the limits of its own data: soil moisture figures are "of qualitative rather than quantitative value" [1]. That kind of caveat is worth reading too.

For a barge operator, a water utility or a farmer, a summer like this is a cost before it is a statistic. One week of heavy rain does not fill a dry river system.
