In a letter to North Kivu's governor dated Thursday 15 October, the joint health unions of the Beni–Butembo–Lubero region gave the provincial authorities 14 days to respond to their demands, or face a strike [1].

The demands are practical. The unions want urgent supplies of personal protective equipment in health facilities in the Ebola-affected zones, and a risk bonus for every health worker exposed to the disease, not only those in specialised treatment centres [1]. "Other agents working in health facilities are also confronted with this risk, and some have already been contaminated," said Mongerwa Nzoli, the unions' deputy co-ordinator in the region [1]. They also ask for better pay and conditions for nurses, hygienists, technicians and laboratory staff in transit and treatment centres [1].

By Sunday 18 October the governor had not responded, according to Radio Okapi [1].

An epidemic response runs on gloves, gowns and payrolls as much as on vaccines. A strike notice with a date and a list is a negotiation, and a list like this one can be answered with invoices.
