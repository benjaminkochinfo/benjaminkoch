The Houthis have surrounded Taiz again after capturing a major road linking the city to southern Yemen [1]. On Tuesday the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, Volker Türk, said: "The Houthis' closure of the only supply route linking Taiz to Aden is placing civilians at grave risk, blocking humanitarian aid and cutting off essential supplies" [1]. He asked that civilians who want to leave be allowed to do so freely and safely [1].

The numbers behind the warning: fighting in Taiz governorate killed at least 32 civilians between 1 August and 19 October, including 14 children [1]. The World Food Programme says shifting front lines and road closures have cut off 800,000 people from life-saving aid, most of them in Taiz, and more than 18 million Yemenis face severe food insecurity [1]. Prices of basic goods in the city have multiplied [1].

Yemen's internationally recognised government has asked the UN to intervene, saying the siege threatens more than 4 million people [1]. Its forces say they have stopped Houthi advances at several points [1].

A siege is fought over roads and warehouses, so the first test of any pressure is simple: whether lorries get through.
