In its third-quarter shareholder letter, Netflix lowered its 2026 advertising revenue forecast to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion, pointing to ad features reaching the market more slowly than expected [1]. The shares fell about 4% after hours, and the fall narrowed as investors read the full results [1].

The rest of the letter was steadier. Third-quarter revenue rose 12% to $12.87 billion, and net income was $3.66 billion, or 87 cents a share [1]. Netflix expects 2026 revenue of $51.1 billion, 0.2% below the midpoint of its earlier range, and says subscription revenue has done better than expected [1]. The advertising tier will reach 15 more markets during 2027, starting in March [1].

The letter also addresses a weak point directly: "We know there's some investor concern about our view hours growth" [1]. Last week the company cut about 6% of its workforce, around 1,000 jobs [1].

The lesson is a familiar one in software: a feature on a roadmap is not revenue. Revenue starts when the product ships and customers use it.
