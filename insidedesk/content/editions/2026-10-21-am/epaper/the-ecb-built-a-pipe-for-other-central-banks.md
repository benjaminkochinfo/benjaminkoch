Most of the attention on Lagarde's statement to the IMF's steering committee will go to rates. The rest of it describes plumbing, which tends to last longer.

The first item is EUREP, the Eurosystem's repo facility for central banks outside the euro area. The Governing Council set its operational features in July, and "more than 30 central banks have expressed interest" in the past six months [1]. A repo line lends euros against collateral. It is a dull tool and an important one, because it means that in a crisis a foreign central bank can get euros without selling in a hurry.

The second is Pontes, the Eurosystem's system for settling tokenised assets in central-bank money, which went live on 21 September [1]. The third is the digital euro: the ECB plans a 12-month internal pilot in the second half of 2027, and says adopting the regulation by the end of 2026 is "key" to that timetable [1].

None of this will move a market this morning. But the international role of a currency is built on pipes like these, not on speeches about it.
