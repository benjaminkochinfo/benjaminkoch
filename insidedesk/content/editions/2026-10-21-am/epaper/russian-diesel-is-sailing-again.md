Russian diesel cargoes are returning to their traditional destinations, Reuters reported, citing shipping data from Kpler and LSEG [1]. At least two tankers have signalled voyages to Turkey and Brazil in recent days [1]. This follows the US suspension of sanctions and the lifting of a Russian export ban that Washington secured [1].

On the US side, energy under-secretary Kyle Haustveit said at the Drake Forum in Titusville, Pennsylvania, that the government is working with domestic refiners to put more crude through the US refining system [1]. Energy secretary Chris Wright said in Houston that the US is also pushing shut refineries to reopen [1].

This is the less visible half of the energy shock. Crude prices get the headlines, but this year the shortage has been in refined products: diesel and jet fuel. More diesel at sea and more refinery runs at home is the supply side doing its slow work. The test is not the announcement but the price of gasoil over the next weeks.
