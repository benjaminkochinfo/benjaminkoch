The European People's Party, the Socialists and Renew Europe, the three groups that form the pro-European majority in the European Parliament, reached an agreement on the automotive package, Renew's leader Valérie Hayer announced in Strasbourg [1].

According to first reports, manufacturers would have to cut tailpipe CO2 emissions of new cars by 87.5% from 2021 levels by 2035, instead of 100% [1]. The remaining 12.5 points would be covered by a 7.5% allowance for green steel made in Europe and a 5% unconditional margin [1]. Combustion-engine and hybrid cars would remain allowed after 2035 [2].

Hayer was candid about the status: "I'm not entirely sure how official it is, but an agreement was reached last night" [1]. That is the right way to describe it. A deal between group leaders still has to pass the groups, the committees and the plenary, and then be negotiated with the member states.

Supporters such as Forza Italia MEP Salvatore De Meo call it the end of "ideological bans" [1]. Whatever you think of that, the number that matters is 87.5, and it will be in the text.
