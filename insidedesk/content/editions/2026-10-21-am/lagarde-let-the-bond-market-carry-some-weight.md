The statement was delivered in Bangkok on 17 October and posted by the Bank for International Settlements on 20 October [1]. It is a formal text, which makes it more useful than an interview.

**The numbers it rests on.** Euro-area headline inflation rose to 3.8% in September, mostly on energy; core inflation rose slightly to 2.5% [1]. Wages "have so far shown no material response to the energy shock" [1]. The ECB raised rates by 25 basis points in June and again in September [1].

**The sentence that matters.** "The energy shock is too large to 'look through' but a measured response remains appropriate" [1]. The reason given is specific: financing conditions have tightened, with long-term rates "rising notably" since the last meeting, which "will slow growth and reduce pass-through by more than projected" in September [1]. In plain words: the bond market has already done part of the ECB's work.

**How the reaction function is written.** Lagarde lists three criteria: the inflation outlook, underlying inflation, and the strength of transmission [1]. You can check each one against published data. That is a better way to run a central bank than a speech about resolve.

**Across the Atlantic.** The US 10-year yield fell to 5.11% on Tuesday and futures priced about an 83% chance that the Fed holds next week, with about 73.7% for a December rate rise [2]. Two central banks, the same energy shock, and both are letting the long end of the curve do part of the work.
