On Tuesday the federal government held an investment summit in Berlin for about 200 chief executives from Germany and abroad [1]. The chancellor, Friedrich Merz, praised German punctuality and early rising [1], and on the same day warned against Berlin plans to socialise housing [2]. Finance minister Lars Klingbeil was blunter with the guests: "I want your money" [1].

**The number.** Economy minister Katherina Reiche spoke of investment commitments of more than €220 billion for this year [1]. It was the figure most likely to make the evening news.

**The footnote.** According to Der Spiegel, a spokesman for Reiche's ministry later clarified that the sum had no direct connection to the summit. It is the general total of investment projects since the start of the year [1]. Which investments will result from the summit itself cannot yet be told [1].

**Why this is good news, quietly.** Governments like large round numbers, and summits are built to produce them. What matters is that the ministry corrected the record the same day, on the record, when asked. A total since January is still a real figure; it is just a different one. The institution did its job: it said what the number was.

For you, the practical reading is simple. When a summit announces a total, ask what period it covers and whether a list exists. If there is no list, it is a mood. If there is one, it is a plan.
