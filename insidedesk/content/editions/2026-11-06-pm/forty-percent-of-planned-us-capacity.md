About 40% of planned US data-centre capacity is in states where governors or governors-elect have pledged or already imposed a pause on new projects, Cleanview told the market this week via Euronews [1]. That share sits beside a second number from Thursday: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.2%, its worst session since 2020, and was about 16% lower on the week through Thursday's close [1].

**What Friday added.** European tech struggled to bounce; Siemens Energy gained about 2.3% and ASMI about 2.7% in choppy trade, while the broader Stoxx still fell [2]. In the DAX, Infineon rose 2.4% and Aixtron 4.6% in the MDAX after both had lost more than 8% on Thursday [3]. Bloomberg reported, via Euronews, that Dutch bank ING is shopping a roughly $500 million (€447 million) portfolio of data-centre loans in one block [1] — a credit-market echo of the equity story.

**The politics underneath.** Local midterm ballots went against data centres in 40 of 41 measures counted [see Psych]. A Democrat-led Congress raises the odds of federal scrutiny, including the Sanders–Ocasio-Cortez moratorium proposal [1]. Wealth Club's Susannah Streeter told Reuters the AI build-out has been "quite a chunk of US growth" via construction, manufacturing and wealth effects, and that hit portfolios would drag consumer sentiment; she expects volatility at least until January clarity on Democratic AI policy [2].

For you, the useful distinction is capacity planned versus capacity energised. A pause on new permits slows the former; the servers already ordered still need power, chips and credit. Nothing here is investment advice.
