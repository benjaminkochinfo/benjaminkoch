**The podium.** When the Federal Open Market Committee held rates at 3.75%–4.00% on 28 October, the vote was 10–2, with both dissenters preferring a 25-basis-point rise [1]. Chair Kevin Warsh stressed the inflation side of the dual mandate and described the stretch to the next meeting as "information rich" — in other words, December is live [1]. AIB's weekly brief put the market's December hike probability near 75% even before Friday's data [1]. Across the Atlantic the Bank of England had already moved, lifting Bank Rate to 4% on Thursday and warning that the rise should not be read as the start of an inevitable sequence [3].

**The print.** October nonfarm payrolls rose 165,000 against a consensus near 59,000; unemployment slipped to 4.1%; average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month [2]. CME FedWatch odds of a December quarter-point hike rose to 76.8% after the release [2]. Treasury yields moved higher across the curve as the print landed [2]. Desk arithmetic: 76.8% is roughly three-to-one on a hike, not a coin toss.

**The gap.** "Information rich" left room for a soft print to reopen the pause. A 165,000 print, with upward revisions of 84,000 to the prior two months, fills that room with evidence the labour market is still absorbing workers [2]. Long-term unemployment rising to its highest since late 2021 is the counter-weight inside the same table [2]. The market's strip simply weights the upside surprise more heavily for December.

**For you.** If you have a dollar loan, a mortgage reset, or a savings rate tied to Fed funds, you are buying the strip's number, not the chair's adjective. The next scheduled decision is 9 December [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
