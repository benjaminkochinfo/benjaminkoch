A fresh wave of bomb threats disrupted at least 18 flights to or from Bulgaria, Czechia, France, Italy, Hungary, Cyprus, Romania, the UK and Portugal on Friday; the planes landed safely and searches found no explosive devices [1]. Danish police also investigated a threat against Copenhagen airport, which said it was operating normally [1]. The alerts followed Tuesday and Thursday waves that forced at least 35 flights to be diverted or searched [1].

**The arithmetic of disruption.** Eurocontrol scheduled 26,709 flights in Europe on Friday; the affected services were 0.0674% of that total, the Guardian reported [1]. Hungary scrambled fighter jets three times to escort civilian flights, defence minister Romulusz Ruszin-Szendi said [1]. Knock-on delays mattered to passengers; they did not close the network.

**Who and why, still open.** Investigators have said there is no evidence so far that the hoaxes are orchestrated by a hostile terror group or state actor such as Russia or Iran, though inquiries continue [1]. Some Thursday threats were signed "fighters of the Male State", a Russian far-right movement [1]. Spain's foreign minister, José Manuel Albares, told TVE the pattern looked like "hybrid aggression" and "psychological warfare" [1]. Separately, Irish media newsrooms were evacuated after threats, and Sky in Britain reported a security threat to authorities [1].

**For you.** If you fly this weekend, build slack for a possible diversion or secondary search; the probability for any one flight remains low [1]. Treat unsolicited threat messages as a matter for the airline and police, not for social media. Procedure — land, search, resume — is working. That is the constructive fact inside a frightening week.
