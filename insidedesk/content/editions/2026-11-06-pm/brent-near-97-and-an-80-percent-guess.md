Brent crude futures were about $96.93 a barrel by early US Friday, down 1.38% on the day after touching almost $100 earlier in the session; WTI was about $89.17 [1]. Since Monday's peak above $110, Brent is down roughly 16%; WTI has fallen about 12% from levels above $100 [1]. Helaba's note, carried by Reuters, put North Sea Brent around $98.20 and WTI at $90.61 in the European morning [2]. The direction is clear even when the last tick moves.

**Two stories in one price.** Rapidan Energy Group's Bob McNally told CNBC he sees an 80% probability that military fighting between the US and Iran resumes, and that both benchmarks could climb back above $100 [1]. Against that, Iran has reportedly sent a nuclear proposal to Washington through Qatar, per a senior Iranian official cited by Reuters [1]. Iran also reported three further attacks on oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, and UK Maritime Trade Operations data pointed to attacks continuing at a near-record rate [1]. Hopes for a midterm-driven diplomatic breakthrough and fear of renewed escalation are being marked in the same session.

**A quieter German print.** Destatis said seasonally and calendar-adjusted industrial production rose 0.7% in September from August, and 0.4% year on year; August's monthly rise was revised down to 1.0% from 2.0% [3]. Auto production jumped 17.1% month on month while machinery fell 6.5% and construction 2.6% [3]. It is a rebound, not a boom, and it arrives on a day when oil is cheaper than Monday and dearer than summer.

For you, the household link is the pump and the heating bill, not the probability. A $13 weekly drop in Brent does not become €13 off a tank overnight; it does change what your supplier can buy next week. Nothing here is advice.
