The number arrived at 14:30 Berlin time, and it was not the sparse print the morning had prepared for. US nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October, against a Dow Jones consensus of about 59,000; the unemployment rate edged down to 4.1% from 4.2% [1]. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% over the year [1]. August was revised up by 53,000 to 186,000 and September by 31,000 to 60,000 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the two revisions alone add 84,000 jobs that were not in last month's picture.

[[chart:us-payrolls-06pm]]

**What Europe did with Thursday's fear.** Before that print, and after it, Frankfurt had already priced a different story. The DAX closed at 25,706, down 2.3%, a day after its record high of 26,692.90 [2]. On the week it was still only about 0.3% lower [2]. The Stoxx Europe 600 was down about 1.7% near 643.72 by the close, wiping out the week's earlier gains [4]. Chip and AI names had led the Asian overnight fall — Japan's Nikkei about 6% lower at the close, South Korea's Kospi down about 6% after an 8.8% plunge that tripped a halt — and Europe opened with the same argument [3].

[[chart:euro-indexes-friday-06pm]]

**The argument, plainly.** Democrats' midterm sweep revived traders' fear that a Congress they do not control will slow data-centre construction and tighten AI rules [3][4]. About 40% of planned US data-centre capacity sits in states where governors or governors-elect have pledged or already imposed a pause, according to Cleanview as reported by Euronews [3]. That is a planning risk, not yet a statute. It was enough, on Thursday, for the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index to fall 12.2% — its worst day since 2020 — and for the Nasdaq to close down about 5.5% into a correction [3]. S&P 500 companies lost a record $2.9 trillion (€2.6 trillion) of market value in that session [3].

**What the jobs print does to the committees.** Traders lifted the probability of a quarter-point Federal Reserve hike on 8–9 December to about 76.8% on the CME FedWatch tool [1]. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, healthcare 42,000, manufacturing 19,000 — broader than the healthcare-heavy pattern of 2025 [1]. Seema Shah of Principal Asset Management called it "a strong rebuttal to fears that the economy is heading into a meaningful slowdown" [1]. Long-term unemployment still rose, to 1.96 million people out of work for more than 27 weeks, the most since December 2021 [1]. Both facts can sit in the same report.

**The constructive reading for you.** A one-day liquidation prices a political guess; a payrolls table prices who got hired. Europe's smaller tech weight cushioned the Stoxx relative to Asia and Wall Street [4], which is why a 2.3% DAX day can still leave the week almost flat [2]. US futures in the European morning pointed to a pause in the sell-off, with Nasdaq-100 futures up about 1.5% at one point [3]. A pause is not a verdict. For you, the useful narrow habit is unchanged: read the BLS table and the closing index, not the loudest post about either. Nothing here is investment advice.
