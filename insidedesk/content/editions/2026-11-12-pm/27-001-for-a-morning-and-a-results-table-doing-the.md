Germany's DAX crossed 27,000 points for the first time on Thursday morning, touching a record of about 27,001 before slipping just below it by midday [1]. The pan-European Stoxx 600 was up 0.4% at 665.79 by mid-morning, a fresh record after Wednesday's record close [2]. These are intraday figures; the settled closes come after 17:30 CET.

**The reason was paperwork.** Not a summit, not a speech. Stoxx 600 companies are on track for quarterly profit growth of 25%, the strongest in four years, according to LSEG data reported by Reuters, with energy companies a large part of it [3]. Siemens rose 4.6% after raising its medium-term targets and beating expectations in its fourth quarter [2]; its order intake passed €100 billion in a record financial year [1]. Allianz, Munich Re and Merck KGaA all rose after results or raised outlooks [2][4].

[[chart:europe-results-movers-12pm]]

**Not everyone was rewarded.** Telefónica fell 2.3% after cutting its full-year revenue forecast, and BAE Systems slipped 1.3% even after raising its free cash flow projection for the second time this year [2]. The table cuts both ways, which is what makes it worth reading. In Europe, technology shares were down about 1.2% on average in the morning, as Nvidia's leadership questions weighed on the sector [4].

**A quieter kind of rotation.** "Many investors have come back to the DAX in search of solid companies," said Thomas Altmann of QC Partners (our translation), as volatility rises in AI stocks; he noted the DAX has matched the Nasdaq 100's performance with far smaller swings [1]. Reuters reported that the Stoxx 600 has risen 4.7% so far this quarter, while the S&P 500 is down 0.4% [3].

**For you.** A record is a number that one bad afternoon can take away. A raised profit guidance is a promise a company will have to show in its next report. If you hold a European index fund, it is the second kind that does the slow work. Nothing here is investment advice.
