The meeting lasted about two and a half hours, BBC Russian reports [3]. In the part the cameras saw, Vladimir Putin asked Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to pass his thanks to Donald Trump "for arranging our contacts with the Ukrainian side", and said a positive result had been reached in at least one area, the exchange of detainees, with "another important step" now being planned [1]. On the Russian side sat aides Yury Ushakov and Vladimir Medinsky and the investment envoy Kirill Dmitriev [1].

Two concrete offers came out of the room, via Russian agencies. Putin proposed a joint Russian–Ukrainian humanitarian mission to Kostiantynivka in eastern Ukraine, modelled on the Oleshky arrangement late last month, and said Russia needed five days to clear the area near the town of mines, Interfax reported [2]. The harder line came with it: Moscow would treat any foreign troops deployed in Ukraine as a "legitimate target", which would mean "the continuation of war" [2]. Foreign troops are a key part of Europe's planned guarantees for Kyiv [2].

On Europe, the tone was polite and the substance unmoved. Putin said he "would like Americans to work with Europe so that they can decide what they want, peace or war", Reuters reported via Interfax [2]. Kushner told him Europe wanted a role, and Witkoff said he hoped to announce later on Thursday a new round of trilateral talks "in which Europeans would take part" [2]. In Dublin, European Council president António Costa answered in advance: "The time of zones of influence in Europe is long gone" [2].

Read it the dull way and the day still produced something. A prisoner exchange is being planned; a mine-clearing period has a number; a humanitarian corridor has a name. None of that ends the war, and the Guardian's analysis calls Putin's rhetoric maximalist [2]. But five days, one town and one list of names are things a negotiator can check, which is more than any speech offers.

For you, the test this week is simple: does a date for the next round appear, and does it have Europeans on the seating plan? After Moscow the envoys fly to Dublin to brief Zelenskyy [4].
