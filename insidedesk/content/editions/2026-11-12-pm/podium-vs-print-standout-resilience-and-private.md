**The podium.** Britain's economy grew 0.7% between July and September, the fastest quarter since early 2024, and Chancellor John Healey said the country was showing "standout resilience" [2]. He is not wrong on the headline. The Office for National Statistics put GDP 1.9% higher than a year earlier, with growth of 0.2% in September alone [1].

**The print.** The same bulletin has a less cheerful section. Construction output fell 0.7% in the quarter and is 1.7% lower than a year ago [1]. New work fell 1.6%, and the largest drag inside it came from private housing new work, down 4.7% [1]. August's growth was revised down from 0.1% to zero [1]. The engine was elsewhere: information and communication grew 2.1%, led by computer programming and consultancy, which the ONS says has been the largest driver of growth across 2026 [1].

**The gap.** "Resilience" is a word about the whole. The table is a word about each part. An economy can grow fast on data centres and back-office software while building fewer new homes, and both can be true on the same morning [1][2]. A podium tends to quote the total; a household tends to live in one of the rows.

**For you.** If you rent or are saving for a first home, the 4.7% matters more than the 0.7%: less private housebuilding is a supply line, not a mood. And a strong headline cuts both ways. Suren Thiru, chief economist at the accountancy body ICAEW, said the figures keep a Bank of England rate rise "firmly on the table" for December [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
