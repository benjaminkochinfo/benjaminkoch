Last week the Bank of England raised interest rates by a quarter point, to 4%, to cool inflation [1]. On Thursday the statisticians said the economy had grown 0.7% in the third quarter [2]. Put those two facts side by side and you have the argument economists were making on Thursday morning.

**The case for another rise.** "These robust figures help keep a December interest rate rise firmly on the table," said Suren Thiru, chief economist at the accountancy body ICAEW [1]. The National Institute of Economic and Social Research went further: "Further rate rises are needed to keep inflation contained," said its economist Fergus Jimenez-England [1].

**The case for patience.** Simon Pittaway of the Resolution Foundation said Britain's resilience "will be severely tested" in the coming months by rising inflation and interest rates [1]. Households, Thiru noted, appear to have drawn on savings to keep spending [1]. Real household consumption rose 0.6% in the quarter [2].

**What drives it.** Inflation has risen this year mostly because of motor fuel, with petrol above £1.70 a litre and diesel near £2, and the ONS said wholesale electricity prices were about double a year ago [1].

**For you.** If your fixed-rate mortgage ends in the next few months, a strong growth number is not good news for your next rate; it is a reason the Bank may not be finished. That is not a forecast of ours. It is what the people quoted above said in public on Thursday, and the Bank will answer in December. Nothing here is investment advice.
