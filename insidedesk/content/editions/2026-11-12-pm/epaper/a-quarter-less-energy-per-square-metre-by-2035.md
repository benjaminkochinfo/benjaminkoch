The climate talks in Antalya spent Thursday on cities and the built environment, and they produced something you can actually measure. At the second ministerial meeting of the Intergovernmental Council for Buildings and Climate, the Antalya Agreement on Energy Efficiency in Buildings was presented to member countries for signature [1]. Deputy ministers and other representatives from 53 member countries and 14 observer countries attended [1].

The core of it is one number: the COP31 presidency's goal of cutting the energy used per unit of building, its "energy use intensity", by 25% by 2035, part of what the presidency calls its Global Implementation Targets [1].

Türkiye's deputy environment minister, Ömer Bulut, said buildings account for about one-third of global energy consumption and greenhouse-gas emissions [1].

A signature on a conference day is not a renovated school. The work it describes happens later and elsewhere: in building codes, energy certificates, heat-pump installers' order books and the slow procurement of public landlords. But a target with a number and a deadline can be checked, and checking is how pledges become invoices. If your own flat has an energy certificate, it is the same idea in miniature.
