The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security is finalising an expansion of its chip export controls to seven more countries, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Norway and South Africa, according to The Information, as reported by Chosun Biz [1]. The rules could be announced as early as this month [1].

Under the plan, those countries would need Commerce approval to import Nvidia's Blackwell-class and more advanced AI chips; recipients would have to report who gets the chips and how they are used, and face ongoing oversight [1]. Data centres run by US companies there could be exempt from licensing if they block remote access from US arms-embargoed countries such as China and Russia and report customers' countries and the scale of computing [1]. Similar licence requirements already apply in some countries, including Saudi Arabia and Vietnam [1].

The target is a workaround: Chinese companies training models on banned chips by renting capacity in third-country data centres [1]. The report lands as Congress scrutinises the department over a halted probe into Megaspeed, a Singapore firm suspected of routing advanced chips to Chinese users, and as lawmakers try to fold three export-control bills into the defence authorisation act [1]. None of this is final until it is published.
