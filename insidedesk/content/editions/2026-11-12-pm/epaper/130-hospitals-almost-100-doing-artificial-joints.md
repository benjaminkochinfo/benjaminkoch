Some hospitals will run short of money in the coming years, Lower Saxony's health minister Andreas Philippi (SPD) told dpa on Thursday, and he wants a federal rescue fund to keep needed rural clinics going until the hospital reform starts to bite in 2028/2029 [1]. He named no sum [1]. The state's hospital federation says the loss of an invoice surcharge from November costs its clinics about €400 million a year, against a promised €55 million of relief from 2027 [1].

Philippi also made the reform's case: of 130 hospitals in the state, almost 100 fit artificial joints, which he calls overcapacity driven by finance rather than need [1]. Lower Saxony will assign the reform's service groups only by the end of September 2027, taking longer to avoid the lawsuits other states attracted [1]. Slower paperwork, fewer court cases: a trade worth making.
