China's commerce ministry said on Thursday it hoped the United States would work with it so that their $60 billion tariff-reduction arrangement comes into effect "as soon as possible", creating more favourable conditions for agricultural trade, Reuters reports [1]. Spokesperson He Yadong said the two sides keep close communication on implementation [1]. Last week the ministry said Beijing was open to buying more US farm goods, but only if the agreed "$30 billion-for-$30 billion" reciprocal arrangement is implemented [1]. That is the sound of a deal moving from the podium to the customs schedule, which is where trade agreements either become real or quietly don't. For farmers on both sides, the date the tariff lines change matters more than any summit photograph. Nothing here is investment advice.
