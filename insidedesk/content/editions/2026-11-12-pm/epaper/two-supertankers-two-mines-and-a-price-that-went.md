Two oil supertankers were disabled by large explosions after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday night, Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Thursday; it said the ships had ignored warnings against using an "unauthorised route" [1]. Other tankers in the waterway are still burning, and oil is washing ashore [1].

**And yet the screen went the other way.** By 10:46 CET on Thursday, Brent crude was down 37 cents, or 0.4%, at $92.39 a barrel, with US crude down 0.43% at $86.16; both had fallen more than $1 in early Asian trade and were heading for a weekly fall [1]. The reason given was supply, not peace: OPEC+ crude production rose in October, led by higher Saudi output despite the disruption in the strait [1]. Growing US inventories added to the weight [2].

**Two stories at once.** "Market players are vacillating between tentative truces in Ukraine and the Middle East and the ongoing atrocities against energy infrastructure and ships," said Tamas Varga of the broker PVM [1]. That is a fair description of a market reading two newspapers at the same time.

**What to watch.** The US Energy Information Administration's weekly oil and product stocks were due on Thursday, a day late because of a national holiday [1]. That table, not a statement from a navy, is what tells you whether the barrels are really piling up. Nothing here is investment advice.
