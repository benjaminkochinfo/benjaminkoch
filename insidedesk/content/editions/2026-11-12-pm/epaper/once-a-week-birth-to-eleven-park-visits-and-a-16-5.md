Children taken to the park at least once a week from birth to the age of 11 scored, on average, 16.5% higher in mental-health assessments than those who went less than once a week, according to a study in the journal Epidemiology and Community Health, reported by Euronews [1]. The study followed 4,219 participants and focused on social-emotional development: managing feelings, building relationships, behaving appropriately, coping with everyday problems [1]. The association held after adjusting for socioeconomic status and family problems [1].

The detail worth keeping is that it worked in both directions the authors could test: children did better during periods when they went more often, and children who went more often overall did better than those who went less [1]. Both "internalising" problems, such as emotional symptoms, and "externalising" ones, such as hyperactivity, were lower [1]. It remains an observational study; it cannot rule out everything else that comes with a family's park habit.

There is a quieter finding about fairness. Children in more advantaged areas visited more often when young; those from less advantaged families visited more as they grew older [1]. Lead researcher Mark Ferguson of the University of Exeter says parks need to be "safe, accessible and welcoming to families from all backgrounds" to help [1].

For you: the park is one of the few things in child-rearing that is free, public and maintained by somebody else's budget. Use it, and be glad of the council that mows it.
