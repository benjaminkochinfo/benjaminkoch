Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Thursday that Starshield restrictions are back on "in the temporary occupied parts of Ukraine", and that they "directly affect our ability to intercept Russian jet powered drones" launched from those areas [1]. "We are trying to get the details from the American side, but so far we know very little," he said, adding that if someone in America treats Ukrainian territory "as if it is not Ukrainian, then this tells us where things are going" [1].

The European Commission answered within hours. Its tech spokesperson Thomas Regnier said it was "not the first time" Ukraine had faced Starshield disruptions: "These reports are deeply concerning and if confirmed, unacceptable." SpaceX, he said, "must have a consistent approach" to sharing its services with Ukraine, and the service "must be restored urgently" [1]. Zelenskyy also asked for more air-defence support, including interceptors and fighter jets [1].

The lesson is older than satellites. A front line that depends on a single supplier's terms of service has a single point of failure, and the remedy is not outrage but procurement: contracts with clauses, redundancy, alternatives that someone has already paid for. Note the careful wording from Brussels, "if confirmed": the facts are still being gathered, and the Guardian reports few details so far [1]. For you, the thing to watch is not the next statement but whether the link comes back, and whether Europe's own budget lines for secure communications grow to match the complaint.
