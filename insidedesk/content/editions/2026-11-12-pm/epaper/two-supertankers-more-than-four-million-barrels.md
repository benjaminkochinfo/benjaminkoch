Two oil supertankers were disabled by "massive" explosions in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday night after ignoring warnings against using an "unauthorised route", Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Thursday, Reuters reports [2]. Iranian state media later said the tankers "exploded after hitting naval mines" [2]. According to the IRGC Navy statement carried by ISNA, the two vessels were transiting the southern part of the strait and carried a combined cargo of "more than 4 million barrels of oil" [1]. The statement did not name the ships [1].

The warning attached to it is the part shipowners will read twice. The IRGC said any ship in the Gulf or the strait that switches off its navigation systems and cooperates with US forces to use the southern route "would face the same fate" [1]. Reuters notes a shift in the pattern: early in the war Tehran announced direct strikes on tankers; more recently it reports ships catching fire after hitting mines on the "unauthorised route", while in several cases the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency has said ships were struck by unknown projectiles [2].

The verified facts are fewer than the claims: two ships, one route, one statement of responsibility, and no names yet. Until the owners, flags and insurers speak, treat the number as Tehran's and the damage as unconfirmed. Nothing here is investment advice.
