Fresenius has placed 13.4 million shares of Fresenius Medical Care, the dialysis group, at €39.00 each, raising about €524 million, according to a company notice on Thursday morning [1]. Its share of FMC's capital falls from 25% to 20% [1]. Desk arithmetic: 13.4 million times €39.00 is €522.6 million, consistent with the "about €524 million" reported, the small gap being rounding in the share count.

**The market shrugged in the right direction.** A placement normally weighs on the stock being sold, because new supply arrives at a discount. On Thursday morning FMC shares were up 0.7%, and Fresenius itself rose 2.2% [1].

**A second deal on the same morning.** Shares in Patrizia, the property investor, jumped 21% to €7.63 after its largest shareholder, First Capital Partner, said it would offer €8.20 a share in cash to raise its holding from 58.89% to as much as 73% [1]. The offer has no minimum acceptance level, and Patrizia plans to move to the Deutsche Börse's Scale segment for smaller companies [1].

**Why it matters.** Neither of these is glamorous. They are the plumbing of a capital market: a parent company turning a holding into cash, a shareholder paying to own more. When they happen without drama, at prices close to the screen, the plumbing is working. Nothing here is investment advice.
