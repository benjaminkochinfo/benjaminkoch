The UK Health Security Agency issued its first yellow cold-health alert of the winter for England's North West and West Midlands, running from 10:00 CET on Thursday to 13:00 CET on Saturday [1]. The Met Office expects overnight lows of down to minus 6°C for some in the coming days, "a fairly harsh frost", and a few centimetres of snow on the highest ground in northern Scotland [1]. Its spokesman Stephen Dixon called that "not massively unusual for this time of year" [1]. Up to 30mm of rain could follow widely on Sunday [1].

The alert system exists for the health service as much as for you: cold raises demand on hospitals and the risk to people over 65 and those with existing conditions, the agency says, and its advice is plain: heat homes, dress warmly, and check on vulnerable neighbours [1].
