A tracker that checks default suppliers' websites every week, updated today, lists six that have announced price changes for 1 January 2027, covering eleven electricity and gas tariffs; every one is an increase [1]. Its annual costs assume 3,500 kWh of electricity and 12,000 kWh of gas [1].

The spread is wide. EnBW's default electricity rises by 3.4%, or €48.64 a year; EWE's by 14.1%, or €178 [1]. On gas, Harz Energie rises by 7.4% (€114), EWE by 15.2% (€243.63), and Stadtwerke Hamm by 23.4%, or €351.35 a year, with its unit price going from 11.29 to 13.97 cents per kWh [1]. LEW's electricity in Landsberg am Lech rises by 13.1% [1]. Some households will not wait until January: the tracker says Stadtwerke Magdeburg raises default gas, and NEW on the Lower Rhine both electricity and gas, from 1 December [1].

The tracker is careful about what silence means. No change found so far "does not mean that prices stay the same": announcements for the turn of the year remain possible until 20 November, the six-week deadline in the default-supply rules [1]. Customers may cancel without notice to the day of a change [1].

Desk arithmetic: Hamm's gas rise of 2.68 cents per kWh on 12,000 kWh is €321.60 a year before the higher standing charge, which goes from €145.66 to €175.41.

For you: a percentage on someone else's tariff tells you little. Your own letter, your own meter reading, and a comparison before the deadline tell you a lot.
