England's health technology assessor, NICE, on Thursday recommended retifanlimab (brand name Zynyz, made by Incyte) together with platinum-based chemotherapy for adults with anal canal cancer that has spread and cannot be removed by surgery [1]. It is the first time NICE has recommended a drug for this form of the disease, and the treatment is available on the NHS from today [1]. About 250 people in England could benefit, NICE estimates [1].

**The numbers, plainly.** In trials, people given retifanlimab with carboplatin and paclitaxel had on average 9.3 months before the cancer worsened, against a median of 7.4 months with placebo and chemotherapy [1]. Average survival was 32.8 months, almost three years, against 22.2 months on chemotherapy alone, with some patients responding for much longer [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 10.6 months more survival, and about 1.9 months more before the disease progresses, on the trial figures as NICE reports them. Not a cure; more time.

**Why it matters beyond the 250.** Around 1,600 people a year were diagnosed with anal cancer in the UK between 2017 and 2019, two-thirds of them women, and HPV infection is linked to about 90% of UK cases [1]. Once it has spread, only around 20% live five years, against around 90% when it has not [1]. That gap is the argument for two dull things at once: earlier diagnosis of a cancer that patient experts told NICE is too little known, and prevention of the infection behind most cases.

**The machinery.** NICE published its guidance at the same time as the medicines regulator, the MHRA, granted the licence, and says it now publishes guidance for medicines 30% faster than before April 2024 [1]. The company offers the NHS a confidential discount, and the committee applied a severity modifier of 1.2, which allows a higher cost-effectiveness threshold for severe conditions [1]. "Very few new treatments have been invented" for this cancer, said Professor Peter Johnson of NHS England [1]. Today, one more was paid for.
