China's statistics bureau said on Monday that GDP grew 4.5% on a year in the third quarter and 1.2% on the previous quarter, after 5.0% and 4.3% in the first two quarters [1]. Over the first nine months the economy grew 4.6%, to 107,107.1 billion yuan; services grew 5.2%, industry 3.8% and agriculture 3.7% [1].

The beat was small, 4.5% against a 4.4% Reuters poll, and the mix tells the story [2]. Industrial output rose 5.5% in September and exports carried much of the year, while retail sales rose only 1.1%, fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first nine months and urban unemployment stood at 5.2% [2]. The bureau itself says domestic demand is "still insufficient" [3].

Beijing has added support: targeted rate cuts and mortgage subsidies in late September, and 550 billion yuan of unused debt quota for local governments in early October [2]. The desk's reading is plain. An economy that grows by selling to others depends on how others feel about buying, and with trade tensions with the European Union escalating [2], that is a dependency, not a cushion.
