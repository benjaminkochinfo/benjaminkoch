A drone entered Lithuania on Sunday morning, preliminarily from Poland's airspace, and came down in the north; debris dented the door of a moving car between Biržai and Pasvalys, and no one was hurt [1]. The head of the National Crisis Management Centre said it was most likely a Ukrainian drone, which could have strayed during Kyiv's strikes on the St Petersburg region [1]. Ukraine's foreign ministry apologised, saying Russian electronic warfare may have diverted it [2].

The part worth your attention is the alert. Fighter jets were scrambled but could not see the object in poor weather, and an air alert went out only in northern Lithuania [1]. Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas called the situation "strange and unacceptable" and ordered a review with the fire and rescue department of why alerts were not sent in time: "If the object had already been detected in one part of Lithuania, everyone should have been informed in time" [1]. He also asked Poland to join the investigation [1].

That is how an alliance's eastern flank gets safer: not by speeches about drones, but by a minister asking why a text message did not go out, and a report that answers him.
