Iraq has lost around $60 billion in oil export revenue during the war, its foreign minister, Fuad Hussein, said in Rome [1]. Before the conflict Iraq produced about 4 million barrels a day and exported about 3.5 million, mostly by tanker through Hormuz; lacking enough storage, it halted most output and fell back on trucks through Syria and a pipeline through Turkey [1]. Exports recovered to 2.6 million barrels a day between 1 and 21 September, the oil ministry says, while foreign reserves fell by $20 billion [1]. Tanks are not glamorous. Not having them costs $60 billion.
