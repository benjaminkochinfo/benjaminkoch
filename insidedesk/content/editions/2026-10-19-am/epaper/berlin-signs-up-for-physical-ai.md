The federal government plans a partnership with Nvidia to bring artificial intelligence into German industry, and government sources confirmed the deal to ZDFheute after the Handelsblatt reported it [1]. It is expected to be announced at the "Invest in Germany" summit in Berlin, which opens this evening with Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang [1].

According to the Handelsblatt, as cited by ZDF, the federal government is ready to put in a three-digit million-euro sum, possibly from funds reserved for its high-tech agenda; the money would partly rent computing capacity on a Deutsche Telekom AI cloud, Nvidia would send engineers and chips, and about a dozen German firms, including Siemens, Audi and Black Forest Labs, would take part [1]. The focus is "physical AI": robots, autonomous vehicles and automated factories [1].

Asked about digital sovereignty, Huang said what matters is not who builds the hardware but who controls the data and applications, which "should be provided here in Germany" (our translation) [1].
