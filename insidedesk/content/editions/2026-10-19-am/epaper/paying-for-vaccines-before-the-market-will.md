The UK government is putting £33.2 million into eight vaccine projects against Ebola, plague, Marburg virus, Lassa fever, Nipah virus, chikungunya and Mayaro virus, through the UK Vaccine Network [1]. The awards were announced by the health department, the UK Health Security Agency and Innovate UK [1].

The premise is stated without decoration. Outbreaks of these diseases come and go, often in poor countries, so there is little commercial incentive for industry to develop vaccines against them [1]. The money covers candidate vaccines, clinical trials and faster design and manufacture, plus the capacity to test and deploy quickly in the countries at greatest risk [1]. UKHSA's Richard Pebody cited Congo's outbreak as a reminder that emerging diseases "are not a distant threat" [1].

Set that next to this edition's lead. The Bundibugyo virus behind Congo's outbreak has no approved vaccine or treatment [2]. That gap is not a scientific mystery. It is a market failure, and £33.2 million is what a government looks like when it pays for one in advance. Small sums, early, are cheaper than large sums, late.
