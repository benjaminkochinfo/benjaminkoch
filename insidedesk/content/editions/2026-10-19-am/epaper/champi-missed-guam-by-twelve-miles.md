Super Typhoon Champi passed about 12 miles (20 km) south of Guam's southern tip on Saturday as a Category 4 storm with 150 mph winds; its northern eyewall only grazed the island, which escaped catastrophic wind damage [1]. Six hours later, the Joint Typhoon Warning Center reanalysed it as a Category 5, with sustained winds of 165 mph (265 km/h) [1]. Sea temperatures along its path are an unusually warm 29–30°C [1].

Yale Climate Connections counts at least eight Category 5 storms worldwide in 2026, perhaps nine if one is confirmed in review, against a 1990–2025 average of 5.3 a year [1]. Champi is the second major typhoon within 50 miles of Guam this year, after Bavi in July [1].

The constructive note is in the island's record. From 1948 to 2025, 27 typhoons of category 1 or above passed within 50 miles of Guam [1]. An island that has done this often builds, drills and warns accordingly. Twelve miles was luck. The fact that luck was enough is engineering.
