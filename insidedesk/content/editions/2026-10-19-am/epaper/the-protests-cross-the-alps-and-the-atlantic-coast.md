Saturday was the first day of cost-of-living protests in Italy, Portugal and Belgium [2]. In Milan a march that began peacefully ended with masked rioters throwing stones and paving blocks at police and burning barricades made from bar tables; at least two officers were injured and about 50 demonstrators, many of them minors, were detained, LaPresse reported [1]. Giorgia Meloni said demonstrating was a right but "setting our cities ablaze is not" [1].

In Lisbon, hundreds set up a tent camp in Rossio Square after marches that drew thousands across Portugal; the main union, CGTP, called for better public services, pensions and wages and action on housing [1]. In Albania, where protests have run for months, police fired rubber bullets for the first time on Saturday, and a court, banks and a post office were set on fire or badly damaged [1].

The grievances differ: schools in France, rents in Iberia, corruption and tuition fees in Albania [1][2]. The common thread is the household budget. That is the good news hidden in a bad weekend: budgets can be answered with budget lines, and some already have been.
