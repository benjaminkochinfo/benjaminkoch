Asia took Friday's American selling into Monday. Early in the session Japan's Nikkei 225 was down 2.15% and the broader Topix 1.17%; South Korea's Kospi had lost 1.60% [1]. On Friday the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had slumped 3.8% [1]. US stock futures leaned the other way, with S&P 500 contracts up 0.2% and Nasdaq 100 contracts up 0.5% [1].

Oil helped. Brent fell $1.94, or 1.77%, to $107.92 a barrel by 02:27 GMT, after settling on Friday at its highest since 19 May; WTI was at $100.58 [2]. The push came from Washington's waiver of new Russia sanctions and tariffs, which may mean more Russian fuel on the market [2]. This edition's Annex reads the order itself.

China's third-quarter numbers beat the poll: growth of 4.5% on a year, against a 4.4% Reuters consensus and 4.3% in the second quarter [3]. The composition matters more than the beat. Growth leaned on factories and exports, while fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% in the first nine months and September retail sales rose just 1.1% [3].

The week's real calendar is corporate. More than 70 S&P 500 companies report, Tesla and Meta among them [1]. Netflix is due on Tuesday; Meta, Tesla, Lam Research and Texas Instruments on Wednesday [4]. After a week in which chip shares were priced on doubts about whether AI spending will earn its keep [1], this is the first fresh evidence: revenue, capital spending and cash flow, printed by the companies themselves.

That is the constructive way to read a red Monday. Moods are cheap and fast. Invoices are slow and audited, and this week a great many of them arrive at once.
