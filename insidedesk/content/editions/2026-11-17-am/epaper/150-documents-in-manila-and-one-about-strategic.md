Southeast Asia's leaders approved or noted at least 150 documents at their 49th summit in Manila, which closed on Sunday, the Philippine News Agency reports [1]. Among them: declarations on critical minerals, the cross-border movement of digital talent, and "Strategic Trade Management for Secure Regional Trade" [1]. On Monday, Washington's new chip rule named four ASEAN members, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, among five countries needing a licence for advanced chips [2]. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. told the leaders' retreat that livelihoods are tied to energy security and called for regional "energy connectivity" and more reliable, affordable systems [1]. Declarations are cheap; implementing ones are not. The strategic-trade text is the one now being tested.
