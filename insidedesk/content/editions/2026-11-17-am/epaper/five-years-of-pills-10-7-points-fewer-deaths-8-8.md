Researchers followed 8,365 postmenopausal women with hormone-sensitive breast cancer, diagnosed between 2005 and 2013, for ten years [1]. Only 42.7% stayed on tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor for five years or more [1].

Compared with women who started but never took it consistently, those who stayed the course had a 10.7% lower absolute risk of death from any cause over ten years, and 5.3% lower from breast cancer [1]. They also had higher absolute risks of high blood pressure (8.8%), raised blood fats (5.1%) and diabetes (2.6%) [1]. Aromatase inhibitors were linked to all three; tamoxifen only to blood pressure [1].

The authors, in JNCI Cancer Spectrum, call for cardiometabolic care to be built into survivorship care [1]. Read it as a checklist for the follow-up appointment, not a reason to skip the pill.
