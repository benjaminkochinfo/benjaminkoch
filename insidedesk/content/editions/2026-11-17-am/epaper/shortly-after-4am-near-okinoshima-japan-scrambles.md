Japan says two suspected Russian aircraft violated its airspace on Tuesday, close to Okinoshima in the south-western area of Fukuoka, shortly after 4:00 local time, which is 19:00 GMT on Monday, or 20:00 CET [1]. The Self-Defence Forces scrambled fighter jets to warn them off, according to the government's top spokesman, Minoru Kihara [1].

The language was unusually blunt for Tokyo. "The violation of our national airspace by Russian aircraft is not only a grave infringement of our country's sovereignty, but also a threat to our security and is completely unacceptable," Kihara said [1]. Japan "lodged a very stern protest with Russia through diplomatic channels and strongly demanded measures to prevent any recurrence" [1].

Why it is worth a column on a morning dominated by Kyiv's bridges: the war in Europe has a Pacific edge. On Thursday the leaders of the US, China and Russia meet in Shenzhen, and President Trump says the war in Ukraine will be on his list [1].

What you should notice is the machinery. A radar track, a scramble order, a time stamp to the minute, a spokesman's statement, a note through diplomatic channels. That is how a state turns a few minutes of intrusion into a record that can be cited later, in a protest, a defence budget or a negotiation. The aircraft leave; the file stays.
