Copper has joined the AI sell-off. On Monday, London Metal Exchange futures fell as much as 1.4% to their lowest since 8 July, a third daily fall, extending a run of five straight weekly losses [1]. At 11:55 in Shanghai on Monday, 04:55 CET, copper stood at $13,240 a tonne, down 1.2%; aluminium was down 0.7% at $2,989.50 and tin down 1.7% at $45,915 [1]. By Tuesday morning Reuters reported copper at its lowest in more than four months, and Australian-listed Capstone Copper down nearly 6% at an eight-month low [2].

The reasons are listed plainly. Copper is widely used in power infrastructure and data centres, so the upheaval at Nvidia weighs on it; expectations of further Fed rate rises add pressure; and China's October data missed across the board, with investment unexpectedly falling [1].

Now the part that does not fit the mood. Falling exchange stockpiles have fuelled steep backwardation in copper and aluminium in recent months, the report notes, "a sign that near-term supplies are tight" [1]. Tin, mostly used as solder in electronics, is in a squeeze on the LME as inventories dwindle [1]. Backwardation means metal for delivery now costs more than metal for later: buyers who need it physically are still paying up.

The futures price is the opinion; the warehouse is the inventory. When they disagree, it is worth knowing which one you are reading. Nothing here is investment advice.
