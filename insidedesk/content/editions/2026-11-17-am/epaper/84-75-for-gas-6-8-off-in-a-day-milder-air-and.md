The Dutch TTF front-month contract, Europe's reference price for gas, settled at €84.75 per megawatt hour on Monday, down €6.20 or 6.8% from Friday's €90.95 [1]. Early on Tuesday it was quoted at €84.21 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Monday's fall took back about three quarters of the €8.45 gained between Monday 9 and Friday 13 November [1].

**Two dull reasons.** OilPrice.com, reporting the fall at 10:00 in Amsterdam on Monday, points to forecasts of milder temperatures across most of Europe this week and to record pipeline flows into American LNG export plants: 21.8 billion cubic feet on Saturday, 12% more than a year earlier, according to BloombergNEF data it cites [2]. Weather and liquefaction trains, in other words, not diplomacy.

**What has not changed.** The same report notes that EU storage sites are only 70% full, against 89% in November 2025, that LNG flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain far below pre-war levels, and that QatarEnergy has extended its force majeure on deliveries until at least the end of December [2]. A cheaper Monday does not refill a cavern.

**For you.** Wholesale gas reaches household tariffs slowly, through suppliers' purchasing books; a single day's fall changes no bill this winter. What it does change is the mood in which suppliers write next year's prices. Nothing here is investment advice.
