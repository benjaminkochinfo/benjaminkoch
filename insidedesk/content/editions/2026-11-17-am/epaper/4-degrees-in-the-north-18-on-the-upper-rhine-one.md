Germany wakes up on Tuesday in two seasons. The national weather service DWD expects only 4 to 6 degrees in the north, 15 in the south-west and locally up to 18 degrees on the Upper Rhine, in its forecast issued at 04:55 CET [1]. Before sunrise, parts of the country between the coasts and the centre could be frosty at minus 1 degree under gaps in the cloud [1]. Desk arithmetic: up to 14 degrees between the cool north and the mildest corner of the south-west on the same afternoon.

**Rain from the south-west.** DWD expects rain to move in from the south-west, reaching roughly the Elbe by midday and Western Pomerania by evening [1]. In the south-western half, a strengthening south-westerly wind brings stiff to stormy gusts, with severe gales in places on high ground [1]. Overnight into Wednesday, rain may be heavy and thundery at times in the west and south-west [1].

**Not a one-day event.** The forecast keeps the pattern going: stormy gusts in the southern half again on Wednesday and Thursday, severe gales on the higher mountains, and on Saturday night a snow line in the Alps falling to around 1,000 metres [1].

**For you.** Days like this are why the weather service's warnings page exists, and why it is worth a look before a drive across the Mittelgebirge or a walk in the woods. A mild afternoon in Freiburg tells you nothing about the frost on a windscreen in Hamburg.
