While the screens on Monday were full of AI valuations, onvista's November round-up of German instant-access savings offers made a duller and more useful point. The headline rates are high: up to 4.5% a year at one broker, though only on uninvested cash in a securities account, for 90 days, and only if you make at least one share or ETF trade a month [1]. Several banks pay 4.0% to 4.1% for three or four months [1].

Then read the second line. Openbank pays 4.0% for four months, then a variable rate, currently 2% [1]. Crédit Agricole pays 4.1% for four months on up to €25,000, then currently 2.5% [1]. Bigbank pays 3.9% for four months, then currently 2.25% [1]. "Viele Angebote gelten nur für Neukunden und für wenige Monate", onvista notes: many offers are for new customers only and for a few months [1].

Desk arithmetic: €10,000 at 4% for four months earns about €133; the following eight months at 2% earn another €133 or so. That is about €267 for the year, an effective 2.7%, not 4%. Taxes and compounding aside.

None of this is a reason to ignore the offers. It is a reason to diary the end date and compare the follow-on rate, the deposit-insurance country and the cap before you move [1]. The interest you are promised is a number; the interest you are paid is a schedule. Nothing here is investment advice.
