Minutes of the Reserve Bank of Australia's 2–3 November meeting, published on Tuesday, show six of its nine board members judged this year's tightening not "clearly sufficient" against inflation risks [1]. The fifth rise this year took the cash rate to 4.85%, an 18-year high [1]. The three dissenters feared the economy might be near "a materially sharper downturn" as unemployment, at a five-year high of 4.7%, climbed and house prices fell [1]. The housing market is on track for its biggest downturn in four decades, Reuters reports, and markets now price about 22 basis points of easing by the end of next year [1].
