Researchers in Jerusalem and Tel Aviv built a 43-item Online Behavior Questionnaire and tested it on two samples totalling 617 adolescents [1]. Teenagers who saw their parents as more involved in their online lives reported more sympathy and more supportive responses when they witnessed cyberbullying [1]. Parental involvement was only weakly related, or unrelated, to time online or exposure to harmful content [1].

It is self-report at one point in time, so it cannot show cause, and the authors call the tool a research instrument for now [1]. The useful question for you at dinner may be less "how long?" and more "what did you see, and what did you do?"
