Canada's Senate national security committee heard the tech industry on Monday evening on Bill C-22, which would require companies to be able to hand over information to investigators with a warrant, and let the public safety minister issue confidential orders to build in surveillance capabilities, reviewed first by the intelligence commissioner [1]. Amendments already cut metadata retention to six months from up to a year and limited when firms must decrypt [1]. Not enough, said Meta's Robyn Greene: a law "not technically feasible to comply with" while offering end-to-end encryption; Meta would "consider" pulling services [1]. Signal's Udbhav Tiwari said that if forced to choose, "we will not compromise the security of Canadians" [1]. The privacy commissioner wants more changes too, notably to a lower threshold, "reasonable grounds to suspect", for subscriber data [1]. Security is also the lock nobody else can open.
