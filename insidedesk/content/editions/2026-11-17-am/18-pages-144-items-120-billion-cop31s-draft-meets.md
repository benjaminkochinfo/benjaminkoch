The UN climate conference in Antalya has a text to fight over. Late on Monday, Australia, which co-hosts COP31 and leads the negotiations, circulated a first draft of the overarching decision [2]. DW reports it runs to 18 pages and 144 items, calls for an "immediate transition away from fossil fuels" and for a "tripling" of adaptation finance for developing countries to at least $120 billion [1]. Ministers arrive for the formal high-level segment on Tuesday [2].

**What is in the draft.** Beyond fossil fuels and adaptation money, the text includes Türkiye's goal of raising electrification of the world's economies to 35% by 2035, and expresses "deep concern" that the 1.5C threshold is now out of reach, calling for action to limit "both the magnitude and duration" of any overshoot [1]. It also backs scaling up carbon capture and storage, which some activists dismiss as a "false solution" [1].

**What is not settled.** UN News reports that countries are divided not only over the draft's contents but over whether such a decision should exist at all [2]. Finance remains the hard core: who pays, in grants or loans, and how much [1]. The Solomon Islands, a small island state, was the first country to announce a contribution to the Green Climate Fund at COP31, while pledges from wealthier countries stayed scarce, UN News reports [2]. Even the science has a timetable dispute: vulnerable nations want the next major IPCC assessment ready by 2028, in time for the second Global Stocktake, and countries remain divided over it [2].

**The voices.** Chris Bowen, Australia's climate minister and chief negotiator, called the tenor of the talks "very constructive" [1]. E3G's Cosima Cassel said the test is "whether the final decision keeps the core elements" once trade-offs begin [1]. Soenke Kreft of the United Nations University described "a somewhat slow first half" and added: "We probably will also have overtime" [2].

**How to read the week.** A text of 144 items has a lot of places for a word like "immediate" to be softened. The draft is not the deal; it is the opening inventory. The reliable test is dull and printable: does the $120 billion figure appear in Friday's text, or only in Monday's? Campaigners are already asking for it to be "grant-based money" [1]. Watch the numbers, not the adjectives.
