"Preliminarily, hits to the North and South bridges in the capital," Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko posted on Telegram just before midnight local time, 23:00 CET, shortly after the Ukrainian air force warned of incoming missiles [3]. He later reported "damage to critical infrastructure systems" and said "some areas are experiencing disruptions to water and heating supplies" [1]. Some of the capital's above-ground metro services were damaged, officials said, after residents were told to go to shelters [1]. Reuters witnesses reported further explosions in Kyiv on Tuesday morning [3].

**Why bridges matter in Kyiv.** Eight major bridges cross the Dnipro, joining the city's western and eastern halves, and an estimated three million residents rely on them to move around the city [1]. Several were already targeted by Russian drones in October [1]. Elsewhere, local officials said three people were injured in a missile attack in the Cherkasy region [1].

**The other bridge.** Crimea's Moscow-installed governor Sergei Aksyonov said on Tuesday that Ukrainian forces had struck the Kerch bridge, the link between the annexed peninsula and Russia, for the second day running, and that traffic had been suspended [2]. On Monday a Ukrainian drone crashed into one of its arches [2]. How badly it is damaged is still unclear; the bridge is protected by several layers of air and sea defences [1]. Kyiv calls it a legitimate military target [2].

**Talks in the same 24 hours.** None of this happened in a diplomatic vacuum. On Monday Ukrainian, US and European officials held online talks; Volodymyr Zelensky said Europe was represented for the first time by the full delegation that is to negotiate with Moscow, and called the meeting "difficult" [1][2]. The Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Russia had not yet agreed to European participation in the next round [2]. Zelensky also said Russia is receiving "dozens of missiles per month" from North Korea, citing Ukraine's military intelligence [2].

**For you.** The useful reading of a night like this is not the strike count but the repair time. Watch how fast Kyiv's utilities restore heat and water, because that, not the communiqués, tells you how a city is holding up in November.
