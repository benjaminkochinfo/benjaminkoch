Tuesday is the last delivery day SMARD has priced: €211.58 per megawatt hour on average for Germany and Luxembourg [1]. Wednesday's auction clears later today, so the chart ends here. Desk arithmetic: Tuesday sits €139.90 below the €351.48 of Tuesday 10 November [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-17nov]]

**The evidence arrives.** Yesterday evening this desk declined to explain why Tuesday's dearest hour is 08:00 to 09:00, at €263.56, because the generation forecasts were not yet published [1]. They are now. The transmission operators expect 570.5 gigawatt hours from onshore wind and 98.9 from offshore on Tuesday [2]. Desk arithmetic: 669.4 GWh of wind, about 6.75 times the 99.2 GWh forecast for Tuesday 10 November, the day of the autumn's record price [2].

**But the wind is late.** The hourly forecast is where the story sits. Onshore wind is expected at 9,684 megawatts between 04:00 and 05:00, still only 16,515 MW in the 08:00 hour, and then a steady climb to 35,312 MW between 20:00 and 21:00 [2]. Solar adds little in a November week [2]. So the breakfast peak meets a grid still short of wind, and the cheapest hour of the day, 23:00 to midnight at €142.62, comes when onshore wind is forecast at 33,370 MW [1][2]. Desk arithmetic: the price falls by €120.94 between the dearest and the cheapest hour, while forecast onshore wind roughly doubles between them.

**Average versus timing.** Desk arithmetic: wind and solar together make up about 48% of the 1,453 GWh total generation forecast for Tuesday [2]. That is a respectable day. It is also a reminder that a daily average hides the order of events: the same wind blowing at 08:00 instead of 20:00 would have produced a different price list.

**For you.** If you are on a dynamic tariff, the printed advice stands, and now it has a reason: heavy loads late in the evening, not over breakfast. On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches this month's bill. Nothing here is investment advice.
