The car insurance letters are arriving, and most carry a higher number. The reinsurer E+S Rück, part of Hannover Re, expects the average third-party liability premium of €316 to rise by 4% in 2027, partial cover (Teilkasko) by 5% and fully comprehensive cover (Vollkasko), now averaging €460, by 7% [2]. Desk arithmetic: about €12.64 more a year on an average liability policy, about €32.20 on an average Vollkasko one.

**Smaller than last year.** A year ago, according to E+S Rück, liability premiums rose by 10% and Vollkasko by up to 16.4% [2]. Repair costs still outrun general inflation, the reinsurer said, but the rise is slowing [2]. The market is also uneven: some insurers are already comfortably back in profit and have little to catch up, others need bigger rises, Hannover Re's motor manager Stefan Schmuttermair said, according to a report published on Monday [1]. The report's conclusion for drivers: compare prices [1].

**Who is thinking of moving.** In an ADAC survey cited in Monday's report, 35% of respondents said they were considering changing their motor insurer and 4% had already decided to [1]. A third would switch for a saving of up to €100, while 65% said service and benefits mattered more to them than price [1].

**The date that matters.** Most policies end on 31 December, and the consumer advice centre in Lower Saxony reminds drivers that the cancellation must have *arrived* by 30 November [3]. Its order of operations is the sober one: sign the new contract first, then cancel the old, so you are never uninsured [3]. It also lists cheaper levers that need no switch at all: a smaller circle of named drivers, an honest annual mileage, an excess of €150 on Teilkasko and €300 on Vollkasko (which it says can save more than 20%), and paying once a year rather than monthly [3].

**For you.** Before you jump, ask your current insurer which no-claims class it will report to the new one; special discounts and rebate protection usually do not travel [3]. Desk arithmetic: from today, 13 days remain until 30 November. Enough for one comparison, one phone call to your current insurer, and one letter.
