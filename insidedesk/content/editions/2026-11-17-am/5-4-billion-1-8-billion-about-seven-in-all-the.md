"We will agree tomorrow," Friedrich Merz said in Munich on Monday, standing next to Markus Söder [2]. Tomorrow is today. The leaders of CDU, CSU and SPD meet in the Chancellery in the early evening, and the chancellor has named three goals: an electricity-tax cut for everyone from 1 January, a timetable for the pension reform and progress on the electoral law [1].

**The tax is the easy part to describe.** Merz has ruled out the cheaper exits, a cut for a few months or by a single cent, and committed to the European minimum for 2027 [1]. That costs €5.4 billion [1]; the government puts the funding need at around five billion euros a year [3]. Until now, only energy-intensive industry, businesses and farming pay the lower rate [3]. The SPD says yes if it is "seriously" funded; Lars Klingbeil's ministry warns against "financial experiments" [1].

**The second bill.** Söder also wants the "cold progression" offset, the hidden tax rise when inflation pushes wages into higher brackets [2]. A full offset would cost federal, state and local government about €4.2 billion next year; even if the states and municipalities carry their share, Berlin needs about €1.8 billion [1]. dpa's sum: the committee is looking for roughly seven billion euros for next year, "about the size" at which the traffic-light coalition broke two years ago [1].

**Where the money might be.** The Federal Court of Audit sees €17 billion of potential in cutting subsidies, though every subsidy has a lobby [1]. The Union wants to use surpluses on the EEG renewables account, which the SPD considers constitutionally risky; the SPD in turn floats higher corporation tax or a higher flat tax on capital income [1]. Further down the agenda: the SPD calls economy minister Katherina Reiche's fuel-price cap, meant to replace the fuel rebate in early 2027, ineffective, because it only triggers if German price rises beat the EU average by more than five percentage points for three weeks in a row, which dpa says has never happened in recent years [1].

**What to read tomorrow.** Not the smiles at the door, but three documents: the funding line for the tax, a date for the pension cabinet decision, and whether the electoral-law talks produce a text rather than "very good progress" [1]. For you: if the cut comes, it shows up on your power bill only after a tariff change; check yours in January, not tonight.
