Germany's standing vaccine committee, STIKO, recommends a chikungunya jab for people aged 12 and over who travel to an area with a current outbreak, the Robert Koch-Institut's guidance, updated on 17 November, says [1]. It also covers longer stays of more than four weeks, or repeated short trips, in endemic areas when there is a higher risk of severe or chronic illness, for example from the age of 60 [1]. Laboratory staff who work with the virus are included [1].

The updated part is about which vaccine. STIKO recommends both vaccines licensed in Germany from the age of 12: the inactivated Vimkunya and the live Ixchiq, but the live vaccine only up to the age of 59 [1]. The RKI notes that serious adverse events after Ixchiq were reported mainly in older people, and that a case of aseptic meningitis occurred in a healthy younger adult [1]. In August 2026, after an EMA safety review, the live vaccine's indication was narrowed to people at high risk of infection [1].

Both are single doses; whether a booster will be needed is not yet clear, though first data suggest protection probably lasts several years [1].

For you, the practical step is unglamorous: if a trip to an outbreak area is on the calendar, see a travel clinic early and ask which of the two you should get. The guidance is the result of committees reading adverse-event reports line by line. That is how vaccine confidence is earned, not by slogans.
