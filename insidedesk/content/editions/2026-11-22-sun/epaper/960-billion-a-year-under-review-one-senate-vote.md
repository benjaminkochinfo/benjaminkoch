The EU and Mexico signed a modernisation of their 2000 trade agreement in May; the EU ratified the interim deal in July; Mexico's Senate has yet to schedule its vote [1]. Christophe Hansen, the EU's agriculture commissioner, said in Mexico City he had a "good feeling" it could pass by the end of the year [1].

The backdrop is the US-Mexico relationship: in July Washington declined to extend USMCA, opening annual reviews over trade worth $960 billion a year, and the US buys roughly 80% of Mexico's exports [1]. "If it's harder to access the U.S. market, then it's only natural we look elsewhere," Hansen said [1].

Once in force, the deal cuts tariffs on the bulk of bilateral food trade [1]. The date that matters is the Senate's, and it has not been set.
