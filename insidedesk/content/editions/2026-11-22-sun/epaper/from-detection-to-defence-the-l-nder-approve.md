The law to strengthen cyber security cleared its last parliamentary step on Friday, when the Bundesrat gave its consent at its 1,070th session [1]. It is due to enter into force the day after promulgation [2].

What changes is the job description. The Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) gets a new task of averting threats to IT security: it may block and redirect malicious traffic and prohibit the operation of an IT system [2]. The BKA and the federal police may collect, delete or alter data on IT systems, including servers from which an attack originates, without the owners' knowledge [2]. The BSI is to detect attacks and their preparation better, and providers of digital services must pass its warnings on to affected customers [2]. Interior minister Alexander Dobrindt called it a shift "from detection towards defence" [2]. On Friday his parliamentary state secretary Daniela Ludwig said purely preventive measures were "unfortunately no longer sufficient" [4].

The Bundestag passed it on 12 November with CDU/CSU and SPD; AfD, Greens and Left voted against [3]. Experts at the hearing asked for a full judicial-warrant requirement, and the Länder had asked for one for BKA interventions too; the government largely refused [2]. A constitutional challenge is "not ruled out", JIPS notes [2]. Bitkom puts the annual damage from cyber attacks to German business at €160.4 billion to €205.8 billion [3].

New powers are only as good as their paperwork: who orders, who logs, who checks afterwards. That is where to watch.
