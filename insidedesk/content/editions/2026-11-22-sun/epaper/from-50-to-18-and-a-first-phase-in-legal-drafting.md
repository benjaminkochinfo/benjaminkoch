Trade deals are finished by lawyers, not by ministers. India's is now with the lawyers.

Speaking on Friday at the Atlantic Council on the sidelines of the World Bank and IMF annual meetings in Bangkok, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said she hoped the India–US deal would be concluded at the earliest [1]. On Thursday Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said the first phase had been finalised and was being legally drafted, and would be operationalised once India secures an export advantage over its competitors in the US market [1]. The US ambassador, Sergio Gor, said the "big items are done" [1].

The base is already moving. Under the framework agreed in February, the tariff on most Indian goods came down from 50% to 18% [1]. The United States remained India's largest goods export market in the first half of the fiscal year, with shipments up 11% on a year earlier to $50.8 billion [1].

The complication is oil. A new US law lets President Trump impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries buying Russian oil, and New Delhi has said it will take all necessary steps for the energy security of its 1.4 billion people [1].

Sitharaman's other theme was the plumbing of the system itself: the IMF and World Bank, she said, must be more "nimble-footed", because countries in balance-of-payments trouble cannot wait "three or four years" for a solution [1].

The thing to watch is not the next optimistic quote. It is the legal text, and whether its tariff schedule says what Goyal's condition requires.
