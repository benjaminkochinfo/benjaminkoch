Kyriakos Pierrakakis, Greece's finance minister and Eurogroup president, put a rule in print this weekend: "The election calendar cannot determine the endurance of the economy" (our translation) [1]. Any additional fiscal room, he said, would go to society [1].

His figures: a family of two average earners with two children took home about €33,000 net in 2019 and more than €44,500 in 2025, part of it, he conceded, absorbed by higher prices [1]. Permanent measures for 2027 come to €2.2 billion [1].

Desk arithmetic: that is roughly 35% more take-home pay over six years, before inflation [1]. The useful test is the one he set himself: whether next year's budget still reads the same once the campaign starts. Nothing here is investment advice.
