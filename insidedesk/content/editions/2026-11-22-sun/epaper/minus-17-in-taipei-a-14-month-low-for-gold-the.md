If you only watched New York, you missed the steepest fall of the week. Taiwanese shares lost 17% over five sessions, their biggest weekly decline since 1990, Reuters reported [1]. On Friday alone Japan and Korea fell more than 5%; the Nikkei closed down 5.06% [1]. Emerging-market stocks lost 3.10% on the day and MSCI's index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan 3.27% [1]. Taiwan is home to much of the world's chip manufacturing, so a fall there is less a mood than a calculation about future invoices.

The more surprising lines are in the shelter column. Gold did not shelter: spot gold fell 2.81% on Friday to $3,520.09 an ounce, a 14-month low, after its largest weekly drop in eight months [1]. Silver fell 4.22% to $40.95 and copper 2.9% to $12,162.50 a tonne [1]. Bitcoin, sold to some as digital gold, was near $59,000 late on Friday, its lowest since June, Investopedia reported [2].

What held was the dull option. US Treasuries rallied and the dollar index barely moved, down 0.05% at 103.90 after touching a 19-month high on Thursday [1]. The yen firmed, with the dollar 0.67% weaker at 158.6 yen [1]. "It's a little bit early to pick a top for the dollar," said Marc Chandler of Bannockburn Capital Markets [1].

The lesson is old and unglamorous. In a scramble, the asset that works is the one backed by a state's tax receipts, not by a story. Nothing here is investment advice.
