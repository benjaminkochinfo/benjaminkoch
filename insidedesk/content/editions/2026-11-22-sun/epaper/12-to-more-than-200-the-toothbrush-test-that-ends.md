The week in the bathroom cabinet: on Friday Stiftung Warentest added 11 new electric toothbrushes to its results, bringing the database to 37 models, with grades from good to poor and prices from €12 to more than €200 [1]. Its headline finding is the kind the Ordinary Ledger likes: recommendable models are available for under €20, and they are "mitunter kaum schlechter" (at times hardly worse) than brushes costing several hundred euros [1].

The new test winner, by the manufacturer's account, is Oral-B's iO Series 9, graded good (1.8) overall and the only brush to score very good (1.3) for cleaning, the category that carries 60% of the weight [2]. Each brush was used for two minutes by at least 30 test persons, with plaque measured before and after [2]. Rotating round heads and elongated sonic heads showed no clear advantage over each other in cleaning, the testers say; choose by habit, since a round head must be guided tooth by tooth [1].

The line to read twice is the one about follow-up costs. Replacement heads are where the price of a brush lives, and the differences between models are "zum Teil enorm" (in part enormous), Stiftung Warentest writes; its table lists them [1]. Desk arithmetic: between the cheapest and the dearest handle there is a factor of more than 16. Before you compare handles, compare a year of heads.
