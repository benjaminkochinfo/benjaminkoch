A violent storm tore through Tupanciretã, an agricultural town in Brazil's Rio Grande do Sul state, in about 40 minutes before dawn on Friday, AFP reported [1]. Civil defence authorities raised the toll on Saturday to four dead and 162 injured, up from an earlier count of 60 injured [1]. Around 8,000 homes were damaged, along with schools and the local hospital, and thousands of electricity poles came down [1].

Brazil's national meteorological institute, Inmet, measured winds of 114 km/h; the civil defence classified the storm as a supercell and did not rule out a tornado [1]. Almost all of the town's roughly 20,000 residents left their homes, yet only about 30 went to public shelters [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 0.15% of the town; the rest found room with family, neighbours or what was left of their own houses.

The state governor allocated seven million reais, about $1.2 million, for reconstruction, and President Lula sent medical teams [1]. Severe weather had hit around 30 municipalities in the state since 16 November, AFP said; Tupanciretã was the only one to report casualties [1]. A volunteer network, Prevots, counted a record 92 tornadoes and similar events in Brazil in 2025 [1].

"We lost everything ... But nobody was hurt, and life goes on," a 64-year-old bricklayer told AFP [1]. The repair bill will be paid in roof tiles, poles and clinic hours long after the cameras leave.
