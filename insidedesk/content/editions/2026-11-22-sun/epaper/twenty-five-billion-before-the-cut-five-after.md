Most of the week's attention went to the night the coalition agreed its electricity-tax cut. The more durable sentence came a day later, from the man who has to get budgets through the Bundestag.

Thorsten Frei, who leads the CDU/CSU group, told Bild that the 2028 federal budget has a funding gap of €25 billion as things stand, and that the tax cut adds another €5 billion [1]. "The challenges are enormous," he said [1]. The coalition, in his words, will not be able to move "in small steps of 100 million euros" and will "have to go to the foundations", though he named none of them [1]. His yardstick for financing every wish: annual growth of four per cent [1].

He wants budget procedures rebuilt to start "from the big goals" and the Union wants talks on 2028 to begin before Christmas [1]. ARD's analysis of the same week quotes the federal finance plan at around €22 billion for 2028, rising to €48 billion by 2030 [2].

Desk arithmetic: Frei's €25 billion plus €5 billion is €30 billion, roughly six times the €5.4 billion the 2027 cut needs [1].

The useful thing about a big number said early is that it can no longer be discovered late. Watch whether "the foundations" turn into a list with line items.
