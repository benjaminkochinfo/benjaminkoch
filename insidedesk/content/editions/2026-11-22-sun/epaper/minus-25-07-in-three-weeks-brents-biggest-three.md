Front-month Brent crude for January delivery settled at $92.57 a barrel on Friday, Dow Jones Market Data reported [1]. That is $3.27, or 3.41%, lower on the week, and the third weekly fall in a row [1]. Over those three weeks the price has lost $25.07, or 21.31%, the largest three-week net decline since the week ending 24 October 2008 [1].

Friday itself was a small up day: plus 59 cents, or 0.64%, the second gain in a row [1]. Brent is 21.78% below its 2026 settlement high of $118.35, set on Tuesday 31 March, but still 52.13% higher than at the start of the year [1]. Desk arithmetic: that leaves the barrel about $31.72 dearer than at the end of 2025, even after November's slide.

Read the two halves together. A fall of a fifth in three weeks is a large move by any standard, and the comparison with 2008 is striking. But a price that is still half as high again as in January has not returned to calm; it has given back part of a spike. For you, the useful number arrives later, at the pump and in heating-oil invoices, where refiners, distributors and taxes stand between the barrel and the bill. Watch whether this week's falls show up there before you watch the next headline. Nothing here is investment advice.
