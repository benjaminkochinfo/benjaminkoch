Budgets are not written once. In Romania this year, one is being rewritten for the second time, and the asking price keeps rising.

The government is drafting a second budget rectification, the mid-year adjustment of spending and revenue, and requests from ministries have climbed to about 70 billion lei, up from earlier estimates of 60 billion [1]. Finance Minister Alexandru Nazare has called the requests exaggerated and said the country cannot afford allocations on that scale [1]. The aim is to close the rectification by the end of November [1].

Behind it, the 2027 budget is already taking shape, with pension increases and tax relief on the list, including lower health-insurance contributions for mothers as proposed by the Social Democrats [1].

The funding line is the plain one. The state is expected to raise more than 30 billion lei from domestic and foreign markets before the year is out, which could push public debt to a record 62% of GDP [1].

Desk arithmetic: the requests rose by 10 billion lei, or about a sixth, between the two estimates [1].

None of this is a crisis on its own. It is the ordinary arithmetic of a finance ministry saying no to colleagues, and of lenders deciding the price of every yes. If you hold Romanian bonds or earn in lei, the date to note is the end of November, when the rectification is meant to be done [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
