The UN climate conference in Antalya was scheduled to end on Friday 20 November [2]. When this note was written, at about 09:20 CET on Sunday, it had not. A third and, in theory, final draft of the cover decision was published overnight, now 94 paragraphs across 10 pages, The Guardian reported [1]. The closing plenary meant to adopt it slipped from 09:00 Turkish time to 10:00, then 11:00, and the UNFCCC website then showed 11:30, which is 09:30 CET [1]. Until a gavel falls, everything below is a draft.

**Fossil fuels, by reference.** The Thursday draft had removed every mention of fossil fuels after Saudi Arabia and other producers objected, Al Jazeera reported [2]. The new text does not restore the words. Instead it opens by having countries "recall" the COP28 agreement in Dubai "in its entirety" — the only UN cover decision in which the phrase fossil fuels has appeared [1]. You are asked, in other words, to read one document through another.

**What is in, what is out.** According to The Guardian's reading, the draft keeps a goal of 35% of the world's final energy coming from electricity by 2035, says as much electrification as possible should be clean, and drops a hard numerical target for carbon capture [1]. Campaigners at 350.org still say the text makes room for a 2035 carbon-capture target, so the exact wording will matter [1]. If approved, Al Jazeera reports, it would for the first time acknowledge that warming is on course to pass 1.5°C, while calling for faster carbon removal to reverse the overshoot [2].

**The finance line.** A $120 billion adaptation-finance target backed by developing countries is not in the draft [1]. Harjeet Singh of the Fill the Fund campaign said parties only "agree to discuss" a tripling pathway in 2027 [1]. COP31's president, Murat Kurum, told the plenary early on Sunday: "We believe that we have put forward a text which all the parties can agree upon" [2]. An EU official told AFP the proposal was a "backwards" step [1].

**For you.** The record to beat is COP25 in Madrid, which ran 44 hours over time and closed at 13:55 CET on a Sunday in 2019 [1]. Length is not the measure. The measure is what each paragraph obliges governments to put in their next national plans, budgets and grid tenders, where the dull machinery takes over from the plenary. Read the final text, not the press conference.
