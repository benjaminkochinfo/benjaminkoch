The Ordinary Ledger takes the week's big numbers and turns them into lines on a household budget. This week the screens were full of falling chip shares. The quieter document that matters for more kitchen tables was a list of rules: from 1 January 2027 the Riester pension is replaced by new state-subsidised products, and on Friday Stiftung Warentest reminded those who already hold a Riester contract what they can do in the meantime [1][2].

**What the state adds.** For every euro you pay in up to €360 a year, the state adds 50 cents; for further euros up to €1,800, it adds 25 cents. The maximum subsidy is €540 a year [1]. The minimum contribution is €120 a year, or €10 a month; each child brings up to €300 a year, euro for euro; and anyone who signs before their 25th birthday gets a one-off €200 [1]. Desk arithmetic: at the minimum €120 the subsidy is €60; at €1,800 it is €540, or 30% of what you pay in. There are three product types: a free pension depot, a standard depot with effective costs capped at 1.0% a year in the savings phase, and a guarantee product covering 100% or 80% of contributions [1].

**What it costs to leave.** If you hold a Riester contract, you can keep saving under the old rules, switch your existing contract into the new subsidy system, or move to a new contract; past subsidies do not have to be repaid either way [1]. The old provider may charge a flat €150 for a transfer in the first five years, and the new provider may charge a one-off fee of at most €150 [1]. Desk arithmetic: a move can cost up to €300 in fees, more than half of a full year's maximum subsidy. And one line deserves a highlighter: if you do switch, instruct the new provider to carry out the transfer, and do not cancel the old contract yourself, or you could lose subsidies [1].

**What it costs to stay well.** If your Riester policy invests in funds, you may be able to swap the funds inside it. Stiftung Warentest's database now names the best available funds for 72 Riester fund policies, drawn from more than 1,800 funds the insurers offer; the swap usually costs nothing, and the testers suggest no more than three funds [2]. Its first choice is a broad-market equity ETF that can be left alone for years [2].

**What the calendar says.** Desk arithmetic: from today there are 40 days to 1 January. The regulator's advice is that you need not use any of them. You get the full subsidy for 2027 even if you sign during the year and pay in the whole eligible amount, for example as a lump sum in November [1]. BaFin also notes that some providers are already advertising aggressively, for example with a bonus for pre-registering [1]. Existing Riester holders keep their contracts on the old terms, Stiftung Warentest says: "Grund zur Eile besteht nicht" (there is no reason to hurry) [2].

**The bottom line.** The reform is a set of percentages, caps and fees, which is to say a set of things you can check. Find your last annual statement, note what your contract costs and how it invests, and let January's salespeople wait. Nothing here is investment advice.
