Your Sunday electricity in Germany and Luxembourg was bought on Saturday at an average of €204.08 per megawatt hour, SMARD's quarter-hourly data show [1]. Saturday's delivery day had cleared at €187.88 [2]. Desk arithmetic: Sunday is €16.20, or 8.6%, dearer than Saturday, and €64.38, or 46%, dearer than last Sunday's €139.70 [1]. A Sunday is supposed to be the cheap day of the week. This one was not.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-22nov]]

**Half the wind.** The forecasts that traders bid against tell the story. Energy-Charts' day-ahead forecast put German onshore and offshore wind at about 611 GWh for Saturday and about 309 GWh for Sunday; solar was forecast at about 94 GWh and about 62 GWh [3]. Desk arithmetic: Sunday's expected wind output is roughly half of Saturday's. When the turbines step back, the plants that burn gas and coal step forward, and they set the price.

**The shape of the day.** Sunday's dearest quarter-hour starts at 17:00, at €242.94, as the lights come on and the sun has gone; the cheapest is the last one, from 23:45, at €181.19 [2]. On Saturday the cheapest slot was at 13:00, at €144.70 [2]. Desk arithmetic: Sunday's cheapest quarter-hour costs about €36 more than Saturday's. The midday dip that makes a weekend cheap barely shows up at all; the curve is flatter and higher.

**The week, added up.** The seven delivery days from Monday 16 to Sunday 22 November ran from €146.25 on Wednesday to €224.83 on Thursday [1]. Desk arithmetic: they averaged €197.66, against €244.57 for the seven days to 15 November, a fall of about 19%. The first 22 days of November average about €233.99. So this was a cheaper week, and a dearer Sunday, and both are true.

**For you.** If you pay a dynamic tariff, today rewards late evening rather than lunchtime, which is the opposite of last weekend. If you pay a fixed tariff, the swing passes through your supplier's procurement books, the dull machinery that turns weeks like this into a price per kilowatt hour next year. Neither the cheap week nor the dear Sunday is a trend on its own. Nothing here is investment advice.
