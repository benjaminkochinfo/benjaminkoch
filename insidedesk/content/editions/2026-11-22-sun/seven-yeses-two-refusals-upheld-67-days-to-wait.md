Europe's medicines committee, the CHMP, met from 9 to 12 November and recommended seven new medicines for approval, plus extended indications for 11 that are already on the market, the European Medicines Agency reported [1]. That brings this year's tally to 79 positive opinions on new medicines, eight negative ones and 108 extensions [1]. The list is less exciting than a launch event, and more useful: each line is a dossier somebody had to defend.

**A new kind of blood-pressure pill.** The most widely relevant recommendation is Baxfendy, AstraZeneca's baxdrostat, for adults whose high blood pressure is not controlled by at least two existing medicines [2]. It is a selective inhibitor of aldosterone synthase, the enzyme behind a hormone that makes the kidneys hold on to salt and water [2]. The committee cites "clinically relevant and statistically significant" falls in systolic and diastolic pressure in placebo-controlled trials; the most common side effect is hyperkalaemia, too much potassium in the blood [2]. If you are on two or three tablets already, that last word is the one your doctor will watch.

**The rest of the list.** The committee also backed two lung-cancer medicines, aumolertinib and sintilimab, two insulin-aspart biosimilars for patients from the age of one, and two generics [1]. It recommended extending obinutuzumab to adults with moderate-to-severe lupus [1]. After re-examination it upheld refusals for two medicines, a sufentanil/ketamine product for children's acute pain and arimoclomol for Niemann-Pick disease type C, and two other applications were withdrawn [1].

**A line about the pill.** The committee adopted a safety letter to doctors on Mounjaro (tirzepatide) and oral contraceptives [1]. Women using the pill are advised either to add a barrier method for four weeks after starting tirzepatide and four weeks after each dose increase, or to switch to a non-oral method [1]. It is the kind of dull, specific advice that prevents real harm.

**For you.** A positive opinion is not a prescription. The Commission's decision normally comes 67 days after adoption [2]. Desk arithmetic: for opinions adopted on 12 November, that points to around 18 January 2027. After that come national pricing and reimbursement, which in Germany is where the G-BA's benefit assessment comes in. Patience here is not bureaucracy for its own sake; it is how the invoice and the evidence meet.
