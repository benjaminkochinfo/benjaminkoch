**The podium.** The Bank of England raised Bank Rate by a quarter point to 4% on Thursday, its first increase since August 2023 [1]. The vote was 8–1 [1]. In the minutes, Alan Taylor was careful about the label: this was "a risk-management 'one-for-now' insurance hike", not "the start of a hiking cycle", and "the next move could be up, down, or sideways" [1]. Andrew Bailey, the governor, said the case for 25 basis points now was clear, and that without softer news on energy he could "see the argument for a further increase" before taking a more conditional approach [1]. The summary line that markets will repeat is shorter still: "Monetary policy is not on a pre-set path" [1].

**The print.** Catherine L Mann voted for 50 basis points, to 4.25% [1]. CPI was 3.5% in September and is expected to reach around 5% by mid-2027; Mann puts mid-2027 at 4.9%, about 25 basis points higher without the latest Budget measures [1]. She says the economy has crossed thresholds where households and firms respond non-linearly, and that a larger step is "good policy" from a credibility view [1]. That is a recorded dissent, not a mood.

**The gap.** Both camps agree on the diagnosis: energy prices have risen further with the Middle East conflict in its ninth month, and the risk of second-round effects has grown even though evidence is still limited [1]. They disagree on the dose. Eight judged 25 basis points enough for now; Mann judged the risk of the smaller step larger than the risk of over-tightening [1]. Taylor's "not a cycle" and Mann's "+50" can hang on the same wall.

**What it means for you.** Tracker mortgages and savings rates move with Bank Rate; fixed deals move when they are refinanced [1]. The useful reading is that a committee of nine disagreed in public, published it the next day, and meets again on 17 December [1]. Nothing here is advice about your mortgage.
