**The podium.** On Saturday, after young people had marched against conscription, Defence Minister Boris Pistorius told the Funke newspapers that the measures in the new military-service law were working. "If the course continues, we will have no growth problems," he said, in this desk's translation; then there would be no need for a discussion about any form of conscription [1]. He also answered the marchers directly: "When the demonstrations say we do not want to go to the front and die in a war, I can only say: we want exactly the same" (desk translation) [1].

**The print.** The ministry counted about 186,200 active soldiers at the end of September, 4,500 more than a year earlier; in early October, its strongest hiring month, the 190,000 mark was passed for the first time [1]. Pistorius told Deutschlandfunk this was the largest strength since 2013, with many soldiers signing on for longer [2]. The build-up plan sets a target of at least 260,000 active soldiers by 2035, and a reserve rising from about 70,000 to at least 200,000 [1].

**The gap.** Desk arithmetic: from 190,000 to 260,000 is about 70,000 more soldiers in roughly nine years, net of everyone who leaves. ZDF adds the detail that matters most: 190,000 is the upper edge of the legal target corridor for 2026, inside which conscription is not on the table, and next year it becomes the lower edge [1]. The bar rises on 1 January whatever October looked like. Pistorius himself allowed that the curve could fall in a few years, and said that then the government would have to react [2].

**What it does not say.** A strength figure counts heads on one day. It does not show how many recruits stay past their first contract, which is where staffing plans usually fail quietly. The October hiring figures arrive in mid-November [1].

**For you.** This is a good number and a fair argument at once. Hear the confidence, then put the target beside the record. The 190,000 is the best figure in 13 years [2]. The 70,000 is the work still to do.
