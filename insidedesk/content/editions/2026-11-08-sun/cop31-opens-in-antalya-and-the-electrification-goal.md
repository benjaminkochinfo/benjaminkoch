After a week of weather stories from other places, the climate desk looks forward. On Monday the world's climate negotiators sit down in Antalya, and the useful preparation is to know what is actually on the table.

**The calendar.** The opening plenary of COP31, formally COP 31/CMP 21/CMA 8, is scheduled for 10:00 on Monday 9 November at the conference venue in Soğucaksu, Antalya, which is 08:00 in Berlin [1]. Talks run until 20 November, with a separate Antalya Leaders Summit for heads of state and government on 11 and 12 November [1][2]. Nearly 200 countries are taking part [2].

**The inheritance.** COP30 in Belém last November adopted a package calling for adaptation finance to be tripled by 2035 and agreed a mechanism to support workers and communities in the energy transition [2]. It left a major gap on fossil fuels: the final agreement had no roadmap away from them, despite calls from more than 80 countries [2]. Antalya is expected to focus on implementing existing commitments rather than adding new pledges [2].

**The host's idea, and its catch.** One of the prominent aims is a voluntary goal on increasing electrification, proposed by Turkey [3]. Reuters notes the catch plainly: unless the electricity is low-carbon, more of it will not curb emissions [3]. Coal was Turkey's largest power source last year at 34% of generation, according to the think tank Ember, making it Europe's biggest coal-fired generator [3]. The energy minister, Alparslan Bayraktar, says a just transition is difficult because the mines employ thousands, and points to a target of 10 gigawatts of nuclear capacity by 2040 [3].

**The view from beside the mine.** A few hundred kilometres west, in İkizköy near the Akbelen forest, villagers who have protested for years against lignite mining want leaders to come and look. "They should come here, those heads of state," said local official Nejla Işık [3]. Human Rights Watch said in October that Turkish authorities had arbitrarily arrested and prosecuted environmental protesters in Akbelen; the government says police intervene when demonstrations are unlawful [3].

**At home.** Green parliamentary leader Katharina Dröge called it an embarrassing signal that Chancellor Merz is skipping the conference, and urged Germany to join a group of countries including France, Spain and Denmark that back solidarity levies on aviation and fossil-fuel firms [4].

**For you.** Ignore the opening speeches. Watch for three dull outputs: whether the electrification goal says anything about the source of the power, whether adaptation finance gets a number, and whether a fossil-fuel roadmap returns. The first week is theatre. The second week is drafting.
