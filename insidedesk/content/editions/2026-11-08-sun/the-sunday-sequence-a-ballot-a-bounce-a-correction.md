On Sundays this desk puts the week back in the order it happened. This one needs it more than most, because the total, a 2.68% fall in the S&P 500 [5], describes no single day anyone lived through.

**Monday: oil first.** Brent fell 7.63% to $104.77 on hopes of US-Iran talks, and airlines rose [1]. The Dow gained 0.41% to 51,983.98, but the S&P 500 lost 0.51% to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq 1.07%, as Oracle said it would raise $20 billion for data centres and its shares fell 8% [1]. The first crack of the week was a funding plan, not a vote.

**Tuesday: the ballot.** Americans voted. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq snapped three-session losing streaks while the Dow finished slightly lower [2]. Futures fell after hours as results came in [2].

**Wednesday: the relief.** Democrats took both chambers, and the Dow jumped 1,569 points, or 3%, its fifth-largest one-day points gain; the S&P 500 rose 1.6% [2]. "Markets like gridlock," said Andrew Slimmon of Morgan Stanley Investment Management [2]. But the same page carried the warning: Goldman Sachs said Democratic state wins made data-centre moratoriums more likely, potentially affecting about 45% of planned US capacity [2]. Nvidia fell 3% on the best day [2].

**Thursday: the bill for the relief.** The Nasdaq fell 5.52% to 24,315.00, its deepest drop in 19 months, confirming a correction; the S&P 500 lost 4.36% [3]. The semiconductor index plunged more than 12%, and Reuters counted $3 trillion of S&P 500 value gone [3]. Volume was 26.9 billion shares against a 20-session average of 17.7 billion [3]. That is what a crowded exit sounds like.

**Friday: the payslip.** Payrolls beat expectations and unemployment fell to 4.1% [4]. Chips bounced 4.2%, AMD 11%, the S&P 500 0.32% to 7,376.86 [4]. Traders lifted the odds of a December Fed hike to 78% [4]. "AI has gone on trial several times in this cycle and come away with an innocent verdict," said LPL Financial's Adam Turnquist [4]. The jury, note, is still sitting.

**The total.** The S&P 500 is down four weeks in a row, 5.56% over the stretch, and 5.65% below its 6 October record [5]. Yet it is still up 7.76% this year [5]. Abroad the week split cleanly: the Kospi lost 11.71% and the Nikkei 9.25%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng gained 5.10% and the Stoxx Europe 600 0.24% [6]. The selling was about one industry's building plans, not about the world.
