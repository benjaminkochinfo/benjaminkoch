The week's AI story was told in index points. The more durable story was told in factory floors, and it is about the least glamorous step in making a chip: packaging.

**What changed.** Packaging used to mean protecting a chip and connecting it to a circuit board. For AI processors it now means joining processors and memory on a shared base so they work as one system at high speed [1]. As shrinking transistors gets harder and dearer, more of the performance gain comes from this step [1]. "Packaging was always that thing that you just didn't worry about," former Intel chief executive Pat Gelsinger told reporters in Taipei; he estimates it could eventually account for as much as half the value of a chip product [1].

**The numbers.** Research firm Yole Group forecasts that revenue from advanced packaging will overtake the traditional route for the first time in 2026, with capital spending on it nearing $28 billion [1]. Nvidia is providing billions of dollars in financing to expand CoWoS packaging capacity at SPIL, a unit of Taiwan's ASE, according to an industry executive cited by Reuters; Nvidia did not comment and ASE does not comment on rumours [1]. AMD has unveiled more than $10 billion of investment across Taiwan's chip industry, including packaging partnerships [1]. Taiwan's Powertech plans to put $400 million into a packaging joint venture with Broadcom in Singapore [1].

**The quote that matters.** "The most important thing right now is capacity," said Powertech's Jim Lin, adding that customers were waiting for the company to ramp up [1]. AMD's Lisa Su, asked about alternatives to TSMC, said simply: "we do need more capacity overall" [1].

**The memory twist.** On Friday TSMC's US-listed shares rose more than 6% in early trading on a report by The Information that it is drawing up plans to make 3D DRAM, a stacked memory, at Nvidia's request [2]. TSMC told the publication its focus remains its core foundry business, and no timeline has been disclosed [2]. Memory is cyclical, India Today notes: today's shortage can be tomorrow's glut [2].

**For you.** When a market tells you the AI build-out is over on Thursday and back on Friday, look at the queues instead. Customers waiting for packaging capacity is not a mood. It is an order book.
