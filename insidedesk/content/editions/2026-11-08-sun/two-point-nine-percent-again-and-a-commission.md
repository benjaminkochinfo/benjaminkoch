This week's health story is not an outbreak. It is a percentage, and it arrives in every payslip.

**The number.** The federal health ministry has set the average additional contribution rate for statutory health insurance in 2027 at 2.9%, the same as this year [1]. It is based on the estimate of the GKV's forecasting group of 14 October, which expects spending to grow much more slowly next year than in 2026 [1]. The exact rate for your fund is set by the fund itself [1].

**Why it held.** The ministry credits a contribution-stabilisation law, which it says relieves the funds by up to €19 billion next year [1]. Spending is then expected to rise by less than 3% [1]. That would be the first time in three years that spending growth is covered by growth in revenue; this year, spending is expected to rise by almost 7% [1].

**The minister's sentence.** "The average additional contribution rate has risen further and further in recent years. We have now stopped this development," said health minister Carsten Linnemann, adding: "But it is clear: this can only be a first step" (desk translation) [1]. The structural problems, he said, must be tackled, and a commission will present further proposals this year [1]. The ministry dates that to the end of 2026 [1].

**What it does not say.** An average is a reference figure, not a bill. Funds above it can stay above it, and funds below can rise towards it. A one-year relief of up to €19 billion is also a one-year measure unless the commission finds something lasting [1].

**For you.** Two quiet tasks this autumn. When your fund writes about its 2027 rate, compare it with the 2.9% reference. And when the commission reports, read what it proposes for hospitals and drugs, because that is where the 7% came from. Neither task is urgent. Both are cheaper than surprise.
