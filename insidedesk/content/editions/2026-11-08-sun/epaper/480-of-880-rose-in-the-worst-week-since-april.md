Index weeks are averages, and averages hide crowds. Morningstar's weekly wrap counts the crowd: of the 880 US-listed companies its analysts cover, 480, or 55%, rose in the week to 6 November, four were unchanged and 396, or 45%, fell [1]. That was in a week when the S&P 500 lost 2.68% and the Nasdaq 3.37% [1].

**Where the damage sat.** The worst sectors were energy, down 12.32%, and technology, down 6.71% [1]. The best were communication services, up 5.25%, and consumer cyclicals, up 2.36% [1]. Small companies held up better than large ones: small caps fell 1.65%, large caps 2.73% [1]. Value fell 2.22%, growth 3.12% [1].

**Why the index fell anyway.** A cap-weighted index gives the biggest companies the biggest vote, and this week the biggest losers were the companies selling chips, memory and data-centre parts, alongside the oil producers [1]. When the heavyweights fall, the index falls, even if the majority of names rise.

**For you.** A headline that says "stocks fell" is usually about an index, not about stocks. If you own a broad fund, you owned both the 396 and the 480 this week. The index tells you the weighted result; the count tells you how narrow the selling was. This week it was narrow. Nothing here is investment advice.
