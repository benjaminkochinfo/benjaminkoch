The week's tech story is usually told from Thursday. It started on Monday, with a funding plan. Oracle said it plans to raise $20 billion, half through common stock and half through corporate bonds, to fund AI data-centre construction, and its shares fell 8% that day [1]. Companies' plans for large-scale fundraising to expand AI infrastructure stirred investor caution, the Asia Business Daily reported from New York [1].

**Then the vote.** On Wednesday, after the midterms, Goldman Sachs analysts said Democratic state-level wins made data-centre moratoriums more likely, potentially affecting about 45% of planned US capacity, according to Investopedia [2]. Franklin Templeton's Stephen Dover said the election was unlikely to reverse demand for AI infrastructure but "could change where facilities are built, how quickly projects proceed and who pays" [2].

**Why the order matters.** If the first crack came from financing rather than politics, the question for next week is not only regulation. It is whether the companies building server halls can keep raising money at prices their shareholders accept. Dover's three variables, where, how fast and who pays, are the right ones to watch, and none of them is decided in a single trading day.
