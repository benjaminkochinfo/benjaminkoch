Germany's choice of a new federal president is moving through its committees. The CDU presidium meets on Sunday in Berlin, where party leader Friedrich Merz is expected to propose Ilse Aigner, the 61-year-old president of the Bavarian state parliament, as the Union's candidate; an official announcement is expected on Monday [1]. Her nomination is considered certain, and the SPD and Greens have signalled they want to vote for her in the Federal Convention [1]. She would be the first head of state from the CSU and the first woman in the office [1]. On Instagram she wrote: "There is a lot to do in the future and I look forward to making my contribution" (desk translation) [1].

**The criticism is about process.** Green Bundestag vice-president Omid Nouripour said he valued Aigner highly and thought she would be a good candidate, but that Merz had once again let an important personnel process slip, and that his group could only formally discuss a proposal once Merz actually made one rather than leaking it to the press [2].

**The date.** The Federal Convention elects the next president on 30 January [2]. Until then the useful thing to watch is not the candidate but the arithmetic of the convention's delegates, which is where an election of this kind is actually decided.
