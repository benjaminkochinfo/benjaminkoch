Europe and Germany receive only a small share of their gas via the Strait of Hormuz, NDR notes, but the region is decisive for the world market: looming scarcity drives competition for the remaining gas, including US LNG, which makes up a quarter of EU-wide deliveries [1]. Almost all LNG reaching Germany now comes from the US, more than 90% [1]. Most German imports still arrive by pipeline from Norway and the Netherlands, where domestic production is falling [1]. Exchange prices tripled by the autumn after the Iran war began, NDR says, which made filling storage over the summer unattractive [1]. Diversification has produced a new single dependence to watch.
