At around 15:00 on Saturday, a man who had driven a car into Kraków's main market square began shooting at random people from inside the vehicle, police said [1]. At least six people were killed, four women and two men, among them three Polish women and a British national, and 16 were injured, five of them from Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia and Britain [1]. The suspect, a 25-year-old Polish man who acted alone, was wounded in an exchange of fire with police [1].

"Until now in Poland we haven't had such drastic cases of terrorism," Prime Minister Donald Tusk said in Kraków [1]. On Saturday night he said police had found the bodies of two women and a man at the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, with "much to suggest" they were members of his immediate family [1]. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the man was "fascinated by far-right ideologies" and held "ultranationalistic" and "very radical" views [1]. More weapons and ammunition were found in his car [1].

Poland is observing two days of mourning [1]. "Poland does not stand alone," said the EU's foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas; Chancellor Merz wrote that Germany stands alongside Poland [1].

The investigation is at its beginning, and early descriptions of motive are the authorities' own. What is already clear is the response: police on the square during the attack, the suspect stopped, the victims' countries informed, and neighbours standing beside a grieving country.
