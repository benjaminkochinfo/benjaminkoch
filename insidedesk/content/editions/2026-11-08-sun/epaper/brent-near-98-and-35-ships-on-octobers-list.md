Crude had its biggest weekly fall since April 2020, but the products you actually burn told a tighter story, HeizOel24 reports [1]. Three things kept a floor under them.

**China's diesel.** China is exporting less diesel in November because its own stocks are empty [1]. Diesel and heating oil are close cousins, so a smaller supply from a big refining country matters for Europe's winter.

**The mines.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards reported three more tankers hit on Thursday, two by mines and one with a fire on board, and two further ships said to have hit mines on Friday morning [1]. The UK's maritime security agency UKMTO already lists 35 ships for October, the highest since the war began [1].

**The ticker.** Brent stood at $97.97 a barrel and gasoil at $1,366.75 a tonne on HeizOel24's panel at 09:00 on Sunday [1]. On Friday morning German heating oil rose about three cents to a national average of €1.71 a litre after Thursday's monthly low [1].

**For you.** The gap between the crude headline and your fuel price is not mysterious. It is the refining and shipping step in between, and this week that step was under strain from two directions at once.
