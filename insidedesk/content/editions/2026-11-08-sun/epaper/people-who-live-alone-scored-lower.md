A survey of about 50,000 people in 92 countries, published in Social Indicators Research, found that people who live alone generally have lower well-being than those who live with others [1]. In the workplace, people on hybrid models, combining home and office, scored higher than those in fully remote jobs [1]. Employment and higher income went with better mental health, and lower formal education with lower well-being [1]. None of this proves cause. It does suggest where to look first when someone you know is struggling: not only at their mindset, but at their circumstances, and at who they see in a week.
