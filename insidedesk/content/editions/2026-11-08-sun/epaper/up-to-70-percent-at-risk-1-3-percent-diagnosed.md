Hospital food is an old joke. The figures are not. In prospective studies, at least 30% to 70% of hospital patients in Germany are malnourished, according to Hannover Medical School (MHH) [1]. Yet in an analysis of representative health-insurance data, researchers from Hannover and Würzburg found malnutrition was diagnosed in only 1.16% to 1.30% of inpatients [1]. Of those diagnosed, just 2.77% to 4.45% received nutrition therapy [1].

**Why it matters.** Malnutrition means a lasting deficit of nutrients such as vitamins, minerals or protein, and it can follow cancer, gut disease, infections or some medicines [1]. In a matched comparison, 90-day mortality was 18.2% among diagnosed patients against 7.3% in the control group, and 44.9% were readmitted within 90 days against 37.8% [1]. Average health costs in the year after diagnosis were €18,052, €4,396 more than in the control group [1].

**What is being done.** MHH's gastroenterology clinic has signed a quality contract with the Techniker Krankenkasse: insured patients are screened on admission, during longer stays and at discharge, with the insurer paying agreed supplements [1]. The data will go to the Federal Joint Committee, which was tasked in April 2026 with setting quality measures for detecting and treating malnutrition in hospitals [1].

**For you.** If someone close to you is in hospital for more than a few days, it is reasonable to ask whether their nutrition has been checked. "Good nutrition is an essential part of good medical treatment," says Dr Katharina Hupa-Breier (desk translation) [1].
