The money desk's week ahead has a timetable worth pinning up. The US Treasury auctions $58 billion of three-year notes on Monday 9 November, $42 billion of ten-year notes on Tuesday and $25 billion of 30-year bonds on Thursday, each at 13:00 New York time, 19:00 in Berlin [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is $125 billion of new debt in four days.

**The figure in the middle.** October's consumer price index is due on Tuesday at 08:30 New York time, 14:30 CET, hours before the ten-year sale [1]. Producer prices follow on Friday 13 November [2]. After Friday's jobs report, traders put a 78% chance on a Fed hike in December [3]. An inflation surprise either way would arrive just before buyers bid for ten-year paper.

**Where yields start.** The ten-year Treasury yield ended the week at 4.86% and the two-year at 4.51%, both lower than a week earlier [2].

**For you.** Auctions are the plumbing of government finance and rarely make news. When they do, it is because buyers asked for a noticeably higher yield to take the paper, which then feeds through to borrowing costs elsewhere. Watch Tuesday evening's result as a reading of appetite, not as a verdict. Nothing here is investment advice.
