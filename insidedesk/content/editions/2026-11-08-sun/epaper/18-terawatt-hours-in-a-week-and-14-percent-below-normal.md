In the week from 26 October to 1 November, Germany consumed 18 terawatt-hours of gas, 14.2% less than usual for that calendar week in 2018 to 2021, according to NDR's tracker of official data [1]. Gas use depends heavily on weather and season: in summer, sometimes only 10 TWh a week is used; in winter it can be more than 30 [1].

**Why it matters this year.** German storage was 60% full on 6 November, about 30 percentage points below the 2017–2021 average [1]. Storage holds about 250 TWh; Germany used almost 850 TWh in 2024, most of it in winter [1]. Experts warn that a particularly cold winter or missing LNG deliveries could make gas scarce, though the Bundesnetzagentur says supply security is assured [1].

**The weather link.** A mild week saves gas that would otherwise be drawn from storage. Desk arithmetic: if 18 TWh is 14.2% below the usual figure, the usual week was about 21 TWh, so the week used roughly 3 TWh less, a little over 1% of storage capacity. That is a small cushion, and it depends on weather nobody controls.

**One caution.** Household consumption is estimated indirectly from standard profiles and is often revised after publication [1]. Treat a single week as an indication, not a trend.
