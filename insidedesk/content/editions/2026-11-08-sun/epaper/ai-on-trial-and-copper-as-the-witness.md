Markets tell stories about themselves, and this week's story borrowed the language of a courtroom. "AI has gone on trial several times in this cycle and come away with an innocent verdict," said LPL Financial's Adam Turnquist on Friday, describing Thursday's sell-off as "a little bit of a knee-jerk reaction" [1]. Truist's Keith Lerner said his firm was "still giving technology the benefit of the doubt" [1].

**The behavioural point.** A trial is a good metaphor for a mood, and a poor one for evidence. Juries are swayed by the last witness. So are traders: Wednesday's relief, Thursday's panic and Friday's bounce were three verdicts in three days on largely the same facts [1]. The VIX, the market's gauge of expected volatility, ended the week at 25.64, up about 32% [2].

**The habit worth copying.** Turnquist did one useful thing: he checked something boring. Copper, a major ingredient in data centres, was still above its 200-day average and its September lows, he said [1]. "If this is real demand destruction on AI, you'd think copper would start to roll over," he said. "It's not a perfect barometer, but I look at it as a reality check" [1].

**For you.** When the mood swings, pick one dull indicator that would have to move if the story were true, and look at it before you decide anything. It will not tell you the future. It will tell you whether the mood has evidence yet. Nothing here is investment advice.
