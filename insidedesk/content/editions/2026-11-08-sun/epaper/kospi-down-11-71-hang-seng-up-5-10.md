If you want to see how specific last week's sell-off was, look east. South Korea's Kospi fell 11.71%, from 5,730.74 to 5,059.69, and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 9.25% to 57,434.77 [1]. Over the same days Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 5.10% to 24,481.86 and the Shanghai Composite 1.39% [1]. Europe's Stoxx 600 barely moved, up 0.24% [1].

**The reason is the product list.** Global Terminal attributes the Kospi and Nikkei falls to slumps in memory and chip-equipment stocks, as investors priced a slower US data-centre build after the midterms [1]. The PHLX Semiconductor index lost 12.15% on the week [1]. Korea and Japan are heavy in exactly the companies that sell into American server halls. Hong Kong and Shanghai are not.

**Note the holiday.** Japan's market was shut on Tuesday 3 November for a public holiday, so the Nikkei's week had one session fewer [1].

**For you.** Regional labels like "Asian markets fell" are rarely precise enough to use. This week the useful label was industrial: markets built on chips and memory fell hard; others rose. A fund named after a continent is, underneath, a fund named after its largest industries. Nothing here is investment advice.
