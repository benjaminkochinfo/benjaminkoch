Cyclone Akvan is expected to cross the coast of north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha by noon local time on Friday 20 November, the India Meteorological Department said on Wednesday, with winds of 90 to 100 km per hour [1]. It has been moving slowly, at around 8 km per hour, and is expected to gain speed from Thursday [1]. "On November 19, heavy to very heavy rainfall will occur in the coastal parts of Odisha and Andhra," said IMD director general Mrutyunjay Mohapatra [1].

**The track has moved, and so has the number.** On Tuesday the IMD's warning pointed further south, to a crossing between Nellore and Tuni as a very severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 120 to 130 km per hour [2]. By Wednesday the expected landfall lay between Narsapur in Andhra and Puri in Odisha [1]. Forecasts are revised; that is what they are for. The preparations do not depend on the last decimal.

**The calendar is the problem.** Akvan is the third cyclone to hit the state in less than a month, after Arnab and Murasu in late October and early November, and the kharif crop is nearing harvest in several districts [2]. As of 14 November, crops stood on 23.87 lakh hectares in Andhra Pradesh, 21.32 lakh of them kharif and 13.60 lakh paddy; officials singled out 9.51 lakh hectares in ten coastal districts, 9.16 lakh of them paddy [3]. Desk arithmetic: a lakh is 100,000, so the coastal priority area is about 951,000 hectares, or roughly 9,510 square kilometres of crops.

**The dull machinery.** The agriculture minister told officials to help farmers harvest paddy, maize and cotton that is ready, move produce to safety, clear field channels against waterlogging, and hold off fertiliser and pesticide during heavy rain [3]. Of 30,808 fishing boats in the state, 30,635 were back ashore by Monday morning, with 173 still being called in [3]. On Wednesday Andhra kept 13 national and 11 state disaster-response teams ready, and Odisha said even residents of two-storey buildings near the coast might have to move because of storm-surge warnings [1].

**For you.** Nothing on your plate changes this week. What is worth noticing is the order of the work: crops cut early, boats counted, damage to be reported within 24 hours and farmer by farmer within a week [3]. That is how a region that has had three cyclones in a month keeps a bad week from becoming a bad year.
