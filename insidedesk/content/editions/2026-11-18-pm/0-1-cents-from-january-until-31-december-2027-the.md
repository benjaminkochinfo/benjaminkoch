The coalition committee of CDU, CSU and SPD agreed overnight to cut the electricity tax for all consumers to the European minimum of 0.1 cents per kilowatt-hour, from 2.05 cents today, according to the decision paper seen by ARD's Berlin studio [1]. The cut costs about €5.4 billion a year [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is 1.95 cents off every kilowatt-hour.

The small print is the story. The money is secured only for 2027. The paper says the aim is "to continue the tax reduction beyond 31 December 2027", but only if "sustainable counter-financing is guaranteed" [1]. The 2027 bill is to be paid from headroom in the EEG account, the fund behind renewable-power support, whose balance depends on the price of green electricity; if it falls short, the coalition promises a "package of measures" during the year [1].

Chancellor Friedrich Merz called it a promise honoured: "we have kept our word" [1]. CDU general secretary Franziska Hoppermann was more candid about 2028: a coalition committee cannot close the gap in the 2028 federal budget "in one evening" [1]. The Greens' Franziska Brantner called the lack of planning certainty a "poverty certificate"; ifo president Clemens Fuest called the cut "economically well justified", then added that "any spending discipline is missing" [1].

A fuel-price cap is to apply from 1 January, also limited to twelve months, with the regulation itself expiring after three months unless renewed; Länder may set higher or lower regional maximum prices [1]. On pensions and bracket creep there was apparently no deal. The parliamentary leaders told MPs by text that all other topics would be discussed "in the coming days" [1].

For you, the useful thing is the dates rather than the speeches. The cut has to be written into a budget the committee has already passed, before the final vote on 27 November [1]. A tax that ends on a fixed date is a question that comes back on a fixed date.
