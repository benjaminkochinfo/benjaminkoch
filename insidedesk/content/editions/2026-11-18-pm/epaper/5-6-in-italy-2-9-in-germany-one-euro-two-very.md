Eurostat confirmed on Wednesday that euro area annual inflation was 4.0% in October, up from 3.8% in September and 2.1% a year earlier, matching its flash estimate of 4 November [1]. Energy prices were 21.0% higher than a year ago, revised up from 20.8% in the flash, and contributed 1.89 percentage points of the 4.0; services added 1.55 points [1].

The average is a poor guide to any single kitchen. Italy's annual rate jumped to 5.6% from 4.2%, with prices up 1.2% in a single month [1]. Germany went the other way: 2.9%, down from 3.3%, with prices 0.1% lower on the month [1]. Desk arithmetic: the gap between the two is 2.7 percentage points under the same central bank rate. Belgium was at 5.2%, Greece 5.7%, Spain 4.9% and France 3.7% [1].

Underneath, the measure excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco rose to 2.6% from 2.5% [1]. Processed food, alcohol and tobacco were up only 0.4% on the year [1]. So the story is still mostly the energy bill, and how fast each country's tariffs pass it on.

For you, the practical point is that "euro area inflation" is a statistic about 21 countries, not a description of your shopping. If your rent, pay or pension is indexed, check which national index it uses. The next flash estimate, for November, is scheduled for 1 December [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
