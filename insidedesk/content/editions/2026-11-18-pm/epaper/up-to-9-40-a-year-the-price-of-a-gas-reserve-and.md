Households that heat with gas are to help pay for a national gas reserve from October 2027, Futurezone reports from the cabinet's draft law, adopted on 11 November and before the Bundesrat since 12 November as an urgent bill [1]. The reserve is to hold 24 terawatt hours, about a tenth of German storage capacity, and may only be released once the Federal Network Agency declares the emergency stage [1].

The running costs are now put at €247 million to €405 million a year, up from an upper bound of €308 million in October's draft [1]. On the government's worked examples, a flat using 13,000 kWh would pay €3.77 to €6.11 a year and a detached house using 20,000 kWh €5.80 to €9.40 [1]. Roughly 40% falls on households and small business and 60% on industry [1].

That split is the quarrel. The industrial energy association VIK backs the reserve but rejects the levy: "Krisenvorsorge ist jedoch eine staatliche Aufgabe," says chief executive Christian Seyfert, crisis provision is a job for the state budget [1]. The utilities' association BDEW and the municipal utilities' VKU also prefer the budget [1].

The dull detail worth knowing: a supplier that passes the levy on must write to you at least a month ahead, and that letter gives you the right to cancel without notice on the day the price rises, under § 41 of the Energy Industry Act [1]. Desk arithmetic: at the upper bound the new levy is about a sixth of the old storage levy of €2.89 per MWh that was abolished on 1 January [1].
