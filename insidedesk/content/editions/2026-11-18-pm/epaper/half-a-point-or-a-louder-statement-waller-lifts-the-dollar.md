Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC that if the central bank wants inflation down it needs more "aggressive action": either a half-point rise at the December meeting, or a post-meeting statement that sets up further increases [1]. The minutes of the 27–28 October meeting, published on Wednesday, showed officials saw a growing risk that they might need to intensify their efforts, even as they held rates that month [1].

The dollar index rose 0.3% to 103.87, its highest since April 2025 [1]. The euro fell 0.3% to $1.09648 and briefly touched its lowest level since then; sterling lost 0.5% to $1.3139 [1]. Markets fully price a quarter-point rise in December, with a small chance of half a point, according to CME's FedWatch tool [1]. Not everyone expects the larger step: LPL Financial's Jeffrey Roach wrote that the committee "will not likely feel the need" for it [1].

Speeches move screens; the dull part arrives later, on invoices priced in dollars. A weaker euro makes those invoices a little dearer at home, and a dollar-denominated barrel with them. Not investment advice.
