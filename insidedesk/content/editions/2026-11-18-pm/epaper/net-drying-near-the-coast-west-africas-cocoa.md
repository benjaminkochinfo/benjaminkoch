West Africa is getting drier, which is normal for mid-November, but the pace of the dry-down will help decide how much cocoa the second half of the main crop yields, ADM Investor Services wrote on Monday [1]. Ivory Coast's production is lagging last year's even though the harvest began a month earlier than usual [1].

World Weather Inc. reported only sporadic, light rain over the weekend and expected the same over the following seven to ten days; what rain there is falls mainly near the coast, and the growing areas are seeing net drying [1]. Meanwhile, cocoa stocks held by the exchanges continue to climb [1].

Both facts are true at once: less fruit on the trees, more beans in the warehouses. Read the two together and resist the louder one: a dry week is a reason to watch the arrivals figures, not yet a verdict on the crop.
