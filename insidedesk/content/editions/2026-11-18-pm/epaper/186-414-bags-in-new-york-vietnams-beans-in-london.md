Coffee is two markets, and on Tuesday they went in opposite directions. March arabica on ICE in New York settled at 281.40 US cents a pound, up 6.50 cents or 2.36%, CocoaIntel reports [1]. January robusta in London settled at $3,071 a tonne, down $73 or 2.32%, its lowest in roughly two and a half years [1].

The reason is in the harvest calendar. Newly picked Vietnamese robusta was reaching buyers in London, with larger volumes expected from late November [1]. In New York, by contrast, the stock of arabica certified for delivery stood at 186,414 bags at the start of Tuesday, a 27-year low [1].

The weather is doing its quieter part. World Weather Inc. expected Brazil to trend wetter, with rain nearly every day, which ADM Investor Services described as beneficial for the crop as long as it does not become excessive; more rain was also expected in Indonesia later this month [2]. Brazil exported 5.02 million bags of green coffee in October, almost 31% more than a year earlier and a record, according to the industry group Cecafé as cited by ADM [2].

For you, the split matters more than the headline. Desk arithmetic: one moved up 2.36% and the other down 2.32% on the same day, so "the price of coffee" did roughly nothing on average and something quite different in each bean. Check which one your blend is made of before you believe any headline about the price of coffee. Nothing here is investment advice.
