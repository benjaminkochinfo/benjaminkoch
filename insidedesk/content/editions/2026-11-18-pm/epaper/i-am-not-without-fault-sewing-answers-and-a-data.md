A day after reports built on more than 15 million internal documents, Deutsche Bank's chief executive Christian Sewing told Handelsblatt: "Ich bin nicht fehlerfrei" — "I am not without fault", in our translation [1]. He rejected the charge that he had pushed risks aside and said client data such as account numbers, balances and login details were, on the bank's findings, not affected [1]. The bank, he said, has put €7 billion into compliance and anti-money-laundering work and raised staff there by 40% to 3,000 [1].

The more useful news is procedural. Hesse's data protection commissioner, Alexander Roßnagel, said he had opened an investigation into whether the bank informed affected customers without undue delay, as data protection law requires [1]. The bank had formally notified his office; whether it did so in time is now being checked [1].

At 15:42 CET Deutsche Bank shares were 2.38% lower at €30.21 [2]. An apology is a statement. A regulator's file is a process, and processes are what customers can rely on.
