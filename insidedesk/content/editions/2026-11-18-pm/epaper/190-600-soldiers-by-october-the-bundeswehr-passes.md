The Bundeswehr had about 190,600 active soldiers at the end of October, the defence ministry said, about 6,400 or 3.5% more than in October 2025 [1]. That already exceeds the legally set growth corridor for 2026 of 186,000 to 190,000 [1]. October was the strongest hiring month of the year [1].

The pipeline behind the headline is wider. Applications reached about 64,500 by the end of October, up 31%, and hires across all military careers 27,400, up 20% [1]. Some 14,200 people are serving as voluntary conscripts or short-term soldiers under the new military service, about 17.5% more than a year earlier [1]. More than 75,000 reservists hold a posting, inside the 70,000 to 80,000 target band [1]. About 558,500 registration questionnaires had been sent out [1].

Boris Pistorius said that if the trend continues, "foreseeably no additional compulsory elements and no conscription" would be needed [1]. "Our measures are working," he said [1].

The numbers are the ministry's own, and corridors are targets set by the same state that reports against them. Still, a personnel statistic that meets its legal band is the kind of evidence a defence debate rarely gets. It is worth more than another round of slogans about duty, in either direction.
