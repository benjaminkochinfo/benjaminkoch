The US Food and Drug Administration has approved secukinumab (Cosentyx) 150 mg for adults with polymyalgia rheumatica who have responded inadequately to corticosteroids or cannot tolerate tapering them, Novartis said on Tuesday [1]. The indication is already approved in the EU [1].

PMR causes acute pain and stiffness in the shoulders, neck and hips, mainly in people over 50; Novartis puts the number living with it in the US at about 966,000 [1]. Steroids are the standard treatment, but up to 40% of patients relapse in the first year, and long-term use raises the risk of osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease and diabetes [1].

In the Phase III REPLENISH trial across 27 countries, every arm was given a 24-week steroid taper [1]. At week 52, 40.5% of patients on Cosentyx 150 mg were in sustained remission against 20.5% on placebo [1]. Desk arithmetic: 20 percentage points. Serious adverse events ran at 15.9% against 14.2%; hypersensitivity reactions (14% v 9%) and urinary tract infections (13% v 7%) were more common on the drug, and one patient died of cryptococcal meningitis [1].

These are the company's figures from a published trial, with the costs listed alongside the benefits. "Getting out of bed, getting dressed, lifting your arms," as one patient advocate put it, are what the endpoint measures [1]. If a parent or neighbour is in their third year of steroids, this is a question for the rheumatologist, not a headline.
