The European Commission on Wednesday proposed a new €45 billion loan to Ukraine to help fill a funding gap of €70 billion for next year, Reuters reports [1]. Up to €25 billion could go on military aid and up to €20 billion on general budget support [1]. The Commission would borrow the money on international markets on behalf of the EU [1].

The repayment clause is the unusual part. Ursula von der Leyen said Ukraine would only need to repay once Russia had paid reparations for the war's damage [1]. The loan would come on top of the €90 billion that EU leaders agreed at the end of last year for 2026 and 2027 [1].

Desk arithmetic: €45 billion of €70 billion is about 64%, leaving roughly €25 billion that, in von der Leyen's words, Kyiv's other partners will be expected to provide [1].

Nothing is settled yet. The proposal needs approval from member states, and three of them, Hungary, the Czech Republic and Slovakia, opted out of the last loan [1]. Von der Leyen said she was confident all would back this one, arguing that even governments wary of military aid could count their share against civilian needs [1]. Volodymyr Zelenskiy asked for "swift adoption" by the Parliament and the Council [1].

Loans written against a future reparations bill are a bet on institutions outlasting a war. That is the kind of bet Europe is built to make; the arithmetic still has to pass 27 capitals.
