Canada and China agreed in Shenzhen on Tuesday to extend and expand a preliminary agreement on trade issues, opening more of the Chinese market to Canadian farmers and seafood harvesters and securing tariff relief for about C$400 million ($280 million) of exports, according to a statement from Mark Carney's office reported by Nikkei Asia [1]. The statement says Canada "has recalibrated our relationship with China -- strategically, pragmatically, and decisively" [1].

On Wednesday Carney told APEC business leaders that Canada was taking "practical steps" with China on financial cooperation, including for times of stress [1]. In January Carney lowered Canada's tariff wall on Chinese electric vehicles by setting up an import quota [1].

C$400 million is small against a G7 economy's trade. It is also exactly the size of thing that gets done: a list, a quota, a renewal date.
