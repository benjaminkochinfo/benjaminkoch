Copper futures on the London Metal Exchange fell as much as 3.6% on Wednesday to $12,765 a tonne, the lowest since April, extending what Investing.com described as the longest run of weekly losses in two years [1]. The pan-European mining index fell 2.3%; Poland's KGHM lost around 4% and Boliden nearly 3% [1].

The reasons given are worth reading in order. Reuters linked the slide to concerns about AI investment and a stronger dollar [2]. Investing.com added weaker Chinese economic data and noted that the copper sell-off intensified after the recent weakness in technology stocks reduced appetite for a metal used in pipes and wires [1]. Zinc fell 1.3%; nickel and aluminium each dropped more than 2% [1].

In London the bill was visible by the afternoon: around 14:40 CET Anglo American was down about 4.76% and Rio Tinto about 3.14% [3]. Gold futures fell about 2% to around $3,786 an ounce, a level Trading Economics called the lowest in over a year, as expectations of Fed rate rises lifted the dollar [3].

Here is the quiet contradiction of the day. A chip maker's guidance is a promise about future data centres; copper is one of the things those centres are built from. On Wednesday the market believed the first and doubted the second. Neither is a verdict. Both are prices, and prices change their minds. Nothing here is investment advice.
