The European Commission has imposed definitive anti-dumping duties on certain cold-rolled flat steel from five countries: 9.5% for India, 27.9% for Japan, 20.7% to 27% for Taiwan, 3% to 7.8% for Türkiye and 16% for Vietnam [1]. An investigation found the products were entering the EU at dumped prices, the Commission says [1].

Cold-rolled flat steel goes into most areas of manufacturing. The EU market for it is worth some €23.2 billion, with domestic production in 12 member states employing more than 8,600 people [1]. Imports from the five countries named were €282 million from India, €240 million from Türkiye, €180 million from Japan, €345 million from Taiwan and €125 million from Vietnam [1]. Desk arithmetic: together about €1.17 billion, roughly 5% of the market's value.

The quieter news is procedural. For the first time, the Commission established a "Particular Market Situation" in an investigation: in India, because government measures affected the domestic price of iron ore; in Taiwan, because Chinese overcapacity pushed cheap Chinese steel into the Taiwanese market and depressed prices there [1]. Where such a situation exists, the Commission may construct a "normal value" rather than rely on domestic sales prices [1]. It calls the case "of systemic importance for trade defence" [1].

That is the point to keep. Trade defence is usually argued in percentages. This one changes the method of counting, and methods outlast rates.
