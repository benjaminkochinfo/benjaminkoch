Published on Wednesday in Nature Communications, a study by researchers at Peking University and colleagues followed 376,713 participants in the United Kingdom and 3,973 in the United States [1]. Cardiometabolic diseases, the authors note, cause over 30% of global deaths [1]. Their question: what does depression or anxiety add for people who already have one of them?

The first finding runs one way. Each additional cardiometabolic disease was associated with a 28.5% higher risk of depression or anxiety in the UK group and 33.4% in the US group [1]. The second runs the other way. Against people with neither cardiometabolic disease nor depression or anxiety, the hazard ratio for death from any cause in the UK participants rose from 1.200 to 2.389 among those with a single cardiometabolic disease once a mental disorder was identified during follow-up [1]. With two cardiometabolic diseases it rose from 1.754 to 2.918 [1]. Desk arithmetic: for one disease, the ratio roughly doubles (2.389 is 1.99 times 1.200).

One result runs against intuition. Among people with cardiometabolic disease, the link between depression or anxiety and death was stronger in those with higher socioeconomic status [1]. The US replication showed "broadly similar patterns" [1]. The authors do not claim to have shown cause; their conclusion is about "prognostic value" [1].

Now the annex, the part about what to do with it. An association in a cohort is not proof that treating anxiety would lengthen anyone's life. But it is a strong argument against an old administrative habit: the heart clinic and the mental-health service with separate waiting lists, separate budgets and separate files. A questionnaire at the diabetes check-up costs minutes. A referral pathway that actually works costs a commissioner's attention. Neither makes headlines.

If you live with one of these conditions, the takeaway is modest and practical. Low mood or constant worry after a diagnosis is worth raising with the same doctor who measures your blood pressure. It belongs in the same file.
