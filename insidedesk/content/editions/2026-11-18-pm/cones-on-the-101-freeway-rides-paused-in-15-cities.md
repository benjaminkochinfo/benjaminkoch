California's Department of Motor Vehicles has barred Waymo's robotaxis from the state's freeways, TechCrunch reports [1]. The order was issued on 11 November but only became public on Tuesday [1]. Desk arithmetic: six days passed between the order and the public hearing about it. The DMV told TechCrunch it acted after three Waymo vehicles drove into an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame, and after a separate incident involving California Highway Patrol vehicles on Highway 280 in San Francisco [1].

The regulator's wording is unusually blunt. It found that Waymo's driverless freeway operations "pose an imminent hazard to public safety" because of "recurring incursions" into active construction zones, "despite a prior restriction and corrective actions" that followed similar incidents on 18 May 2026 [1]. The restriction stays until Waymo can show "effective corrective actions" [1].

Waymo went further than it was told. It has stopped its robotaxis using freeways in every market it serves, including those outside California; it runs commercial service in 15 US cities [1]. A spokesperson said the encounters on the 101 "did not involve any injuries or collisions", and that the service keeps running on surface streets, "serving over half a million trips each week" [1]. Freeway rides only began in late 2025, paused abruptly in May and came back in July after a software update aimed at construction zones [1].

Read it as a working system, not a scandal. A permit holder made the same kind of mistake twice, the licensing office used the power it has, and the company complied, then widened the pause beyond the state. No one was hurt. That is how dull institutions are meant to work: a written condition, a record of incidents and a lever that can be pulled without a court case. A road crew's cones are not glamorous data, but they are the test that matters.

For you, if you are a rider, the change is longer trips across the Peninsula, which freeway access had cut from 45 minutes or more [1]. For everyone else, the useful number is not the half-million weekly trips. It is the date the DMV signs off, and what it asks to see first.
