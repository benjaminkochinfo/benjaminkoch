The US Food and Drug Administration on Tuesday approved Lembaz (adrabetadex), the first treatment shown to help people with the severe, infantile-onset form of Niemann-Pick disease type C live longer [1]. It is approved to reduce the risk of death in adults and children weighing at least 7 kg whose neurological symptoms began before the age of six [1].

NPC is an inherited lysosomal storage disorder affecting an estimated 1 in 150,000 people: cells cannot process cholesterol and other fats properly, which build up, particularly in the brain, and take away movement, coordination and the ability to swallow [1]. Two therapies were already approved, the FDA notes, but neither had been shown to reduce the risk of death, and both carry age and weight limits that exclude the youngest patients [1].

The evidence is the survival of 36 treated patients across three clinical studies and an expanded-access programme, compared with a matched external control group [1]. Treated patients had a 55% lower risk of death; median survival was 8.5 years against 4.9 [1]. Desk arithmetic: a gap of 3.6 years in median survival. The benefit was seen whether or not patients also took miglustat, which is used off-label [1].

The price is paid in procedure and side effects. A trained clinician injects the drug into the spinal canal every two weeks [1]. The label carries a boxed warning for hearing impairment, including permanent loss, and requires a full hearing evaluation before treatment and regularly during it [1].

Be sceptical of the design, not of the decision. Thirty-six patients and a historical comparison would not carry a drug for a common illness. For a disease of one in 150,000, that is the record there is, and the regulator has said plainly how it was built. What matters now is the dull part: audiology appointments booked, every fortnight's injection logged, and the survival curve kept up to date after approval.
