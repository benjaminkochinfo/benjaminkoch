**The podium.** Britain's inflation rate rose to 3.6% in October, from 3.5%, the ONS said on Wednesday morning [2]. The Treasury's answer came from Emma Reynolds, chief secretary: the conflict in the Middle East and Russia's invasion of Ukraine "have pushed up costs around the world", and the household energy payment "is providing £100 directly into 20 million bank accounts in January, which will help to give families breathing space now" [1]. The Bank of England added its own line: inflation "is expected to rise further by the middle of next year due to cost pressures already in the pipeline" [4].

**The print.** The same release, as the Guardian read it, put the average price of diesel up 7p a litre to 199.5p, the highest on records going back to 1996, and petrol up 5.2p to 174.7p, a four-year high [1]. Motor fuels rose at an annual rate of 33.2% [1]. Transport, particularly fuel, made the largest upward contribution to the monthly change in the CPI [2]. On the same morning Cornwall Insight's final forecast put January's price cap for a typical household at £2,166 a year, up 26%, or £443 on the current cap [3]. Cornwall says the £100 payment is a one-off outside the cap and therefore not in its forecast [3].

**The gap.** Desk arithmetic: £100 is about 23% of Cornwall's forecast £443 annual increase. The payment is real money and arrives on a date. The price cap is a rate that runs every day the heating is on. Neither number is wrong; they are simply different kinds of number, and only one of them repeats. Not everything is pressure: food inflation slowed to 0.9%, the lowest since September 2021, and core CPI held at 2.8% [1][2].

**For you.** If you pay a British energy bill, read the unit rate and the standing charge, not the headline payment. Cornwall's forecast is 32.54p per kWh of electricity and 10.90p for gas, before Ofgem sets the real figure [3]. Nothing here is investment advice.
