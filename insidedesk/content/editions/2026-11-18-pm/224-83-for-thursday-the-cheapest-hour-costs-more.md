Thursday's day-ahead auction has cleared, and SMARD lists the price for delivery on 19 November at €224.83 per megawatt hour on average for Germany and Luxembourg [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €78.58, or about 54%, above Wednesday's €146.25, and the highest daily average since Wednesday 11 November, when it was €316.65 [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-18pm]]

**The floor moved, not just the peak.** Last night this desk pointed out that Wednesday's discount sat almost entirely between midnight and lunch. Thursday takes that discount away. The cheapest hour on Thursday is midnight to 01:00 at €169.92 [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is €23.67 above Wednesday's whole-day average, and the seven hours from midnight to 07:00 average €184.92, against €99.01 for the same hours on Wednesday.

**The day has a shape you know.** The dearest hour is 08:00 to 09:00 at €276.45; the price eases to about €230 around lunchtime and climbs back above €260 between 16:00 and 19:00 [1]. In the 18:00 hour, when households cook and cars plug in, Thursday's price is €261.84 against €212.43 on Wednesday [1]. Desk arithmetic: 16 of Thursday's 24 hours clear above €200.

**What the data can and cannot say.** The grid operators' forecast had onshore wind producing about 970.6 GWh on Wednesday, up from about 570.5 GWh on Tuesday, which fits a cheap Wednesday [2]. Their forecast for Thursday was not on SMARD when we read it at 16:30 CET, so this desk will not name the cause of Thursday's jump [2]. A price list is a fact; the reason waits for the generation data.

**For you.** On a dynamic tariff, Thursday offers no night-time bargain: the best hour is still around €170 at the exchange, and the gap between the cheapest and the dearest hour is about €107, not the €132 of Wednesday [1]. A timer still helps; it just helps less. On a fixed tariff, none of this reaches this month's bill. Nothing here is investment advice.
