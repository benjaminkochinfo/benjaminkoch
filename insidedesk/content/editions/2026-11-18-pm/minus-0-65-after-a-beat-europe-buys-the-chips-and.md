At 15:42 CET the DAX stood 0.65% lower at 25,876.50 points, 168.87 below Tuesday's close of 26,045.37 (Desk arithmetic) [1]. It had opened 0.14% higher at 26,082.63, touched 26,139.16, and then drifted down to a low of 25,858.79 [1]. On the week it is 3.35% lower so far [1]. These are snapshot figures, not the settled close; Xetra trading ends at 17:30.

That is the plain shape of the first full European session after Nvidia's results: a small gain in the morning, given back by the afternoon. Last night's invoice was good. It was simply not the only bill on the table.

[[chart:europe-wednesday-18pm]]

**Read the chart in two halves.** The top half is the Nvidia trade. Siemens Energy led the DAX, up 2.82%, with Infineon up 1.57% [1]. Earlier in the afternoon dpa-AFX counted Siemens Energy and Infineon among the tech-linked names that recovered, alongside Siltronic, Aixtron and Suss Microtec in the MDax; Siltronic gained more than 7% on a Berenberg buy recommendation [2]. Across the region, European tech stocks rose 1.3% and ASML 3.6%, Reuters reported shortly before 14:00 CET [3]. Nvidia's own Frankfurt-listed shares were up about 4.68% around 14:40 CET [4].

The bottom half is everything else. SAP fell 2.82% and Deutsche Bank 2.38%, the latter still the most traded DAX share by volume after Tuesday's leaked-files reports [1]. Commerzbank lost 2.20% and Deutsche Telekom 2.18% [1]. The chip rally did not reach software, and it did not reach the banks.

**The metal said something else.** The day's sharpest European move came from miners. By 09:08 CET Reuters had the sector down 3% at a near nine-month low, with copper prices unwinding on concerns about AI investment and a stronger dollar; Glencore fell 3.5% and Aurubis 2.9% [5]. Around 14:40 CET Anglo American was down about 4.76% and Rio Tinto about 3.14% [4]. There is an irony worth keeping: the same AI build-out that lifted the chip makers is the story copper is now pricing more cautiously.

**The index level.** Around 14:40 CET the Stoxx 600 was at about 648.68, down 0.31% from Tuesday's 650.72, after Tuesday's fall, its biggest one-day drop since 20 March [4][5]. The FTSE 100 was down about 0.54%, the CAC 40 about 0.30%, the Euro Stoxx 50 about 0.15% [4].

**The paper that did not move.** Two inflation prints landed before lunch. Eurostat confirmed euro area inflation at 4.0% for October, up from 3.8%, with energy up 21.0% on the year and the index excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco at 2.6% [7]. In Britain the ONS reported CPI at 3.6%, up from 3.5%, with core CPI at 2.8%, little changed [8]. British government bond yields fell, Reuters said, because the underlying picture was somewhat softer than expected [3]. The energy bill is still being passed through, one invoice at a time.

**Across the Atlantic.** New York opened split. At the open the Dow was down 0.26% at 50,627.36, while the S&P 500 rose 0.21% to 7,355.69 and the Nasdaq Composite 0.65% to 24,486.18 [6]. Reuters reported that Nvidia now expects revenue to grow 75% in its next fiscal year, up from a previous 70% [6]. Brent was 0.7% lower at $92.70 a barrel in Reuters' report at 13:52 CET, after data showing higher US crude and fuel inventories; the 10-year Treasury yield was 5.095% [3].

**What it means for you.** Wednesday did not hand back Tuesday's loss; it sorted the holdings. One company's quarter is evidence for its suppliers, not a pardon for everyone else.

The next exam is already scheduled. Anthropic is due to price its shares later on Wednesday ahead of a debut on Thursday, which Reuters said could value the company at as much as $1.4 trillion [3]. A listing price is not a business result. It is a negotiation written down. Nothing here is investment advice.
