Donald Trump landed in Shenzhen at 09:45 CET on Wednesday, and Xi Jinping met him on the tarmac, returning the rare airport welcome Trump gave him in Washington in September, Nikkei Asia reports [1]. In the evening Xi and Peng Liyuan hosted APEC leaders at a banquet, where Trump and Vladimir Putin were seen shaking hands as Xi looked on, the eve of the trilateral meeting the White House has said will happen on Thursday [1]. Xi was also pictured shaking hands with Japan's Sanae Takaichi, after more than a year of frosty ties [1].

**The speech asked for results.** In his banquet welcome Xi called on leaders to work toward "concrete results" when they meet on Thursday morning, according to Xinhua as quoted by Nikkei [1]. Earlier, Putin told business leaders that "economic domination and coercion are now a thing of the past", and Malaysia's Anwar Ibrahim said: "You can't say in one voice [that you're] promoting trade and investment, but only to your country" [1]. Speeches are free. That is why this desk counts them last.

**The line item.** The American Soybean Association wants China's remaining 10% retaliatory tariff on US soybeans removed; soybeans were left out of a September framework that lowered tariffs on $60 billion of goods [2]. "This retaliatory tariff puts U.S. soybeans at a distinct disadvantage compared to South American competitors," ASA president Scott Metzger wrote to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and trade representative Jamieson Greer [2]. By the time of Nikkei's last update, at 15:51 CET, no outcome on that tariff had been reported [1].

**Everyone else's bilaterals.** South Korea's Lee Jae Myung met Xi; according to Seoul's national security adviser, Xi said he was willing to support efforts to ease tensions on the Korean Peninsula, "including a North Korea-U.S. dialogue" [1]. Xinhua's account made no mention of a possible Trump-Kim meeting [1]. Two readouts of one meeting is normal diplomacy. It is also a reminder to wait for the third: the text.

**For you.** A handshake in Shenzhen does not change what a European importer pays this week. What would show up, eventually, in prices is a tariff schedule changed in writing, and the first place to look for American farmers is Thursday's export sales report [2].
