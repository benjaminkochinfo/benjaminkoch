North Rhine-Westphalia has had three days of steady rain, and on Thursday it showed. According to the German Weather Service (DWD), 30 to 50 litres a square metre have fallen over the past three days, DerWesten reported in its live blog [1]. Cellars have flooded, trees have come down and the first motorway crashes have been blamed on standing water [1].

The morning commute in Aachen stopped not on the tracks but under them. At 07:37 CET the main station was at a standstill: the trains were ready, but the pedestrian tunnel to the platforms had filled with water, so passengers could not reach them [1]. The fire brigade finished pumping shortly before 08:00 and trains resumed soon after, with delays expected to linger, Deutsche Bahn said [1].

Essen's fire brigade counted 46 rain-related call-outs between 16:00 on Wednesday and 08:00 on Thursday: flooded streets, underpasses full of water and a fallen tree [1]. The common cause was mundane. Street drains blocked by leaves were often behind the larger pools on the roads, and crews repeatedly closed stretches briefly to clear them [1]. At one medical practice, rainwater pushed in from the entrance and the electricity had to be switched off for a while [1].

On the A2 towards Dortmund, between Vlotho West and Herford Ost, two crashes followed each other within a short time; a 21-year-old's car overturned after aquaplaning and two drivers were lightly injured [1]. On the A45 near Dortmund a car skidded on the wet overtaking lane on Wednesday evening and hit a lorry, which broke through the central barrier; the carriageway towards Hanover was closed for hours [1].

It is not over. The DWD's continuous-rain warning for the Aachen area runs until 22:00 CET on Friday, with 40 to 60 litres a square metre expected and up to 70 where clouds pile against the hills [1]. In the Ruhr, gusts of up to 70 km/h were forecast until 20:00 CET on Thursday [1].
