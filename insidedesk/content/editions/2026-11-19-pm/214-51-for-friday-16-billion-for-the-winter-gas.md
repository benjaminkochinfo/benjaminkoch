Friday's power is settled. For delivery on Friday 20 November, the German-Luxembourg day-ahead auction averages €214.51 per megawatt hour, according to Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts, which republishes the Federal Network Agency's market data [1]. The cheapest quarter-hour comes at 01:00, at €165.55; the dearest at 15:45, at €259.55 [1]. The morning hump is still there, with 08:00 to 09:00 averaging €250.96, but this time the afternoon matches it: every hour from 15:00 to 19:00 averages above €244 [1].

Desk arithmetic: Friday is 4.6% cheaper than Thursday's €224.83 [2], and the gap between its cheapest and dearest quarter-hour is €94.00. On SMARD's own figures, the first 19 delivery days of November average €239.01, about 41% above October's €169.48 [2].

## The study that names the culprit

On Thursday afternoon Reuters reported a study by the energy analysts Montel, commissioned by the Greens' parliamentary group, that puts a winter-sized number on the gap [3]. The sharp rise in gas prices could push the wholesale cost of the electricity Germany consumes this winter up by around €16 billion [3]. In Montel's model calculations, the average exchange price from October 2026 to February 2027 would be about 68% higher than at last winter's gas prices: just over €166 per megawatt hour instead of about €99 [3]. Montel and Reuters are explicit that this is not a price forecast [3]. November's European gas reference price stood at just over €85 per megawatt hour, more than double a year earlier [3].

The mechanism is old and unglamorous. On the exchange, the most expensive power station still needed sets the price, and in winter that is often a gas plant; the more wind and sun there is, the weaker the gas price's grip [3]. "Expensive gas ruins the power price too," said the Greens' Michael Kellner, who called the electricity-tax cut "a joke" by comparison [3].

## The awkward bit of bookkeeping

Here is the contrarian footnote. The coalition plans to fund its 2027 electricity-tax cut, worth a little over €5 billion in lost revenue, through lower spending on green-power support [3]. That support shrinks precisely when exchange prices are high [3]. The same expensive gas that lifts your bill is helping to pay for the relief on it. How much of any of this reaches you depends on your contract [3]. Check its price-adjustment clause before you check the headlines. Nothing here is investment advice.
