RWE and Japan's Kawasaki Heavy Industries have put a 34-megawatt gas turbine into trial operation in Lingen, Lower Saxony, on the site of the Emsland gas power station [1]. It started on 6 November and, according to RWE, is Europe's largest fully hydrogen-capable gas turbine [1]. It can burn natural gas, hydrogen or any mix in between, from zero to 100% hydrogen [1]. It began on natural gas, with hydrogen to be added step by step [1].

The point is the Dunkelflaute, the dark, still stretches when wind and solar deliver little for days and something has to run regardless of the weather [1]. The hydrogen for the trial comes from electrolysers right next door, part of the GET H2 Nukleus project, powered according to RWE by renewable electricity [1]. The field test is planned to last several months and will check combustion stability, durability and whether emission limits are met; burning hydrogen can also produce nitrogen oxides, so the system uses water or steam to curb them [1].

Two dull caveats do the real work. Hydrogen is currently much more expensive than natural gas, which is why the flexible fuel mix matters economically [1]. And 34 megawatts says nothing about how long a plant can cover a lull: that depends on the stored fuel, and turning power into hydrogen and back loses usable energy, as the Federal Environment Agency points out [1]. A turbine is a promise. A full tank is a policy.
