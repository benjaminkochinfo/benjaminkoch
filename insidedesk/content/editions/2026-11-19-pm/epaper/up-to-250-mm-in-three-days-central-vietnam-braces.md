Vietnam's National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting has warned that rivers from southern Quang Tri to Quang Ngai could flood between 19 and 22 November, DTiNews reported on Thursday [1]. River levels in Hue are expected to reach warning levels one to two, and those in southern Quang Tri, Danang and Quang Ngai around level one; the flood-disaster risk is rated at level one [1].

The warning is about what is coming, not yet what has happened. On Thursday river levels were still below warning level one [1]. Between 07:00 and 15:00 local time, 54.2 mm of rain fell at Kim Son reservoir in Ha Tinh and 35.8 mm at Quang Minh in Quang Tri [1]. From Thursday night to Saturday, forecasters expect 90 to 180 mm across Quang Tri, Danang and eastern areas from Quang Ngai to Gia Lai, locally more than 250 mm, with bursts of over 80 mm in three hours that can bring flash floods and landslides [1].

At the same time a strengthening cold air mass is spreading south. Northern Vietnam is expected to stay at 14 to 17°C, mountain areas at 11 to 14°C and higher elevations below 9°C; the forecasters warn the cold could affect livestock and crops [1]. Bach Long Vy island recorded gusts of force seven [1].

For low-lying riverside towns the risk is to transport, fish farms, fields and daily life [1]. A level-one warning issued two days ahead is not drama; it is the machinery working. The test is whether the reservoir operators, district officials and boat owners use the two days.
