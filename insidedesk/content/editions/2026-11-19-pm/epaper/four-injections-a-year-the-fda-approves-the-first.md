The US Food and Drug Administration has approved Regeneron's Livsayis (cemdisiran) for adults with generalised myasthenia gravis who test positive for anti-acetylcholine-receptor antibodies, the commonest form of the disease, the company said overnight [1]. Myasthenia gravis is a rare autoimmune illness in which antibodies disrupt the signal between nerve and muscle, bringing weakness that can affect speech, swallowing, vision and breathing [1].

Livsayis is a small interfering RNA that lowers circulating levels of the complement protein C5, a driver of the damage [1]. It is the first siRNA drug approved for the disease and the only approved treatment given under the skin four times a year [1].

The evidence is a single phase 3 trial, NIMBLE. At 24 weeks, scores on a daily-activities scale improved by 4.5 points on the drug against 2.2 on placebo; 76.6% of patients improved by at least three points, against 44.1% [1]. On a clinician-scored muscle test the gain was 4.2 points against 1.5 [1].

The label carries a boxed warning for serious meningococcal infections, as other C5-blocking medicines do, and the drug is available only through a restricted safety programme [1]. The most common side effects were injection-site reactions, viral infections and urinary tract infections [1].

For European patients the calendar matters more than the headline: an application is under review in the EU, with a decision expected in the second half of 2027, according to Regeneron [1]. Four injections a year instead of frequent infusions is the kind of change that shows up not in a chart but in a diary.
