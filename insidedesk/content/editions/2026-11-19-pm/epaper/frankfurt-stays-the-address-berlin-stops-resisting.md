UniCredit and the German government said on Thursday they had agreed terms for the Italian bank's takeover of Commerzbank, valued at €41 billion [1]. In a letter to Lars Klingbeil, chief executive Andrea Orcel promised to keep the headquarters in Frankfurt and to keep supporting the Mittelstand [1]. Klingbeil said the commitments "align with the core concerns of the federal government" and that it now supports "a swift process and an orderly transition of control" [1]. Berlin still holds around 12% from the financial-crisis bailout; the EU approved the deal on Monday [1].

Commerzbank shares rose 1.4% in early trade [2]. UniCredit hopes for final regulatory approvals this month [1]. If the deal completes, the proof will be in the loan books, not the letter.
