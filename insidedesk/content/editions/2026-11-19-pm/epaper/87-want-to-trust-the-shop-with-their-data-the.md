The European Commission's Digital Omnibus would simplify data-protection rules including the GDPR; Parliament and member states are now debating it [1]. On Thursday the vzbv said the proposals mainly create legal uncertainty that big platforms can exploit, and, with other European consumer and digital-rights groups, sent an open letter to the European Parliament [1]. "Keine weitreichenden Ausnahmen, keine Schutzlücken," said its head, Ramona Pop: no sweeping exemptions, no gaps in protection [1].

Its own survey, run for it in October 2025 among 1,000 internet users aged 16 and over, found 87% think it very or fairly important to be able to trust a company with their personal data before using its services [1]. The constructive bit: the vzbv wants machine-readable privacy signals, so you set your cookie choice once and sites must respect it [1]. Fewer banners, same protection. That is a simplification worth the name.
