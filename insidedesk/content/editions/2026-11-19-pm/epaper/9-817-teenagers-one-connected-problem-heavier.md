Researchers at Texas A&M's School of Public Health analysed 9,817 Americans aged 12 to 17 from the 2023 National Survey on Drug Use and Health [1]. The more heavily teens drank, and the more different ways they used cannabis (smoking, vaping, edibles), the higher their chances of major depression; girls, older teens and multiracial teens were more likely to be depressed regardless of substance use [1]. The study is in the Journal of Substance Abuse [1].

It is a cross-sectional snapshot, so it cannot say which comes first [1]. The recommendation is practical anyway: lead author Sunghyun Chung says parents, doctors and schools should "screen teens for both issues together" [1]. One questionnaire instead of two is a small change in a clinic. It may catch a teenager who would otherwise fall between two waiting lists.
