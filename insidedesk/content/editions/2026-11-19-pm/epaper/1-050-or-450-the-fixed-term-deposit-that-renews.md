A fixed-term deposit is a promise with an end date. What happens on that date is set by the contract, not by the saver's memory. Some banks pay the money and interest to a reference account; others offer to reinvest; some contracts renew automatically if the customer does not decide in time [1]. The original rate need not apply to the new term: the bank can use the rate it offers on the day of reinvestment [1].

The consumer site Verbraucherschutzforum puts a number on it, with a freely chosen example rather than any bank's terms. €30,000 for twelve months at 3.5% earns €1,050 before tax; rolled over for another year at 1.5%, it earns €450 [1]. That is €600 less, and the money is locked again, because classic Festgeld normally cannot be cancelled early [1].

The detail most people miss: an instruction or notice deadline can expire before the maturity date, so reacting on the day may be too late [1]. Deposit insurance, up to €100,000 per depositor per bank in the EU, protects the money, not the rate [1].

The moment is apt. Verivox reported on Thursday that many banks are raising overnight rates, with offers up to 4.25%, mostly for new customers [2]. Find your maturity date, then the notice date before it, and put both in the calendar. Nothing here is investment advice.
