The Shenzhen declaration has four points: UN reform, more refined petroleum, the immunity of state officials, and a dialogue on nuclear risks [2]. Ukraine is not one of them [2]. It arrived instead in the press conferences.

Trump said he and Putin had already had a "long discussion" on the war, and that Putin "and I hope the other side, would like to see an end to that war" [1]. Putin said Ukraine and Europe were not yet ready to make compromises, and that Russia had invited EU representatives to Moscow to understand Europe's position better [1]. Addressing comparisons with Yalta, he said: "Nobody divided up the world at Yalta" [1]. And: "When it comes to today's conflicts, the realities on the ground will have to be accepted" [1].

The BBC noted that while the talks were under way, and as Putin spoke of being ready for peace, Kyiv was under attack from Russian drones [1]. Putin also promised a "far more painful" response to Ukrainian strikes on the Crimea bridge [1].

Point three of the declaration says the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction" [2]. Read next to the Yalta line, it is a text about who is answerable to whom. For Europe, the useful habit is the institutional one: an invitation is not a negotiation until there is an agenda, a venue and a mandate on paper.
