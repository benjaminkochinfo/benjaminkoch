The Philadelphia Fed's manufacturing index fell to 6.3 in November, a six-month low and well short of the consensus forecast of 20.0 [1]. New orders came almost to a halt, although shipments surged and employment kept growing [1]. The awkward line was prices: the prices-paid index climbed to 58.2 and prices received rose to 47.0, a sign that firms keep passing higher input costs to customers [1]. More than half expected cost shifts across their industry, and most expected competitors to raise prices [1].

Slower orders with faster prices is the combination central banks like least. It arrived on the same morning as US jobless claims of 203,000 [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
