When you cancel a Debeka life or pension insurance early, the insurer pays out the surrender value and, besides the usual cancellation costs, deducts a charge that depends on the capital markets [1]. The consumer federation vzbv and the Hamburg consumer centre consider the clause behind it opaque and therefore invalid, and the vzbv has brought a class action to have a court say so [1]. That is the consumer advocates' position; the court has not yet ruled.

The figures are Debeka's own. According to its annual reports, 241,994 contracts ended early through cancellation between 2022 and 2024 [1]. The Hamburg centre believes the market-linked deduction has been used since 2008; people who took out a policy after 2007 and cancelled from mid-2022 may be affected, and the deduction is not necessarily shown separately on the statement [1].

The date that matters is 31 December 2026. Anyone who paid such a charge in 2023 and wants to sue individually must do so by then, or the claim will in many cases be time-barred [1]. An individual case will most likely need an expert report, which costs time and money [1]. The alternative is to register, free and without cost risk, in the class-action register at the Federal Office of Justice, which also suspends the limitation period while the case runs [1]. The vzbv offers an online check at sammelklagen.de [1].

The unexciting advice: find the settlement letter first. A deadline is only frightening when the paperwork is in the attic.
