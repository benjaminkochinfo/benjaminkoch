At 16:34 CET the DAX stood at 25,594.83 points, down 0.55% or 141 points [7]. In the morning it had climbed as high as 25,945.52; at 13:59 it was still only 0.10% lower, and by mid-afternoon it was trading near the day's low [1][7]. These are snapshot figures, not the settled close; Xetra trading ends at 17:30. One week ago the index touched a record of 27,001; it has since fallen by almost 5% [2]. The MDax was down 0.80% at 29,640.91 at 16:35 CET, the TecDAX 0.30% lower and the Euro Stoxx 50 down 0.24% at 6,139.55 [8][9][10].

[[chart:europe-thursday-19pm]]

The falling line hides a clean split. Chip makers rose on the eve of Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, though they gave back part of it: Infineon was up 2.78% at 13:59 and 0.86% by 16:31, Siemens Energy 1.23%, Aixtron 3.01% in the MDax [1][7][8]. The other side of the ledger was older and duller money. Henkel's preference shares were down 2.05% and SAP 4.00%, extending its losing run to a fourth day [2][7]. Desk arithmetic: the four biggest risers in the chart averaged a gain of about 2.2%; the index still sat below Wednesday's close.

The largest single move had nothing to do with chips. BASF said it had ended its efforts to take over Evonik, citing the rejection of two non-binding cash offers [11][12]. Evonik's shares fell by as much as 23% in the afternoon and were down 16.44% at €18.20 at 16:35 CET [8][11].

Banks had their own reason. Commerzbank rose 1.4% and UniCredit 1.8% in early trade after Berlin and the Italian bank agreed on the main points of the takeover [3]; by 16:31 Commerzbank had slipped 0.13% into the red, while Deutsche Bank was still up 1.27% [7]. In Milan, UniCredit was up 2.1% and the index 0.5% at 13:35 CET, the best in Europe; London was down 0.4% [6]. Switzerland lagged because its heavy, defensive names fell, Novartis among them [1][4].

New York opened in the same two minds. At 16:00 CET the Nasdaq Composite was 1.46% higher at 24,787.73 and the S&P 500 up 0.75% at 7,378.46, while the Dow was flat at 50,191.91 [5]. Walmart raised its full-year outlook; jobless claims edged up to 203,000 [5].

A record listing is one invoice, paid once. The quarterly reports of the companies that sell it chips are the invoices that come back. Nothing here is investment advice.
