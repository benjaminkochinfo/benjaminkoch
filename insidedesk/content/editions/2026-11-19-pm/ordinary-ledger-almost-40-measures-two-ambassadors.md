On Wednesday the federal cabinet adopted Germany's first national financial-education strategy: almost 40 measures, with more information on investing on the stock market, on pensions and on protection against financial fraud [2]. The former World Cup winner Philipp Lahm and the actor Tomomi Themann are to act as ambassadors [2]. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil (SPD) said financial education was a question of fairness [2].

The detail is thinner than the headline. According to Cash., the measures sit in seven fields, among them pensions, capital-market skills and entrepreneurial skills, and the programme is to run for about five years [1]. The September draft had spoken of more than 30 measures in the same fields [1]. A full official list was not available at Cash.'s deadline [1]. From 2027 a "Task Force Finanzbildung", chaired jointly by the finance ministry and the Bundesbank, is to steer it, with the Länder sending representatives [1].

## The question on Thursday

The consumer federation vzbv answered on Thursday, and its point is the dull, important one. "Entscheidend ist nun die Umsetzung," said Anke Wolf, head of its consumer-education team: what matters now is delivery [3]. If financial-industry companies may also offer courses under the strategy, she argued, it needs binding quality standards and an effective review of the material to rule out influence; "a voluntary code of conduct is not enough" [3]. She also said schools play too small a role in the strategy so far, although they are the most important place to reach children regardless of background [3].

The cooperative banks' BVR welcomed the decision and asked for measurable success criteria and attention to possible conflicts of interest [1]. On the conflicts, then, the bankers and the consumer advocates agree [1].

## What it means for you

Nothing changes in your account this week. But the next free webinar on pensions or ETFs may well carry the strategy's name, and the useful habit is the old one: look at who made it and what they sell. A course is a product too. Nothing here is investment advice.
