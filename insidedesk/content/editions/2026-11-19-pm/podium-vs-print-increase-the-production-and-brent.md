**The podium.** Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping met for 90 minutes in Shenzhen, their first trilateral talks, and issued a four-point declaration [2]. Point two is the one with a price attached: "The three countries plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations. They reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows" [1]. Before the meeting, Putin had said the conversation would touch on food and energy security [2].

**The print.** The same day's market report from Milan shows the barrel moving the other way; the report does not tie the move to Shenzhen, and neither does this desk. At 13:35 CET Brent stood at $92.60, up 0.6%, and WTI at $83.77, also up 0.6%; in Amsterdam the TTF gas contract rose 3.7% to €83.87 per megawatt-hour [3]. Europe's energy shares fell 0.5% and utilities were lower [3].

**The gap.** The declaration is a plan without numbers: no volumes, no refineries, no dates [1]. That does not make it worthless. It makes it a statement of intent, which markets price at what it is. The Bundesbank wrote on the same morning that, beyond crude prices, refining capacity in the Middle East and in Russia will matter for German fuel and heating-oil prices, and that upside risks to inflation dominate because of the conflicts in the Middle East [4]. A pledge on refined products that includes Russia is therefore relevant; it is also, by the Bundesbank's own list, one of the risks.

**For you.** If you heat with oil or fill a diesel tank, the number that reaches you is the pump and delivery price, not the communiqué. Watch the barrel and the TTF over the next weeks, not the photograph. Nothing here is investment advice.
