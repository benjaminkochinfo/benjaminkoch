**The podium.** Friedrich Merz opened his reply in the general debate on Wednesday unusually quietly. 'Der Prozess zur Aufstellung dieses Haushalts hat sicher keinen Schönheitspreis verdient', he said: the process of drawing up this budget certainly deserved no beauty prize [1]. He called the budget 'vertretbar', defensible, rejected the charge of a 'sozialer Kahlschlag', and warned that without a change of course every euro of federal revenue would soon go on welfare, interest and defence [1][2].

**The print.** The 2027 budget plans total spending of €560 billion and about €210 billion of new debt [2]. ZDF notes that Merz rejected new borrowing before the election and now answers for a budget that borrows around €210 billion in one year; he justified it with defence and infrastructure, 'leider über neue Schulden', unfortunately through new debt [1]. Desk arithmetic: €210 billion is about 37.5% of €560 billion, though part of the borrowing finances special funds outside the core budget.

**The gap.** The speech asked for goodwill; the opposition replied with words. The Left's Heidi Reichinnek spoke of social cuts and unlimited rearmament, the Greens' Britta Haßelmann called the budget unserious, and Tino Chrupalla spoke of an 'Ausgabensucht', an addiction to spending [2]. None of that changes the figure. What can is Friday's vote and the year that follows it.
