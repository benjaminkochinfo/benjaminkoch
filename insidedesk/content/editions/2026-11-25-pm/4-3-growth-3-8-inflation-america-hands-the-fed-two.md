Real GDP in the United States grew at an annual rate of 4.3% in the third quarter, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said on Wednesday, revised up 0.9 percentage points from the advance estimate of 3.4% [1]. The revision came mainly from investment, led by intellectual property products, notably software, and from consumer spending [1]. Real final sales to private domestic purchasers grew 5.8% [1].

At the same release time, October's personal income data showed the PCE price index up 0.3% on the month and 3.8% on a year earlier; excluding food and energy it was up 3.3% [2]. Personal income rose 0.7% and disposable income 0.8%, partly on one-off Medicare premium rebates, while real consumer spending was flat [2]. The saving rate was 4.1% [2].

The market reading was not kind. The market analyst Andreas Lipkow wrote that the figures painted a robust economy with persistent inflation pressure, and that the growth gave the Fed further arguments for raising rates [3]. That is one reading, not a forecast. The quieter point is in the revision: an economy whose statisticians find 0.9 points of extra growth, much of it software, is one where the AI spending argued about on trading screens is already showing up in the accounts [1].
