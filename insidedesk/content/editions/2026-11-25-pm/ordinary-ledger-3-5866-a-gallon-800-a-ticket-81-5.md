AAA expects 81.5 million Americans to travel at least 50 miles from home between Tuesday 24 and Monday 30 November, 0.1% fewer than last year's 81.6 million and the end of a three-year run of records [1]. Driving falls by 200,000 people to 72.9 million, which AAA puts down partly to this season's record petrol prices; August, September and October all set monthly records at the pump [1].

The ledger for Wednesday: the national average for a gallon of regular is $3.5866 [2]. The highest Thanksgiving Day average so far was $3.58, in 2022 [1][2]. On 19 November it was $3.72; a month before that $4.30, and a year before $3.09 [2]. Desk arithmetic: Wednesday's price is about 71 cents below mid-October's and still about 49 cents above last November's.

The air is where the bill moved most. Round-trip domestic flights booked for the holiday average $800, 17% more than last year, while flyers fall 0.2% to 5.96 million [1]. Buses, trains and cruises are the only mode growing, up 4.1% to 2.6 million [1]. People did not stop going home. They changed how they get there, which is what prices are for.
