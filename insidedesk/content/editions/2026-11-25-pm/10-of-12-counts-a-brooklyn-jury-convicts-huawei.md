A federal jury in Brooklyn found Huawei and three subsidiaries guilty on 10 of 12 counts on Tuesday, including bank and wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracies to steal trade secrets and launder money, the Justice Department said [1]. The jury acquitted the company of racketeering conspiracy, the most serious charge, and of conspiring to obstruct justice [1].

Over the 12-week trial, prosecutors said Huawei had run its Iran business through Skycom, a subsidiary it presented to banks as independent, and used false statements to obtain credit, loans and access to US dollar clearing [1]. The case began in 2018 with the detention in Canada of the finance chief, Meng Wanzhou; she was released in 2021 under a deferred prosecution agreement [1].

The verdict lands in a week when chips and China dominate Washington's technology debate. The attorney general, Todd Blanche, said on Wednesday that Nvidia was 'a good corporate citizen' and signalled no investigation of Jensen Huang [2]. One file closed in court, another declared closed at a press gaggle: you can judge which process carries more weight.
