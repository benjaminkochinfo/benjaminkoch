A Russian attack on Ukraine's power sector overnight damaged energy and gas infrastructure and cut electricity in five regions, Ukrainian officials said on Wednesday [1]. Strikes halted generation at a DTEK thermal plant and badly damaged its equipment, and caused a large fire at a Naftogaz gas production site in the Poltava region, stopping operations there [1]. In Odesa, home to nearly a million people, engineers are working to keep the lights on after attacks on substations [1].

The traffic runs both ways. Industrial sites in Syzran, in Russia's Samara region, came under Ukrainian drone attack overnight and one person was killed in the regional capital, local authorities said; Syzran and Samara host three major refineries [1]. At least 14 Russian refineries have been struck since the start of November, despite Donald Trump's request to Kyiv to stop, and Vladimir Putin has vowed retaliation [2].

In Berlin, the chancellor spoke the same morning of a Russia that 'eskaliert', escalates, in the Baltic, in Poland, on the Black Sea and in Germany [3]. The more useful German contribution is less quotable: transformers, generators and spare parts, delivered before the cold, not after.
