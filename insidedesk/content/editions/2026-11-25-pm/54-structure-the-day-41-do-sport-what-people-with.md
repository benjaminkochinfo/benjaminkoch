87% of people with depression use self-help measures to get through a depressive episode, according to the 2026 Deutschland-Barometer Depression of the Stiftung Deutsche Depressionshilfe, a representative survey of 5,323 adults aged 18 to 69 taken in September [1]. The leading measures are deliberately structuring the day (54%), becoming an expert on one's own illness (49%), a healthy lifestyle (49%), relaxation techniques (45%) and sport (41%) [1].

The reasons are telling. Most use self-help to contribute something to their own recovery (58%); one in three uses it to bridge the wait for a treatment place [1]. The classic measures are used more often and rated more helpful than digital offers [1]. More than a third (38%) now talk to an AI about mental health problems, well ahead of prescribed apps (13%) [1].

The foundation's chair, Ulrich Hegerl, is careful: self-help can 'keinesfalls ersetzen', in no way replace, drug or psychotherapeutic treatment, but there is good evidence for sport, relaxation techniques and prescribed apps [1]. The headline here is not the chatbot. It is that people already do the sensible things, and wait too long for the rest.
