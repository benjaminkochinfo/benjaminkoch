Germany has no shortage of flu vaccine, according to the health ministry, the Paul-Ehrlich-Institut and the pharmacists' federation ABDA. As of 8 November about 20.2 million doses were available, and the number of tested and released doses passed last year's total on 18 October and is now about 1.7 million higher than in 2025 [1]. 'Ein bundesweiter Engpass … ist derzeit nicht feststellbar', a ministry spokeswoman said: no nationwide shortage can be found [1].

The family doctors see it differently from the surgery. Nicola Buhlinger-Göpfarth, who chairs the Hausärztinnen- und Hausärzteverband, said vaccine had partly arrived 'viel zu spät' and not in the quantities ordered, with the biggest problems for the products for people over 60; practices phone round pharmacies and move appointments [1]. ABDA concedes single practices or pharmacies may run out for a while, and that some deliveries, including the over-60s vaccine, moved much more slowly than in earlier years [1].

Karl Lauterbach, now chairman of the Bundestag research committee, blamed the late development of the adapted vaccines, not politics, and said there was still time because the season only really starts around Christmas [1]. The useful part is plain: a vaccine that arrives in December still works in January.
