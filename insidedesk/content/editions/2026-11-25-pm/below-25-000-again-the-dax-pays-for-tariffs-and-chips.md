Shortly after the close of Xetra trading, Boursorama showed the DAX at 24,733.22 points at 17:50, down 1.14% on the day and back below 25,000 [1]. It had opened higher; at 10:26 Reuters had it up 0.3% at 25,084, with a Consorsbank analyst saying the fight for 25,000 was 'noch nicht ausgefochten', not yet over [3]. The MDax was down 0.46% at 28,659.53 [1].

[[chart:europe-wednesday-25nov-pm]]

Three things weighed through the day. Washington announced proposed tariffs on 14 economies, including the EU, which a Frankfurt analyst called badly timed for export-oriented firms [2]. Statisticians revised German growth for the third quarter down to 0.1% [2]. And the selling in American technology shares spread across markets again [2]; at 17:50 Berlin time Boursorama showed the S&P 500 down 2.86% and the Nasdaq Composite down 3.88% [1]. Carmakers decoupled from the fall, helped by a positive 2027 sector outlook from a large US investment bank [2].

Across Europe the picture was the same in different sizes: Madrid down 2.00%, the Euro Stoxx 50 down 1.68%, Milan down 1.74%, Paris down 0.75% and London only 0.32% [1]. A fall of about one per cent on a day of tariff headlines is not a crash. It is a market doing its arithmetic in public.
