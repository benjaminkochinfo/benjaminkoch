The general debate is the budget week's big stage, and the opposition used it for labels. AfD leader Tino Chrupalla spoke of an 'Ausgabensucht', an addiction to spending, and said Friedrich Merz would go into the history books as the chancellor of insolvency; the debt budget, he said, must be stopped by a Normenkontrollklage at the Constitutional Court [1]. The Left's parliamentary leader Heidi Reichinnek spoke of social cuts and unlimited rearmament, and the Greens' leader Britta Haßelmann called the budget 'unseriös' [1].

Chrupalla, who spoke before the chancellor, also called Ukraine corrupt and accused Merz of political failure; Merz replied without naming him that anyone who did not understand that Russia wanted more than Ukraine did not understand the world [2].

Words in a general debate are cheap by design. The parts of the budget that bind are the €560 billion of spending and the roughly €210 billion of new debt that parliament votes on on Friday [1].
