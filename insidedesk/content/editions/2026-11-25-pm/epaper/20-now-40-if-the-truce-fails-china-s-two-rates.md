Under the overcapacity proposal, China faces the highest capped rate of the 14 economies at 20%, on top of duties of roughly 25% from Donald Trump's first term [1]. That rate complies with the US-China truce that expires on 10 January; if the truce unravels, China's rate would rise to 40%, the trade office said, according to Reuters [1]. Vietnam would face 20% with a trade agreement and 35% without [1].

The US official briefing reporters put China's total at about 45%, give or take by product; the Congressional Research Service estimated the average US tariff on Chinese goods at about 36.5% in July [2]. At the same time both sides are moving to remove tariffs on some of each other's goods, which Jamieson Greer called 'stability-generating activity' [2].

China's foreign ministry said on Wednesday that the 'so-called overcapacity' does not really exist [2]. Conditional rates are a negotiating tool. The date that matters is 10 January, not 25 November.
