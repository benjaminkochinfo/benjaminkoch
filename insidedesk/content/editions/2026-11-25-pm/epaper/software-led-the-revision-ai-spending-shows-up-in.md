When the Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter US growth from 3.4% to 4.3% annualised on Wednesday, the biggest single reason was investment [1]. The revision to investment was led by intellectual property products, notably software, based mainly on revised producer prices for system and application software publishing [1].

Consumer spending was revised up too: within goods, mainly pharmaceuticals, computer software and accessories, and petrol and other energy goods [1]. Health care services were revised down after new survey data [1]. Real gross domestic income rose 4.4%, close to the output measure [1].

Markets spent Wednesday selling AI shares. The national accounts, meanwhile, recorded the spending that those shares are supposed to be worth. Both can be right: an investment can raise growth this year and still disappoint its owners later. The accounts measure what was bought, not whether it pays.
