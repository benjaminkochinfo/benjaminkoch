52.2% of Australians with experience of mental health treatment have stopped or delayed it at some point because of barriers to care, with cost the most common reason, according to the Consumers Health Forum's 2026 National Consumer Sentiment Survey, published by the Australian Association of Psychologists on Wednesday [1]. Among people under financial stress 71.4% reported an interruption or delay, against 33.9% of those without [1].

Only 27% were very or extremely confident they could get mental health support quickly if they needed it [1]. Among adults aged 18 to 34, 37.5% report a current mental health condition, up from 31.4% in 2025 [1].

'Getting a referral or making it to a first appointment is a big step', said the association's executive director, Tegan Carrison; people then need to afford the next one [1]. The association wants a single, higher Medicare rebate and up to 20 subsidised sessions a year [1]. It is a lobby's request, but the survey's point stands on its own: the first session is the easy part to fund.
