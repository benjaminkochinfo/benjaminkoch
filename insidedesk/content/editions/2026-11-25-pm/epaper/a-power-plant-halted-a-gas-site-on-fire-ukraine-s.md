Russian strikes overnight halted power generation at a DTEK thermal plant and badly damaged its equipment, the company said, without saying where the plant is [1]. The same attack caused a large fire at a Naftogaz gas production site in the Poltava region of central Ukraine and stopped operations there [1]. Electricity was cut in five regions [1].

The two targets belong together. Ukraine has spent three winters learning to run a grid with holes in it, and domestic gas production is what keeps its own heating plants and boilers going without imports. Each lost unit has to be replaced by power bought across the border or by gas bought on the market, at exactly the moment Europe's prices are rising with the cold (see the lead).

In Odesa, engineers inspected a damaged substation that officials described as part of an attempt to cut the port city off from the rest of the country [1]. The defence is unglamorous: spare transformers, mobile generators and repair crews who work at night.
