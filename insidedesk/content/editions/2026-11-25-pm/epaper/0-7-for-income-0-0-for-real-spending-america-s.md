US personal income rose 0.7% in October and disposable income 0.8%, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said; part of the rise came from one-time Medicare Improvement Fund premium rebates [1]. Nominal consumer spending rose 0.3%, but after prices real spending was flat, falling $5.8 billion [1]. Real disposable income rose 0.5% after a 0.2% fall in September, and the saving rate was 4.1% [1]. A household that receives a rebate and saves it is behaving sensibly. It also tells you not to count October's income as a trend.
