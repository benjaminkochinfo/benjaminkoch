Germany's economy grew 0.1% in the third quarter, price, seasonally and calendar adjusted, the statistics office said on Wednesday, 0.1 points less than in its flash estimate of 30 October; exports and September retail sales were weaker than first assumed [1]. 'Flachte die wirtschaftliche Entwicklung im 3. Quartal 2026 ab', said Destatis president Ruth Brand: growth flattened [1].

Inside the total, private consumption rose 0.1% and government consumption 0.3% [1]. Gross fixed investment fell 1.2%, and investment in equipment, mainly machinery and vehicles, fell 3.5% [1]. Exports fell 1.0% and imports 1.4% [1]. Manufacturing value added fell 0.3%, partly because low water on the rivers limited barge transport for refineries and chemicals; information and communication grew 1.5% [1].

Compared with a year earlier, output was 1.2% higher [1]. A downward revision of 0.1 points is not a recession. A 3.5% fall in machinery spending is the number to watch, because that is what tomorrow's production is built with.
