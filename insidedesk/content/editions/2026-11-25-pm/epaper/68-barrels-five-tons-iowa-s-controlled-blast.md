The US Army carried out a controlled, mitigated detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown on Wednesday 'to eliminate the risk of an uncontrolled explosion' [1]. Two blasts were heard shortly after 09:00 local time, 16:00 in Berlin, and officials said the mitigation had exceeded expectations, the BBC reported, citing CBS News [4]. The stockpile was nearly 5 tons of degraded, highly unstable lead azide in 68 deteriorating plastic barrels [3].

Iowa's governor declared a disaster emergency in three counties. Residents of Middletown and anyone within a five-mile cone south-west of the plant were advised to evacuate, and people up to 20 miles away to stay indoors with windows closed [2]. A federal health official said pregnant women, young children and people with chronic illness in the affected area should leave [2]. The Army's updated map estimated the plume could reach northern Missouri, more than 100 miles away [2].

There is no safe level of lead exposure [2]. The New York Times reported that the Army had first hired a firm to neutralise the explosives, then dropped the contract [2].
