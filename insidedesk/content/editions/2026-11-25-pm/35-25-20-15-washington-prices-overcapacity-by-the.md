The US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, determined on Wednesday that the policies of 14 economies relating to 'structural excess capacity' in manufacturing are unreasonable and burden American commerce, and proposed tariffs on each of them under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 [1]. Mexico and Norway, investigated since March, got no determination for now [1].

[[chart:us-301-caps-25nov-pm]]

The proposed caps run from 15% for the European Union, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan, through 18% for Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand, and 20% for China and Singapore, to 25% for India and 35% for Vietnam [1]. All are upper bounds, netted against some existing duties and with exemptions in annexes [1]. Greer told CNBC the rates are 'not really additive': 'In effect, it'll look more like a top up' [2]. China is the exception; a US official said its 20% would come on top of first-term tariffs of roughly 25%, taking the rate to about 45% [2].

Read the timetable before the headline. Comments are due by 5 January, hearings begin on 25 January, and Greer said none of it would take effect 'earlier than a couple months from now' [1][2]. China's foreign ministry said the 'overcapacity' 'does not really exist' [2]. For a German exporter the EU line matters most: 15%, net of most-favoured-nation rates, in line with the framework deal the two sides already signed [1].
