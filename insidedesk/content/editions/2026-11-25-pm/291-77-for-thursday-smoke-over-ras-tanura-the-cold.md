Thursday's electricity in the German-Luxembourg day-ahead auction averages €291.77 a megawatt hour, up from €246.29 for Wednesday [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is 18.5% more in a day, and the highest daily average since Wednesday 11 November. The cheapest quarter-hour is €195.81 at 03:45; the dearest is €388.02 at 17:45, when offices, kitchens and street lights all want power at once and the sun has long gone [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-25nov-pm]]

Nothing dramatic happened to the grid. It is simply the last week of November behaving like the last week of November. The weather service expects snow in south-east Bavaria on Thursday, 5 to 10 centimetres and locally up to 15 in 12 to 18 hours, with possible freezing drizzle in the south and east, and a raised risk of glaze in the north-west on Sunday night [5]. Cold, still, grey days ask more of gas plants and less of wind and solar, and the auction prices that honestly. The evening peak is the expensive part. The night is not.

The second price tag of the day came from the Gulf. Yemen's Houthis said on Wednesday that they had attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery, the kingdom's largest, and that fires had broken out [2][4]. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura and that fragments fell, with no injuries in a first assessment; it did not say the refinery had been hit [2][4]. NASA satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick black smoke covering about 600 square kilometres around the site [2]. Aramco and the Saudi government did not comment [2].

The refinery processes 550,000 barrels a day, about 16% of Saudi refining capacity, and the terminal next to it can load about 6.5 million barrels a day of crude, mostly for Asian buyers [2][4]. It has been hit before this year: Aramco shut the refinery after an Iranian drone attack on 2 March, and loadings at the terminal resumed only in late June [4].

Brent responded the way markets respond to smoke. It traded at $93.60 a barrel at 16:16 Berlin time, up $2.20 or 2.41%, with WTI at $83.51 [2]. Reuters had Brent at $93.06 half an hour earlier, up 1.82% [3]. An analyst at PVM called it a rising geopolitical risk premium and added that traders covering short positions before the American Thanksgiving holiday could add to the move [3].

Look at what sat underneath the headline, though. The American Petroleum Institute counted US crude stocks up by 126,000 barrels in the week to 20 November, against an average analyst estimate of a 1.5 million barrel rise [3]. An economist at NLI Research told Reuters that perceptions of a global supply glut since the IEA's report this month had kept prices in check [3]. OPEC+ meets on Sunday, with the UAE no longer bound by its quotas and Iraq asking for more [3].

So you have two prices moving on the same Wednesday for different reasons. German power rose because the weather is cold and the evening is dark: a cause you can see from the window, and one that passes when the wind returns. Brent rose because of a claim that one side made and the other did not confirm, in a market that otherwise worries about too much oil, not too little. The first will be settled by Friday's auction. The second will be settled by an inspection report, if anyone publishes one.

The dull lesson is the useful one. A household on a fixed tariff will not see Thursday's €388.02 at all; it pays a price agreed months ago, which is exactly what fixed tariffs are for. A household on a dynamic tariff can move the dishwasher to 03:45. And a refinery that has been attacked twice this year and restarted twice is less a sign that the system is breaking than a reminder of how many people work to keep it running.
