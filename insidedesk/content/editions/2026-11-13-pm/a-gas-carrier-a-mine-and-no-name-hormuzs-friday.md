On Friday it was a gas carrier. Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and suffered an explosion in the Strait of Hormuz while using what it called an "unauthorized and dangerous route", Iranian state media reported [1]. No details of the vessel were immediately available [1]. The Guards added that it had been "under the protection of American fighter jets" [2].

That follows Thursday's claims about four oil tankers, which Iranian bodies described in differing ways: first an "accident" with a "violent explosion", later "hitting a naval mine" [3]. The tankers were not named either [3]. Against that stands the other side of the ledger: there has been no confirmation from UKMTO, the US or any independent source, analysts at ISW-CTP say they have "not observed any indicators" that Iran has successfully re-mined the strait, and the US military recently said all mines had been cleared [2].

What can be counted is counted elsewhere. Data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, cited by Reuters, show at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October, the highest monthly tally since the conflict began in late February [1]. Reuters also notes that traffic has rebounded and oil prices have fallen on expectations of de-escalation [1]. Desk arithmetic: 37 attacks in 31 days is about 1.2 a day; 10 in 10 days is about one a day. Slower, not stopped.

For you: a gas cargo matters to Europe's winter more than a headline does, but only once it has a name, an owner and a destination. Until then, the useful numbers are the ones you can audit: incident tallies, insurers' notices and the ships that actually arrive. Nothing here is investment advice.
