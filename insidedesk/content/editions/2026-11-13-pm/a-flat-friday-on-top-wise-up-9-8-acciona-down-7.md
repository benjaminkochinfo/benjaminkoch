At 15:27 CET on Friday, the DAX and the Euro Stoxx 50 were each up 0.07%, the CAC 40 up 0.03% and the FTSE 100 up 0.21%, per XTB [1]. Among the fifty Euro Stoxx members, 23 were up and 27 down [1]. That is what a calm Friday looks like from above. These are snapshots, not closes; the settled numbers arrive after our press time.

**Underneath, it was not calm.** Wise rose as much as 9.8% after a settlement in a Belgian investigation and 23% revenue growth; Acciona fell about 7%, last in the Stoxx 600, after its renewables arm admitted it would miss a €2 billion asset-sale target [1]. Richemont, the owner of Cartier, lost between 4% and 6%, at one point 9%, although its half-year operating profit rose 33% to €3.13 billion [1]. Investors marked down slower jewellery growth, 14% in the second quarter after 24% in the first, and a 7% fall in Middle East and Africa sales [1]. The profit was a record; the share price graded the slope.

[[chart:europe-friday-13pm]]

**The week is the bigger number.** XTB put the Stoxx 600 on course for a weekly gain of about 2.4%, its best since June [1]; dpa-AFX had the DAX heading for about 3.7% on the week [2]. The reasons are on the invoices: XTB cites third-quarter profits for Stoxx 600 companies rising about 25%, the fastest in four years, with energy up roughly 151% and the rest still about 10% [1]. Europe's indexes carry about 8.8% technology, against more than 35% on Wall Street, which is why the turmoil around Nvidia's chief reached Frankfurt only faintly [1].

**The bill arrives in bonds.** Bunds fell more than 100 ticks over the week, and the US ten-year yield touched 5.17%, its highest since 15 October, per Newsquawk [3]. Shares have had a good week. Borrowing has had a dear one. You will feel the second longer than the first.

**For you.** A flat index on a Friday is not a quiet market; it is ten loud ones cancelling out. If you own a fund, the average is your story. If you own three shares, read the three reports. Nothing here is investment advice.
