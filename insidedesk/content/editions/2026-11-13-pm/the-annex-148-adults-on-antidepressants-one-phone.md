The Annex reads the method behind the headline. Today's: a randomised trial by Josefin Brehmer, Gerhard Andersson and colleagues at Linköping University, published in the Journal of Affective Disorders [1].

**The claim.** A smartphone game called Fig, offered with optional online therapy modules and a therapist on call, reduced depression and anxiety symptoms in adults who were already taking antidepressants but still felt unwell, PsyPost reported on Thursday [1]. The game rewards routines around medication, sleep, meals and exercise by letting players build a virtual world [1].

**The sample.** 148 adults in Sweden, recruited through social media and paid adverts; average age 40, 82% women; 66% in a current major depressive episode [1]. Half got nine weeks of the package at once; the other half waited [1].

**The numbers.** About 51% of the treatment group improved their depression score by at least 30%, against 19% on the waitlist; only 16% improved by at least 50% [1]. Medication adherence improved slightly, quality of life did not differ, and 69% of survey respondents were fairly or very satisfied [1]. How much people used the game was not linked to how much they improved [1].

**The fine print.** Some 41% of the treatment group did not complete the post-treatment assessment, against 18% of controls [1]. Everyone knew which group they were in, all outcomes were self-reported, 21% of the treatment group never installed the game, and the trial was registered after recruitment had begun [1]. The developer, Mindforce Game Lab, funded the study; the authors say it played no part in the analysis [1]. The adherence result weakened under the most conservative handling of dropouts [1].

**For you.** A waitlist is the kindest possible rival: doing something usually beats doing nothing. The authors themselves say the effects cannot be credited to the game rather than the whole package [1]. If your medication only half works, that is worth raising with your doctor; a structured routine and access to a therapist are the parts of this package you can ask for today.
