Budgets are dull documents, which is why they are honest. In Thursday's Bereinigungssitzung on the 2027 draft budget, a new line appeared: "expenditure in connection with the acquisition and holding of stakes in companies in the energy sector", €4 billion (our translation) [1].

The line does not say what it buys. "Die Welt" reported that the money is meant for the one-third stake in Urenco, the British-based uranium enrichment group, that RWE and Eon hold in equal parts; the "Süddeutsche Zeitung" reported similar plans, and dpa had them confirmed from Bundestag circles [2]. Britain and the Netherlands each own the other thirds; Urenco also runs an enrichment plant at Gronau in Germany [2].

According to "Die Welt", the two utilities see no strategic value in a fuel maker after Germany's nuclear exit, and are uneasy that Urenco is moving further into military work at Britain's request [1]. The "Süddeutsche" reported, citing coalition circles, that the firm is to produce highly enriched uranium for British nuclear submarines [2].

Officially: nothing. The economy ministry's spokesman would not comment on "confidential talks" at Friday's government press conference; the finance ministry, both utilities and Urenco declined too [2]. Shares in Eon rose 1.5% and RWE 0.8% on Friday afternoon [3].

For you: the number is in a public document, and Parliament votes on it. That is the right order of things. The explanation should follow before the vote, not after. Nothing here is investment advice.
