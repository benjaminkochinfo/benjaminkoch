On Thursday a litre of Super E10 cost €1.949 on the national daily average, according to ADAC figures reported by dpa-AFX on Friday [1]. That is a good six cents, or 3.2%, less than a week earlier [1]. Diesel fell by a similar amount, to €2.188 [1]. Unusually, diesel is still well above petrol [1].

Analysts quoted by dpa-AFX tie the fall to last week's US congressional elections, which raised hopes of an end to the Iran war and pulled crude down [1]. Brent was above $113 a barrel before the vote; on Thursday it was back above $93 after President Trump damped those hopes again [1].

The agency adds the honest footnote: it usually takes several weeks for crude to travel from the well to a refinery and become fuel, so pump prices that react within days are "a psychological market effect" [1]. In other words, the price at the pump moves on headlines; the barrels in the tank were bought earlier.

Desk arithmetic: on a 50-litre tank of E10, six cents a litre is about €3 a fill. A diesel tank costs about €12 more than the same tank of E10 at Thursday's averages.

The ledger's view: a price that falls on a headline can rise on the next one. Brent was already climbing again on Thursday [1]. Fill up when you need to, not when the news tells you to.
