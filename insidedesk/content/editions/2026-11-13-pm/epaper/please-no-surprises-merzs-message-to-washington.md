Friedrich Merz chose plain words in Dublin on Friday. Ahead of expected talks next week between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin in China, he said: "Some here in Dublin fear this could turn into a 'second Anchorage moment'" [1]. His message to the United States: "please, no surprises. Continue to play as part of 'Team Europe' and 'Team Ukraine'" [1].

Then came a date. The Coalition of the Willing, Ukraine's allies, will meet in Berlin on 19 November, a German government spokesman confirmed; Emmanuel Macron and the British prime minister Andy Burnham are expected, and Merz said more than 20 heads of state and government would come [1]. "Our goal is to achieve two things: first, to secure funding for Ukraine's defence for 2027 and, second, to reach a unified position regarding upcoming peace talks," he said [1].

The unified position is the harder half. Al Jazeera reports continuing disagreement over naming a European lead negotiator [2]. Sweden's Ulf Kristersson called a united voice "extremely important", and Estonia's Kristen Michal suggested António Costa and Kaja Kallas [1]. Ursula von der Leyen called it "self-explanatory" that Europe is at the table [2].

For you: a summit about money is easier to judge than a summit about mood. On 19 November, look for a figure for Ukraine's 2027 defence funding and a name for Europe's negotiator. If both are there, the trip to Berlin paid for itself.
