Gulf oil exports, excluding Oman, reached 18.4 million barrels a day in October, the International Energy Agency said on Friday, still 26% below pre-war levels [1]. Crude did most of the work: 15 mb/d, or 82% of pre-war flows [1]. Products and LPG, the agency adds, remain at roughly half [1].

The OPEC+ table names the pumpers. Saudi Arabia produced 9.99 mb/d in October, up from 8.93 in September; Iraq 4.60, up from 4.06; Kuwait 2.03, up from 1.58 [1]. Desk arithmetic: those three added about 2.05 mb/d in a month. Iran slipped from 1.94 to 1.86 mb/d [1].

The IEA's own caveat: attacks on shipping "undermine maritime security, which is pivotal to a sustained recovery" [1]. A recovery counted in barrels is real; one counted in guarantees is not yet. Nothing here is investment advice.
