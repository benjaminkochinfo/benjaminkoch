SpaceX said on Thursday it plans to invest more than $74 billion in two AI supercomputing campuses: more than $24 billion at its Starbase site in Texas and roughly $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona [1]. Its AI segment generated more than half of the company's revenue in the third quarter, Reuters reports [1].

The headline number is a plan; the permits are the story. The Arizona campus needs approval from Cochise County, a petition with 2,400 signatures asks officials to deny it, and a county public hearing is set for December [1]. SpaceX promises about 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent ones, says the campus would use dry cooling with no planned industrial groundwater use, and says it is working with local electric co-operatives in a way that could cut members' bills by up to 20% [1].

In Texas, the Starbase site would make its own electricity rather than draw on the public grid; a city notice this week showed plans for a gas-fired power plant and supercomputing facility on about 444 acres, and the city commission is due to consider a property-tax abatement on 9 December [1].

Desk arithmetic: $74 billion over about 1,500 promised permanent jobs across both sites is roughly $49 million per permanent job.

Announcements are cheap; hearings are where they get priced. Watch the county agenda, not the press release. Nothing here is investment advice.
