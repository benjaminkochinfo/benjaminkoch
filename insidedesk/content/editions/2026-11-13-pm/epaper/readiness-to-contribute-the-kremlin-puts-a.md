On Thursday the US envoys said Vladimir Putin had agreed to let Europeans into the next round. On Friday the Kremlin put a condition on it. "Once we understand their genuine intention and readiness to contribute to the peace process, then it will be possible to make a decision on holding dialogue on a Ukrainian settlement in an expanded format," spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters [1]. Moscow is open to some contacts with Europe first, he said, but no concrete discussions have taken place [1].

Asked about Emmanuel Macron as an interlocutor, Peskov said reports "name completely different candidates" every hour [1]. He declined to comment on Kaja Kallas representing the EU; Kallas replied that it was not up to Russia to choose, adding: "They don't want anybody strong on the other side" [1]. Estonia's prime minister Kristen Michal proposed António Costa and Kallas [2].

The substance has not moved. Russia says it wants a full settlement, not a ceasefire, and Sergei Lavrov said this week Moscow wants a broad European security framework before the specifics of the war [1]. Putin's territorial demands include a buffer zone in parts of Ukraine Russia has not formally claimed; Ukraine refuses to give up Donbas territory it has not lost in fighting [1].

For you: an invitation with conditions is a negotiating position, not a seat. The checkable facts are still missing: a date, a venue and a list of names.
