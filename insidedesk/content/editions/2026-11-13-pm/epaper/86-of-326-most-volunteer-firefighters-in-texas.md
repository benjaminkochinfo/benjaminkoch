Of 326 volunteer firefighters in Texas surveyed in 2024, 86, or 26.4%, said they had an annual occupational health screening, according to a Texas A&M study in Workplace Health & Safety [1]. Volunteers are about two-thirds of the state's firefighting workforce, but the national screening standard is optional and the Texas screening law covers only career firefighters [1]. The international cancer agency classed firefighting as carcinogenic in 2022 [1]. The top barriers were cost, not knowing where to get screened, lack of insurance, no screenings on offer and time [1]. The authors suggest screening in the firehouse, with telehealth follow-up [1].

The risk does not check whether you are paid. Neither should the check-up.
