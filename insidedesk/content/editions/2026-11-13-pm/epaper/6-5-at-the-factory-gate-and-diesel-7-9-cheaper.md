America's producer price index for final demand rose 0.3% in October, after 1.4% in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said at 14:30 CET [1]. Over twelve months it is up 6.5%, the largest rise since November 2022 [1]. Markets had expected 0.4% and 6.6%, per Newsquawk [2].

Read the line items, not the headline. Diesel fuel prices fell 7.9% in October and crude petroleum 10.3% [1]. But truck transportation of freight rose 3.9%, and accounts for more than 80% of the month's rise in services prices [1]. Goods over twelve months: up 10.2%, the most since October 2022 [1]. Cheaper fuel does not reach the shelf until the haulier has re-priced his contracts, and that happens on his calendar, not the oil market's.

Core prices, excluding foods, energy and trade services, rose 0.5% for the second month running, 4.9% on the year [1]. Newsquawk called the report "dovish-leaning"; it reported Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent describing energy as a supply shock that is now easing [2]. Futures now put about a 12% chance on a half-point Fed rise in December, per XTB [3].

For you: a softer headline is not a cheaper basket. It is a slower rise in a dear one. The next PPI is due on 15 December [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
