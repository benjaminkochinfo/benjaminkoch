The remnants of Typhoon Cempaka, which recurved across the Pacific earlier in the week, reached British Columbia's South Coast on Friday [1]. Tofino on western Vancouver Island has already had around 250 mm of rain and could see another 50 to 100 mm or more by the end of the weekend, The Weather Network reports [1]. Environment and Climate Change Canada has issued rainfall warnings and expects the highest totals on northern Vancouver Island, locally above 120 mm, including Zeballos, where last weekend's storm caused a landslide [1].

By Saturday evening gusts could approach 100 km/h over parts of Vancouver Island [1]. Coastal flooding statements cover parts of southern Vancouver Island and Metro Vancouver, because "high tides are likely to surpass predicted levels due to storm surge enhancement," ECCC warns [1]. A red flood alert stands for the Sea-to-Sky Highway, and evacuation alerts and orders have been issued around Squamish and Pemberton [1].

The date matters: 13 November is the fifth anniversary of the Abbotsford floods, when thousands were displaced and five people died [1]. Abbotsford is expected to miss the worst this time [1].


Open-Meteo's modelled data for a grid point near Tofino put rain since 1 November at 385.3 mm by Thursday 12 November, against a 1991–2020 average of 197.9 mm for the same days [2]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 1.9 times the usual, before Friday's rain is counted.

Resilience rarely makes headlines. It looks like a warning issued early, a highway closed in time, a storm drain cleared of leaves. Never drive through a flooded road, the forecasters add; it takes little water to float a car [1].
