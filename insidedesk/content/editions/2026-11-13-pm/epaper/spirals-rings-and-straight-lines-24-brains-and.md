Researchers at the University of Chicago and colleagues analysed public recordings from 24 epilepsy patients with electrode grids on the surface of the brain, made while they played a spatial memory game or held letters in mind [1]. Electrical waves crossing the cortex formed straight lines, spirals and expanding or contracting rings, and the shapes shifted with the stage of memory and even with the letter being viewed [1]. Decoders trained on wave shape alone told memory states apart about 70% of the time, against a 50% chance level, according to the university [1].

The caveats are plain: the design is observational, so it cannot show that a spiral causes a memory, and no single pattern looked the same in every person [1]. "I like to think of it as a fingerprint for each person," says lead author Anup Das [1]. Brain stimulation to help memory is the team's hope, not a finding [1].
