Canva, the Australian design platform, is cutting 22% of its staff, 1,271 people, TechCrunch reported on Wednesday [1]. In a note to employees, co-founder and chief executive Melanie Perkins was unusually plain: "This has been our hardest year yet, and as we shared earlier this year, our growth has slowed for the first time in a decade" [1].

The changes are organisational as much as numerical. Canva is merging its product, growth and platform teams to cut what it calls communication friction, reducing "subspecialisation" so that people take on broader roles, and asking staff to work from an office a few days a week [1].

The pressure is easy to name. Canva has shipped its own AI models to cut costs and reduce reliance on outside ones, but it still lowered its revenue growth forecast to 20% from 30% because of rising AI costs, and early investors cut its valuation to about $35 billion earlier this year, TechCrunch wrote [1]. Meanwhile general-purpose chatbots such as ChatGPT and Gemini can now produce posters and presentations from a sentence [1].

Two things can be true at once. Twenty per cent growth would be the envy of most companies, and a fifth of a workforce is a lot of families. "Success in our first chapter doesn't guarantee success in the next," Perkins wrote [1]. For you, if you rely on the tool at work, nothing changes today; the test is whether a flatter company keeps the dull things working: templates, exports, support.
