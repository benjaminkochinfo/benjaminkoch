About 5,000 patients registered with Punjab's free programme for chronic myeloid leukaemia have gone more than three months without regular supplies, The Thursday Times reports [1]. The four medicines, including nilotinib, are dispensed monthly through five hospitals in Lahore, Multan, Rawalpindi and Faisalabad [1].

The cause was paperwork, not chemistry: delays in government procurement and bidding, according to reports [1]. Health Minister Khwaja Salman Rafique said oncologists had advised expanding the list from four medicines to 27, which led to a central procurement process that is still under way; meanwhile the five hospitals may now buy locally [1]. Doctors warn that long gaps in treatment can be dangerous [1]. A bigger formulary is progress only if the old one keeps arriving.
