Researchers at the University of Tübingen showed 28 men with schizophrenia or related disorders, recruited from the University Hospital's inpatient units, and 28 age-matched men from the community two-second videos made only of moving dots: a walking figure, or a face [1]. On faces, the patients named the emotion correctly about 59% of the time, against 76% for the healthy men; on walkers, 55% against 63%, where 50% is guessing [1]. They found the same emotions easy or hard as everyone else, just less accurately, and patients who did better on bodies also did better on faces [1].

The sample was small, all men, and nearly all patients were on medication [1]. The authors call it a blueprint for targeted treatment [1]. Knowing that the difficulty is broad, not selective, is how a clinic decides what to train.
