Brent settled $8.98, or 8.7%, lower at $94.62 a barrel on Wednesday, and was trading 0.7% lower at $93.9 in early Asian trade on Thursday, a fourth straight fall [1]. WTI closed at $88.12 and slipped to about $87 [1]. Traders read the Democrats' midterm win as a constraint on further escalation with Iran, while a senior Iranian official told Reuters the two sides remain far from a deal [1].

The plainer driver is ships. Flows through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered close to pre-war levels, though freight costs remain high; JPMorgan estimated a market surplus of 1.4 million barrels a day in October [1]. Commercial stocks in big consuming economies are still near 15-year lows [1]. A price can fall in a day; a cushion is rebuilt by the barrel. Nothing here is investment advice.
