A consortium led by US billionaire Todd Boehly is preparing a securities swap to take over sanctioned Russian oil major Lukoil's international assets, and a deal is expected within weeks, four people close to the process told Reuters [1]. The assets include oilfields, refineries and petrol stations in Europe, the Middle East and Africa [1].

The mechanics are the interesting part. A bank acting for the consortium has bought billions of dollars of frozen Lukoil securities from around ten US and British investment funds and from depositary banks, at 30 US cents on the dollar, the people said [1]. The plan is a cashless exchange of those securities for the foreign assets [1]. Last December the US Treasury rejected an earlier bid, saying Lukoil should not receive a windfall in shares or cash; the Boehly group moved ahead by promising to put the shares in an escrow account rather than hand them to Lukoil, two people said [1]. The Treasury did not respond to Reuters' requests for comment [1].

The price has already moved. The sale process, which began after US sanctions on Lukoil in October 2025, initially valued the portfolio at $22 billion, but two people said its value has deteriorated as some assets stopped operating [1]. Exxon Mobil and Chevron were among more than a dozen bidders; Carlyle dropped out last month [1]. The consortium also includes Emirati investors IHC and Al Hail Holding, and is backed by the US International Development Finance Corp [1].

For you, the point is that sanctions are not only a switch thrown in a capital. They are escrow terms, custody banks and discount prices negotiated over a year. Whether this deal closes will say more about their reach than any statement. Nothing here is investment advice.
