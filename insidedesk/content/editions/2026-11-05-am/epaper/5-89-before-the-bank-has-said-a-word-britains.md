If you are buying a home in Britain, today's rate decision has largely been priced into your quote already. Moneyfacts' average new mortgage rate rose to 5.89%, a three-year high, from 5.83% at the start of October; it was last higher in November 2023, at 6.07% [1]. The average two-year fixed deal has risen from 4.83% in January to 5.96% [1]. The cheapest two-year fixes at Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest and Santander are on average 0.44 percentage points dearer than at the start of September [1].

The reason is that lenders price off swap rates, which move on expectations, not on announcements. A Reuters poll of 63 economists found 54 expecting a quarter-point rise to 4.0% from 3.75% today, Mortgage Strategy reported [1]. Markets price three to four further rises in 2027, which would take Bank Rate to between 4.75% and 5.0%, Reuters wrote; some economists think the Bank may push back on that [2].

That is where the constructive bit lies. "If the MPC indicate a calmer path on future rate expectations, fixed mortgage rates could reduce slightly before the year is over, as some may have overcompensated for swap rate rises," said Moneyfacts' Rachel Springall [1]. She also warned that "most of the damage is unlikely to unwind entirely" [1].

For you, the dull steps beat the headline: check when your current fix ends, ask whether your lender will hold an offer while you wait, and read the Bank's minutes, not just the number, at 13:00 CET. Nothing here is investment advice.
