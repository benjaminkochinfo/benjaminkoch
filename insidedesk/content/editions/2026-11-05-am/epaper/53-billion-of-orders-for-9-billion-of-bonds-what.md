Meta drew a record €53 billion of investor orders for its first euro-denominated bond sale on Wednesday, Reuters reported, citing Bloomberg News [1]. It was seeking up to €9 billion, in six parts with maturities from just over four years to more than 38 [1]. The sale followed a $15 billion five-part dollar offering on Tuesday, and Meta's total debt stood at $83.67 billion at the end of September, according to LSEG data [1].

Desk arithmetic: €53 billion of orders against €9 billion on offer is roughly six euros of demand for every euro sold. That tells you lenders want long-dated paper from a profitable borrower. It does not tell you what the data centres it pays for will earn; that answer arrives later, in invoices. Nothing here is investment advice.
