Asian markets split on Thursday morning, and the dividing line ran through the chip factories. South Korea's KOSPI fell as much as 5.2%, enough to trigger a five-minute sell-side trading curb, known as a sidecar [1]. Samsung Electronics slid 5.5% and SK Hynix 8% [1]. Investing.com tied the selling to disappointing results from the US chip designers Qualcomm and Arm, after the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.1% overnight to a six-month low [1].

Tokyo felt the same draught in its chip names. Advantest fell 9.4%, Tokyo Electron 7.7% and SoftBank 5.3%, while the Nikkei 225 edged down 0.3% and the broader TOPIX rose 1.5% [1]. In plain terms: the index of the biggest names fell, and the wider market rose.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng went the other way, up 3.5%, with Chinese chipmakers leading after the People's Bank of China cut the rate on its relending facility for share buybacks and purchases by 25 basis points to 1% [1]. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 2.4% on a September trade surplus of A$2.83 billion, far above the A$940 million economists expected [1].

Under it all sits oil. Brent settled 8.7% lower on Wednesday at $94.62 a barrel, its lowest in about two months, which Investing.com said capped the regional losses [1]. These were intraday moves; Seoul and Tokyo close after this page goes out, so treat them as the morning's weather, not the day's climate. Nothing here is investment advice.
