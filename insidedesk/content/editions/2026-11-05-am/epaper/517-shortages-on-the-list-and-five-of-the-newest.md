As of 4 November, manufacturers had reported 517 current supply shortages of human medicines to Germany's Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices (BfArM); 371 are flagged as critical to supply and 167 as relevant to hospitals [1]. A shortage often affects only certain pack sizes [1].

The newest entries tell a small story. Entecavir, a tablet for chronic hepatitis B, appears again and again: two Aliud Pharma strengths reported on 3 November, expected to last until 31 March 2027, plus Hexal, Puren and Bristol-Myers Squibb's Baraclude since late October, most citing increased demand [1]. The same day brought Aliud's Metoprolol-Z 50 retard, a heart drug, flagged critical, until 16 February [1].

Hospitals feature too. A Burg Pharma electrolyte infusion with glucose has been short since 1 November and is expected to stay so until 30 September 2027, with Ringer-acetate solution named as an alternative [1]. The tuberculosis drugs Eremfat (rifampicin) and Isozid (isoniazid) from Riemser have been on the list since 19 October [1].

The list is not a crisis bulletin; it is the system working in public. Firms report, the regulator publishes, and pharmacists and doctors plan around it. The register itself says a medicine with the same active ingredient is not always an equivalent substitute, so talk to your doctor or pharmacist before switching [1].
