When a glacier collapse in Nepal recently sent a flash flood towards a school, its headteacher moved all 1,600 students to higher ground nine minutes before the water arrived, the IFRC said on Wednesday [1]. The school was destroyed; every child survived. Years earlier he had been trained in disaster preparedness by the Nepal Red Cross [1].

The federation wants COP31 governments to "move beyond commitments" and fund that kind of dull readiness [1]. It cites IFRC–WWF analysis estimating that nature-based solutions could cut the intensity of climate hazards by about 31% on average and help developing countries avoid at least $148bn a year in losses by 2030 [1]. In 2025, 9.6 million people were supported by anticipatory action [1]. A drill costs an afternoon; the alternative costs a school.
