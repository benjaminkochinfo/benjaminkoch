German gas storage stood at 60.81% on Wednesday morning, down 0.21 points on the day, against a European average of 74.33% [2]. On 1 November, the legal reference date, it was about 61.2%; the requirement was an average of 70% [1]. Desk arithmetic: roughly nine points short, and withdrawals have begun.

A year ago the same date read 75.00% [2]. The Greens' Michael Kellner told tagesschau the levels were "as low as never before" and that there was no safety buffer [1].

The institutions answer calmly, and with reasons. The economy ministry says supply is secure because what counts is actual withdrawal, running imports and other precautions, not storage alone [1]. Bundesnetzagentur chief Klaus Müller said meeting the storage targets is "no longer strictly necessary" for secure supply, because Germany now has LNG terminals [1]. Missing the target carries no sanctions, said Tim Kehler of the gas and water industry association; about 150 terawatt hours are in store, "noticeably more" than was withdrawn last winter [1]. The regulator says it sees no need to pull booked storage capacity [1].

The honest reading is that the buffer is thinner and the plumbing is wider. Germany's LNG now comes mainly from the United States, and Gulf gas plays no significant role in its supply, according to the regulator as quoted by the Allgäuer Zeitung [2]. The test is not a percentage on 1 November. It is a cold fortnight in January.
