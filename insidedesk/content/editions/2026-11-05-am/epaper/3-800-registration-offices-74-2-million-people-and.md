Anyone who has moved house in Germany knows the trip to the registration office. On Wednesday the federal cabinet adopted a regulation that requires every registration office to offer the online service developed by Hamburg, at wohnsitzanmeldung.gov.de [1][2].

The numbers are already larger than the headline suggests. The service is connected to more than 3,800 registration offices and is available to 74.2 million people [1]. What the regulation adds is the obligation, and an end to the patchwork: digital minister Karsten Wildberger called it "a real breakthrough for digital administration" [1]. The federal government takes responsibility for making sure the service meets the legal requirements, so each office no longer has to check that for itself [1]. It is the first online service made binding nationwide under the Online Access Act in this way, and others can be added if they meet criteria such as a high degree of digitalisation, accessibility and secure funding [1].

Two practical points. The website has a search function that tells you whether the service already works for your address [2]. And you need a user account such as BundID with the online function of your ID card switched on [2]. If your ID card's PIN is still in an envelope somewhere, finding it may be the slowest step.

The constructive reading: one city built a dull, working form, the Länder and the federal government agreed to use it, and nobody had to build it sixteen times. "One for all" is how administrations get faster, not by speeches about digital states.
