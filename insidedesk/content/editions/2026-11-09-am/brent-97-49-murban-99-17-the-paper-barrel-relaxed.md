On Sunday President Donald Trump said he was highly unlikely to order fresh attacks on Iran as long as talks to end the conflict make progress [3]. Brent futures opened lower on Monday and stood at $97.49 a barrel, down 83 cents or 0.84%, at 09:27 Beijing time (02:27 CET), with US crude at $90.48 [1]. Brent had settled at $98.32 on Friday [1].

**The physical barrel disagreed.** Murban, the Abu Dhabi grade traded as real cargoes, rose $3.28, or 3.42%, to $99.17, about $1.70 above Brent futures, Gulf News reported, citing Oilprice.com [1]. Its reading: buyers are still paying up for Gulf supply even as the paper market prices peace [1]. It adds a fair caveat: physical quotes can be thin and volatile [1].

**Why the invoice is higher.** On Saturday at least two tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, The Maritime Executive reported; one, tentatively identified as the product tanker Navig8 Messi, caught fire about eight nautical miles off Khasab in Oman, and its crew was evacuated with no reported injuries [2]. A second vessel was hit by a projectile on an outbound transit; all crew safe [2]. UKMTO says traffic is in the range of single to low-double-digit transits a day [2]. IMF PortWatch recorded one vessel transit on 1 November, against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 a day, according to Gulf News [1]. The same Saturday, US forces disabled an Iran-linked bulker, the Huda, which US Central Command said had tried to break the naval blockade of Iranian ports [2].

**For you.** Your heating oil and diesel are made from physical barrels, not from futures headlines. A falling Brent screen helps; it helps less while the strait itself still runs at a fraction of normal. Read the price and the shipping count together. Nothing here is investment advice.
