From today until Sunday, the Federal Health Ministry is running its nationwide vaccination action week, 9 to 15 November [1]. The aim is modest and right: explain what the jabs do, and close the gaps people have forgotten about [1]. No new rule, no new mandate. Just a reminder, timed to the start of the respiratory season.

**What is recommended, plainly.** The standing vaccination committee, STIKO, recommends an annual flu jab for everyone aged 60 and over, the chronically ill, pregnant women and medical staff [1]. A Covid-19 booster is advised mainly for people at higher risk of severe illness, and for those whose work brings them into contact with them, such as in care [1]. Against pneumococci, STIKO recommends a single dose of the 20-valent conjugate vaccine from 60, and for chronically ill people of any age [1]. A single RSV jab is standard from 75, and from 60 for care-home residents or people with serious underlying conditions [1].

**Why it matters.** According to Dresden University Hospital, Germany registered more than 240,000 laboratory-confirmed flu cases in the 2025/26 season. Around 65,000 patients needed hospital treatment and more than 2,000 died, most of them over 60 [2].

**The gap on the ward.** Here is the uncomfortable figure. In the Robert Koch Institute's OKaPII survey, 56% of hospital employees questioned had a flu jab in the 2024/25 season [2]. Doctors were, as in earlier years, "still clearly" more likely to be vaccinated than nursing staff [2]. The RKI found that trust in the vaccine's safety and the wish to protect others drove the decision, and that gaps in knowledge and uncertainty remain [2]. Many clinics now bring the jab to staff, on wards, in outpatient clinics and in admin offices [2].

**For you.** If you are over 60, care for someone, or work in a clinic, take your vaccination record to the GP's surgery this week. It is a ten-minute job. Health systems are rarely saved by breakthroughs in November. They are saved by appointments kept.
