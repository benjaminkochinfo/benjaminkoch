China's National Bureau of Statistics said on Monday that producer prices, the cost of goods at the factory gate, rose 5.7% year on year in October [2]. That beat the 5.3% expected in a Reuters poll and is the fastest rate since June 2022, the third month of acceleration in a row [1]. Mining and raw-material industries drove it [1].

**The shelf tells a different story.** Consumer inflation slowed to 0.7% from 0.9% in September, below the 0.8% expected, the first slowdown after two months of increases [1]. Core inflation, without food and energy, eased to 0.8% from 1% [1]. Petrol was the largest contributor to the rise in consumer prices, offset by a sharper than expected fall in food prices, according to the bureau's chief urban statistician Dong Lijuan [1]. Desk arithmetic: 5.7% minus 0.7% leaves a gap of five percentage points between factory and shop.

**Why the gap.** Zhang Zhiwei of Pinpoint Asset Management told CNBC that very strong external demand drove factory prices, while domestic demand stays weak because of the property market, with "no clear sign of stabilization" there yet [1].

**For you.** China's factory-gate prices are, with a lag, part of the import price of the things European households buy: tools, electronics, components. A 5.7% reading is a reason to watch import-price statistics in the coming months, not a reason to panic about the shop next door. Nothing here is investment advice.
