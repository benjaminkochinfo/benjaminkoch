xAI has installed eight converted jet-engine turbines in Mississippi to power one of its data centres, according to state permits and satellite imagery reviewed by The New York Times [1]. The generators may emit carbon monoxide at twice the rate of other turbines on the site, and the permits allow a 25% higher limit for volatile organic compounds [1]. Each ProEnergy unit produces 50 megawatts, enough for 15,000 homes, an executive told a conference [1]. Desk arithmetic: eight units are up to 400 MW. With such units, "they can have a data center running in 24 months," Rystad Energy's Audun Martinsen said, versus waiting years for grid access [1]. The shortage in AI is not only chips; it is the patient, permit-heavy work of connecting to a grid.
