Researchers at the Tokyo University of Science showed 40 students a 20-minute recorded biology lecture, either with whole slides or with each element appearing as the teacher reached it [1]. In the step-by-step group, eyes found the item being discussed after about 1 second on average; with whole slides, after 5.75 seconds [1]. Post-test scores were 17.70 against 16.70 out of 20, a medium-sized difference that only narrowly cleared statistical significance [1]. Students did not report the lesson feeling any easier [1].

The authors list the limits themselves: one lesson, one university, an immediate test that repeated the pre-test, and no note-taking [1]. "That gives experimental support to something teachers have learned through experience," said co-author Hiroko Ichikawa [1]. Good teaching is often a habit before it is a finding.
