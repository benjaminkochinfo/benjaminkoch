Last week's sell-off ran on fear of what a Democratic Congress might do to AI and data centres [1]. This week brings something duller and more useful: accounts. Earnings from CoreWeave, Applied Materials and Cisco Systems are due, and Reuters says they will put the profitability of AI ambitions "to the test" against the cost of the build-out [1].

**The mood is fragile.** Monday opened steady. MSCI's broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan rose 0.8% after sliding 4.6% last week [1]. S&P 500 futures were up 0.6% and Nasdaq 100 futures 0.7% by 12:08 Tokyo time (04:08 CET), after an early dip of as much as 1% when Nvidia's board revealed an investigation into whether its chief executive had been targeted in a foreign espionage plot [2]. Kyle Rodda of Capital.com put it plainly: "The markets are far from being out of the woods yet" [1].

**Two readings, one week.** Barclays strategists wrote that "the verdict on AI is still out", while still citing solid growth and a capex cycle likely to be extended [2]. On Tuesday, US consumer prices add a rates question to the profits question [1].

**For you.** A headline about regulation moves prices for a day. A guidance line in an earnings report, or an order book from a chip-tool maker, says whether the spending is being paid for. This week you get the second kind. Read them before the commentary. Nothing here is investment advice.
