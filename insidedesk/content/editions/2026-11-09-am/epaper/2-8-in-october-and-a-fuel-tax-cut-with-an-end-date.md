German consumer prices rose 2.8% in October from a year earlier on the statistics office's first estimate, down from 3.3% in September and below the 3.0% economists had expected [1]. The main reason was the cut in fuel tax at the start of the month [1]. Energy was still 12.2% dearer than a year before; food rose 0.4% and services 2.5% [1].

The cut is due to run until the end of the year, and the Bundesbank expects inflation to rise noticeably at the start of next year when it expires [1]. Economy minister Katherina Reiche has been asked by the coalition committee to draft a fuel-price cap, to come in by early 2027 at the latest [1]. Two dates for the kitchen calendar: 31 December, and whenever that draft appears.
