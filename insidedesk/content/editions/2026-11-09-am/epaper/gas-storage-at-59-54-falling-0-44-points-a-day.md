German gas storage stood at 59.54% on Sunday afternoon, down 0.44 percentage points on the day, according to the running tally the Allgäuer Zeitung compiles from European storage data [1]. The European average at the same point was 72.98% [1].

The trend is the story. The same tally lists 61.17% on 1 November 2026, against 75.00% on 1 November 2025 and 95.05% as early as 1 September 2024 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Germany started this November 13.83 points lower than last year, and has drawn down 1.63 points in the first eight days.

Two things argue for calm rather than alarm. The Bundesnetzagentur said in mid-October that supply was "stable" and that it rated the risk of a tight gas supply as low, because storage is only one building block alongside Norwegian pipeline gas, LNG terminals and imports via France and Belgium [1]. And the Gulf matters little directly: Germany buys its LNG mainly from the United States, the regulator noted [1].

What the low level does change is sensitivity. Full German stores cover roughly two to three cold winter months, the paper notes [1]; part-full stores leave less room for a long cold spell. Gas heats around 56% of German homes [1]. The figure to watch is not today's level but the daily withdrawal rate when the frost comes.
