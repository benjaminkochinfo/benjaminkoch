A deep low is crossing the top of Norway today. MET Norway, the national weather service, has a yellow warning for strong gusts in parts of Finnmark and Troms from 09:00 CET until midnight: locally around 30–35 m/s from the west, and occasionally storm force 10 on the coast [1]. The wind picks up first in the west and eases last in the east [1].

At sea the language is plainer still. Coastal forecasts from Torsvåg to the Russian border call for northwesterly strong gale force 9 from Monday morning or afternoon, locally storm force 10 [1]. Further south, a yellow warning for glaze ice from freezing fog and drizzle in the lowlands of southeastern Norway runs until 11:00 CET [1].

The storm arrives on wet ground. A yellow heavy-rain warning for parts of Troms and Nordland runs until 13:00 CET today, with locally 40–50 mm in 24 hours expected and 50–80 mm already fallen in the 48 hours to Sunday morning [1]. At a modelled grid point for Tromsø, daily totals from 2 to 8 November ran between 4.0 and 32.4 mm, with Sunday the wettest day [2]. Desk arithmetic: the seven days add up to 123.7 mm.


Yellow means be aware, not be afraid. If you are travelling north this week, the dull advice wins: check the ferry, check the flight, and check the warning map before the weather does it for you.
