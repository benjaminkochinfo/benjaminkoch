Abu Dhabi will host a new round of trilateral talks between the United States, Russia and Ukraine on Tuesday 10 and Wednesday 11 November, US special envoy Steve Witkoff announced, Gulf News reported [1]. Al Jazeera, writing on Sunday, still had the talks scheduled for those two days [2].

The format is the point. Witkoff described working-level negotiations between Russian and Ukrainian officials, mediated by Washington, covering post-war security arrangements, de-escalation measures, humanitarian issues and the next steps towards a peace agreement [1]. That is not a leaders' summit with a handshake photograph. It is officials, agendas and drafting.

The weekend did not make the room easier. Russia struck Ukraine's energy system again, leaving hundreds of thousands without power, and President Zelenskyy accused Moscow of escalating to pressure Kyiv before the talks [2]. Russia's defence ministry said it would keep striking fuel and energy facilities it says serve the military [2].

Still, working-level rounds are where smaller, practical steps can be tried first, and humanitarian issues are explicitly on this agenda [1]. They are slow, deniable and unglamorous, which is why both sides can afford to show up. Gulf News also reported that US negotiators planned to meet France, Germany, the UK, the EU and NATO in Brussels to discuss security guarantees for Ukraine and reconstruction [1]. If you want to judge Abu Dhabi, judge it by a document, not a mood.
