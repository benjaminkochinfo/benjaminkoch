Ukraine struck the Afipsky refinery in southern Russia overnight into the weekend, the sixth attack on a Russian oil-refining plant since 30 October, according to a report citing a person familiar with the situation [1]. Afipsky can process about 180,000 barrels a day; regional emergency services said a facility in the area caught fire after drone debris fell on it [1]. Separately, Yanos in Yaroslavl, a plant of about 300,000 barrels a day that supplies the Moscow region, halted crude processing after a drone attack on 3 November [1].

**The arithmetic of the campaign.** Per Bloomberg calculations from public data, the five facilities hit have a combined capacity of at least 1.3 million barrels a day, plus one plant in the Ufa group, which can handle up to 470,000 barrels a day [1]. Processing rates are at multiyear lows, and Moscow already restricts exports of most diesel, gasoline and jet fuel [1].

**Why Europe should care about diesel.** Before the export ban, Russia supplied around 10% of global seaborne diesel, and the restrictions aggravate a shortage created by the war in the Middle East [1]. Damage to refining can also free more crude for export, so the effect is a shift from products to crude, not a simple cut [1].

**The calendar.** US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine are scheduled for 10–11 November in Abu Dhabi [1]. Diesel is what moves your groceries and your heating in many homes; its price depends on refineries and ships, and this week on a meeting room. Nothing here is investment advice.
