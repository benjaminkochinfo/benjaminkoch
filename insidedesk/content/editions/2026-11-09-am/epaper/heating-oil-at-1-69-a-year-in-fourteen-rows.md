If you heat with oil, the most useful document this week is not a forecast. It is a table. Berliner Morgenpost publishes esyoil's average price per litre on the 15th of each month, and read in order it tells the year plainly [1].

On 15 October 2025 a litre cost €0.88; on 15 November 2025, €0.95; on 15 February this year, €0.92 [1]. Then came the jump: €1.34 in mid-March, €1.37 in May, a dip to €1.14 in June, and €1.69 by mid-September and €1.73 by mid-October [1]. In the past week the daily figures have barely moved, between €1.69 and €1.71 at esyoil and €1.75 and €1.77 at Tecson [1].

Desk arithmetic: 3,000 litres, a common order for a detached house, cost about €2,850 at last November's €0.95 and about €5,070 at Sunday's €1.69. That is roughly €2,220 more for the same tank.

The paper also lists the wholesale backdrop on Sunday afternoon: Brent at $97.97 a barrel and gasoil at $1,366.75 a tonne, per HeizOel24 [1]. The analyst Steffen Bukold writes for esyoil that oil "could therefore remain expensive for a longer time" (desk translation), citing the unresolved Gulf conflict and the war in Ukraine [1].

What you can control is smaller and sturdier: the quantity, the timing of a part-order, and comparing dealers including delivery charges [1]. Nothing here is advice.
