For much of October, Singapore lay under hazardous haze from wildfires burning across large parts of Indonesia [1]. Last week the city-state amended its transboundary haze law, giving it more powers to punish those responsible for the fires [1]. Environment minister Grace Fu said the aim was to "shape the behaviour" of businesses: "I'm hoping that we will not have to prosecute, but the fact that it's there will just make businesses realise that there are consequences" [1]. The harm, she added, falls on Indonesians as well as Singaporeans [1]. That is how clean air is usually won: with a statute, an inspector and a fine that nobody wants to pay, not a speech.
