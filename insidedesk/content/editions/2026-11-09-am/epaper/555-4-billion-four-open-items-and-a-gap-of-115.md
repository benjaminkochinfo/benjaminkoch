The coalition wants to finish its 2027 budget this week; the Bundestag budget committee's final session, the Bereinigungssitzung, begins on Thursday [1]. The draft so far: core spending of €555.4 billion and new borrowing of €118.7 billion, or €203.7 billion including the special funds for defence and infrastructure [1].

**The open items.** Union parliamentary leader Thorsten Frei told ZDF's "Berlin direkt" that the plastic tax would not happen, arguing it would have the wrong economic and ecological effect [1]. It was meant to raise about €670 million in 2027 and about €1.5 billion a year from 2028 [1]. A sugar tax worth €690 million is disputed but expected; Frei said its proceeds would go to the health fund [1]. In the SPD group there are wishes to stop savings on housing benefit and the abolition of the immediate children's supplement, together about €770 million [1]. If all four measures fell away, ZDF calculates an extra need of a good €2 billion [1].

**The cushion.** The autumn tax estimate gives the federal government about €3.9 billion more, but the better economic forecast lowers its permitted borrowing under the debt brake by €2.5 billion, leaving about €1.4 billion of room [1]. A further cut to the federal pension subsidy is being discussed [1].

**The real number.** According to the finance ministry, about €25 billion must be found in 2028, about €40 billion in 2029 and about €50 billion in 2030: roughly €115 billion unfunded [1]. This week's arguments are the small change. Thursday is where the habits are set.
