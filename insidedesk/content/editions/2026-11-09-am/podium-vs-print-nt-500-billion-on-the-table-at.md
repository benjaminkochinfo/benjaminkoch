**The podium.** Taiwan's market regulator and finance ministry spent the weekend promising calm: curbs on short selling from Monday and readiness to deploy the NT$500 billion ($15.2 billion) National Stabilization Fund [1]. The market took the hint. The Taiex rose as much as 6.4% early on Monday, its best move since 31 July, ending a seven-session decline that had pushed it into bear territory [1]. Taiwan's Central News Agency called it the tenth intervention in the fund's history, and had the index more than 2,600 points higher at one stage, above 42,300, after Friday's close of 39,701.64 [2]. At its intraday high, TSMC alone accounted for 1,227 of those points [2]. Homin Lee of Lombard Odier said the measures "are overpowering" the noise around Nvidia's chief executive [1].

**The print.** The rules are concrete. The Financial Supervisory Commission caps intraday short-sale orders for borrowed shares at 3% of a stock's 30-day average daily volume, down from 30%, and raises the minimum short-selling margin to 130% from 90% [1]. The money is less concrete. A Goldman Sachs desk note counted between NT$0.76 billion and NT$122.7 billion actually deployed in each of the fund's nine previous episodes, against NT$500 billion usable [1]. Desk arithmetic: even the largest of those is about a quarter of the headline.

**The gap.** The same note says the market rallied 6% on average in the week after an activation and 22% over a year, yet in six of the nine cases it suffered further drawdowns of 5% to 28% within six months [1]. Both lines are true at once. A backstop changes the mood of the first week; the earnings and order books decide the next six months.

**The neighbours.** By 03:13 CET most of the region was green: Nikkei 225 up 3.07%, Kospi up 1.32%, Hang Seng up 0.68%, Shanghai up 0.62%, while Australia's ASX 200 slipped 0.19% [3]. Those are first-hour figures, not closes.

**For you.** If you own a world or emerging-markets fund, Taiwan is a large slice of it, and Monday's bounce came from a rule change and a promise, not from new profits. Read the promise as a seatbelt, not an engine. Nothing here is investment advice.
