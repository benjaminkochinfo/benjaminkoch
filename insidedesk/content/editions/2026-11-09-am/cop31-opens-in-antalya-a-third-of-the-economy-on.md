Today the talking starts. Delegations from nearly 200 countries gather on Türkiye's Mediterranean coast for two weeks of climate negotiations [1]. The opening plenary is set for 10:00 Antalya time, which is 08:00 in Berlin [4]. The mood music arrived first, and it is not cheerful: war-driven fears over energy security, fast-rising power demand from AI, and the world's largest historic emitter shunning climate cooperation for the second year running [1].

**The host's yardstick.** Türkiye has built the summit around one goal: electrify more than a third of the global economy by 2035, up from around a fifth today, and it is asking countries to sign a pledge in support [1]. The criticism is just as plain. The pledge does not require the extra electricity to be low-carbon, which has already started an argument over whether electrification on its own counts as climate progress [1]. Türkiye will also ask governments and businesses to sign principles for developing and deploying AI sustainably [1].

**The headwinds, named.** Reuters reports that the Iran war has nations scrambling for oil cargoes and firing up coal plants, and that South Korea, India, Thailand and others have leaned harder on coal [1]. "It's hard to be optimistic," said John Holdren of Harvard [1]. Last year's COP president, the Brazilian diplomat André Corrêa do Lago, offered the other reading: "The war has strengthened the case for accelerating the energy transition" [1].

**The honest expectation.** Singapore's environment minister, Grace Fu, said she was "not expecting a major upgrade in mitigation targets". She added that the Paris Agreement already contains "enough to work on", and that adaptation and side agreements offer room for progress [2]. Coming from a negotiator, that is not defeatism. It is a job description.

**Today's theme.** Day one is Food, Agriculture and Health: food security, irrigation and water efficiency, and climate and health [3]. Energy and Transport, the home ground of the electrification pledge, follows on Tuesday [3].

**For you.** Skip the opening speeches. The business lobby asked for one thing, in the words of the International Chamber of Commerce's John Denton: clear policy signals that "turn adaptation needs into bankable projects" [1]. That is the right test. A pledge you can invest against is worth more than a target you can applaud.
