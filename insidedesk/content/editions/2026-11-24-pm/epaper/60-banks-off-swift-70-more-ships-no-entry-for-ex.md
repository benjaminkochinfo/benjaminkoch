The European Commission's proposed 22nd sanctions package would extend the transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove them all from SWIFT, target crypto and payment networks that help Russia evade sanctions, list more than 70 further shadow-fleet ships and ban every tanker insured in Russia [1]. Russian ex-combatants would be barred from entering the EU; "They cannot be allowed to wander freely across our countries," Ursula von der Leyen said [1].

The package also reaches into sport and culture: Russian teams could not compete under the Russian flag at events in the EU, and money from Russian sponsors to European bodies would be restricted [1]. Russia's nuclear sector, which Kyiv wanted included, is not covered [1].

It needs unanimous backing. EU ambassadors hold a first discussion on 25 November, and the leaders' summit on 26–27 November is expected to focus on a new €45 billion loan for Ukraine [1]. Twenty-two packages in, the method is clear: incremental, legal, slow. That is what makes them hard to undo.
