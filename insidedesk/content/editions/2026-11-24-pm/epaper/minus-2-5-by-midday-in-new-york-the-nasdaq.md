By 11:20 in New York (17:20 CET) the Nasdaq Composite was 2.5% lower and the S&P 500 1.2%, while the Dow traded around the flat line, helped by Amgen, Nike and Salesforce, CNBC reported [1]. Nvidia was down more than 7% after Monday's 31% jump; Micron and AMD fell more than 10%, Broadcom 3% and Oracle more than 3% [1]. The S&P 500 was heading for its eighth loss in nine days, AP noted, even as much of the rest of Wall Street rose [2].

UBS's Mark Haefele said his team does not see the volatility as "a signal that the structural AI investment story has broken", but expects more differentiation between established platforms with strong balance sheets and companies dependent on debt or scarce capacity [1]. That is a sober sentence for a jumpy week: the question is no longer whether AI matters, but who can pay for it. US markets close at 22:00 CET. Nothing here is investment advice.
