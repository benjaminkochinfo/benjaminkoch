The euro was quoted at $1.0968 in the late afternoon, Boursorama's ticker showed at 17:33 CET [1]. Earlier in the day the US dollar index had eased by 0.486 points to 103.77 against a basket of currencies, DTN reported [2].

The move is modest, and it comes on a day when traders again priced a 95% chance of a quarter-point Fed rise in December [3]. That combination looks odd until you remember that currencies trade on the gap between two central banks' expectations, not on one. When a rise is already fully priced, there is little left to buy the dollar for.

For a household in the euro area the practical effect is tiny: a dollar price for a flight or a piece of software moves by fractions of a percent from one day to the next. The trend over months matters; a Tuesday does not. Nothing here is investment advice.
