January Brent settled on Monday at $89.37 a barrel, down $3.20, and WTI at $80.17, down $3.24, after news that Libya's Sharara pipeline had reopened [1]. The field produces 350,000 barrels a day, DTN notes, and Libya accounts for nearly 5% of OPEC output [2]. On Tuesday morning Brent was back at $90.54, as six tankers had been targeted near the Strait of Hormuz in 24 hours [2].

The bigger picture is quieter than the headlines. Brent has fallen by more than a fifth this month, Bloomberg reported, and Persian Gulf crude exports are back at 74% of pre-war levels according to the International Energy Agency, which expects a surplus this quarter [3]. Onshore stocks are still at a ten-year low, so a surplus at sea is not yet a cushion on land [3]. Nothing here is investment advice.
