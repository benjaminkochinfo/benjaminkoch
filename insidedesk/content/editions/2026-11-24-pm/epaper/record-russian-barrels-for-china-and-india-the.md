China and India are on track for record imports of Russian oil this year, plugging the supply gap left by the Iran war, according to tanker data and industry insiders cited by Reuters via CNBC [1]. Temporary US waivers on Russian oil sanctions, aimed at keeping markets supplied during the Middle East crisis, widened the pool of vessels and suppliers available to both buyers [1].

It is the uncomfortable arithmetic of sanctions in a supply shock. A waiver that lowers prices for everyone also raises volumes for the sanctioned seller. Deutsche Bank says observed global liquids inventories rose in October, implying an oversupply of 1.7 million barrels a day [1], and UBS's Giovanni Staunovo points to strategic reserve releases keeping supply "relatively comfortable" [1]. When the surplus is firm, the waivers become a choice rather than a necessity. That is when they deserve a second look.
