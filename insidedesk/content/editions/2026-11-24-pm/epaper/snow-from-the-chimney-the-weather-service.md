A high-pressure system is drawing cold polar air from the north-east over Germany, with widespread hard frost at night, the Deutscher Wetterdienst wrote on Tuesday [1]. Many low-mountain ski areas are still waiting for snow, but overnight in the Rhine-Main area it snowed in a narrow strip near industrial plants: so-called industrial snow, visible on the satellite image as a band from the Höchst industrial park south-west of Frankfurt [1].

The recipe is precise. A cold, stable high with an inversion, cold air near the ground under warmer air, traps moisture and particles low down. Cooling towers and large heating plants add warm, wet air; it cools, condenses on aerosol particles, freezes and falls as snow, usually over a zone a few hundred metres to a few kilometres long, stretched along the wind [1].

The amounts are small, but the DWD warns that a thin cover can make roads, pavements and bridges slippery when temperatures are well below zero [1]. It is a lovely piece of physics. It is also a reminder that a city's weather is partly made by the city, and that the road gritting schedule should know where the chimneys are.
