37% of German companies with at least ten employees use artificial intelligence in 2026, up from 26% in 2025, the statistics office reported on Tuesday [1]. Among large firms with 250 or more staff the share is 73%, among medium-sized ones 49% and among small ones 33%; all three groups grew, by 16, 13 and 10 percentage points [1].

The most common uses are generating images, video and audio (59% of users), analysing written language (54%) and speech recognition (45%), mostly for marketing and sales, administration and accounting [1]. Of the firms that do not use AI, 18% have considered it; their reasons are lack of knowledge (65%), data-protection concerns (61%) and legal uncertainty (60%) [1].

While Anthropic's share price slid below its issue price in New York, this is the other half of the AI story: slow adoption by ordinary firms, constrained by training and lawyers rather than by enthusiasm. Share prices measure expectations. Surveys like this one measure use, and use is what eventually pays for the expectations.
