Every age group recorded higher confidence in November, the Conference Board said, with the biggest rise among Generation Z respondents; gains were largest among consumers with higher household incomes [1]. By party, confidence among Democrats and independents improved sharply, while it fell slightly among Republicans, who nonetheless remain the most confident group [1].

The write-in answers were "slightly less negative" than in previous months, the board's Fergus McCormick said; people still complained about high prices and rising costs, but some cited hope for the economy, and comments about war and conflict ticked up [1]. The survey ran from 1 to 17 November, just after the midterm elections [1].

The psychology is old and well documented: people rate the economy partly through the lens of who governs, or who will. That does not make the mood false. It makes it a mixture of facts and feelings, and the useful habit is to look at the jobs answers, which moved most, rather than at the headline alone.
