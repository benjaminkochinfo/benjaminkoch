Notebooks cost 14.9% more in October than a year earlier and desktop PCs 13.1% more, the statistics office reported on Tuesday; hard drives rose 40.8% and storage media and USB sticks 22.1% [1][2]. Games consoles were up 13%, mobile phones bought without a contract 12.2% and wearables 6.1%, against overall consumer price inflation of 2.8% [1].

[[chart:de-electronics-prices-24nov-pm]]

The cause is not mysterious. A memory shortage has taken hold of the market since 2025, ComputerBase notes [1]. Before that, the PC market had spent five years working off pandemic overproduction: from 2020 to 2025 desktop prices fell 16.3% and notebooks 9.3% [1]. Desk arithmetic: a desktop that cost €100 in 2020 cost about €83.70 in 2025 and about €94.70 now, so about two-thirds of the five-year fall has been undone in a single year.

Not everything on the list moved up. Televisions were 9.2% cheaper than a year ago, cameras 2.5% and audio equipment 0.9%; e-book readers and digital picture frames rose only 0.2% [1]. The index adjusts for quality, so a notebook that gains memory and price at the same time is not counted as pure inflation [1].

For a household this is a timing question, not a reason for alarm. If the old laptop works, it will still work in spring. If the television is the thing you were going to replace anyway, the numbers are on your side this year. Nothing here is buying advice; it is the statistics office's list, read slowly.
