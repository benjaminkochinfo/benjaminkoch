Anthropic's shares fell 12% on Tuesday and slipped below the $83 price at which the company sold them last week, CNBC reported [1]. The stock opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq on Thursday after Anthropic raised $81.3 billion, the largest offering of all time, and has fallen steadily since [1]. Desk arithmetic: $83 is about 25.6% below that first price.

The offering was oversubscribed, and roughly half the institutional investors who placed orders got nothing, CNBC reported earlier; so far those firms "don't appear to be racing" to buy the shares on the market [1]. Chipmakers and neoclouds fell with it, and Nvidia, 31% higher on Monday after Jensen Huang's return, was down more than 7% in the morning [2][1].

The same morning three brokers began coverage with positive ratings: Canaccord Genuity buy with a $135 target, Oppenheimer outperform with $150, and Wedbush, a day earlier, outperform with $120 [1]. Canaccord's own note is the most honest sentence of the day: "The risks are unusually large", it wrote, particularly around infrastructure commitments and the durability of the company's edge, before concluding that growth and valuation justified buying [1].

Both things can be true. A company can be important and its shares can still have been priced on a good day. The first week of trading is a vote by people with money on that day's mood; the annual report is the slower vote that counts. Nothing here is investment advice.
