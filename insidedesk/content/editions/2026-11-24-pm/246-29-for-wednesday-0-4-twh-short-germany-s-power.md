Wednesday's electricity costs €246.29 a megawatt hour on average in the German-Luxembourg day-ahead auction, a little below Tuesday's €254.15 [1]. The cheapest quarter-hour is €201.83 at 04:45, the dearest €304.34 at 17:30, when the lights go on and the wind does not always oblige [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-24nov-pm]]

Behind the daily price sits a quieter number from the statistics office. In the first nine months of 2026 Germany exported 48.8 terawatt hours of electricity worth €4.3 billion, 25.5% more by volume and 51.3% more by value than a year earlier [2]. Imports fell 13.3% to 49.2 terawatt hours but rose 20.4% in value to €5.7 billion [2]. The import surplus shrank to 0.4 terawatt hours, 97.5% less than the 17.9 terawatt hours of the same months of 2025; in euros it shrank only 24.8%, to €1.4 billion [2].

The reason is in the averages. Imported power cost €116 a megawatt hour from January to September, up 38.9%; exported power fetched €87, up 20.6% [2]. Desk arithmetic: Germany pays about €29 more for each megawatt hour it buys than it earns for each one it sells. That is what a grid with a lot of wind and sun looks like: it has the most to sell in the hours when everyone else has plenty too.

It is neither a crisis nor a triumph. It is interconnectors doing their job: moving power to where it is short, at the price of the hour.
