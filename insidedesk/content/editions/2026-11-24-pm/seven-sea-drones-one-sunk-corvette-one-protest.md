Ukraine's security service says it launched seven domestically made naval drones from a mothership to strike two Russian vessels near the eastern Libyan city of Benghazi; the corvette Merkury exploded and sank in port and a dry cargo ship that Kyiv says carried weapons was hit [1]. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the operation "exemplary" and released drone footage of the strike [1].

Libya's UN-recognised government in Tripoli condemned the "conduct of any foreign military operations within Libyan territory or territorial waters" and demanded an explanation from Kyiv [1]. That is a reminder that a successful strike also creates a diplomatic file, and that Ukraine needs friends in the Mediterranean as much as it needs range.

The war at home did not pause. Russian attacks killed nine people and injured 81 across Ukraine over the past day, local authorities reported; in Zaporizhzhia a five-storey residential building was partly destroyed, with two dead and 48 injured, among them an infant [2]. Russia launched 96 drones overnight, 73 of which were downed or suppressed, the air force said [2]. A Russian drone briefly violated Polish airspace during a night attack on western Ukraine; the Polish military authorised a shoot-down but held fire when it turned east, citing the risk to civilians [1].

Two readings are tempting and both are wrong. Benghazi does not mean the war is turning, and the Zaporizhzhia figures do not mean nothing changes. What changed is geography: Russia's logistics to Africa now have to be defended at sea, far from home, and that costs ships, escorts and money.
