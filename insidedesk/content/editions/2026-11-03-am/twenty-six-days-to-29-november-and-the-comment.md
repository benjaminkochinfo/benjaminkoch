There is a date on the calendar for Vietnam. The US administration has set 29 November as the deadline to conclude a trade probe, launched in May, into the country's alleged intellectual-property violations [1]. Vietnam has been designated a "priority foreign country", the first jurisdiction in 13 years to be put in that most serious category of the US watchlist [1]. Desk arithmetic: from today, that is 26 days.

Nobody has yet said what would follow. Reuters reports it is not clear whether any tariff tied to the probe would be general or aimed at particular sectors [1]. What is on paper already: a general 12.5% US tariff on Vietnamese goods, imposed in July after a forced-labour investigation, and the memory of the 20% "Liberation Day" rate that the Supreme Court struck down [1]. Many economists, Reuters notes, think the aim is to get back to at least that level [1]. That is their reading, not a published plan.

The more useful document is the comment file, because it shows who would pay. Tesla asked the Trade Representative to "take into account the supply chain needs of American manufacturers", saying some advanced components and precision equipment cannot be sourced in the US [1]. Samsung's US unit pointed out that Vietnam makes a large share of the phones and network gear it sells in America; the company is Vietnam's biggest foreign investor, with about $25 billion put in [1]. Retailers and makers of shoes, clothes, bicycles and games consoles also argued against new tariffs [1].

On the other side of the file sit US producers of steel, seafood, plywood, solar panels and wood products, who asked for tariffs, and the software, film and music industries, who want firm commitments on piracy rather than a duty [1]. The Business Software Alliance cites a 2017 survey that about three-quarters of PC software in Vietnam was unlicensed; the Motion Picture Association counts over 12.5 billion visits last year to pirate sites it says are hosted there [1].

Behind all of it is one line in the trade statistics: Vietnam's exports to the US rose 42% in the first eight months of the year, making it the US partner with the largest goods surplus, ahead of China and Mexico [1]. If you buy from Vietnam, the practical step is the boring one. Price your spring orders on the 12.5% that is law today, and keep a column for 29 November. Nothing here is investment advice.
