"I guess I'll become Mr. Veto," President Trump told a New York Post podcast if Democrats win Congress, adding that "at some point, we'll probably make deals" [1]. Reuters, citing three sources, reports that aides have been working since April on executive measures on immigration, tariffs, housing costs and spending that need no new law [1]. The White House rejected suggestions of Republican losses [1].

The dull checks still apply. Courts can strike down actions that go beyond what a statute allows. A Senate majority can block nominees. And no administration can stop congressional oversight: hearings, subpoenas and document requests [1]. A veto stops a bill. It does not stop a question.
