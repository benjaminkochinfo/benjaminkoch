The electronic patient record (ePA) is about to do a second job. From January 2027, pseudonymised ePA data is due to be passed to the Research Data Centre at the BfArM in Bonn, for research in the public interest [1]. At first only the electronic medication list will be sent; discharge letters, lab results and medication plans are planned for later years [1].

A forsa survey for the AOK, of 8,583 adults between 7 and 30 September, asked people what they think [1]. 76% said it was very good or fairly good that ePA data will be used this way [1]. Among people with statutory insurance, 79% found it acceptable if the data carried no name, date of birth or other direct identifiers [1]. 64% trust that the data will be protected from unauthorised access; 19% are sceptical [1]. The lowest support, 61%, was for the fact that pharmaceutical companies may also apply to use the data for public-interest research [1].

Only 10% say they plan to object, and 12% are undecided [1]. You can object at any time, including afterwards: data already sent is then deleted at the centre, except where a running project is already using it, which may continue for that project only [1]. AOK users are being told in the app; others can object through their insurer or its ePA ombudsman office [1].

AOK chief Carola Reimann calls the results "a large advance of trust" [1]. That is the right word. Trust lent in advance is repaid in boring ways: secure processing, applications on record, and a deletion button that works.
