While America votes, Israel moves into the slow, formal part of its own election. The results of the 27 October vote are due to be certified on Wednesday [2]. That afternoon, President Isaac Herzog begins meeting the 11 parties that won seats, and he will take them in an unusual order: smallest first, starting with Religious Zionism-Zehut at 16:00 CET, and ending with Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud at 19:30 CET on Thursday [1]. His office says the aim is to "encourage thinking outside the box" [1].

The arithmetic explains why. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats, and the opposition "Change" bloc of Gadi Eisenkot won 48. Neither reaches the 61 needed [2]. The Arab-led Joint List and Ra'am won a record 19 seats between them, and both say they will work with the opposition to replace the government. The Joint List says it would not join a cabinet but would vote with one [2]. Desk arithmetic: 48 plus 19 is 67.

The law leaves the president room. He is not bound to choose the candidate with the most recommendations, only the one most likely to form a government. That person then has 28 days, plus up to 14 more [1]. The consultations will be streamed live [1].

There is noise around the process: Netanyahu has alleged fraud without evidence, and protesters gathered outside Eisenkot's home [2]. The head of the Central Elections Committee answered on Sunday. Even if the suspicions were proved, he said, they "would not undermine the integrity of the electoral process or the election results as a whole" [2]. The members of the new Knesset are sworn in on 10 November, whatever the talks produce [2].
