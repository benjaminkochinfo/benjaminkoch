The settled figures for Monday are larger than the intraday ones. Brent crude futures closed $8.66, or 7.63%, lower at $104.77 a barrel, Reuters reported [1]. US WTI settled 7.36% lower at $96.46, according to CNBC [2]. The trigger was diplomacy: Tehran said contacts with Washington to end the war were continuing, and President Trump said a deal could happen [1].

Then came Tuesday. Brent rose 48 cents, or 0.5%, to $105.25 by 02:04 CET, and WTI was up 47 cents at $96.93, after the Wall Street Journal reported, citing US officials, that the United States had struck Iran's Qeshm Island [1]. Some officials described the strikes as defensive, to stop Iran attacking commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz [1]. "Just when markets were starting to price in a more diplomatic path, this reminder of ongoing military activity has flipped the tone," said Tim Waterer of KCM Trade [1].

Desk arithmetic: the early Tuesday rise recovers about 5.5% of Monday's fall. One report moved the price less than one day of talks did.

The physical facts have not changed overnight. The war that began in late February has sharply reduced traffic through the strait, which used to carry about a fifth of the world's crude and gas [1]. Iran's president says the supreme leader has given the government authority for peace talks [1]. Prices that move on sentences will keep moving on sentences until tankers move again.
