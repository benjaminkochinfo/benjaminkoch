Psychologists call it "symbolic immortality": the sense that part of you will live on through people, work or legacy. A Hebrew University team surveyed 2,912 Israeli adults in February 2025, in wartime [1]. Those who more strongly expected to be remembered reported fewer symptoms of depression, anxiety and post-traumatic stress. They also reported less problematic drinking, gambling and social-media use [1]. The links were modest to small, but consistent [1]. Belief in an afterlife, by contrast, was not consistently linked with better mental health [1].

The authors caution that the direction may run the other way: poor mental health can make a legacy feel out of reach [1]. Still, the things that feed the feeling are ordinary ones. They are relationships, community and work that others rely on.
