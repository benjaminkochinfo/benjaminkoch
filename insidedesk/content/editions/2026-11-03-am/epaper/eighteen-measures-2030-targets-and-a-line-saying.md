Germany should be a "sovereign AI nation" by 2030, according to a report from the federal government's AI task force, which POLITICO obtained and which was due to be presented on Monday to the National Security Council and at an AI summit in Berlin [1]. "The next 12 to 18 months will therefore be decisive in setting the course," it says [1].

The plan has 18 measures [1]. They include expanding AI computing infrastructure and linking it into a European network, strengthening the semiconductor value chain, and working with France and Canada, and with Mistral, to secure "sovereign access" to leading models [1]. The state would act as a "strategic anchor customer", using public contracts and pilot projects to help European AI providers into the market [1]. German AI should specialise in industry, with "highly specialized models" for production [1].

The targets are specific. By 2030, at least 80% of large companies and 55% of small and medium-sized ones should use AI, and 90% of federal agencies should have at least one AI application in production [1]. AI data-centre capacity is meant to grow by 300% compared with 2025 [1].

The sentence that matters most comes near the end. The measures "neither constitute funding decisions nor prejudge" future budget procedures, the report says [1]. That is honest, and it tells you where to look next: the federal budget lines and the procurement notices. A strategy shows its intent in the budget, and buying as an anchor customer will show up in contracts that someone has to sign.
