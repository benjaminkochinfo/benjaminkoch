Real rules arrive with real dates, and fraudsters know it. The Verbraucherzentrale is warning about a new wave of fake emails in the name of Commerzbank [1]. They claim that under a European payments directive "PDS3", payment providers have since "31.10.026" been obliged to use stronger two-factor authentication, so your 3D-Secure card security must be renewed [1]. Spelling as in the original.

Then comes the deadline: 24 hours, or your credit card and your whole account will be blocked [1].

The consumer advisers list the signs: an odd sender address, an impersonal greeting, and above all the enormous pressure to act now [1]. Genuine changes to your banking rules are announced weeks ahead, and you can check them in the bank's own app or on the website you normally use [1]. Never through a link in an email.

If you have already clicked and typed in your details, Commerzbank says on its help page: stay calm, block your online banking yourself or ring its round-the-clock blocking line on 069 50 50 27 86 [1]. Check the sender only through the official contact routes you already know [1].

The constructive point is a habit you can apply to any bank, utility or tax office. A real institution is slow on purpose. It sends letters, sets notice periods and lets you check. A message that cannot wait one day for you to look it up has told you what it is.
