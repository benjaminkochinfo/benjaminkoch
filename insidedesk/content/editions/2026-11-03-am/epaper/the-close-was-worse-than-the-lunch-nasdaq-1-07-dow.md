Last night's evening update had to work with late-session figures. The settled close is now in, and it is weaker. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.07% to 25,164.32 and the S&P 500 lost 0.51% to 7,541.40, CNBC reported [1]. Both had been up by as much as 1.4% and 1.1% during the day [1]. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 213.67 points, or 0.41%, to 51,983.98 [1].

The split had names on it. Sherwin-Williams rose almost 6% and Home Depot 3.6%, and Honeywell, 3M and Boeing each gained more than 2% [1]. On the other side, Marvell fell almost 13% and Lumentum 18% on their $115 billion deal, and Oracle lost 8% after saying it would sell $10 billion of shares and $10 billion of bonds to fund its AI build-out [1]. IDEXX Laboratories, which sells tests to vets, jumped 20% after beating estimates [1].

The day's dullest number may matter most. US construction spending fell 1.9% in September, the biggest monthly drop since April 2020, against an expected rise of 0.4% [1]. Investors spent the day buying the shops that sell paint while the building sites were spending less.

Asia opened Tuesday on the tech side of that ledger, with Seoul's Kospi down a little over 3.5% at one point [2]. Nothing here is investment advice.
