Health ministry state secretary Katja Kohfeld gave a first look on Monday at the long-awaited health-security bill (GeSiG) [1]. Three parts: a "Personalreserve Gesundheit" that could bring retired doctors and other staff back in a crisis and train helpers; larger stockpiles of medicines and devices, since today's reserves mostly cover two to six weeks; and a central health situation picture shared across the system [1].

The KBV's Stephan Hofmeister welcomed the data sharing but rejected obliging practices to take part: "We do not believe that is a good instrument" [1]. He said outpatient care handles 97% of statutory treatment cases, more than 586 million a year, including 61.7 million check-ups and screenings, which could be paused in an emergency [1]. Preparedness is mostly lists, rotas and storerooms. That is good news: those can be built in peacetime.
