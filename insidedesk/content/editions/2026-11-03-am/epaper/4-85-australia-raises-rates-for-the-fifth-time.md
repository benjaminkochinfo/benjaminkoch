The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate target by 0.25 percentage points to 4.85% on Tuesday [1]. It was the fifth increase of 2026, and the cash rate is now at its highest since late 2008, ABC News reported [2]. The board was split: six members voted to raise, three to hold at 4.60% [1].

The reason is in the bank's own forecasts. Inflation is now expected to stay elevated through much of 2027 and not to reach the top of the 2–3% target range until late 2027, "around three quarters later than previously forecast" [1]. The board points to fuel and commodity prices driven by the Middle East conflict, slightly tighter domestic capacity, and firms that are raising prices or plan to [1].

It is not blind to the cost. The same statement says the economy is slowing, housing prices have fallen significantly and unemployment has risen [1]. Savers whose banks pass the rise on will earn more; borrowers will pay more [2]. Desk arithmetic: on a A$600,000 variable mortgage, 0.25 points is about A$1,500 a year of extra interest at the start, before repayments reduce the balance.

ABC notes it takes 12 to 18 months for a rate change to work fully through the economy [2]. The board will be "attentive to the data", it says, and makes no promise either way [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
