At 17:59 CET on Sunday, an unidentified drone crossed into Latvian airspace. An orange alert went out in the eastern districts of Krāslava and Ludza [2]. The military launched a BLAZE interceptor drone [1][2]. At 18:16 CET the object left Latvia across the Russian border [1]. Three minutes later, near that border, Latvia set off the interceptor's self-destruct mechanism "to prevent a possible risk of violating the state border", the armed forces said (our translation). People heard the explosion [1]. The alert was lifted at 18:30 CET [1].

Desk arithmetic: the incursion lasted 17 minutes, and the alert in the two districts about half an hour.

You could read this as a failure, since nothing was shot down. Read it the other way. A small country on NATO's eastern edge had a defence ready within minutes. It used it, and then chose to destroy its own equipment rather than give anyone an incident on the other side of the line. That restraint is the job.

The armed forces say they monitor the airspace continuously with NATO allies and have sent more air-defence units to the eastern border [1]. They also say plainly that while Russia's war in Ukraine goes on, more foreign drones may approach or enter Latvian airspace [1]. It is not the first time. On 24 October Latvia reported finding the wreckage of a crashed drone, and on 18 October debris from what is believed to be a drone was found in Lithuania's Pasvalys district [2]. Lithuania's prime minister said that drone carried explosives [2].

Each incident adds to a slow, unglamorous build-up of sensors, interceptors, alert procedures and units on a border. That is how deterrence is built.
