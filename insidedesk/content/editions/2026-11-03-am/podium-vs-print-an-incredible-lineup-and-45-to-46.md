**The podium.** John Ternus has run Apple since September, and Monday was his first results day in charge [2]. He had a record to read out: revenue of $114.5 billion for the quarter to 26 September, up 12% on a year earlier, a September-quarter record for the iPhone and an all-time high for services [1]. Then he turned to the shop window: the iPhone Duo, "the most transformational iPhone since the original", new home hardware, touch on the Mac and "an incredible lineup to carry us into the holiday season" [1].

**The print.** On the investor call, the numbers for that holiday season were more sober. Apple expects a gross margin of 45% to 46% for the December quarter, down from 47.9% in the quarter just reported, because memory chips keep getting dearer, Reuters reported [2]. The finance chief, Kevan Parekh, said he expects memory costs to keep rising into next year [2]. The shares fell 4% during the call before paring the loss to about 2% [2]. They had already closed nearly 2% lower in regular trading [3].

**The gap.** The two statements are not in conflict; they describe two sides of the same invoice. The 47.9% already included a favourable effect of about one percentage point from tariff refunds, Apple said [1]. Desk arithmetic: without that, the September quarter was nearer 46.9%, so the new guidance is roughly one to two points below the underlying figure, not three. Apple has also raised iPhone 17 prices by $100 and some MacBook prices, and Ternus said "these price increases provided some offset" [2]. Its purchase commitments rose by $33 billion in the quarter, the operations chief said, as it works with memory suppliers [2].

**What the speech left out.** Demand is not the problem. Ternus told Reuters the Duo is "constrained as of today", with no date for supply to catch up, and that a "very small percentage" of units had come back for display problems [2]. The constraint is the supply chain and its prices. For you, the lesson is the usual one: the lineup is the headline, the purchase commitments are the story, and the margin guidance is the number that gets checked in three months. Nothing here is investment advice.
