California's attorney general Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia on Thursday, related to reports of former chief executive Jensen Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy [1]. The state's Department of Justice is asking whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China and whether the company misled investors and the public [1].

"The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors," Bonta said [1]. "Companies cannot mislead their investors," he added [1]. The Guardian reports that Nvidia has approached Arm's chief executive Rene Haas in its search for a new CEO [2].

A subpoena is a request for documents, not a finding. That is exactly why it is useful: it moves a scandal from gossip to paperwork, where it can be checked.
