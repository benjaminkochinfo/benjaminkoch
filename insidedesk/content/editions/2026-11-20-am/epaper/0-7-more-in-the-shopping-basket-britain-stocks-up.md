UK retail sales volumes rose 0.7% in October, after a fall of 0.6% in September, the Office for National Statistics said on Friday [1]. They were 2.3% higher than a year earlier, and 0.4% higher over the three months to October than in the three months to July [1].

The reason is unusually concrete. Supermarkets reported more sales of long-life items such as tinned goods and UHT milk after the government launched its "Be Ready" campaign on 22 October [1]. Clothing shops recovered from stock problems in September, and online clothing retailers credited more autumnal weather [1]. Fuel volumes fell back to August levels as prices rose for a third month [1].

Online spending rose 1.5% on the month and was 9.7% higher than a year earlier; the online share of sales edged up to 29.2% [1]. The ONS reminds readers that monthly figures are volatile and should be read alongside the three-month rate [1], and the survey's response rate was 57.5%, below its recent average [1].
