The driving-licence reform passed by the Bundestag was on the Bundesrat's agenda on Friday, with its transport committee recommending approval [1]. Its declared aim is to make the licence cheaper, digitise driving schools and cut red tape [1].

Learners would no longer have to attend theory lessons at the school; they could learn entirely by app or online, and the theory test's question catalogue would shrink [1]. For a car licence, today's twelve compulsory special drives would go as a fixed number: country-road, motorway and night drives stay mandatory, but the instructor sets how many [1]. After passing the theory test and at least six practical lessons, learners could practise with a close person, once an instructor has briefed both; each Land decides whether to allow it, and it is to be trialled first [1]. All driving schools' prices would be listed in the Mobilithek database [1].

The committee regretted that schools' pass rates will not be published, arguing that prices alone allow only a limited comparison [1]. It has a point: the cheapest course is the one you pass first time.
