Christine Lagarde told the Frankfurt European Banking Congress on Friday, her eighth and last appearance there as ECB president, that the ECB's currency swap lines "will be more accessible, too" [1]. She said EUREP, the ECB's repo facility for central banks, was made permanent in February and opened to central banks worldwide, and that more than 30 have expressed interest [1]. "A reserve manager who knows that euro securities can be turned into euro liquidity without a fire sale has more reason to hold them in normal times," she said [1].

She also noted that international card schemes handle more than 60% of euro-area card payments, and 14 of the 21 euro-area countries have no domestic scheme; if the digital-euro legislation is adopted by year-end, the ECB aims for a first issuance in 2029 [1]. Sovereignty, in central-bank language, is mostly a question of who runs the pipes.
