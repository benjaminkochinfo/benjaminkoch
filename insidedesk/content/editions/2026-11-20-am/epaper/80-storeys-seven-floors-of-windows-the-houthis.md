A Houthi attack on Thursday damaged the 80-storey Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, shattering windows across seven to eight floors and bending sections of the metal facade, Gulf News reported [1]. Saudi Civil Defence said several floors and neighbouring buildings were damaged but no one was injured [1]. Entrances to the district were closed and people evacuated [1].

It was the second attack to damage a prominent building in the district in less than two weeks, after a strike on a Saudi Aramco building on 8 November [1]. Reuters described the attacks as the latest blow to the kingdom in its war with the Iran-aligned Houthis, and oil traders bought briefly on the news [2].

The choice of target is a message about money rather than oil: a sovereign wealth fund's headquarters is a symbol of a country's plan for the decades after crude. Glass can be replaced in weeks. Confidence in a financial district is rebuilt more slowly, by the dull business of insurance claims, reopened entrances and Monday mornings that go to plan.
