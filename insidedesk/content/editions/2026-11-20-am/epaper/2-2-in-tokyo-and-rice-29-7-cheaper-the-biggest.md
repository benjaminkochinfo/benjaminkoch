Japan's consumer prices excluding fresh food were 2.2% higher in October than a year earlier, the Statistics Bureau said on Friday; headline inflation was 2.3%, and the measure that also excludes energy rose 2.2% [1]. Core inflation has now been positive for 62 months in a row and above 2% for two [2]. The median market forecast compiled by QUICK had been 2.3% [3].

Food excluding fresh food slowed to 2.2% from 2.9% in September, though prepared foods rose 4.2% and fried chicken 9.6% as costs were passed on [2][3]. Imported beef was 12.2% dearer, which the Nikkei links to drought in the United States [3].

The striking line is rice: 29.7% cheaper than a year earlier, the largest drop since comparable records began in January 1971 [2][3]. The Nikkei attributes it mainly to easing supply and demand and building inventories [3]. Last year's shortage was answered not by a speech but by stock in warehouses.

Reuters reported that the data cement the case for a near-term Bank of Japan rate rise; the yen traded around 159.50 per dollar [4]. Nothing here is investment advice.
