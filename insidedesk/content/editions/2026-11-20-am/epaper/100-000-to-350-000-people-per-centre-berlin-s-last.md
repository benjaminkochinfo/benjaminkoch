Before Friday's Bundesrat decision on whether to send the emergency-care reform to the mediation committee, the federal government offered the Länder a written protocol declaration, the Deutsches Ärzteblatt reported on Thursday [1]. In it, Berlin promises to require, in a later law, the agreement of each Land's hospital-planning authority when sites for integrated emergency centres are chosen, with arbitration if no agreement is reached [1].

The declaration also sets catchment sizes: each centre should serve at least 100,000 people, and at most 150,000 at hospitals with basic emergency care or 350,000 at those with extended or comprehensive emergency care; Hamburg wants a cap of 200,000 regardless of level [1]. On ambulance services, Berlin promises to clarify that necessary standby and investment costs count when fees are calculated; North Rhine-Westphalia and Hamburg want that clarified by 1 January 2027 [1].

The German County Association urged the Länder to stop the reform for now. "The reform must secure the financing of the ambulance service," said its president Achim Brötel (translated) [1]. The plenary outcome had not been published when this edition was written. Whatever it is, the arithmetic of catchments and standby costs is where your local emergency department will be decided.
