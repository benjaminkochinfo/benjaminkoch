COP31's second draft cover decision, published on Thursday afternoon in Antalya, runs to nine pages against Monday's 18 and contains no explicit reference to fossil fuels, RTÉ reported [1]. It also drops a proposed target of at least $120 billion a year in adaptation finance by 2035 [1]. The Guardian notes the hoped-for "transition away from fossil fuels" was replaced by an "irreversible global transition" to lower emissions and climate resilience, while carbon capture and storage stays in [2].

The draft still calls for adaptation finance to at least triple by 2035, without a baseline or dollar figure, and lists early-warning systems worldwide by 2027 and a 35% electricity share of final energy use by 2035 as areas for cooperation, not obligations [3]. Panama has written to the presidencies that it would formally object to any deal that fails eight tests, the Guardian reports [2]. "We didn't come here to beg," said Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings, chair of the African Group of Negotiators [2].

Draft texts are where ambition is traded for unanimity. The early-warning line is the one with a date and a deliverable; it is also the one most likely to save lives soonest.
