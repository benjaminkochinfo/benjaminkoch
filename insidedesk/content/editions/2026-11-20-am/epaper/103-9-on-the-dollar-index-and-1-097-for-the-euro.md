The dollar index stood near 103.9 on Friday, its strongest since early April 2025, after eight sessions of gains and on course for a fourth weekly rise, Reuters reported [1]. The rally accelerated on 10 November, when hotter-than-expected US inflation reinforced bets on a Federal Reserve rate rise; markets now price a quarter-point increase in December [1]. The euro last bought $1.0972, near its lowest since April 2025, and sterling sat near a one-year low at $1.3094 [1]. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said evidence did not suggest inflation was heading back to 2% on its own [1].

For a euro earner, a stronger dollar is a quiet surcharge on anything priced in dollars, from oil to software subscriptions. Nothing here is investment advice.
