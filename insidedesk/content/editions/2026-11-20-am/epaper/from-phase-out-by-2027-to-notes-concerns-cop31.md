The first draft of COP31's cover decision called on developed countries to phase out unilateral trade measures, such as the European Union's carbon border tax, by 2027, the Hindustan Times reported [1]. The second draft, released on Thursday, only notes concerns about measures with negative cross-border impacts, particularly for developing countries [1].

That shift matters to anyone who trades steel, cement or fertiliser with the EU. A deadline in a UN text would not have repealed the EU's mechanism, but it would have given its critics a date to point to. A note of concern is a sentence to quote, not a calendar.

The draft is not final [2]. The new text is nine pages against Monday's 18, and drops other contested items too, including all references to fossil fuels and a $120 billion adaptation-finance target [2]. Negotiators were still consulting on the last scheduled day in Antalya [1].

For you, the lesson from trade diplomacy is the usual one: watch verbs. "Phase out by" binds a calendar. "Notes" binds nobody.
