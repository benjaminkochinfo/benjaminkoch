A study published on Thursday in the CDC journal Preventing Chronic Disease used parent reports for 17,394 adolescents aged 12 to 17 from the 2019–2024 National Health Interview Survey [1]. Frequent anxiety symptoms were reported for 14.5% in 2019, 22.8% in 2022 and 18.2% in 2024; frequent depression symptoms for 6.6%, 9.0% and 6.0% [1].

Regional differences were significant for 17 of 21 indicators [1]. Frequent anxiety ranged from 16.4% in the South to 21.2% in the Midwest, frequent depression from 6.3% to 8.8% in the same two regions, and witnessed neighbourhood violence from 3.5% in the Northeast to 10.6% in the Midwest [1]. Lack of health insurance ranged from 1.8% in the Northeast to 7.5% in the South [1].

Two calm readings follow. First, depression symptoms are back below their 2019 level, and anxiety is well off its peak: the post-pandemic wave receded. Second, it did not recede everywhere equally, and the authors call for region-specific responses [1]. National averages are useful for headlines; services are built locally.
