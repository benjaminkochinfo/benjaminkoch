Japan's government scaled back its subsidies for electricity and gas from September, and October's consumer prices show the effect, the Nikkei reported on Friday [1]. Electricity bills, which had been 2.4% cheaper than a year earlier in September, were 1.6% dearer in October; city gas inflation widened from 0.2% to 5.3% [1]. Energy added 0.2 points to overall inflation [1]. From July to September the government had cut a standard household's combined electricity and gas bills by about ¥5,000 [1]. Petrol, still subsidised, was 2.3% cheaper [1].

Jiji adds the second cause: higher crude prices as tensions in the Middle East rose, which turned energy prices positive again [2].

The lesson travels well. A subsidy does not remove a price; it moves who pays it, and for how long. When it ends, the index tells you within a month. Germany's electricity-tax cut, debated this week, will be read the same way one day.
