Storms brought 25 to 100 millimetres of rain to most Queensland cropping regions in the week to 18 November, likely disrupting planting and harvesting further, Australia's agricultural economists at ABARES reported on Thursday [1]. ABARES's eight-day outlook has 10 to 100 mm for New South Wales and Victoria over 19 to 26 November [1]. ABARES says such falls would support pasture and summer crops but "further delay winter crop harvest activities and increase the risk of grain quality downgrades" [1].

Rain at harvest is the quiet climate story: no disaster, just wheat that sits wet in the paddock and is sold as feed instead of bread grade. Water allocation prices in the Victorian Murray fell from $279 to $255 per megalitre over the week [1].
