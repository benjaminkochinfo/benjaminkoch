A North Rhine-Westphalia resolution on Friday's Bundesrat agenda asks the federal government to finish the details of the KRITIS umbrella law in force since March 2026, set risk-based minimum standards for physical protection and fund resilience measures [1]. It cites cyberattacks, disinformation, unauthorised drone flights and sabotage of energy sites [1]. A Bavarian motion proposes video surveillance up to 50 metres beyond a site's boundary, storage of footage for up to a year and the use of artificial intelligence [1].

The NRW text also worries that technical data on energy sites in open map projects such as OpenStreetMap could endanger them, and wants such data published only as far as strictly necessary [1]. Protection matters; so does a public that can see where its grid runs. That balance is worth arguing in the open.
