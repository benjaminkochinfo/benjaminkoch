**The podium.** On Friday the Bundesrat, by a clear majority, sent the federal bill on children's start chances and Kita quality to the mediation committee and asked for a fundamental rework [1][2]. The argument is old and specific. When the Länder complained in October that Berlin was imposing duties without paying for them, the federal government, as the Bundesrat summarises it, rejected the financial demands and countered that the costs, if anything, are more likely overestimated (translated) [1]. The education minister Karin Prien (CDU) signalled willingness to compromise but stressed that funding day care is fundamentally the job of the Länder and municipalities, MDR reported [2].

**The print.** The bill itself contains a schedule. Berlin would give the Länder about €9.2 billion over 2027 to 2034: €1.493 billion in each of the first two years, €1.243 billion in 2029, then €993 million a year from 2030 to 2034 [1]. The support ends in 2034 [2]. The duties do not: every child's development would be assessed by the age of four at the latest, with targeted support built on that, and Kitas with many children in difficult circumstances would get more staff [1].

**The gap.** Both statements can be true at once, and that is the point. Costs can be overestimated in 2027 and still exceed €993 million a year in 2031, because the assessments, the extra staff and the follow-up support are permanent while the federal share is degressive and time-limited [1]. The Länder call this the principle that whoever orders, pays [1]. Manuela Schwesig (SPD), Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania's premier, warned that money for early education could fall away because of the new tasks [2].

**For you.** If you have a child under four, nothing changes yet. The bill is stopped for now [2]. The figure to watch when the committee meets is not the total but the last line of the schedule.
