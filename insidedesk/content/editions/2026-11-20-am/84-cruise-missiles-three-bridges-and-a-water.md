Russia launched an hours-long missile and drone attack on Ukraine overnight into Friday [1]. Ukraine's Air Force said Russian forces fired an unspecified number of ballistic missiles, 84 cruise missiles and 404 attack drones, and that air defences shot down or suppressed three ballistic missiles, 79 cruise missiles and 380 drones; strikes were recorded at 23 locations [1].

At least eight people were killed, six of them in Kyiv, according to Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi, and at least 28 injured [1]. At least three employees of Kyiv's water utility were among the dead [1]. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Southern and Darnytskyi bridges were damaged and asked residents not to drive [1]. The power company DTEK reported outages in five districts, with some supply restored by 09:30 local time [1].

The attack reached far beyond the capital. Lviv's mayor said two people died there and a ticket terminal at a railway station took a direct hit; in Kryvyi Rih, a drone attack cut power to boiler houses, water pumping stations and hospitals; in Kharkiv a hospital was struck and at least four people were injured [1]. An energy official told the Kyiv Independent, on condition of anonymity, that Russia targeted the Western Ukrainian 750-kV substation, one of the network's most critical nodes; the extent of damage has not been made public [1].

It came a day after Vladimir Putin vowed retaliation for Ukraine's strike on the Crimean Bridge, and while he met Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen [1]. An unidentified "flying object" fell near Lipowiec in eastern Poland, Polish authorities said [1].

For you, the numbers worth holding are the boring ones: how fast the water and heat come back, how quickly a bridge reopens. They measure a state's resilience better than any count of missiles.
