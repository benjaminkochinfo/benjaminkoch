Long-term care in Germany has become dearer faster than demography alone would explain, the federal government says: the number of people needing care rose much more over the past decade than expected, the gap between income and spending is widening fast, and personal contributions keep rising [1]. Its answer is the care reorganisation bill, on which the Länder could comment in the Bundesrat on Friday [1].

**What the bill does.** The contribution ceiling of long-term care insurance would rise by €300 a month, as it already has in health insurance [1]. From 1 January 2028 a surcharge of 0.52% would apply for co-insured spouses and partners, with exceptions such as parents of disabled children and relatives caring at home [1]. The surcharge for childless members would rise by 0.3 points to 0.9% from 2027 [1]. Insured people aged 60 and over would get a "Check-up 60+" screening [1]. The €131 monthly relief amount now available in care grades 1 to 5 would be abolished and replaced from grade 2 by a "social-space budget" that people can spend on what they need; for under-25s it would be up to €300 a month [1]. Existing care grades are protected [1].

**What the committees found.** The Bundesrat's health committee asked that non-insurance benefits such as pension contributions for family carers be paid from taxes rather than contributions [1]. It criticised that grade 1 would lose the relief amount, which it said contradicts the bill's preventive aim and burdens family carers [1]. The labour and social affairs committee warned the change could "massively weaken" low-threshold everyday support, and, with the finance committee, complained that extra costs for Länder and municipalities in social assistance for care are not covered by Berlin [1].

**For you.** If someone in your family has care grade 1 and uses the €131 for a cleaner or a day out, that is the line to watch in the Bundestag. The rest of the reform is about who pays; that one is about who loses a service.
