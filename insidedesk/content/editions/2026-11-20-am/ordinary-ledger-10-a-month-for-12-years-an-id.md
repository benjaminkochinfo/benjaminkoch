**Line 1: the early-start pension.** The Länder approved it on Friday [1][2]. From the birth cohort of 2020, children get a state payment of €10 a month into an individual, capital-funded retirement depot from their sixth to their eighteenth birthday, €1,440 per child in total, with no application needed [1]. Parents can top up by up to €6,840 a calendar year [1]. If they open no depot, the federal government invests the money collectively, administered by the Bundesbank [1][2]. It is paid out only from age 65, and cannot be borrowed against, sold or seized [1].

**Line 2: fewer trips to the town hall.** Under the digital identities law, which passed unanimously, anyone who last applied for their identity card at 70 or older can keep using it indefinitely [1]. After a move you will no longer need to update your address in your passport; registering the move with the authorities is still required [1]. The government expects fewer in-person appointments as a result [1].

**Line 3: the wallet.** The same law brings the European digital identity wallet, which from 2 January 2027 is meant to let you prove your identity and age, open a bank account and sign contracts from your phone [1]. It is free and voluntary, and analogue services stay [1].

**Line 4: the ceilings.** The Bundesrat also approved next year's social-insurance thresholds [1]. The monthly ceiling for pension contributions rises from €8,450 to €8,850; for health and long-term care insurance from €5,812.50 to €6,375 [1]. That last jump includes an extra €300 a month on top of the usual wage-linked adjustment [1]. Earnings above the ceiling are not charged contributions [1].

**For you.** If you have a child born in 2020, there is nothing to sign: the €10 arrives by itself [1]. If you have a parent over 70 dreading the Bürgeramt, the card in their wallet may already be their last one.
