The declaration that Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping released after meeting in Shenzhen on Thursday has four numbered points and fits on one screen [1]. The three committed to "reform the UN", said they "plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations", agreed that the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction", and reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought", with a dialogue on nuclear risks to follow [1].

Read it the way a purchasing department would. Point two is the only one with a physical product in it, and it has no volume, no date and no buyer [1]. Point three narrows what courts may do to state officials; point one asks the UN to change without saying how [1]. Point four is the most valuable and the oldest: the nuclear sentence has been said before, and is worth saying again.

What the page does not mention is Ukraine. Overnight, Russia launched 84 cruise missiles, 404 drones and an unspecified number of ballistic missiles at Ukraine, according to Ukraine's Air Force; at least eight people were killed and three Kyiv bridges were damaged [2]. The day before, Putin had promised retaliation for the strike on the Crimean Bridge and said he would not negotiate ceasefires on energy or maritime trade, while Volodymyr Zelensky met Coalition of the Willing leaders in Berlin, who called for exactly those ceasefires [2].

Markets read the summit too, and drew a narrower conclusion. RTHK reported that sentiment in Hong Kong and Shanghai was weighed down by signs that Washington is tightening tech curbs on China "despite meetings between the leaders" this week [3]. A handshake is a photograph; an export rule is a filing.

For you, the useful document from Thursday is not the declaration but the list of what it does not settle: no ceasefire, no fuel quantities, no tariff schedule. Keep that list. It is what next week's news will be measured against.
