At noon on Friday Brent crude stood at $90.67 a barrel, against $92.72 at the same time on Thursday, according to fastenergy's market data [1]. Early in Asian trading it had briefly risen to $92.77 on supply worries [2]. Either way, Reuters reported that Brent was on course for a weekly fall of about 3.5% and US crude for almost 6%, the third losing week in a row for both [2].

**The fear is real.** The Iran-aligned Houthi movement fired missiles at Riyadh again, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said on Thursday that two tankers had hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz and were burning [2]. UOB analysts count attacks on tankers in the strait at "roughly one incident per day in November" [2]. Rakuten Securities' Satoru Yoshida said the Riyadh attacks prompted buying [2].

**The flow is realer.** The reason the price is falling anyway is plain: crude shipments through Hormuz have returned to roughly pre-war levels, and traders expect a supply surplus [2]. fastenergy's analyst calls supply from the Middle East "astonishingly stable" despite everything (translated) [1]. Saxo added that risk-off sentiment from the AI sell-off is weighing on demand expectations [2].

**At your tank.** German heating oil averaged €157.80 per 100 litres on Friday, up from €156.70 on Thursday, as the dealer passed on the slightly firmer start to the day [1]. The euro, at about $1.098 in the morning, did not help much [1].

**For you.** Days like this produce two kinds of headline: attacks, which are vivid, and shipping volumes, which are not. The second kind is the one your heating bill follows. If you are filling a tank this winter, watch the weekly averages rather than the alarming nights.
