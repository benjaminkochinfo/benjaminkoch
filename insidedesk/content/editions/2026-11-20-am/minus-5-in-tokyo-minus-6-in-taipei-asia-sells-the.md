This is the morning edition, arriving at lunchtime. By the time you read it, Asia has closed, Europe is half a day in, and New York's futures are where the morning's arguments go to be settled. So instead of telling you what might happen at the open, here is what did happen, in the order it happened, and what it is made of.

**New York first.** On Thursday the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 1,101.11 points, or 2.19%, to 49,097.06, its first close below 50,000 since June [1]. The S&P 500 fell 2.02% to 7,175.83 and the Nasdaq Composite 2.25% to 23,879.20, after the Nasdaq had been up as much as 2% earlier in the session [1]. CNBC noted that the averages hit their lows at the very moment Anthropic's shares began trading; the stock opened at $111.50, 34% above its offer price, and closed up 17% [1]. A long-awaited listing turned into the moment people chose to take profits. That is not a judgement on one company. It is what a crowded room does when the door everyone was watching finally opens.

**Then Asia, harder.** Tokyo's Nikkei 225 fell 3,007 points, or 5.06%, to 56,436, with SoftBank Group down 14.48%, Advantest 10.32%, Kioxia 11.66% and Tokyo Electron 7.06% [2]. The index lost 7.7% over the week [2]. Seoul's Kospi closed 5.21% lower at 4,869, with Samsung Electronics down 7.01% and SK Hynix 5.92% [2]. Taiwan's Taiex finished 6.23% lower at 36,033.29, and on the exchange's own data 179 listed stocks closed limit-down; the non-electronics sub-index fell 6.12%, almost as much as electronics at 6.26% [3]. Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 1.9% and Shanghai's Composite 1.22%, its lowest close since July 2025 [2].

**What was actually new overnight.** One thing in the Asian tape was not mood. New US Commerce Department rules took effect on Thursday, and TSMC, according to two people cited by Reuters, has told staff to halt affected orders from Chinese chip designers on processes of 16 nanometres and below unless customers obtain US export licences [4]. TSMC said it "will comply with the new Bureau of Industry and Security regulations as applicable" [4]. RTHK reported that this weighed on Chinese chipmakers and on Hong Kong-listed AI model developers [2]. A rule with a date and a process node is a cost you can model. Much of the rest of the day was the price of not knowing.

**Frankfurt by lunch.** The DAX opened 1.09% lower at 25,187.51 after Thursday's 25,463.81 close [6], and Reuters reported Infineon, Aixtron and Suss Microtec down by roughly 5 to 6% early on [5]. Later in the morning the index slipped below 25,000, about 2% under Thursday's close [7]. It set a record of 27,001 only last week [9]. The CapTrader analyst Timo Emden put it in one line: against AI doubts there "seems to be no suitable medicine at the moment" (translated) [5].

**The other price that came out at 08:00.** While screens were red, Destatis published something slower. German producer prices were 8.1% higher in October than a year earlier, the steepest rise since February 2023, and 1.4% higher than in September [8]. Energy was 18.7% dearer than a year ago; without energy, producer prices rose 3.6% [8]. This is the invoice a factory receives before it sends yours. It moves monthly, it does not care about IPOs, and it will still be there when the Nikkei has recovered or not. The Money desk has the detail and a chart.

**Where that leaves you.** Days like this are loud because the numbers are big and come in quick succession. The useful habit is to sort them: a share index tells you what people will pay for future profits this afternoon; a producer-price index tells you what it costs to make things this month. The first is having a bad week. The second has been having a long year. Neither is a reason to do anything before lunch.
