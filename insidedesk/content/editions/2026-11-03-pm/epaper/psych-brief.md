Monash University researchers followed 7,434 Australians aged 55 to 75 for nearly two decades in the HILDA household survey [1]. Loneliness did not change in the three years before retirement or the three years after, said the lead author, Johannes Kunz [1]. That held across income, wealth and job type, with or without grandchildren, volunteering or clubs [1]. About one in six (17.5%) reported often feeling very lonely [1]. Loneliness rises steadily from the late 60s, Kunz said, and losing a spouse matters more than leaving a job, so support should follow later life, not the retirement date [1]. The paper is in Scientific Reports. For pension-age debates, one worry can be crossed off the list.
