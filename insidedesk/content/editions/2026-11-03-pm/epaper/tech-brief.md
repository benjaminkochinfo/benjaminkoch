On a day when European tech shares fell 1.6% on AI worries, the AI winner in London was a power company. Drax jumped 9% after partnering with Brookfield and the AI infrastructure platform Radiant to develop a data centre in a project that could attract more than £20 billion ($26.8 billion) of investment [1].

Note the verb: "could". The scarce input in compute is increasingly not chips but a site with a grid connection and steady power, which a power station already has. "All the investments are fuelled by the need for more compute," said Gilles Guibout of BNP Paribas Asset Management, while allowing that some projects could be delayed [1].
