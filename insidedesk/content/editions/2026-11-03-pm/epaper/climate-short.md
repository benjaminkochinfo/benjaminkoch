Arizona is not where you expect a flood. On Sunday night, Beaver Creek broke its banks in Camp Verde and more than 100 homes were under water; as it receded on Monday, most were destroyed, AZFamily reported [1]. More than 4 inches of rain fell in parts of the Phoenix area and 6 inches in the north, two people died in Flagstaff, and damage stretches across Yavapai, Coconino and Navajo counties [1]. NBC News described record rainfall in "usually dry Arizona" [2].

The emergency machinery is turning. Governor Katie Hobbs issued a statewide emergency declaration on Monday, and the governors of New Mexico and Colorado asked President Trump to declare a major disaster, which unlocks federal money [2]. In Colorado, homes along the Animas River faced evacuation [2].

The line that matters most is the plainest one: many residents in the area do not have flood insurance, and a fundraising page is open for families along the creek [1]. One homeowner walked through 8 inches of mud and said, "It could be worse" [1].

That is the quiet risk of a dry place. Cover is bought for the hazard people remember. If your home sits near a creek, wherever you live, it is worth reading what your policy says about water before the next storm reads it for you.
