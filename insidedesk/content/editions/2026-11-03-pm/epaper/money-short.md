German government bonds fell on Tuesday morning, which means yields rose. The Bund future slipped 0.13% to 122.5 and the 10-year yield climbed to 3.31% [1], then to about 3.33% by early afternoon, up from 3.29% at Monday's close [2]. The trigger was oil: reports of US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz pushed crude up again, and with it inflation expectations and the odds of another ECB hike [1].

Bond desks were also looking across the Atlantic, though not at the winner. Commerzbank analysts wrote that "Democratic control of Congress should be Treasury-positive on balance", because control of the budget in the House would narrow the room for extra spending [1]. In the same breath they flagged the cost: a higher risk of another government shutdown on 11 December [1].

That is the useful habit for tonight. An election changes who signs the bills; the bond market cares about whether the bills are paid on time. The 11 December funding date will still be there whoever wins, and it is in the diary already.

For a saver in euros, 3.3% on a ten-year Bund is the benchmark against which every fixed deposit offer should be measured. Nothing here is investment advice.
