A survey of 1,000 US adults by the Sylvia Brafman Mental Health Center found that 49% said worries about AI had harmed their mental health or well-being. Among Gen Z respondents the figure was 71%, against 28% of baby boomers [1].

The detail worth noticing is about behaviour. 64% said they had done something in the past year to prove their value, such as working extra hours, taking on unpaid duties or answering messages after hours. 42% said AI made them faster, while 33% said they were then expected to produce more [1].

These are self-reported answers, as the researchers point out, not measured working conditions [1]. Still, a clear job description and a manager who explains the plan cost little to provide.
