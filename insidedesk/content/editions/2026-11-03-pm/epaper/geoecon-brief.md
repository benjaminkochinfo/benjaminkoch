China exported 6,165 tonnes of rare-earth permanent magnets in September, the most this year and 23% more than in August, according to Shanghai Metals Market's reading of customs data [1]. The EU took 2,454 tonnes, up 20.5% and its most since February, after an October deal promising a licence "green channel" for European buyers [1]. Japan's share fell 19.1% to 172 tonnes amid a diplomatic impasse [1]. US imports for January to September were about 15% below the same months of 2024, the Silverado Policy Accelerator found [1]. The magnets go into car motors, wind turbines and robots. October's customs figures are due in the coming days, and they will show whether September was a backlog clearing or a new normal [1].
