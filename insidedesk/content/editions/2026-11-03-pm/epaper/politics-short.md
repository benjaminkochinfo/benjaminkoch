Volodymyr Zelenskyy flew to Berlin today on a trip that had not been announced, and stood beside Friedrich Merz at the chancellery [1]. The words were familiar. Merz said Ukraine "can continue to rely on Germany", with more help on air defence, energy and winter aid in the coming months [1]. Zelenskyy said that while many eyes are on America's election, "the role of Europe – and specifically Germany – remains crucial" [1].

The dates are what you can hold them to. On Wednesday the European Commission hosts a meeting with Ukraine, the United States, France, Germany, the UK, NATO and the World Bank, at national-security-adviser level, on post-war reconstruction and "prosperity", spokesperson Paula Pinho said [2]. On 10–11 November, US envoy Steve Witkoff has announced talks in Abu Dhabi that the Kremlin has confirmed [1][2]. On 19 November the coalition of the willing meets in Berlin, and Merz said that meeting will decide further support for Ukraine in 2027 [1].

Zelenskyy called a Russian suggestion of talks in Moscow "counterproductive" [1]. Merz said it was "crucial for Russia to finally engage in serious negotiations" [1]. Speeches are cheap; meetings with agendas, minutes and a World Bank seat at the table are not. Three of them are now in the diary.
