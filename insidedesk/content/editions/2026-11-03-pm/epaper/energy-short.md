The grid regulator has put a price on Germany's insurance against windless winter nights. The Bundesnetzagentur awarded 11 contracts for about 5,160 megawatts of new plants in its first auction for backup capacity, tagesschau reported on Tuesday [1]. All of it went to gas-fired projects, most of them to RWE and LEAG [2].

The useful numbers are the dull ones. The average award was €155,296 per megawatt of creditable capacity per year, well below the cap of €244,000 [1]. That comes to about €800 million a year, and about €12 billion over the 15-year contracts [1]. The plants are meant to be available from November 2031, and the cost will be passed to electricity customers through a levy [1].

Competition did some work. For the 4,500 megawatts on offer, 41 bids worth almost 12,000 megawatts came in [1]. The award overshot the target because the law counts the bid that first crosses the line [1].

The next round, another 4,500 megawatts, is due to be announced on 10 November, with bids due on 29 December [2]. Economy minister Katherina Reiche called the result "a great success" [2]; the Greens called it market power for the old coal companies [1]. Both can be checked in 2031, on the invoice.
