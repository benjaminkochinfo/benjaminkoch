The rule decided the result before the bids did. Plants in Germany's first backup auction had to deliver 80% of their capacity for at least ten hours in a row, which made gas far more competitive than battery storage, Clean Energy Wire reported [1]. Battery projects bid; the economy ministry said none "prevailed in the price competition" [1].

Some winners are not new plants at all: LEAG will convert lignite units at Jänschwalde and Boxberg to gas [1]. Converted coal units run at 40 to 45% efficiency against about 60% for modern gas plants, the Frankfurter Allgemeine reported, as cited by Clean Energy Wire [1].

The design changes next spring. A 2,000-megawatt round in May 2027 drops the ten-hour rule [2]. You will be able to see then what batteries bid when the rules let them.
