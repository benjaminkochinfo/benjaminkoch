Sweden's parliament today rejected the Centre Party leader, Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist, as prime minister by 324 votes to 25 [1]. She had said herself that she had no chance. It is the second failed attempt to form a government in the 51 days since the election [1]. The rules are plain: after four failed votes, Sweden holds a snap election. The interim centre-right prime minister, Ulf Kristersson, is expected to lead the next attempt, though his outgoing coalition is also short of a majority [1]. That is not chaos. A constitution that counts the attempts and names what happens at four is doing exactly what it was written to do.
