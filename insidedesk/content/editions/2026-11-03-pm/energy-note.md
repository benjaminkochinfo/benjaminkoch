The streak is over, though only just. Today's day-ahead auction set an average of €248.99 per megawatt hour for Germany and Luxembourg for delivery on Wednesday 4 November, the desk's average of the 24 hourly results that SMARD published this afternoon [1]. That is €3.86 below Tuesday's €252.85 and the first delivery day under €250 since 28 October [1]. The six days in between all cleared above it, peaking at €314.85 on Thursday 29 October [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-03pm]]

**Don't mistake a dip for a turn.** Wednesday's hourly prices still run from €209.12 for the 03:00 hour to €310.92 for 17:00 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the average is still about 61% above the €155.11 of Tuesday 27 October, the last day before the calm set in [1]. You are not looking at cheap power. You are looking at slightly less dear power.

**The turbines came back on land.** In the first 15 hours of Tuesday, to 15:00, onshore wind generated 132.8 gigawatt hours and offshore wind 61.2, SMARD's provisional figures show [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is 194.0 gigawatt hours, almost exactly the 195.5 that all German wind produced across the whole of Monday [1]. Onshore alone has already beaten Monday's full-day 73.7 by four-fifths [1].

**Gas has barely stepped back.** Natural gas plants produced 193.9 gigawatt hours in the same 15 hours, against 316.3 for all of Monday [1]. Desk arithmetic: at that hourly rate a full day would come to about 310, hardly less than Monday. Lignite gave a little more ground: 153.8 gigawatt hours to 15:00, against 276.1 for Monday, a pace of about 246 for the day, roughly 11% less [1]. More wind so far has meant fewer fossil hours at the margin, not an empty gas fleet. These figures are provisional and will be revised [1].

The quiet lesson is the oldest one in this market: the auction prices the weather that traders expect, not the speeches made about it. Nothing here is investment advice.
