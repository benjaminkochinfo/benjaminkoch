On Thursday the Bank of England is expected to raise Bank Rate to 4.0% from 3.75%. Economists polled by Reuters expect it, and it would be the first increase since August 2023 [1]. The reason is energy. Consumer prices rose 3.5% in the year to September, against the Bank's July forecast of 3.0%, and in September it warned that inflation would rise above 4% early next year [1].

The Committee is not of one mind. It voted 6–3 to hold in September, and the Reuters poll points to 7–2 for a hike this time, with the external members Alan Taylor and Swati Dhingra seen as the likeliest to vote for no change [1]. "Their hands are increasingly being forced, whether they like it or not," said Stefan Koopman of Rabobank [1].

The interesting part is what comes next. Markets price three to four more quarter-point hikes in 2027, which would take Bank Rate to between 4.75% and 5.0% [1]. Even the hawks may not want to sign up to that. Oxford Economics thinks the minutes, or the Governor, Andrew Bailey, may signal that the rate is unlikely to reach the level priced in [1]. Catherine Mann has argued that part of that pricing is a risk premium the Bank created itself by waiting and seeing [1].

There is a quieter test as well: the Bank's first assessment of the budget John Healey presented last week, whose help for households and drivers economists expect to shave only a little off inflation [1].
