A cross-sectional study in BMJ Mental Health, drawn from the 2025 OxWell Student Survey, asked 5,437 adolescents aged 11 to 18 about hair pulling, skin picking and nail biting [1]. The headline is reassuring: 58.5% reported at least one such body-focused repetitive behaviour, so it is ordinary, not exotic [1]. Nail biting was most common at 43.6%, followed by skin picking at 31.3% and hair pulling at 14.5% [1]. The habits travel together: 84% of hair-pullers and 76% of skin-pickers reported at least one other [1].

**The annex is in the distress.** Among those with the behaviours, 22.2% reported moderate-to-high distress, and 3.3% of the whole sample reported the highest level [1]. The authors' plain summary: around one in ten adolescents reported distressing behaviours [1]. Those were more common in girls and in trans and gender-diverse young people than in boys [1].

**What travels with it** is the figure to sit with. Distressing behaviours were reported by 6.3% of teenagers whose internalising symptoms were in the normal range, against 34.2% of those in the clinical range [1]. Among teenagers who were never bullied the share was 9.6%; among those bullied more than weekly it was 27.1% [1]. In the adjusted analysis, internalising symptoms were the strongest predictor, with an odds ratio of 1.97 per 10-point increase, while gender and frequent bullying stayed independently linked [1]. A gap between neurodivergent (20%) and neurotypical (10%) teenagers disappeared after adjustment [1].

The study is a snapshot, so it cannot say whether the picking follows the anxiety or the bullying, or the other way round [1]. Its clinical point is modest and useful: "Assessing distress, rather than behaviour alone, may improve identification of adolescents with unmet clinical need" [1].

**For you.** If a teenager at home bites or picks, the habit alone is common and usually harmless. The useful question is not "why do you do that?" but "does it bother you?", and if the answer is yes, whether something else is weighing on them too.
