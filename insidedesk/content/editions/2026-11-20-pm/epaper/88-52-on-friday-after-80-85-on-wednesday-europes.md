Europe's gas benchmark ended the working week going up. The Dutch TTF contract for December delivery stood at €88.52 per megawatt hour in Friday's intraday trading, up €4.06 or 4.8% from Thursday's €84.46 [1]. At 15:40 it had been at €87.32, Teleborsa reported [2]. The price becomes final only at the London close, so Friday's figure may still move [1].

The two-day rise follows a sharp dip. On Wednesday the contract fell 5.3% to €80.85, its lowest close of the month on the series, and on Monday it had already dropped 6.8% to €84.75 [1]. Desk arithmetic: from Wednesday's low to Friday's intraday price is a rise of 9.5% in two sessions [1].

Put the week together and the drama shrinks. Friday's €88.52 is 2.7% below the €90.95 of Friday 13 November and below the €94.86 of Monday 2 November [1]. Over the past twelve months the series has ranged between €78.08 and €103.94; Friday sits above 63% of those prices [1].

That is the sober reading: gas is not spiking, it is swinging, by 5% to 7% a day, inside a range that is high by pre-crisis standards. Swings matter for power, because gas plants often set Germany's evening electricity price. They matter less for households on fixed contracts, and more for anyone whose tariff is re-priced each month. Nothing here is investment advice.
