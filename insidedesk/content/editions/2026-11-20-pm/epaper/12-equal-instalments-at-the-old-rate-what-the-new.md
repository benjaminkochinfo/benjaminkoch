The new consumer-credit law also reaches the overdraft, the Dispo [1]. When granting one, banks must now check whether the customer could clear it, typically within 12 months [1]. If the bank cancels an overdraft, it must offer repayment in 12 equal monthly instalments at the existing interest rate [1]. An overdraft can now also be revoked, like other credit [1].

For anyone who falls behind on any loan, lenders must in suitable cases offer reasonable concessions, such as a longer term, a payment pause or lower interest, before enforcement, and must point to debt-counselling services; there is no right to a particular solution [1]. A loan can be revoked within 14 days, by letter, email or an online "revoke contract" button, but not by phone [1]. Keep the confirmation.
