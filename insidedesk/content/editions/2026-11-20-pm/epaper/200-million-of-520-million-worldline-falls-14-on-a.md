Shares in Worldline, the French payments group, fell 14% in Paris on Friday afternoon after the Financial Times reported on how the company presented its cash position, according to Investir [1]. The report says Worldline regularly delayed payments to its suppliers and asked clients for advances ahead of its financial publications, at least between 2021 and 2023 [1].

The figure that matters is in a PwC report prepared at the end of 2023, which, according to the account, put the effect of these practices at €200 million on a free cash flow reported at €520 million [1]. That is not a rounding difference: on those numbers, close to two-fifths of the headline would have come from timing rather than from business.

Investir is careful to note that such shifts can be legal and can flatter figures at a given moment without reflecting a lasting improvement in activity [1]. Worldline rejects the reading and says its accounts are "prepared in accordance with all applicable rules and are not affected by any irregularity" (translated) [1].

For readers, the lesson is general rather than about one company. Free cash flow is often treated as the honest number, the one that cannot be adjusted the way earnings can. It can. A payments business sits between merchants and their banks, so the timing of what it owes and what it is owed is the business itself; that makes the end-of-quarter calendar worth reading as closely as the total.

The sell-off came on a day when Paris was already lower: Investir had the CAC 40 down 1.2% and below 8,000 points in the afternoon, on course for its worst week since early March [1].
