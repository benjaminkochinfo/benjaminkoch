Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel used banking language for energy at the Frankfurt European Banking Congress on Friday. Europe's dependence on external suppliers remains a vulnerability and energy is too expensive, he said, Econostream reported [1]. "So, the portfolio has still in my interpretation concentration risk on fossil fuels," he said [1].

He welcomed the German government's decision to cut electricity taxation but said further action was needed: a more diversified energy portfolio, less fragmentation in grid infrastructure and more renewables [1]. He also pointed to an offsetting effect from the energy-tax decision on inflation early next year [1].

A supervisor who found that kind of concentration in a bank's loan book would ask for more capital or fewer exposures. Europe's version of that is grids and storage, and neither is built in a quarter.
