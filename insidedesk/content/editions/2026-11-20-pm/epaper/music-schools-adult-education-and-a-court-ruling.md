Bavaria, Saarland and Schleswig-Holstein presented a motion in Friday's Bundesrat plenary asking for the use of self-employed teachers to be made "legally reliable and practicable" [1]. Schools, universities, adult-education centres, music schools and cultural institutions depend on them, the three Länder argue [1].

The trigger is a stricter reading of the rules by the Federal Social Court and the umbrella organisations of the social-insurance funds [1]. Under it, almost all existing freelance teaching risks being classified as dependent employment and charged with social contributions [1]. The Länder say the institutions cannot carry those extra costs, and that teachers who are employed instead usually end up with lower net pay [1].

Their wish list is concrete. A federal law should draw the line between freelance and employed work in a way that is legally safe and light on bureaucracy [1]. Small-scale part-time teaching should be permanently exempt from pension, health, care and unemployment insurance [1]. Teaching artists should keep their status in the artists' social insurance scheme, the transition period should be extended by at least two years, and back payments of contributions for past years should be ruled out [1].

The motion now goes to the committees; the Bundesrat votes later on whether to adopt it [1]. For a Volkshochschule, the question is simple arithmetic: whether a Tuesday evening language class still costs what it did.
