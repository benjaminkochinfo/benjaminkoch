Russian drones flew a combined distance of more than 19,000 kilometres over Kyiv between 1 August and 20 October, nearly half the Earth's circumference, according to an analysis by the Ukrainian investigative outlet Texty [1]. In the same period of 2025 the figure was 4,300 kilometres, so this year's distance is more than four times larger [1].

More than 630 drones were detected over the city, twice the number a year earlier [1]. Each one also stayed longer: the average distance flown inside the city limits rose from 17 kilometres in 2025 to 30.5 kilometres this year [1]. The analysis reconstructed flight paths from monitoring-channel reports and counted only the parts of each route inside Kyiv [1].

The explanation offered is technical. Russia is using more jet-powered drones, which are faster, harder to intercept and change their flight patterns, so more of them get through the outer air defences and over the capital [1]. Ukraine is answering with faster interceptor drones and adapted electronic warfare; in October the then-head of the Brave1 defence initiative said more than 1,000 new interceptors had been ordered and 22 teams were working on high-speed models [1].

A longer track is not the same as a hit. But every extra kilometre over a city is time spent under an alert, and that is a cost a population pays even when the interceptor wins.
