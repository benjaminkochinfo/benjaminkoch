The world's olive oil output could reach a record 3.7 million tonnes in the 2026/27 season, according to preliminary figures in the Food and Agriculture Organization's latest food outlook, reported on Friday by the Spanish trade journal Mercacei [1]. Spain alone is expected to produce 1.6 million tonnes, 22.7% more than last season [1].

The reason is weather, in both directions. Favourable temperatures and abundant rain between March and May helped flowering and fruit set in Spain [1]. Turkey is expected to rebound to 400,000 tonnes on adequate water, and Greece and Portugal to recover on rain and, in Greece, little olive fly [1]. Italy may produce less: unusually hot, dry weather in July and August hurt rain-fed groves in the south [1]. Tunisia, after an exceptional 2025/26, is forecast to fall considerably, though still above its five-year average [1].

The FAO calls its numbers indicative at this stage, because late-summer heat can still cut yields and oil content [1]. Its broader point is less comforting than the headline: with production concentrated around the Mediterranean, irregular rain, heat waves and water stress will keep swinging harvests and prices from year to year [1]. Prices have already fallen to multi-year lows at the start of the season, which triggered a supply-management mechanism in Spain in early November [1]. A record harvest is good news for the shopper; for the grower on dry land it is one good year in a volatile series.
