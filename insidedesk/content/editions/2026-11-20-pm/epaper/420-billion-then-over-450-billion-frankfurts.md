Two central bankers spent part of Friday at the Frankfurt European Banking Congress talking about tanks, or rather about how to pay for them.

ECB president Christine Lagarde gave the numbers. EU countries spent around €420 billion on defence last year, a fifth more than the year before, and are set to spend over €450 billion this year, she said, citing the European Defence Agency [1]. Her point was what the money buys. Europe spends ten times less on defence research and development than the United States, relative to the size of its economy, and recent ECB analysis suggests each additional euro of public R&D spending can generate almost €2.50 of GDP over the long term [1]. Spent on research, defence money "can benefit the whole economy, whatever the security outlook," she said [1].

Bundesbank president Joachim Nagel went to the funding. "Defense, in my understanding, is a European public good," he said, according to Econostream, and reiterated his support for considering joint European defence financing under conditions [2]. He stressed that this would not relieve member states of their obligations under the Stability and Growth Pact and should not simply add European spending on top of national budgets: "Why not financing defense together on the European level? Why not?" [2]

The two lines fit together more neatly than they first appear. Joint money is easier to defend when it buys something every member shares, and research is the most shareable thing in a defence budget. The harder question is the one neither answered on Friday: who decides which projects get it.
