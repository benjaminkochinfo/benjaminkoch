Pancreatic cancer that has spread is among the hardest diagnoses in medicine; most patients survive months, not years [2]. That is the background against which updated results for daraxonrasib, a daily pill that shuts off RAS mutations found in most pancreatic cancers, were presented at the EORTC-NCI-AACR symposium in Barcelona on Thursday [1][2].

In 61 newly treated patients with metastatic disease, given the pill plus standard chemotherapy (gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel), the confirmed response rate was 60.7%, and 8.2% had complete responses, about one in 12 [1]. Disease was controlled in 90.2%, median progression-free survival was 12.8 months, and an estimated 76.6% were alive after one year, with median follow-up of 18.3 months [1]. Cancer Research UK called it "the biggest leap forward in decades" [2].

The caveats are in the design. This is an open-label phase 1/2 study without a control group, and the figures come from the company's release [1]. Doctors told the BBC it is unclear how long the effect lasts and that it should not be seen as a cure [2]. The randomised phase 3 trial, RASolute 303, which compares the combination and the pill alone against chemotherapy, is still running [1]. Until it reports, the honest summary is: rare good news in this cancer, with real numbers, and the decisive number still missing.
