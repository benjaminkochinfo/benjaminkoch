The Bundesrat on Friday approved the regulation setting Germany's social-insurance thresholds for 2027; it is due to take effect on 1 January [1].

The contribution ceiling for pension insurance, the monthly pay above which no pension contributions are charged, rises from €8,450 to €8,850 [1]. For statutory health and long-term-care insurance, the ceiling rises from €5,812.50 to €6,375 a month [1]. The compulsory-insurance threshold, above which employees may switch to private health insurance, goes from €77,400 to €84,150 a year [1].

The thresholds follow wages with a delay; the government puts 2025 wage growth at 4.38% [1]. The health figures rise further than that because, under the law to stabilise statutory health contribution rates, both the health contribution ceiling and the compulsory-insurance threshold get an extra €300 a month in 2027 on top of the regular adjustment [1].

What it means in practice: anyone earning above this year's health ceiling will pay health and care contributions on a larger slice of pay from January, and so will their employer. Those thinking of moving to private insurance need a higher salary to qualify. For everyone below the ceilings, nothing changes on this line of the payslip, which is the quiet majority of the ledger.
