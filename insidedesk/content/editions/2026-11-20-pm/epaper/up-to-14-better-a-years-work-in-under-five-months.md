OpenAI has published new details of its partnership with AMD: engineers used undisclosed AI models to help optimise the chipmaker's upcoming flagship GPU and CPU designs [1]. The headline figure is an improvement of up to 14% in PPA (power, performance and area) for the GPU circuitry, with unspecified single-digit PPA gains on the processor [1].

The work was done in SystemVerilog RTL, the language used to describe the circuits of high-end chips; OpenAI says its model found "further improvements by rewriting the code that describes the chip's circuits" [1]. It claims the effort condensed a year's worth of conventional design work into "under 5 months", and that the model reasoned "at the level of an expert microarchitect" [1].

Two cautions. First, these are OpenAI's own figures about OpenAI's own models, reported by Tom's Hardware; there is no independent measurement yet, and the chips themselves have not shipped [1]. Second, PPA is a design-stage metric. Whether a 14% better circuit becomes a 14% better product depends on the foundry, the memory, the software and the price.

The timing is still worth noting. On the same Friday AMD's shares were down 9.1% by 17:11 CET in a broad chip sell-off [2]. The market was marking down what chips will earn; this announcement is about how fast and cheaply they can be designed. Those are different questions, and only the second one has an engineering answer.
