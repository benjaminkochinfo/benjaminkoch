Europe closed where it had been heading all day, only further. At the Xetra close the DAX was down 2.45% at 24,839.65 points and the MDax 2.54% at 28,756.57 [9][10][11]. Milan's FTSE MIB lost 2.48% to 48,703.72 [12], Paris's CAC 40 1.89% to 7,927.75, closing on its low of the day [13], Madrid's IBEX 35 1.63% and London's FTSE 100 a more cautious 1.02% at 10,346.34 [9][15]. The broad Stoxx Europe 600 ended 1.89% lower at 626.34 [14]. Every main index finished in the red.

[[chart:europe-friday-20pm]]

**The week, in one subtraction.** From last Friday's close the DAX lost 7.1% and the MDax about 6.3% over the five sessions, the Stoxx 600 5.7% and the CAC 40 5.3% [10][16][14][13]; finanzen.net had counted 6.68% off the DAX for the week by 15:41 CET [2]. Reuters had flagged by mid-morning that a Stoxx 600 loss of more than 5% would be its worst week since the week ended 6 March [3]; Investir said the same of the CAC 40 [4]. The Stoxx 600 is still up 5.8% for the year at Friday's close [9], down from 6.2% in Reuters' mid-morning count [3]. The year's gains are smaller; they are not gone.

**What was sold, and what was bought.** The late selling was concentrated. ANSA's late snapshot at 17:09 CET listed Soitec down 11.29%, ASML 9.56%, Aixtron 9.35% and STMicroelectronics 9.3%, with Prysmian, which makes cabling for servers and data centres, down 6.29% [1]. Banks followed, with Société Générale down 3.63% and BBVA 3% [1]. The money did not leave the building. It went into government bonds: the German ten-year yield fell 10.4 basis points to 3.3% and the Italian ten-year 8 points to 4.39% [1]. Gold, the usual shelter, was not one: it fell 3.98% to $3,586.9 an ounce [1]. Reuters had Nestlé, Unilever and British American Tobacco up more than 1% each in the morning [3].

**New York's start.** Wall Street opened lower but split. At the bell the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.20% at 23,592.67, the S&P 500 0.35% at 7,150.39 and the Dow 0.05% at 49,071.82, Reuters reported [5]. By 16:15 CET the 10-year Treasury yield had dropped almost seven basis points to 4.98%, and the University of Michigan's November consumer sentiment index rose to 51.5, its highest since August, with one-year inflation expectations down to 3.7% from 4.7% [6]. ANSA's 17:09 reading had the Dow up 0.35% and the Nasdaq down 2.3% [1]: old economy bid, new economy offered.

**The plain reading.** Friday also happens to be monthly options expiry in the US [7], which tends to add volume rather than meaning. The meaningful part of the day is the bond move. When investors pay more for a ten-year Bund on a day with no new German data, they are telling you less about Germany than about their own nerves.
