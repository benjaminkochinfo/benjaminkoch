Saturday's power is settled, and it is cheaper. For delivery on Saturday 21 November, the German-Luxembourg day-ahead auction averages €187.88 per megawatt hour, according to Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts, which republishes the Federal Network Agency's market data [1]. That is 12.4% below Friday's €214.51 and the lowest daily average since Wednesday's €146.25 [1][2].

The discount sits in the middle of the day. The cheapest hour is 13:00 to 14:00, averaging €147.70, and the six hours from 10:00 to 16:00 average €166.47 [1]. Then the evening returns: 18:00 to 19:00 averages €230.73, and the dearest quarter-hour, at 18:00, clears at €232.19 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the gap between Saturday's cheapest and dearest quarter-hour is €87.49, a little narrower than Friday's €94.00 [1].

## Weekends are cheaper; November is not

On the figures to Saturday, November's 15 weekdays average €244.03 and its six weekend days €213.86, desk arithmetic on SMARD and Energy-Charts data [1][2]. The weekend effect is about €30, roughly one-eighth of the price. It is not a rule: Saturday 7 November cleared at €259.83, dearer than most weekdays in October [2]. Across the first 21 delivery days, November averages €235.41, about 39% above October's €169.48 [1][2].

## Gas did not take the weekend off

The fuel that often sets the evening price moved the other way: the Dutch TTF front-month contract stood at €88.52 per megawatt hour in Friday's intraday trading, up 4.8% from Thursday [3]. If it holds, the evening hours are where it would show first.

## For you

Unless you have a dynamic tariff and a smart meter, the €147.70 lunchtime hour does not reach your bill; fixed-price contracts do not follow the exchange day by day. If you do, Saturday's cheap window is 10:00 to 16:00, and the expensive one is the hour when you cook. Nothing here is investment advice.
