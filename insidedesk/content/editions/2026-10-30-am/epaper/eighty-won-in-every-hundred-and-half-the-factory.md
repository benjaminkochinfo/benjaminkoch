Every AI server needs memory, and this week South Korea's SK hynix showed what that is worth. SK hynix reported third-quarter revenue of 104.6 trillion won and operating profit of 83.3 trillion won, an operating margin of 80% [1]. A year earlier revenue was 24.4 trillion won [1]. Desk arithmetic: the company kept about 80 won of operating profit from every 100 won it sold, against about 47 a year ago.

The more durable figure is in the order book. SK hynix says it has signed long-term agreements with 14 major customers, including the large cloud companies, covering more than 50% of its total production capacity for 2027 and 2028 [1]. The contracts carry a price range agreed with customers, with a mechanism that follows market conditions inside it [1]. That is the dull machinery that turns a boom into something a supplier can plan a factory around.

Why the confidence? The company says leading-edge processes are technically hard and new plants take a long time to build, so it expects demand to exceed supply "through 2030" [1]. That is the company's own outlook, not ours, and outlooks are not contracts.

What it does with the money is plainer. Net cash rose to 98.7 trillion won, and the company plans about 100 trillion won of shareholder returns for 2026, including a special dividend from gains on its Kioxia stake [1]. It also dropped plans to list its Solidigm unit, citing market conditions [1].

For you, the sum is simple: the memory in your next laptop or phone comes from factories whose output, by the maker's own account, is half spoken for by data-centre customers for two years. Nothing here is investment advice.
