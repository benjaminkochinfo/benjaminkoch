Binance, the world's biggest cryptocurrency exchange, said on Thursday that a "security incident" had affected about $6.3 billion of assets [1]. If confirmed as a theft, it would eclipse the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit in 2025, the previous record [1]. Early checks suggest an engineer's compromised laptop let an attacker make unauthorised withdrawals, the exchange said [1].

**The part that worked.** About half of the affected assets have been frozen with the help of other platforms, Binance said, and it pledged to "cover all losses" [1]. Tether said it had frozen around $1.6 billion and Circle more than $800 million, together about $2.4 billion [1]. Desk arithmetic: those two issuers alone account for roughly 38% of the $6.3 billion. Freezing is possible because the dollar tokens have central issuers who can act. That is a quiet argument for accountable institutions inside a market that was built to avoid them.

**The part that hurt.** Withdrawals are disabled until the environment is confirmed secure, co-chief executive Yi He said [1]. Bitcoin dropped more than 4% after the alerts, and Ethereum and Solana fell sharply [1]. Reuters says the hack also frayed nerves in wider markets, weighing on rival exchange Coinbase [2], which itself says it was unaffected [1].

**What to watch.** Binance says it will publish a full report when its investigation ends and is working with law enforcement [1]. If you hold crypto anywhere, the practical advice in Binance's own alert applies broadly: rely only on official channels for updates [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
