On Thursday the Houthis said they had attacked several sites in Saudi Arabia, including Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest oil refinery, the Rabigh refinery and an Aramco facility at Abqaiq [1]. The Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a cruise missile fired towards the kingdom's western region, and earlier on Thursday said it had destroyed a ballistic missile aimed at the eastern region [1][2]. Saudi Arabia has not confirmed any damage at the named sites in the reports.

The market did what markets do with claims: it priced the risk. Brent had settled 44 cents lower on Thursday at $114.88 a barrel, after falling by more than $2 earlier in the session on hopes for US–Iran talks; later reports of the attacks helped it recover some of the loss, one trader told Reuters [2]. In early Asian trade on Friday Brent was up 86 cents at $115.74 by 02:22 CET [1].

The calmer signals sit in company diaries. ExxonMobil and Chevron have cancelled plans for their executives to travel to the UAE's biggest oil show next week, citing security, two sources told Reuters [2]. That is a decision about people, not barrels.

For you, the useful question is the dull one: does any refinery's output or any export loading change? Until Aramco or the Saudi authorities say so, a claim remains a claim. Nothing here is investment advice.
