Alongside Friday's rate hold, the Bank of Japan's board agreed unanimously to more than double the pace at which it sells its holdings of exchange-traded funds from January 2027 [1]. Sales rise to about 860 billion yen a year at book value, from about 330 billion yen [1]. That cuts the time needed to sell everything to about 43 years, from around 112 [1].

The funds are a legacy of years spent trying to lift prices by buying shares. investingLive reads the change as adding modest supply to Japanese equities from 2027, with the 43-year horizon keeping the annual effect small [1]. Desk arithmetic: 860 billion yen is about 2.6 times the old pace. The useful point is the manner of it. A central bank unwinding a crisis tool slowly, on a published schedule, gives everyone else time to plan. Nothing here is investment advice.
