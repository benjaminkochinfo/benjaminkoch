Alexithymia is the difficulty of identifying and describing your own emotions. A school survey of 7,964 secondary pupils in six Chinese provinces and regions, published in BMC Psychology, found that loneliness and poor sleep went with more of it, while resilience and perceived social support went with less [1]. Loneliness had the largest association [1]. Teenagers found negative emotions harder to name than positive ones [1]. The full model explained 23.9% of the variation in scores [1]. The design is a single snapshot taken in early 2024, so it cannot show cause; the authors call these "potentially modifiable correlates" worth testing over time [1]. A pattern, not a prescription, and a hopeful one: sleep and company are things a household can work on.
