Intel shares fell 23% in extended trading on Thursday after the company forecast December-quarter revenue of $15–16 billion, below the $17.07 billion analysts expected, and said it does not expect positive cash flow this year [1]. It expects the deadly October explosion at its Kiryat Gat plant in Israel to erase roughly $600 million of revenue over two quarters; the plant is due to ramp up again by the end of this week after a two-week shutdown [1].

The quieter line came from finance chief David Zinsner: the cost of 18A, the process behind its "Panther Lake" chips, has fallen 40% since the first quarter, which he said should help margins over the longer term [1]. "No result is good enough, and bad results receive extra punishment," Seaport's Jay Goldberg said of the mood [1]. Moods pass. Unit costs compound. Nothing here is investment advice.
