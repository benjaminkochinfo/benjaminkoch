An employee of the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant has been killed in a drone attack, the International Atomic Energy Agency said early on Friday [1]. The agency said it had been informed of the death and identified the victim as a reported welder at the plant's centralised repair workshop [1]. It did not say who launched the drone.

Rafael Grossi, the IAEA's director general, said: "Any attack on nuclear plant staff is completely unacceptable and violates the Seven Indispensable Pillars for Nuclear Safety and Security during an armed conflict" [1]. The agency repeated his appeal for "maximum military restraint" to prevent a nuclear accident [1].

The pillars are not poetry. They are the agency's own checklist for keeping nuclear plants safe in a war, and one of them is about the people who work there. A plant needs maintenance whether or not it is producing power, and maintenance needs workers who come home.

For you, the agency's statement reports a death, not a radiation release [1]. What matters is the quieter erosion: every lost technician makes a plant on the front line a little harder to keep dull and safe.
