A proclamation signed on Wednesday closes a Section 232 inquiry that began in September 2025. It finds that the US relies on imports for most robots, industrial machines and their parts, and that this threatens national security [1][2]. No new duty follows for now. The commerce secretary and the trade representative are to negotiate with trading partners and report back within 180 days. Tariffs, quotas, fees, or a programme to encourage investment in US production stay on the table after that [2]. Supply Chain Dive counts this as the third time in three months the White House has chosen talks over immediate tariffs after such an inquiry, following wind turbines earlier this month and aircraft in July [1].

For machine exporters, Germany's among them, nothing changes at the US border today. Existing tariffs on the steel, aluminium and copper inside a machine still apply [2]. The finding stays on the books, though, so the next 180 days matter.
