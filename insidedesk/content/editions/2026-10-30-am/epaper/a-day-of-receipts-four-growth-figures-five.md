Friday is the day the euro area's statisticians hand in their homework, and the timetable is dense. All times CET, all forecasts as compiled by Dow Jones Newswires [1].

**Growth.** France publishes its first estimate of third-quarter GDP at 07:30, with a forecast of 0.1% growth on the quarter after 0.0% in the second [1]. Spain follows at 09:00 (forecast 0.6%, after 0.7%) [1]. Germany and Italy report at 10:00. For Germany the forecast is flat on the quarter and 1.1% on the year, after 0.3% quarterly growth in the spring; for Italy, 0.1% [1]. Eurostat's euro-area flash comes at 11:00, with 0.2% expected on the quarter and 1.1% on the year, after 0.6% and 1.2% [1].

**Prices.** France's October inflation at 08:45 is forecast at 3.2% (EU-harmonised 3.6%), Spain's harmonised rate at 09:00 at 5.1%, Italy's at 11:00 at 4.5% [1]. Six German states publish from 10:00, and Germany's national flash comes at 14:00: forecast 3.0% on the year after 3.3%, harmonised 3.1% [1].

**Also on the list.** German unemployment for October at 09:55 (rate forecast unchanged at 6.4%), euro-area unemployment at 11:00, and the ECB's Survey of Professional Forecasters at 10:00 [1].

**How to read it.** Forecasts are other people's guesses, and first estimates get revised. A flat German quarter would be a pause, not a verdict. The value of a day like this is that the numbers arrive on schedule, from offices that publish their methods. Nothing here is investment advice.
