Germany's towns, districts and the companies they own owed €378.9 billion at the end of 2025, Destatis reported on Thursday, 9.9% more than a year earlier [1]. That is €4,907 for every resident [1]. The rise a year before had been 6.3% [1].

**Where it comes from.** This "integrated" count adds the debts of municipal core budgets to those of their off-budget units, funds and companies, so that a town that runs its own utility is compared fairly with one that does not [1]. Most of the growth came from the core and off-budget budgets, up 15.5% and 14.7%; both ran a record deficit in 2025 for the second year running [1]. Debts of municipal companies and funds rose 4.5% [1].

**Where you live matters.** Hesse's municipalities carry the most, €6,763 a head, ahead of Saarland at €6,408 [1]. Brandenburg's carry the least at €2,733, then Saxony and Thuringia [1]. Debt rose in every state; fastest in Lower Saxony (+13.3%) and Baden-Württemberg (+13.1%), slowest in Saarland (+2.3%), where the state continues to take over municipal cash loans [1]. Berlin, Hamburg and Bremen are not included [1].

**The calm point.** Destatis is careful: the model says nothing about who is legally liable, and it cannot be set against municipal assets [1]. Debt that builds schools and pipes is not the same as debt that pays this year's salaries, and the release does not split them. What it does is put the number in public, by state, so councils can be asked about it. That is how the dull machinery stays honest.
