Oil opened Friday higher. Brent crude futures rose 86 cents, or 0.75%, to $115.74 a barrel at 02:22 CET, and US West Texas Intermediate rose 72 cents to $102.72, CNBC reported, carrying Reuters [1].

**The trigger.** On Thursday, Yemen's Houthis said they had attacked several sites in Saudi Arabia, including the Ras Tanura refinery, the kingdom's largest, the Rabigh refinery and an Aramco facility at Abqaiq [1]. A Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a cruise missile aimed at the west of the country [1]. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three tankers hit sea mines and caught fire while using an unauthorised route south of the Strait of Hormuz [1].

**The month.** Brent was on course to finish October nearly 14% higher and WTI about 15% higher, which would be a fourth straight monthly gain and the steepest since July [1]. WTI is heading for its first monthly close above $100 since April [1].

**The diplomacy.** Iran has received a letter from US Vice President JD Vance but had not answered it, the Tasnim news agency reported, according to the same account [1]. "Continued kinetic action around the Strait of Hormuz amidst doubts of a viable diplomatic pathway are keeping oil prices elevated," said Saul Kavonic of MST Marquee [1].

**The calm point.** A monthly close is just a date on a calendar, but it is the date many supply contracts and heating-oil invoices are written against. If you heat with oil, the receipt you can check is your supplier's quote, not the screen. Nothing here is investment advice.
