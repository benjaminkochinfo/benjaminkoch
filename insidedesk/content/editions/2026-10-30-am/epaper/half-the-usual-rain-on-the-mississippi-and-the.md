America's weather this autumn has split along a line. The latest US Drought Monitor, published on Thursday for the week to 27 October, shows drought spreading from the southern Mississippi Valley into the southern Midwest, while heavy rain improved conditions across the High Plains and the West, Feed & Grain reports [1].

**The dry side.** Extreme to exceptional drought, the two most severe categories, now covers much of central and western Tennessee, Arkansas, Louisiana and parts of Mississippi [1]. Most of Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and south-western Tennessee have had 8 to 16 inches less rain than normal over four months, generally less than half the usual amount [1]. Extreme to exceptional drought also covers a large swathe of central and southern Missouri [1].

**Why it matters beyond the farm.** The driest areas sit along the lower Mississippi river system, where low water has already raised barge costs and pressured Southern grain basis, the local price against the futures benchmark, this autumn [1]. A band from Iowa through the northern Ohio Valley, by contrast, has stayed largely free of damaging dryness [1].

**The wet side.** Southern Colorado had 4 to 8 inches of rain near the New Mexico border, enough for two-category improvements, and New Mexico, Arizona and Nevada saw the broadest relief, although long-term deficits limited full drought removal [1]. The outlook to 2 November offers limited rain for the hardest-hit areas [1].

**The calm point.** The Drought Monitor is a weekly map drawn by four public institutions, and it is boring in the best way: the same categories, the same day, every week. That is how a slow problem becomes visible before it becomes a crisis.
