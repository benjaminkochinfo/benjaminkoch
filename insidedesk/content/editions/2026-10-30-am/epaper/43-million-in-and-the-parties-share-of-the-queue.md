More than 43 million Americans have already voted in the midterm elections on Tuesday 3 November, according to University of Florida Election Lab data reported by the BBC [1]. Michael McDonald, who runs the tracker, says four to five million early votes have been cast each day, a much higher rate than in most previous elections [1]. The lab's dashboard was last updated at 21:45 Eastern Time on Thursday [2].

Texas stands out. More than 5.8 million ballots have been cast there, already about 1.75 million more than its 2022 numbers, McDonald told the BBC [1]. Minneapolis said it had set an early-voting record for a non-presidential election [1].

The party figures need a careful reading. In the roughly half of states that record party registration, 44% of early ballots came from registered Democrats, 31.2% from Republicans and 24.7% from voters with no affiliation [1]. Those are registrations, not votes: the lab notes that officials do not start counting ballots until Election Day [2], and the data does not show which candidates unaffiliated voters chose [1].

McDonald says overall turnout appears on track to outpace 2020, and possibly 2018, which beat the 1914 record for a midterm [1]. For you, the honest headline is the one about participation. Who won will be written by the counters, from Tuesday night, state by state.
