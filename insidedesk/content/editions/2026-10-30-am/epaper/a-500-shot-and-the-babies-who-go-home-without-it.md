The monoclonal antibody that protects newborns against respiratory syncytial virus, RSV, costs about $500 a dose, and most US commercial insurers will not reimburse hospitals for giving it, CIDRAP reports [1]. Hospitals usually negotiate bundled payments for delivery and newborn care; insurers argue that not every infant needs the antibody, because some mothers were vaccinated in pregnancy and some babies are born outside RSV season [1].

Families can get the shot free at a paediatrician's office, but some infants are not seen within the recommended first seven days, said Dr Michelle Fiscus of the Association of Immunization Managers [1]. Only about 40% of eligible pregnant people received the maternal RSV vaccine, according to the American Academy of Pediatrics [1]. The practical advice in the report is simple: if you are expecting this season, ask your hospital in advance whether it gives the antibody before discharge [1].
