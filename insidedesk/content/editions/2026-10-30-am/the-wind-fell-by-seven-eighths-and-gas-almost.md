On Thursday, German wholesale power cost more than on any other day this year: the day-ahead price for Germany and Luxembourg averaged €314.85 per megawatt hour [2]. A day later the receipts are in, and they read less like a mystery than like a roll call.

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-30oct]]

**Who left.** On Monday 26 October, onshore wind turbines generated 688.8 gigawatt hours, according to the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD figures; on Thursday they generated 97.3 GWh [1]. Offshore farms fell from 136.9 GWh to 11.6 GWh over the same days [1]. Desk arithmetic: wind as a whole dropped from about 826 GWh to about 109 GWh, a fall of roughly seven-eighths in three days.

**Who filled in.** Gas-fired plants went the other way, from 105.4 GWh on Monday to 283.3 GWh on Thursday [1]. Desk arithmetic: that is about 2.7 times as much. Demand barely moved: grid load was 1,343.0 GWh on Monday and 1,357.5 GWh on Thursday [1]. So the country did not suddenly use more electricity. It used the same amount from dearer machines, and paid the price of the dearest one needed in each hour. The daily average rose from €116.57 on Monday to Thursday's €314.85 [2].

**Today.** For Friday 30 October the auction cleared at an average of €268.62, about €46 below Thursday [2]. Lunchtime is the cheapest stretch, at €215.00 for 12:00 to 13:00 CET, and the early evening the dearest, at €345.93 for 17:00 to 18:00 CET [2].

**The calm point.** A still, grey week in late October is not a failure of the grid; it is the grid doing exactly what it was built to do, calling up reserve plants when the wind drops. The bill for that is visible, hour by hour, a day in advance, and that openness is worth more than any speech about it. On a dynamic tariff, the middle of the day is your friend today.
