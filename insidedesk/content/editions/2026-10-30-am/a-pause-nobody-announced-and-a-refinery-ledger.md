Ukrainian units that carry out long-range strikes were told in mid-October that the campaign against Russian oil refineries was effectively on hold once missions already under way were finished, the Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday [1]. Reuters, which carried the report, said it could not immediately verify it [1]. The unofficial pause is expected to last until after the US midterm elections and possibly deeper into November [1]. Kyiv has not announced anything; until now it had refused to stop, calling the drone campaign its only viable answer to years of bombardment [1].

The background is a quarrel over diesel. Reuters reports that Donald Trump grew increasingly frustrated with the strikes as diesel prices hit record highs, and answered by announcing a deal to buy diesel from Moscow and a waiver of sanctions he had signed into law in September [1].

The part you can check sits in the oil trade's own notes. JPMorgan analysts wrote late on Wednesday that there had been no further confirmed attacks on Russian refining since the 15 October strike on the Ryazan refinery, and that "the silence has allowed Russian refinery runs to rebound by 350,000 barrels a day last week, to 4 million barrels a day" [2]. That is consistent with the reported pause. It does not prove an order was given.

What did not pause is the other direction. Russian drone and missile attacks killed at least 20 people in Ukraine on Thursday, including 14 on a platform at Dnipro's main station, five in Chernihiv and Kremenchuk and one in a late-evening missile strike on Kyiv, Ukrainian officials said [1]. About 350,000 households lost electricity [1]. Ukraine's prime minister said Russia was "deliberately hunting civilians" [1].

For you, the honest reading is narrow. One side's restraint is reported, not declared; the other side's strikes are counted daily by mayors and railway staff. The ledger to watch is the one that cannot be talked up: refinery runs, power cuts, and whether next month's talks actually convene.
