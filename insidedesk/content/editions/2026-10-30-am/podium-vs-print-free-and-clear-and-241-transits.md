**The podium.** On Sunday, flying over the strait in an army helicopter, Adm. Brad Cooper, the commander of US Central Command, gave the American version in one sentence: "Today, primary transit routes in the Strait of Hormuz remain free and clear of Iranian sea mines thanks to incredible efforts by our people" [1]. The same release says more than 3,000 commercial vessels have crossed with US support over six months, that US forces have helped Gulf partners export more than 1.5 billion barrels of crude since the summer, and that "over 95% of ships" have sailed through safely in recent months [1]. On Thursday Iran's Revolutionary Guards answered that the "unauthorised" southern route is mined, and said three tankers hit mines there; Iran's army reported a fourth east of the strait [3]. None of the ships has been named [3].

**The print.** UKMTO, the British maritime agency that takes incident reports from merchant ships, keeps a weekly ledger. For the week ending 23 October it counted 114 outbound and 127 inbound transits, against 125 and 129 the week before, "approximately 70% below pre-conflict levels" [2]. Only 47 of those crossings were visible on ships' own tracking signals; the rest were US-stated escorted transits, about four for every one seen on AIS, which UKMTO says it "cannot independently verify" [2]. It has logged 86 incidents in the strait since 28 February, and since 6 July, 57 of 79 projectile strikes were on the southern route [2].

**The gap.** Both statements can be true at once, and that is the point. Cooper speaks about mines in the main lanes. The ledger measures something else: how many owners are willing to send a ship. Desk arithmetic: 241 transits in the latest week, against 254 the week before and 275 the week before that [2][5], a fall of about 12% in a fortnight. Overnight, UKMTO said a tanker 25 nautical miles north-east of Khasab had been hit on Thursday by two explosive devices attached to its hull from a small boat; the crew were safe, the fire was out and the ship carried on [4]. That is not a mine, and not a reassurance.

**The verdict.** Podium and print measure different things. For you, the number that moves fuel bills is the second one: ships through, cargoes delivered. Watch the next weekly count, not the next statement. Nothing here is investment advice.
