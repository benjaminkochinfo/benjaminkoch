October in Britain managed to be wetter, warmer and sunnier than average at the same time. Provisional Met Office figures show the UK received 13% more rain than in a typical October, with northern Scotland 31% above its long-term average [1]. It was also the fifth-sunniest October since records began in 1910, and the mean temperature was 0.9°C above average, the ninth month in a row above the norm [1].

**The rain came in a rush.** Most of it fell in the last third of the month, when Storms Austen and Boelo swept through; 24 October was provisionally the UK's joint-sixth wettest day on record [1]. England had its wettest week in two years, the Environment Agency said, but warned that it was not enough to ease the drought left by the hottest summer on record [1]. Sustained above-average rain will be needed through the winter [1].

**Then the tap closed.** High pressure has taken control and the jet stream has moved north, so the first days of November are largely dry, with frost and fog at night in the north and west and a brisk easterly in the south [1]. The BBC's forecasters see a weak low drifting up from France at the weekend and, beyond next week, a likely return of Atlantic systems, with "a lot of uncertainty" and no definite storm in sight [1].

The lesson is the reservoir manager's, not the headline writer's: a drought is a balance sheet, and one good month is a deposit, not a repayment.
