Testing for tuberculosis has long depended on sputum, the phlegm coughed up from the lungs. Many people, children among them, cannot produce it. The second edition of WHO's guidelines on TB diagnosis, published on 3 November, recommends tongue swabs as a new sample type for exactly those people [1].

The 167-page document also recommends a new class of near point-of-care tests that let clinics at the edges of a health system diagnose TB, and pooling of sputum samples to test for TB and rifampicin resistance more cheaply where resources are exceptionally tight [1]. A living website will list the recommended tests and their prequalification status, and operational guidance is due in the coming months [1]. The cheapest test is the one that reaches the patient.
