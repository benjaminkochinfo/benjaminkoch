Newborns often blink fewer than five times a minute; adults average around 20 [1]. Researchers from Tsukuba and Kyoto filmed 113 children aged three to six while they played a card-sorting game that switches between colour and shape rules [1]. Children who blinked more sorted more accurately when the rule changed, and the link held after allowing for age, at a modest partial correlation of 0.22 [1]. Blink rate also tracked activity in the upper-right prefrontal cortex, measured with a light-based headband [1].

The study, in *Communications Psychology*, measured each child once, so it cannot show cause [1]. The authors are blunt: there is no evidence that blinking more helps, and the measure "should not be used to evaluate or diagnose individual children" [1]. A cheap window into development, then, and not a test.
