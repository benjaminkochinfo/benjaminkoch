The European Commission is preparing targeted changes to the rules of its €18 trillion internal market, meant to help EU firms diversify their sourcing away from countries including China, according to people familiar with the work quoted in Bloomberg reporting summarised by Briefs [1]. A Commission spokesperson declined to comment [1].

What is reportedly on the list is the unglamorous kind of thing that changes trade. It includes motor-vehicle type-approval rules, the foreign-subsidies framework, forced-labour provisions and the trade secrets directive [1]. The changes are to be bundled into a package due by mid-December. Its centrepiece is a "diversification instrument" that would map where the EU depends heavily on outside suppliers of goods and services and set out ways to reduce that exposure [1]. A second tool would let Brussels restrict market access for selected goods and services faster, and across a wider scope, than current instruments allow. A solidarity mechanism would set out options if a third country targets one or more member states [1].

The backdrop is a number. The EU's goods deficit with China was about €360 billion last year, and Eurostat data put it at roughly €361 billion [1]. Separate safeguards for chemicals, furniture and machinery, which could take the form of quotas or tariffs, could come as early as the first quarter of 2027 [1]. Officials are said to be using as a reference a recent arrangement capping Chinese hybrid cars at 350,000 a year [1].

None of this is law yet. Trade chief Maroš Šefčovič plans to take stock of talks with Beijing early next year [1]. For a purchasing manager, the useful document is not the headline. It is the dependency map, once it is published.
