Oil spent the American election night drifting rather than jumping. Brent futures fell 26 cents, or 0.25%, to $103.34 a barrel by 02:57 CET, and U.S. WTI crude fell 53 cents to $95.56, Reuters reported via CNBC [1]. In Tuesday's session Brent had already eased more than 1% to its lowest level in nearly four weeks, as traders weighed talks to end the wars in Ukraine and Iran [1].

**The talks are the price.** A senior Iranian official told Reuters on Tuesday that Iran and the United States remain far from a deal, while a U.S. official described "defensive" strikes on Iran's Qeshm island that another official said did not signal a return to major combat [1]. Qatar has stepped up mediation [1]. Each sentence moves the barrel a little; none of them settles it.

**The inventories are the invoice.** The American Petroleum Institute's figures, cited by market sources, showed U.S. crude stocks falling by 5.27 million barrels in the week to 30 October [1]. Eight analysts polled by Reuters had expected a rise of about 880,000 barrels [1]. Gasoline stocks rose by 1.43 million barrels and distillates, which include diesel and heating oil, fell by 1.19 million [1]. "Diesel inventories remain tight," Haitong Futures wrote [1].

That last line is the one that reaches you at the pump and the heating-oil tank. The official U.S. Energy Information Administration figures are due at 16:30 CET today and can confirm or overturn the industry count [1]. Nothing here is investment advice.
