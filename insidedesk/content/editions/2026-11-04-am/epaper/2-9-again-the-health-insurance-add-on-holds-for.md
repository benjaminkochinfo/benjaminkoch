One line on next year's payslip has stopped climbing, at least on paper. The Federal Health Ministry has set the average supplementary contribution rate in statutory health insurance for 2027 at 2.9%, unchanged from this year [1]. The figure rests on the October estimate of the official forecasting group, the GKV-Schätzerkreis, which met on 14 October [1].

**Average is not yours.** Each health fund decides its own supplementary rate [1]. The 2.9% is a benchmark, so the number that matters is the one in your fund's letter. Desk arithmetic: on a gross monthly salary of €3,500, 2.9% comes to €101.50, which employer and employee normally share.

**Why it held.** The ministry credits a law to stabilise contributions, which it says will relieve the funds by up to €19 billion next year [1]. Spending is expected to rise by less than 3% in 2027, after almost 7% this year; for the first time in three years, the ministry says, rising income would cover rising spending [1].

**The minister's own caveat.** "That can only be a first step," said Health Minister Carsten Linnemann; a finance commission is due to propose structural reforms by the end of 2026 [1]. That is the right order: a held rate first, then the harder question of what the system costs. You do not need to do anything today. When your fund writes to you about 2027, compare its rate with 2.9%. Nothing here is financial advice.
