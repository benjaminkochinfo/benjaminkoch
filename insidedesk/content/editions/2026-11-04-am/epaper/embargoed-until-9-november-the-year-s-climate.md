The report has a date; you just cannot read it yet. The World Meteorological Organization will brief journalists in Geneva on Friday 6 November on its State of the Climate 2026 update, under embargo until 9 November [1]. Secretary-General Celeste Saulo and the WMO's head of climate information, John Kennedy, are listed to present it [1]. In Antalya, COP31 organisers expect around 50,000 visitors a day in the Blue Zone and have brought in 630 low-emission buses [3].

One input is already public. The WMO's October seasonal update expects the Niño 3.4 sea-surface temperature anomaly to reach about +3.8°C over November to January, peaking around December, with a narrow spread between models [2]. An embargo is not secrecy. It is a timetable, and timetables are how institutions keep their promises.
