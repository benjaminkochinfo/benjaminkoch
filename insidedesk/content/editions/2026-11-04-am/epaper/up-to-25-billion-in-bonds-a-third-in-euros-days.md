Meta set out on Tuesday to raise $20 billion to $25 billion in bonds, two-thirds in dollars and one-third in euros, according to people familiar with the deal cited by CNBC [1]. It comes days after S&P Global Ratings moved Meta's outlook to AA- negative from AA- stable, citing "uncertainty" about heavy capital spending and negative free cash flow [1].

The invoices are large. Meta expects $135 billion to $145 billion of capital spending this year, posted negative free cash flow of $2.12 billion last quarter, raised $25 billion in bonds in April, and last week announced plans to raise $95 billion in equity [1]. Desk arithmetic: at the top of the range, this sale plus April's would be $50 billion of bonds in a year. A rating outlook is the dull line that tells you what lenders will charge for it.
