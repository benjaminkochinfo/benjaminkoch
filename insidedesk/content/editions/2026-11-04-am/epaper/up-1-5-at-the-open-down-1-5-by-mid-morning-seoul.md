Wednesday began well in Seoul. The Kospi rose 1.48% in early trade [1], helped by Tuesday's New York session, where the Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 2.64% and AMD 3.65% [2]. Briefly it traded above 5,800 [2].

Then the American count arrived. By about 10:45 local time (02:45 CET) the Kospi was down 1.5% at 5,564.34, after a range of 5,546.89 to 5,828.13 [2]. Foreign investors sold a net 695 billion won of shares; retail investors and institutions bought [2]. SK hynix fell 3.65% and Samsung Electronics 1.82% [2]. Aju Press linked the turn to early results suggesting Democrats could take both chambers of Congress, and to the worry that this might mean tighter oversight of AI data centre building [2].

Tokyo, back from Tuesday's holiday, opened lower and kept going: the Nikkei 225 was down 3% at 61,183.06 in the same report, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng slipped only 0.3% and Shanghai 0.2% [2]. The won strengthened 0.6% to 1,338.30 per dollar [2].

Desk arithmetic: from the session high of 5,828.13 to 5,564.34 is a fall of about 4.5% inside two hours. Nothing in Korea's factories changed in those two hours. What changed was a guess about Washington's next two years, made before the count was finished. For you, that is a reason to read morning moves as opinion, and closing prices as slightly firmer opinion. Nothing here is investment advice.
