More than 57 million Americans voted before Tuesday, a midterm record on University of Florida Election Lab figures [1]. How they voted changed more than how many did. Across the 27 states and Washington, D.C., that reported both methods in each of the last two midterms, mail voting fell 13% while early in-person voting rose 46% [1]. Texas logged 7.7 million early ballots, 97% of them cast in person [1]. In North Carolina, about 94,000 mail ballots came back, 40% fewer than in 2022. One analyst pointed to a new rule requiring voters to seal an inner envelope, on pain of spoiling the ballot [1]. The lesson is a modest one. Small rules about envelopes shape how a country votes.
