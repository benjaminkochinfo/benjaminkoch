**The podium.** AMD's third quarter was the biggest in its history: revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45% on a year earlier, above the top of its own guidance [1]. Lisa Su, its chair and chief executive, said the data centre business had nearly doubled and that growth should "accelerate further" in the fourth quarter. Then came the sentence for the long run: AMD is "confident in our ability to sustain very strong revenue growth and expanding profitability over the coming years" [1].

**The print.** The same release carries an outlook table. Fourth-quarter revenue is expected at about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, which would be 66% growth on a year earlier [1]. Non-GAAP gross margin is expected at about 55% [1]. In the third quarter it was 56% [1]. CNBC notes that the 55% sits slightly below the 55.3% analysts had pencilled in, according to StreetAccount [2]. Desk arithmetic: the revenue range runs from about $16.5 billion to $17.5 billion; a one-point lower margin on $17 billion is roughly $170 million of gross profit that the higher revenue has to cover.

**The gap.** It is a small gap, and both statements can be true. Su spoke of "the coming years"; the table speaks of one quarter. Margins can dip while a new product ramps and rise again later. On the call she said AMD had secured "the wafers, substrates, advanced packaging, memory, and other key components" for much higher growth next year [2], and securing scarce parts is rarely cheap. The release does not say why the margin is guided lower, so neither should we.

**What the market did.** The shares rose about 8% after hours [2]: investors paid for the $17 billion and forgave the point. By early Wednesday in Berlin, though, the election count had taken over, and Nasdaq-100 futures were down about 2% [3]. A record quarter does not insulate a share from the night's news. For you, the habit is the same as ever: read the outlook table before the quote, and the quote before the price. Nothing here is investment advice.
