Onshore wind turbines in Germany generated 1,009.2 gigawatt hours on Saturday and 1,019.9 GWh on Sunday, according to SMARD, the Bundesnetzagentur's data portal [1]. A week earlier, on Wednesday 21 October, they made 242.3 GWh [1]. Gas-fired plants went the other way: 241.4 GWh on Wednesday, 42.5 GWh on Saturday and 43.5 GWh on Sunday [1]. Desk arithmetic: gas burned about a sixth of Wednesday's amount. (Sunday had 25 hours, as the clocks went back.)

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-26oct]]

The auction followed the weather. The German-Luxembourg day-ahead price averaged €211.94 per megawatt hour for delivery on 21 October, €30.12 on Saturday and €13.12 on Sunday [1]. For Monday it is €116.57 [1]. Desk arithmetic: Monday's power costs about nine times Sunday's, and still about 45% less than last Wednesday's.

The month as a whole is still dear. Over 1 to 26 October the daily price has averaged €154.41, against €85.93 over the same days of 2025 [1]. A windy weekend does not repay a month. And the Deutscher Wetterdienst expects a "transition to calm high-pressure weather" from midweek, cooling to average late-October temperatures [2]. Calm and cool is the combination that brings gas back into the merit order.

The lesson is not that wind is cheap on a stormy Sunday; everyone knows that. It is that the system had the turbines, the lines and the auction ready when the storm came, in the same week Brent had its best week since 2022. That is what boring, finished infrastructure looks like in a crisis. Nothing here is advice.
