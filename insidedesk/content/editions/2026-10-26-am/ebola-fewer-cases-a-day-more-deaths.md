The Democratic Republic of the Congo's bulletin for 23 October reports 60 new confirmed Ebola cases and 46 deaths [1]. Since the Bundibugyo outbreak began there have been 9,876 confirmed cases and 4,803 deaths; 48.6% of patients have died [1].

The weekly view is mixed. Over the past seven days the outbreak averaged 65.9 new cases and 38.1 deaths a day, against 71.6 cases and 31.6 deaths the week before [1]. No new health zone has been affected since 16 October; the outbreak remains in 66 zones across eight provinces [1]. Desk arithmetic: cases a day fell by about 8%, deaths a day rose by about 21%.

Of the 46 deaths, 31 happened in the community, outside a treatment centre [1]. That is the number to worry about: a death at home is a death the system did not reach in time, and often a body handled without protection.

The machinery is working, unevenly. Teams followed up 88.4% of contacts that day, 32,023 of 36,218 [1]. Since 26 August, 9,415 people have received the Ervebo vaccine [1]. Positive tests were 13.3% of 450 samples [1]. None of these figures will trend online. All of them decide how this ends.
