Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday as part of an effort to reduce regional tensions in coordination with the United States, an official briefed on the matter told Reuters [1]. No details of the talks were published [1].

The positions around the visit were public and contradictory. Iran's ambassador to Pakistan said on Saturday that Tehran had conveyed, through Pakistani mediators, that it was ready to reopen the Strait of Hormuz if Washington ended its blockade of Iranian ports [1]. The Tasnim news agency, close to the Revolutionary Guards, rejected a similar Al Arabiya report as "lies and fantasy" and said Iran "fundamentally does not accept" such an exchange [1]. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a new war would not change his country's positions [2].

The market did not wait for clarity. By 0232 GMT on Monday Brent crude futures were down $2.12, or 1.82%, at $114.60, and US crude was down 2.07% at $101.13 [3]. Desk arithmetic: the drop gives back roughly a third of Friday's gain of nearly $3 [3].

Read that calmly. A delegation is not an agreement, and the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia went on over the weekend [3]. But the oil market has just told you what the conflict's exit is worth to it: a couple of dollars on a rumour, and much more on a signature. Nothing here is advice.
