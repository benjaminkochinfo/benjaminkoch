Goldman Sachs has outlined a scenario in which oil climbs to $120 a barrel if attacks on vessels in the Middle East escalate, and one in which a return to normal export levels brings it down towards $80, The Economic Times reported, citing Reuters [1]. Daan Struyven, co-head of global commodities research, said shipping risks had become a key driver of prices [1]. JPMorgan analysts said the war's trajectory remained difficult to assess [1].

Desk arithmetic: from Monday morning's $115, the escalation case is about $5 away and the normalisation case about $35. The market is priced far closer to the bad lane than the good one. That is what a strait is worth when it is closed. Nothing here is advice.
