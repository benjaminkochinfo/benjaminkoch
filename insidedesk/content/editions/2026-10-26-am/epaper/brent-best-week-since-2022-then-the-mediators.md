Brent futures gained 3.1% on Friday to settle at $116.72 a barrel, the highest settlement since 29 April; WTI rose 3.8% to $103.27 [1]. Brent added 6.2% over the week, its third weekly gain in a row, and Reuters called it the highest weekly close since mid-2022 [1][3].

On Monday, by 0232 GMT, Brent was down $2.12 at $114.60 and WTI down $2.14 at $101.13 [2]. Desk arithmetic: the gap between the two benchmarks is about $13.5 a barrel, a measure of how much more the world pays for oil that does not have to cross the Atlantic from America.

The reasons pull both ways. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia continued over the weekend [2]; Qatari mediators were in Tehran [3]. The price will settle somewhere between a missile and a meeting. Nothing here is advice.
