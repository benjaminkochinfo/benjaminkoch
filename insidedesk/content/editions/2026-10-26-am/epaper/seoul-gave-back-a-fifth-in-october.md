South Korea's main index fell 1.1% on Monday morning, having shed almost 20% so far this month, Reuters reported [1]. AP put the Kospi at 5,494.29 [2]. Japan's Nikkei dipped 0.5% after losing 4.0% last week [1].

China's market is falling for a different reason. Chinese blue chips lost 1.0% as the Communist Party's fifth plenum began in Beijing; the market has now fallen for 11 weeks straight amid concerns about domestic demand, even as exports remain strong [1]. Hong Kong's Hang Seng edged 0.2% higher to 23,137.93 [2].

Desk view: Korea is the purest AI-hardware market in the world, which is why it rose so far and is now falling so hard. A fifth off in a month is a repricing of expectations, not of factories. The memory chips still ship. Whether the price paid for them holds is what Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon will show this week [1]. Nothing here is advice.
