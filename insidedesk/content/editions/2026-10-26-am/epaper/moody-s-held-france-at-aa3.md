Moody's left France's rating at Aa3 on Friday, with the negative outlook it has carried since last October, Reuters reported; bond markets had been pricing a downgrade [1]. "The Aa3 rating assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted, while financing costs and social tensions will abate somewhat in the next few months," the agency said [1].

The conditions are concrete. The 2027 bill targets a deficit of 5% of output against 5.4% this year, but the lower house has already scrapped some savings [1]. On Thursday France's ten-year yield hit 5.14%, its highest since July 2002, and the premium over German bonds reached a 15-year high [1]. On Monday the euro held at $1.1204 as "political unrest in France kept investors wary" [2].

A rating is a dull paragraph that decides what a country pays to borrow. Moody's has written down what would change its mind. That is more useful than any speech in the Assemblée this week.
