The Rhine at Kaub, the shallowest stretch for shipping between Bingen and Koblenz, averaged 31.3 centimetres on Sunday, by this desk's daily mean of the 15-minute readings published by the federal waterways administration [1]. At 05:15 on Monday the gauge read 52 cm [1].

[[chart:kaub-26oct]]

For context: the lowest daily reading of the 2010–2020 reference period was 25 cm, on 22 October 2018. From late September until Thursday this year the daily mean stayed below that line, falling below zero on several days, with a low of −4.8 cm on 1 October [1]. Friday's mean of 25.9 cm put it just over; Saturday slipped back to 23.8 cm; Sunday's 31.3 cm is the highest daily mean since at least 25 September [1]. Desk arithmetic: Monday's 05:15 reading is about double the 2018 low.

The gauge number is not the water depth in the channel, which is deeper. But it is the number shippers and insurers price on. A few centimetres more at Kaub mean more tonnes per barge, fewer trips and lower freight surcharges, eventually even on heating oil delivered up the Rhine.

Do not expect a flood of relief. The Deutscher Wetterdienst expects a transition to calm high-pressure weather from midweek, with stormy gusts lingering on the North Sea on Wednesday [2]. Dry weeks follow storms, and the river remembers both.
