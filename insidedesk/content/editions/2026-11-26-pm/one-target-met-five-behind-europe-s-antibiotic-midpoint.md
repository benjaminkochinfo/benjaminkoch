Health at a Glance: Europe 2026 takes stock halfway to the EU's 2030 targets on antimicrobial resistance [1]. One of six is already met: the incidence of MRSA bloodstream infections [1]. Elsewhere the picture is worse. The estimated incidence of bloodstream infections with carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae was 72% higher in 2025 than in 2019, and resistant E. coli infections were also above their 2019 level [1].

Human antibiotic use fell 5% between 2019 and 2025, from 19.9 to 18.9 daily doses per 1,000 people; to reach the target the EU would need a further 16% cut [1]. Austria and Finland have already met their national goals [1]. In animals, veterinary antimicrobial sales fell 24% between 2018 and 2024 [1].

ECDC estimates resistant infections cause more than 35,000 deaths a year in the EU/EEA [1]. The OECD's modelling says packages of stewardship, better hand hygiene, delayed prescribing and safer food handling save lives and cost less than they save [1]. It is the least glamorous medicine there is. It is also the one that pays for itself.

This is reporting, not medical advice.
