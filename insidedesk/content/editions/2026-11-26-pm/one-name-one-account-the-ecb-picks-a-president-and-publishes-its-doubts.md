Two things came out of the euro's institutions on Thursday. In Brussels, a majority of eurozone governments backed Pablo Hernández de Cos, 55, head of the Bank for International Settlements and governor of the Bank of Spain from 2018 to 2024, to succeed Christine Lagarde; he secured more support than the former Dutch governor Klaas Knot [1]. Lagarde leaves on 7 February 2027. France's Laurence Boone is in line to take over market operations from Isabel Schnabel, and Germany's Markus Brunnermeier to replace chief economist Philip Lane [1]. The appointments are to be made in December, after consultation [1].

In Frankfurt, the ECB published the account of its 28–29 October meeting, when it kept the deposit rate at 2.50% [2]. It is drier reading and more useful. Oil and gas futures had moved above the adverse scenario of September's staff projections; inflation fixings peaked at 4.5% for January 2027 [2]. Members agreed communication should convey 'that another rate hike would likely be necessary unless the inflation outlook improved significantly' [2]. The pause, they wrote, 'did not reflect diminished concern' [2].

Economists quoted by Euronews call Hernández de Cos slightly more dovish than Knot, but expect the change of leadership alone to alter little [1]. That is the right expectation. A central bank that works is a committee with minutes, not a person with a temperament. On Thursday you saw both, and the minutes were louder.
