Russia attacked overnight with Iskander-M, Zircon and S-400 missiles and 113 drones, including decoys; Ukraine's air defences shot down or neutralised 87 targets, Euronews reported [1]. At least three people were killed in Kharkiv, where a residential building and an educational institution were hit, and seven were injured in Kyiv [1].

Ukrainian drones struck the Nevinnomyssky Azot chemical plant in Stavropol region, which produces components for the explosives octogen and hexogen, and the power station beside it; two people were wounded [1]. A new fire broke out at the Kuibyshev refinery in Samara, and the Subkhankulovo pipeline station in Bashkortostan was hit [1]. Russia's defence ministry claimed 343 Ukrainian drones destroyed; Euronews could not verify that [1].

Through the International Committee of the Red Cross, Russia returned the bodies of 250 servicemen to Ukraine and received 22, according to Russian sources partially confirmed by a Russian search organisation [1]. Zelenskyy warned that Russia is preparing a 'new massive strike' on energy facilities [2]. The war's arithmetic is grim, but the repatriations show one mechanism still functions on a schedule.
