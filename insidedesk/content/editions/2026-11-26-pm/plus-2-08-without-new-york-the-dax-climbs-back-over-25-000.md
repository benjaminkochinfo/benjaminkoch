The DAX ended Thursday 2.08% higher at 25,215.52 points, back above 25,000 after Wednesday's fall to its lowest level since July, dpa-AFX reported [4]. The rebound followed the turn that US technology shares made late on Wednesday [4]. 'Nach den turbulenten Tagen bietet die Pause Anlegern eine willkommene Gelegenheit zum Durchatmen,' wrote eToro analyst Maximilian Wienke: the holiday gives investors a chance to catch their breath [4]. New York was closed for Thanksgiving; trading thinned out through the afternoon without spoiling the mood, as Andreas Lipkow wrote for wallstreet:online [2].

[[chart:europe-thursday-26nov-pm]]

The buying went to what had been sold hardest: chipmaker Infineon and Siemens Energy, plus carmakers still lifted by Wednesday's analyst notes [2]. Handelsblatt reported signs that the planned US tariffs on EU goods could turn out milder than feared [3]. Chief analyst Jochen Stanzl put it plainly: on Monday investors bought because of Jensen Huang's return, 'jetzt wegen des Preises', now because of the price [3].

Across Europe the rise was broad: by Boursorama's figures after the cash close, Milan was up 2.36%, Madrid 2.26%, the Euro Stoxx 50 2.11%, London 1.75%, Paris 1.61% and Zurich 1.04% [1]. One good day after a bad one restores the level, not the argument. The question Stanzl raised, whether the AI billions pay off when rates rise, is still open [3].
