Countries such as the UK and Japan have advised households to keep at least three days of essentials, and online rumours have turned that guidance into talk of an impending world war, Euronews and AFP report [1]. UK media reported that tinned-food sales at Iceland briefly rose 40% in late October [1]. 'Misinformation prevails in an information vacuum,' said Yotam Ophir of the University at Buffalo: when advice lacks context, people 'fill the gap with their own alternative explanations' [1].

The calm answer is more detail, not less advice. A three-day shelf is a plan, not a prophecy.
