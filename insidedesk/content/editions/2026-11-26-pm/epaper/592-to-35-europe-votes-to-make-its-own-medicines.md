MEPs in Strasbourg approved the Critical Medicines Act by 592 votes to 35, with 30 abstentions, Agence Europe reported [1]. It sets criteria for projects that can receive national or EU support to build or modernise drug production in the EU; supported firms must prioritise supplies to the European market [1]. Public tenders will reward suppliers in proportion to the share of medicines and active ingredients made in the EU, and at least five member states can launch joint procurement [1].

Shortages are solved in factories and tenders, not in press releases. This law works on both.
