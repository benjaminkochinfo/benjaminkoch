China, the EU and other partners pushed back on Thursday against the tariffs of up to 20% that Washington proposed after its Section 301 probe into excess capacity [1]. For the EU, South Korea and Taiwan, which have trade deals with the US, the duties would be broadly capped at 15% [1]. That is the same number the EU agreed last year. Trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič still rejected the decision and said Washington should respect that 15% [1].

Why object to a number you already accepted? Because the instrument matters. A deal is a contract with two signatures. A Section 301 finding is a unilateral verdict that can be revised by one side. 'The EU and the US share a real concern about global overcapacity, much of it state-driven and concentrated in third countries. We regret the lack of joint action,' said Commission spokesman Olof Gill [1].

The new levies would not stack on sector tariffs such as those on cars and steel, a senior US official said [1]. China's commerce ministry called the probe an act of unilateralism [1]. The dull lesson: in trade, the paperwork is the policy.
