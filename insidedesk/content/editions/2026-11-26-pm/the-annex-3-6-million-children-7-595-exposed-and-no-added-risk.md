Many women with ADHD stop their medication when they become pregnant, for lack of evidence, and that can hurt their mental health and family stability, BMJ Group notes [1]. A study published in The BMJ on Wednesday adds the evidence. Researchers used health registers in Denmark, Norway and Sweden covering 3,643,376 children born between 2000 and 2021, followed for a median of nine to ten years [1]. Of these, 7,595 (0.2%) were exposed to ADHD medication in pregnancy [1].

After adjusting for factors such as the mother's age, education, smoking and her own ADHD diagnosis, neither exposure overall nor by drug type — methylphenidate in 69% of cases, amphetamines 20%, atomoxetine 7% — was linked to a higher risk of any neurodevelopmental disorder [1]. The authors write that concerns about such risks 'do not justify discontinuation of ADHD medication in pregnancy' [1].

The limits are stated too: the study is observational, and unmeasured factors such as ADHD severity or genetics could play a part [1]. That honesty is the point of the Annex. A good study tells you what it found and what it cannot rule out, and lets the doctor and the patient decide.
