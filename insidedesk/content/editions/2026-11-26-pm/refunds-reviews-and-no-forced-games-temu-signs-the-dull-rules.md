Temu has committed to change several practices to comply with EU consumer law, the Commission said on Thursday [1]. The list is unglamorous: more accessible company information, better customer support, simpler withdrawals, returns and refunds, more reliable reviews, clearer pricing, an end to messages pressing customers to buy, removal of forced gamification features, substantiated environmental claims and a clearer checkout [1].

'Temu's commitments are an important step towards ensuring consumers can shop online with confidence,' said commissioner Michael McGrath, adding that the Commission would 'continue to closely monitor' compliance [1]. In May the Commission fined Temu €200 million after investigators found dangerous baby toys and faulty chargers on the platform; in early November preliminary findings said it had not changed enough [1].

The case now passes to national authorities [1]. That is the right order of things. Regulation rarely wins with one dramatic fine. It wins when a company's lawyers conclude that obeying the boring rules is cheaper than arguing about them.
