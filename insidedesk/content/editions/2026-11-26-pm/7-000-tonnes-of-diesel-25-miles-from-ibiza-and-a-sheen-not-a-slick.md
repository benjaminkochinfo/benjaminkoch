The Med Emre, a Malta-flagged chemical tanker carrying up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel from Algeciras to Barcelona, capsized 25 nautical miles off Ibiza on Wednesday night [1]. All 17 crew were rescued; seven went to hospital and one later died [1]. The crew had reported a leak and a 90-degree list minutes after a distress call around 22:30 local time [1].

The hull has not sunk completely, which the Balearic emergencies director Pablo Gárriz said makes operations possible at the surface rather than at a depth of 1,000 metres [1]. A plane and Spain's pollution-control vessel Clara Campoamor are assessing possible damage; 'there is still little information available about a possible fuel spill', the Balearic president Marga Prohens wrote [1].

The stakes are real. Ibiza's marine environment, with its Posidonia seagrass meadows, has been on Unesco's World Heritage List since 1999 [1]. In 2002 the Prestige spilled more than 60,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil off Galicia [1]. This time the response began within hours. That is not luck. It is two decades of procedure.
