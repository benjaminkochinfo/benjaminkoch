US shoppers spent more than $82.5 billion online from 1 to 22 November, 9.1% more than a year earlier, according to Adobe Analytics, which tracks visits to US retail sites [1]. Every one of those 22 days topped $3 billion, and two passed $4 billion, a first before Thanksgiving [1].

Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion of online sales for Black Friday, up 9.2%, after $6.9 billion for Thanksgiving Day [1]. Cyber Monday is still expected to be the biggest day of the year, at $15.1 billion [1]. For November and December together Adobe expects $275.1 billion [1].

Two lines deserve more attention than the totals. First, buy now, pay later purchases reached $6.1 billion, up 6.5% [1]. That is consumption financed in instalments, and it shows up in household budgets in February. Second, the figures are nominal: in a year when prices are rising, a 9% increase in spending is partly a 9% increase in what things cost.

None of this is a verdict on the American consumer. It is a till roll. The households behind it will tell you, in January's card statements, whether it was confidence or credit.
