Consumer prices in Tokyo's 23 wards excluding fresh food rose 2.9% in November from a year earlier, up from 2.6% in October and the fastest pace since July 2025, the internal affairs ministry reported on Friday [1][2]. The market median had been 3.0% [1]. Headline inflation was 2.8%; excluding fresh food and energy it was 2.5%, down from 2.6% [1][2].

The jump has a plain cause. Government subsidies for electricity and gas, paid for three months, ended with November's bills [1]. Energy prices rose 9.5%, after 2.2% in October; electricity was up 9.5%, also pushed by higher grid charges, and city gas 13.7% [1].

[[chart:tokyo-cpi-nov-27nov]]

But this is not only energy. Food excluding fresh food rose 3.1%, private rents 1.9% and hotel charges 3.6% on visitor demand [1]. JPMorgan's Ayako Fujita told Reuters that higher prices for public transport and medical care suggest inflation "is no longer driven only by temporary supply shocks" [2]. The counterweight is rice, down 33.8%, the largest fall since comparable records began in January 1971, as stocks pile up [1].

The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 1.25% in September and holds its next two-day meeting ending on 18 December [2]. A majority of analysts polled by Reuters expect a rise then and another by the end of March [2]. One economist told Reuters that market turmoil over AI and chips could still make the bank wait [2]. The quieter figure for the committee is the 2.5% underneath: a touch lower than last month, still above the 2% target [1][2].
