**The podium.** Friedrich Merz arrived at Thursday's special summit with a sentence built for the doorstep. The loan to Ukraine for 2027, he said, must come from all of them: the Union "consists of 27 member states, not 23 or 24" [1]. He had threatened earlier to veto the bloc's next seven-year budget unless every country signed up [1][3].

**The print.** The Council's own record of the evening is shorter. "24 member states supported the proposal," António Costa told reporters, and one of them, Bulgaria, only for the non-military part, "with a focus on regions with its national minority" [2]. "We will revert to this question in December" [2]. Al Jazeera puts the proposal at €45 billion, on top of the €90 billion loan agreed nearly a year ago [3]. On the 2028–2034 budget, the summit's main business, Costa reported: "We didn't reach an agreement" [2]. Some governments wanted further cuts, most opposed them, and some wanted cuts to cohesion reversed; a large majority asked not to squeeze agriculture and cohesion [2].

**The gap.** Desk arithmetic: the chancellor asked for 27 and got 24, one of them partial. That is the sentence he said Europe must not settle for, and it is what Europe produced on the night.

**The dull part.** Neither side of this lost. A doorstep line sets a price; a press-conference record tells you the market rate. The useful thing is that the record exists, counted and published within hours [2]. December now has three jobs: the loan, the budget and the new ECB leadership, which the same summit sent out for consultation [2].
