**The podium.** Elon Musk wrote on X that every child in the US will get two SpaceX shares: one newly issued by the company, one from him [1]. SpaceX said it would contribute one Class A share to every currently active Trump Account, the programme meant to give every American under 18 a stake in the stock market; the White House says it has opened almost 70 million accounts [1]. Musk's own share follows, he wrote, "when my lockup expires in June next year" [1].

**The print.** At Wednesday's close of $127.44, the pledge was valued at nearly $18 billion [1]. Anthropic, which went public this month, has taken a different route: 5% of its shares committed to a new fund for the "public good", worth around $54 billion at current prices, which it said would give it flexibility to make donations [1].

**The gap.** Desk arithmetic: two shares at $127.44 are about $255 per child, at one day's price. One model sends a small, volatile asset into 70 million individual accounts; the other keeps a large block in one fund with a board deciding where it goes. Neither has yet been tested by a bad year for the share price.

**The dull part.** What will matter is the paperwork: custody, fees, what happens to fractional entitlements, when a child may sell, and how the shares are taxed. Generosity announced on a social network becomes real only in an account statement.

Nothing here is investment advice.
