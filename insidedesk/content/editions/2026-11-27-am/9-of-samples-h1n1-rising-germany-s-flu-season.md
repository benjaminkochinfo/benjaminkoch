Acute respiratory illness in Germany stayed at a moderate level in calendar week 47, 16 to 22 November, according to the Robert Koch Institute's weekly report published on Thursday [1]. Rhinoviruses and enteroviruses still dominate [1]. But influenza viruses have been found in sentinel samples every week since the season began, mainly A(H1N1)pdm09, and the RKI reports rising circulation of both influenza and SARS-CoV-2 [1].

The numbers are modest so far. Of 160 sentinel samples sent to the national reference centre, 100 contained respiratory viruses: rhino- and enteroviruses in 31%, parainfluenza in 12%, influenza A and B in 9%, SARS-CoV-2 in 6% and RSV in 1% [1]. In hospitals, severe acute respiratory infections rose but stayed low; 7% of these patients received an influenza diagnosis and 4% Covid-19 [1].

Notified influenza cases rose again, on a similar curve to last year at this time; Covid cases rose too, but fewer have been reported than last season [1]. Wastewater tells the same story: SARS-CoV-2 load below last year's but climbing, influenza slightly up, RSV low [1].

That is a season approaching, not one arrived. For practices, it is the time to stock vaccines and staff rotas; for hospitals, to plan beds before they fill. For you, it is the window in which a jab still has time to work before the curve turns.
