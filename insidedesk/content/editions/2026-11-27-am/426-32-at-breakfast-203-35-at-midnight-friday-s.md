Germany's day-ahead price for delivery on Friday 27 November averages €301.82 per megawatt hour across its 96 quarter hours, according to Energy-Charts data from the Bundesnetzagentur's SMARD platform [1]. That is up from €291.77 for Thursday and the highest daily average since the €316.65 of 11 November [1].

[[chart:de-power-day-ahead-27nov]]

The shape is the story. The most expensive quarter hour of the day starts at 08:15, at €426.32; the cheapest is the last, from 23:45, at €203.35 [1]. Desk arithmetic: the breakfast quarter hour costs 2.1 times the midnight one. On Thursday the dearest quarter hour came in the evening, at 17:45 (€388.02); on Friday it has moved to the start of the working day [2].

What it means for you depends on your contract. Most households pay a fixed tariff and will never see this curve; those on dynamic tariffs see it directly, with grid fees, levies and taxes on top. For industry buying on the spot market, it is the difference between running a furnace at 08:00 or at 22:00.

The chart puts Friday in its season: daily averages mostly between €100 and €210 in September and October, above €240 on 16 of November's 27 delivery days so far (desk count) [1]. Cold weeks are expensive not because of one dramatic hour, but because every quarter hour asks a little more.
