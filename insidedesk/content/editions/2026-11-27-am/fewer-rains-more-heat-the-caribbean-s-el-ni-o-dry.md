A strong El Niño is expected to bring significantly drier conditions, more heat and warmer seas to parts of the Caribbean in the 2026/27 dry season, the Caribbean Institute for Meteorology and Hydrology warned at a regional outlook forum in St Vincent on Wednesday [1]. The eastern and southern Caribbean are most exposed to below-normal rainfall and reduced water availability [1].

"What that translates to for the Caribbean is a higher chance for drought," said CIMH climatologist Cédric Van Meerbeeck [1]. He added that the year after an El Niño onset tends to bring "record warm or near-record warm conditions" [1]. The drier odds last until about February, the end of the northern hemisphere winter [1].

Drought does not mean no rain. Van Meerbeeck said rainfall events will be "fewer and farther in between" but can still be intense, with flash floods and landslides, especially on volcanic islands [1]. Warmer water also raises the risk of coral bleaching and of a busier hurricane season next year [1]. The CIMH map of agricultural drought alerts for the end of December shows some territories on watch, warning or emergency, and others with no concern [1].

His policy ask was not a technology. It was a conversation: "national discussions on how to allocate water sustainably across the sectors of society" [1]. Farms, hotels, households and hospitals drink from the same reservoirs. Deciding the order before the dry months is the cheapest adaptation there is.
