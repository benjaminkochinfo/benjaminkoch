The averages in Friday's Tokyo inflation data are made of very ordinary things, and Nikkei's breakdown lists some of them [1]. Boxed lunches, bento, cost 44.0% more than a year ago after price revisions by large companies [1]. Restaurant hamburgers were up 16.4% [1]. Plastic bags, repriced in the summer, rose 32.7% [1]. Hotel rooms were up 3.6% on visitor demand and private rents 1.9% [1].

Then the other side of the bag. Rice prices fell 33.8%, the largest drop since comparable records began in January 1971, because stocks have piled up [1]. Fresh food overall rose only 1.8%, after 7.0% in October when long rains had pushed up lettuce, cabbage and spring onions [1].

Electricity bills rose 9.5% and city gas 13.7% after government subsidies ended [1].

Desk arithmetic for a household: if rice and bento both appear in your weekly shop, you can feel prices falling and rising at once, and both feelings are accurate. An index is the average of a thousand such contradictions. That is why statisticians publish the components, and why you should read them.
