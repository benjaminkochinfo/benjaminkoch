Commerzbank said on Thursday that UniCredit, its shareholder, has formally requested an extraordinary general meeting [1]. The agenda: remove nine of the ten current shareholder representatives on the supervisory board and elect ten new ones, after which UniCredit expects Andrea Orcel, its chief executive, to be elected chairman [1]. Michael Gorriz would stay; six of the ten nominees, Orcel included, hold senior posts at UniCredit [1].

UniCredit also intends to call a vote of no confidence in an unspecified number of management board members unless the supervisory board presents a severance agreement for them [1]. Commerzbank said its board would examine the request "entsprechend seiner gesetzlichen Pflichten" and call the meeting if the legal requirements are met [1].

Chairman Jens Weidmann said a newly composed board must meet the governance standards of a systemically important bank [1]. Chief executive Bettina Orlopp pointed to the best nine-month result in the bank's 156-year history [1]. The market's verdict on Thursday: Commerzbank fell 2.55%, the only DAX member to close lower [2].

This is what a takeover looks like after the headlines: a list of names, a meeting date, a vote count. The drama was the bid. The governance is the paperwork that follows.
