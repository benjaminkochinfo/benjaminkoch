Japan Tobacco will take a ¥1.4 trillion ($8.9 billion) charge after losing control of its Russian subsidiaries, pushing it to a full-year net loss and a lower dividend forecast, The Japan Times reported [1]. Russian presidential decrees this month placed all seven of its local subsidiaries under temporary administration, so the company lost management control and removed them from its accounts [1]. It now expects an operating loss of ¥368 billion instead of a ¥1 trillion profit [1].

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi raised the seizures, and Russia's arrest of a Japanese journalist, with Vladimir Putin in China [1].

On Friday JT shares rallied more than 4% in Tokyo, AFP reported, after a report that it was considering joining Altria in a takeover of Britain's Imperial Brands; Imperial had jumped 13% in London on Thursday [2].

The lesson for anyone running a plant abroad: an asset you cannot control is, for accounting purposes, an asset you do not have. Moscow did not need a court to move ¥1.4 trillion off a Japanese balance sheet. It needed a decree.
