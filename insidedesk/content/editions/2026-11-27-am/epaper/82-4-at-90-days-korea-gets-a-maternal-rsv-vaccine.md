Pfizer Korea presented Abrysvo, the country's first RSV vaccine approved for use in pregnancy, in Seoul on Thursday, Korea Biomedical Review reports [1]. Given at 28 to 36 weeks of gestation, it protects infants from birth to six months [1]. A professor chairing the event expects it to be available to pregnant women in early 2027 [1].

The evidence cited is solid. In the final analysis of the phase 3 MATISSE trial, it cut the risk of medically attended severe RSV lower respiratory disease in infants by 82.4% within 90 days of birth and 70% within 180 days [1]. Australia's real-world STREETON study found 86.3% effectiveness against hospitalisation through two months and 77.8% through six [1].

Price is the open question. Korean parents now pay 400,000 to 700,000 won out of pocket for antibody injections [1]. A CDC price list from March 2026 put Abrysvo at $319.07 a dose in the US private sector, against $595.06 for Beyfortus [1]. Pfizer did not disclose its Korean price [1].

Korea's immunisation committee is reviewing priorities for its national programme for the first time in three years, with RSV vaccines and antibodies both candidates [1]. That review, not the launch, is the real event: public programmes turn good products into high coverage.
