The US had 6,039 confirmed measles cases as of 24 November, across 47 jurisdictions, including 18 among international visitors, according to the CDC's website [1]. The agency reports nine measles-related deaths this year [1]. Public state figures add up to at least 14, including nine in Pennsylvania and three in Wisconsin [1].

The gap has a bureaucratic cause. Earlier this year the CDC switched to National Center for Health Statistics mortality data instead of state figures [1]. "The discrepancy between state and federal measles-related death figures is concerning," immunologist Frederic Bertley told Reuters [1].

The rest is not in dispute. Cases are at a 35-year high, driven largely by unvaccinated people, and this month the US lost its measles elimination status [1]. 671 patients, 11% of cases, were hospitalised; among children under five, 17% [1]. Pennsylvania alone has reported 1,679 cases, 87 in the past week [1].

Two counting systems that disagree are a nuisance. They are also, quietly, a sign that two counting systems still exist. The remedy for measles is the same under either total: two doses of a vaccine that has worked for decades.
