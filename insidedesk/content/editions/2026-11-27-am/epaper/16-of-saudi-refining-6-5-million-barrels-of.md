The Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted and destroyed four drones at Ras Tanura in the Eastern Province on Wednesday, without saying whether the refinery was hit [1]. Footage circulating online appeared to show smoke from the site; the Houthis said their strike caused fires, a claim not immediately verified [1].

The site matters. Ras Tanura is the kingdom's oldest refinery, with 550,000 barrels a day of capacity, about 16% of Saudi refining [1]. The adjoining terminal can load about 6.5 million barrels a day, mostly for buyers in China, Japan, South Korea and India [1]. It has been attacked repeatedly this year, including by Iranian drones on 2 March and a claimed Houthi missile on 27 October [1].

AFP reported on Friday that the Houthis struck a Saudi refinery, temporarily halting some operations, after an attack on another Gulf hub earlier in the week [2]. Oil extended Thursday's gains of more than 1% [2].

The market reaction has been modest, and the reason is in the history: Saudi Arabia shifted exports towards Yanbu on the Red Sea after the war with Iran began [1]. Redundancy is the least exciting word in energy, and the most valuable.
