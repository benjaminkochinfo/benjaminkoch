Ukraine's military intelligence agency, the GUR, said on Thursday that North Korea has temporarily halted some military cooperation with Russia [1]. By its account the pause covers deliveries of KN-23 ballistic missiles and artillery shells, and Russian military flights over North Korean territory [1].

The GUR's explanation is diplomatic pique: it says North Korea's foreign minister, Choe Son-hui, announced the pause last month after a Russian deputy foreign minister claimed Moscow would help ease tensions between Seoul and Pyongyang [1]. It also says Pyongyang is holding off a Russian request to raise its military presence in Russia from about 8,500 to 50,000 troops [1].

The same agency says North Korean units remain in the Kursk region and that a North Korean missile unit has been in Voronezh since mid-summer [1]. South Korean and Western intelligence have said Pyongyang sent more than 10,000 soldiers to Kursk in 2024 [1].

Treat this carefully. It comes from one side's intelligence service, in wartime, and neither Moscow nor Pyongyang has confirmed it in the reporting. The useful test is physical: whether the volume of North Korean-type missiles and shells reported in Russian strikes falls over the coming weeks. Intelligence claims are hypotheses; debris is evidence.
