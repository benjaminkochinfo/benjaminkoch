Dozens of properties in Cedar, near Nanaimo, were placed under an evacuation order on Thursday after flooding, a day after the Regional District of Nanaimo declared a local state of emergency, CBC News reports [1]. Orders followed in Courtenay and from the Snuneymuxw First Nation, and Highways 19 and 4 were closed in places on Thursday evening [1].

The storms coincided with king tides, which can run 0.3 to 0.6 metres above average, and Environment Canada said water levels could rise by as much as 0.7 metres at high tide [1]. The River Forecast Centre had predicted 50 to 100 millimetres of rain on the island and warned that saturated soils from earlier storms meant rapid runoff [1].

By Thursday afternoon the storms had passed and "the highest tide is now behind us", an Environment Canada meteorologist said, with a few calm days before another system on Sunday [1].

The warnings, alerts, orders and reception centres worked in their dull sequence [1]. The advice stays the oldest in the book: never drive across a flooded road.
