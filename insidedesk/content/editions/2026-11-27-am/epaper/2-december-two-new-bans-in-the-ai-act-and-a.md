The EU's Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation 2026/1744, is often described as a delay, and for high-risk systems it is: those rules now apply from 2 December 2027 for Annex III uses such as recruitment and credit scoring, and from 2 August 2028 for products such as medical devices [1]. But next Wednesday, 2 December 2026, two new prohibitions take effect [1].

The first covers AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate images or sexually explicit content of an identifiable person without explicit consent; the second, AI-generated child sexual abuse material [1]. A provider can be caught even if its tool was not designed for this, where such output is a reasonably foreseeable result and the system lacks adequate safeguards [1]. Breaches of Article 5 prohibitions can draw fines of up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover [1].

The same date ends a transition: generative AI systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must comply with the machine-readable marking obligation for synthetic content [1].

This is regulation at its least glamorous and most useful: a date, a definition, a fine. The test is not the press release in Brussels but whether image tools ship with safeguards that work on the morning of 2 December.
