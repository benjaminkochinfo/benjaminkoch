New Zealand's official count, released on Friday, left the governing National, ACT and New Zealand First parties with 65 seats, exactly as many as Labour, the Greens, Te Pāti Māori and Opportunity combined, RNZ reports [1]. Both groupings are one short of a majority in a 130-seat parliament enlarged by a ten-seat overhang [1]. On election night the coalition had led by 67 seats to 64 [1].

The swing came from 485,000 special votes and a second count of ordinary votes [1]. Labour's Kieran McAnulty took Wairarapa from National by 81 votes, so a recount is likely [1]. National's party vote was 28.35%, against 38.1% in 2023 [1].

That puts Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong and her six colleagues in a pivotal spot [1]. RNZ thinks the most plausible path is Opportunity supporting the centre-right on key votes, or abstaining, in exchange for concessions; Wong's bottom lines include stronger competition powers, limits on lobbying and donations and a ten-year cross-party health plan [1]. A fresh election is possible but improbable [1].

There is no fixed deadline, but parliament must meet by 14 January [1]. Turnout was 77.2% [1]. The machinery is working exactly as designed: slowly, with recounts, in public.
