Successive severe storm cells have damaged more than 60,000 hectares of crops in Victoria's Mallee, about A$50 million of damage according to crop assessors' initial surveys, Stock & Land reports [1]. More than 50 growers were hit, with 50 to 100% of yield stripped from prime lentil and barley crops [1].

"As far as Victoria goes, this ranks up there with the biggest I have seen," said crop consultant Marty Colbert [1]. Bumper yields magnified the loss: "30pc of six tonnes a hectare is a lot more than 30pc of 3t/h," he said [1].

Hopetoun growers Matt and Alex Webster lost 50 to 96% of the yield on 1,400 hectares of barley, lentils and wheat [1]. "This is the first crop insurance claim for us," Matt Webster said; they insure every year because their land runs along a known storm line [1].

The Bureau of Meteorology warned of more severe storms with heavy rain and possible large hail for the Mallee and northern Victoria on Friday [1]. Insurance is not glamorous adaptation. It is a premium paid in good years so that one bad afternoon does not end a farm.
