Among 15- to 22-year-olds surveyed in 16 countries, a median of 93% have a close in-person friend and 37% a close online-only friend, Gallup reports [1]. In-person friendships of medium or high quality were linked to higher life evaluation; online-only friendships were not, and those with one were more likely to report loneliness and moderate-to-severe anxiety and depression [1]. The strongest correlates of a good life were all offline: household income, feeling safe at home and the caregiver relationship [1]. Most samples were non-probability web panels [1]. The finding is still a quiet argument for the youth club.
