Russia struck Kyiv with ballistic missiles overnight into Friday, wounding seven people and hitting a shopping centre and two other buildings, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said [1]. Moscow said it had hit drone-related sites [1]. Hours earlier, in his nightly address, Volodymyr Zelenskyy said: "Unfortunately, we have information about a new mass strike being prepared by the Russians" [1]. He asked Ukrainians to watch the missile alarms "in the coming days" [1]. It was his second such warning this week [1]. The last big one is not abstract: on 20 November, by Zelenskyy's count, Russia fired more than 400 drones and 110 missiles, killing eight people [1].

That is one calendar: nights, alarms, repair crews. The other runs on flights and press conferences, and on Thursday it was busy.

**Moscow and Mar-a-Lago.** Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin on Thursday [3]. They were following up US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, the first direct trilateral round between Russia, Ukraine and the US in nine months, which produced an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange; numbers and timing are still being worked out [3]. Kushner spoke of "some openings and some opportunities" and said the envoys would go on to Dublin to meet Zelenskyy [3]. One aim, he said, was Moscow's consent to European participation in later rounds [3]. In the meeting, according to the same report, Putin repeated his opposition to European troops in Ukraine and his claim to four Ukrainian regions [3]. In Florida, Donald Trump wrote that he had asked Putin in China to pull back "all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe", called the war "A COMPLETE BLOODBATH" and a quick deal "a strong possibility" [1]. The Guardian noted that the post did not say which territories he meant, and did not name Ukraine [2].

**The long arm.** On the battlefield the trend is the opposite of a pause. Ukraine launched nearly 500 strikes at Russian targets more than 500km away in 2026, more than in the first four years of the war combined, according to an AFP analysis of ACLED conflict-monitor data up to 20 November [2]. Overnight, Ukraine said its drones hit a refinery in Krasnodar, Transneft oil tanks in Bashkortostan and the Marinovka airfield near Volgograd [1]. At sea, Zelenskyy said seven naval drones hit the corvette Merkury and the cargo ship Angara off Libya on Monday; Libyan officials told the AP the Merkury was heavily damaged, not sunk [1]. The attack killed two Libyan army personnel, and Ukraine's foreign minister expressed "regret" over its "unintended consequences" [2]. At least four Russian vessels have been targeted this month, Al Jazeera reports; a suspected Ukrainian drone also hit the Russia-bound cargo ship Sparta II in the Baltic near Finland [1].

**Brussels.** At the special European Council, Council President António Costa reported that 24 member states supported the Commission's proposal for an additional loan to Ukraine, Bulgaria only for the non-military part; "We will revert to this question in December" [4]. Al Jazeera puts the loan at €45 billion for next year, on top of a €90 billion loan agreed nearly a year ago [1]. On the seven-year budget, Costa was blunt: "We didn't reach an agreement" [4]. Friedrich Merz had arrived saying the Union consists of "27 member states, not 23 or 24" [5].

**Reading the two calendars.** You will hear a great deal this weekend about openings and possibilities. Notice which of the week's facts are countable: a prisoner swap agreed in principle, a loan with 24 signatures, a budget without agreement, about 500 long-range strikes, seven wounded in Kyiv. The rest is podium. Peace, if it comes, will arrive the way the loan does: as a document with a number of signatures, a date and a ledger behind it. Until then the alarms in Kyiv keep the more reliable timetable.
