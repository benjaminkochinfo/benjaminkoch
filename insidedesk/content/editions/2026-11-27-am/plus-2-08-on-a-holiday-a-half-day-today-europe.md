The DAX closed on Thursday at 25,215.52, up 513.39 points or 2.08%, back above 25,000 a day after its lowest close since July [1][2]. It was broad in the way rallies rarely are: 39 of the 40 members rose, led by Volkswagen preference shares at plus 4.60% [2]. The only faller was Commerzbank, down 2.55%, as UniCredit moved on its supervisory board [2]. The MDax gained 2.34% to 29,263.97, according to a dpa-AFX report [3]. Wall Street was closed for Thanksgiving [3].

Asia kept going on Friday morning, with chipmakers that had been sold hardest earlier in the week, SK hynix, Samsung and Kioxia, in demand again [4].

[[chart:asia-friday-27nov]]

At about 03:30 CET the Nikkei 225 stood at 58,172.04, up 1.2%, and the Kospi at 4,798.76, up 1.9% [4]. Hong Kong was up 0.6% and Shanghai 0.3% [4]. Reuters reported S&P 500 futures up 0.8% and Nasdaq futures up 1% in Asian hours [5]. The proportion matters more than the direction: the Nikkei's week is plus 2.7%, against minus 8.4% for November [5].

Today New York trades a shortened session [6]. Thin holiday volume flatters every move, up or down. A Capital.com analyst told Reuters a seasonal "Santa Rally" "currently appears elusive" [5]. Treat this morning's green screens as a weather report, not a climate.
