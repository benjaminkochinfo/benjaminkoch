On 11 June the ECB's Governing Council raised its three key interest rates by 25 basis points, taking the deposit facility rate to 2.25% from 17 June [1]. It was the first increase since September 2023; the rate had been cut step by step to 2.00% by June 2025 and then left alone [2].

**The reason was stated, not implied.** "The war in the Middle East is generating inflation pressures," the statement said, "and the decision to raise rates is robust across a range of scenarios" [1]. Staff projected headline inflation at 3.0% in 2026, 2.3% in 2027 and 2.0% in 2028, with inflation excluding energy and food at 2.5% in 2026 and 2027 [1]. Growth was cut to 0.8% for 2026 [1].

**The textbook and the decision.** The standard advice is that a central bank should look through a supply shock: higher oil prices are a one-off level change that tighter money cannot pump out of the ground. The ECB's answer, read carefully, was about second-round effects. Staff expected the energy path "to some extent" to "feed into food, goods and services inflation" [1]. You do not raise rates to lower the price of diesel. You raise them so that the diesel price does not become next year's wage claim and the year after's rent.

**'Robust' is the honest word.** A central bank that justifies a move by its baseline is betting on its forecast. One that calls the move robust across scenarios is admitting it does not know which scenario it is in. That is less comfortable and more truthful. It also refused to promise anything: "The Governing Council is not pre-committing to a particular rate path" [1].

**Hindsight.** It did move again: a further 25 basis points on 10 September, to 2.50%, saying inflation "is set to remain well above target for an extended period" [3]. The projection for 2026 stayed at 3.0%; the one for 2027 rose to 2.5% [3]. For September, Eurostat's final figure put euro-area inflation at 3.8%, with core at 2.5% [4]. For you, the lesson of June is a practical one: when a committee says it is acting on risks rather than on its central case, plan with the risks too.
