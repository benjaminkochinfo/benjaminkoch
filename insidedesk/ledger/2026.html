The 2026 Ledger

The year so far, month by month, in the order the invoices arrived.

The desk’s look back at 2026: written in October, with hindsight, and without pretending anyone saw it coming.

  1. 3 January 2026 · Geo-economicsA president removed, a barrel unimpressedVenezuela changed hands over a weekend. The oil market read the supply sheet and went back to its glut. It was right about Venezuela, and about nothing that came next.
  2. 20 February 2026 · Politics & InstitutionsThe court sends the invoice backSix justices said a tariff is a tax, and taxes belong to Congress. The refund goes to whoever signed the customs entry, which is rarely the person who paid the higher price.
  3. March 2026 · Energy & IndustryThe month a strait became a priceTwenty million barrels a day stopped moving, and the world's emergency cupboard opened wider than ever before. Both facts were true. Only one of them was a solution.
  4. 14 April 2026 · Markets & MacroA reference forecast is a hope with a footnoteThe IMF did not publish a baseline in April. It published a reference case and said, in plain print, that the world was drifting away from it. Read the footnote first.
  5. 22 May 2026 · Money, FX & CommitteesA new chair, the same arithmeticThe Federal Reserve changed its chair in the middle of an energy shock. If you expected the new face to bring cheaper money, the committee's next four months are the answer.
  6. 11 June 2026 · Money, FX & CommitteesFrankfurt raises into a supply shockThe textbook says look through an energy shock. The ECB chose not to, for the first time in this cycle, and said exactly why. The interesting word was 'robust'.
  7. 14 July 2026 · Security & OrderSixty days was the honest clauseA June memorandum promised safe passage through Hormuz, free of charge, for sixty days only. The price fell as if the strait had reopened. It had reopened on paper.
  8. August 2026 · Geo-economicsSix months, five ships a dayHalf a year into the war, the strait carried a twentieth of its old traffic. The world adapted around it, by pipeline, by detour and by drawing down the cupboard. The cupboard was the part with a bottom.
  9. 10–16 September 2026 · Money, FX & CommitteesThe bypass breaks, both banks hikeIn one week the pipeline that carried Saudi oil around Hormuz was hit, the ECB raised rates and the Fed did the same. The Fed decided on the very day the ECB's increase took effect. The tank had made the argument for both.
  10. 13 October 2026 · Markets & MacroResilience is an inventorySix months after April's warning, the IMF's growth number had not moved and its inflation number had. The Fund's own explanation is a list of things that run down.
