MONEY · DESK NOTE

A blackout week belongs to the prints

The Fed has gone quiet before its 28 October decision. That leaves the floor to inflation in London and Tokyo, surveys in Europe and a rate decision in Beijing.

Money, FX & Committees Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Monday, 19 October 2026, 08:30 CEST· 2 min read·3 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

The Federal Reserve has entered its communications blackout ahead of its 28 October decision1. For a week the most-quoted committee in the world says nothing in public, and that is a feature, not a gap. When the speakers go quiet, the data has the floor.

There is plenty of it. UK inflation for September arrives on Wednesday, after 3.1% in August; markets had priced a 95% chance that the Bank of England raises rates on 5 November, CMC Markets wrote on Friday2. The People’s Bank of China sets its lending rates on Tuesday, euro-area consumer confidence comes on Thursday, and on Friday the euro-area purchasing managers’ surveys and Japan’s September inflation2. CMC also notes that markets expect the Fed itself to raise rates sooner than the Bank of England, after hotter-than-expected September consumer and producer prices in the United States2.

Beijing is the quiet exception. Economists largely expect the People’s Bank to hold its benchmark lending rates through the rest of the year and leave the work to fiscal measures, such as the 550 billion yuan of unused debt quota passed to local governments in early October3.

Currencies are waiting too. The yen barely moved this morning at 157.89 per dollar, and the offshore yuan held at 6.68391.

The desk’s view is old-fashioned. A committee that talks every day trains markets to trade adjectives. A blackout sends everyone back to the invoice: what prices actually did last month, measured by statisticians who do not give interviews. You can do the same this week. Skip the previews. Read the releases.

What to watch

  • UK inflation for September on Wednesday, against August's 3.1%: the last big print before the Bank of England meets on 5 November.
  • Japan's September inflation on Friday, with the yen near 158 per dollar.
  • Euro-area consumer confidence on Thursday and the October PMIs on Friday: the first October evidence on how households and firms are carrying the energy bill.
Desk comment

Silence from the podium is not a lack of news. It is the week the print gets to speak first.
