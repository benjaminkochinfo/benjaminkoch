SECURITY · LEAD

A curfew is a timetable, and a timetable can be checked

France moved from Sunday's list of meetings to Monday's local curfews, an interior minister kept in post, and a bond market that has already priced the night. Order is measured in hours and invoices, not adjectives.

This morning's edition left France with a list of meetings. By Monday afternoon the list had grown teeth.

After an emergency meeting with top officials, Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said he had instructed prefects to impose "very limited" and "localized" curfews where needed, and dismissed the idea of putting all of Paris under one. Marseille had already banned most public gatherings for Monday. That is not a slogan. It is a set of hours on a notice, with a start time, an end time and a map. You can check whether it was posted, whether it was kept, and whether the streets were quieter when it ended.

Before you close the tab

One thing you can do this week

This week, when a dramatic picture of unrest reaches you, open one official source beside it — a prefecture notice, an interior-ministry tally, a company repair figure — and write the number next to the image. Ten seconds. Most arguments get quieter.

A habit, not advice. Nothing here tells you what to buy, sign or switch.

What went right

Germany's manufacturers still have work on the books. Destatis reported that the real stock of manufacturing orders rose 0.5% in August on July, seasonally and calendar adjusted, and stood 10.7% above a year earlier, with the backlog range unchanged at 9.0 months 1. That is not a boom, and it is not a riot video. It is orders already taken and not yet shipped.

The day in one sentence

France answered a violent weekend with local curfews and a minister kept to write new methods, the bond market charged a wider premium for French debt, and a cheaper barrel still failed to cut the heating-oil quote.

In euros at home

Carrefour's chief executive said fifty of the chain's stores had been looted, with repair work expected to cost more than €10 million 2. Desk arithmetic: that is more than €200,000 per named store on average if the total is spread evenly — a crude average, not a claim about any one shop. Your local bill is the broken glass on your own street, counted by insurers and owners, not by a national average.

Desk arithmetic on the chief executive's reported total. Individual store losses will differ.

Small words

OAT–Bund spread

An OAT is a French government bond; a Bund is a German one. The spread is how many extra hundredths of a percentage point investors demand to lend to France rather than Germany for the same maturity. On Monday that premium widened to about 145 basis points on the ten-year 2. It is the market's short sentence about relative risk.
