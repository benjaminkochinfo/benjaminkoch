Chip shares fell. The bond prices say why.

The AI sell-off is usually told as a story about mood. Told in bond prices, it is a story about the cost of the loan that builds the data centre.

Tech & Compute Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Monday, 19 October 2026, 08:30 CEST· 2 min read·3 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

Friday’s selling in chipmakers, which took the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 3.8%2, is usually told as a story about mood. The more useful version is told in bond prices.

CoreWeave’s most-traded bonds, paying 9.75% and due in 2031, traded as low as 78 cents on the dollar on 14 October, a yield of 16.39%, having started October at about 851. Its 9.625% bonds due 2032 slumped to 75, for a 16.5% yield, against 103.625 on 2 June1. Both recovered some ground the next day, to 83 and 801. In the same week the data-centre developer Cipher Digital announced an $850 million offering of zero-coupon convertible notes, and JPMorgan analysts cut Applied Digital’s project bonds to neutral, citing a looming “supply overhang” of debt still to be raised1.

That is the mechanism. Much of the AI build-out is financed by lenders who expect to be paid back in fixed instalments. When the price of that debt rises, the cost of every future data centre rises with it, and the shares of companies selling into those data centres get marked down in sympathy. It is not a verdict on the technology. It is a repricing of the loan.

The same report carries the counter-signal. CleanSpark’s bonds held up better, helped by good news from its client Meta, after investors read early retention data for Meta’s Muse assistant as evidence that the company can earn money on its AI spending1. Meta reports on Wednesday, alongside Tesla, Lam Research and Texas Instruments3.

Those reports are the invoices. Read three lines: capital spending, cash flow, and who is paying whom. The rest is the podium.

What to watch

  • CoreWeave's 9.75% 2031 bonds: back above the mid-80s where they started October would mean the scare has eased; a return to the high-70s would mean it has not.
  • Meta's capital spending and cash flow on Wednesday, the clearest test of whether AI spending is being paid for by revenue.
  • Further zero-coupon and convertible deals from data-centre builders: the cheaper the coupon, the more the buyer is betting on the share price.
Desk comment

Share prices argue about the future. Bond prices quote it, with a coupon attached.
