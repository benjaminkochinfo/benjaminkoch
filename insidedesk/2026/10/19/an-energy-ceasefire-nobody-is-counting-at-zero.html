An energy ceasefire that nobody is counting at zero

Kyiv counts 76 Russian strikes on its energy system since the announcement. Moscow counts 1,015 Ukrainian drones in a day. A ceasefire without a neutral ledger is a podium.

Security & Order Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Monday, 19 October 2026, 08:30 CEST· 2 min read·2 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On 11 October President Trump announced that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an “energy ceasefire”, without giving details1. A week later both sides are publishing counts, and neither count is zero.

Zelenskyy wrote on Sunday that Russia had attacked Ukraine’s energy, oil and gas sectors 76 times in the week since the announcement, that facilities in four regions had been struck overnight, and that tens of thousands of families were without power1. Kyiv had initially said it was observing the ceasefire, then resumed strikes on Russian oil facilities on Thursday after a three-day pause1. On Sunday Russia’s defence ministry said it had destroyed 1,015 Ukrainian drones over 15 regions, Crimea and the Black Sea. In St Petersburg the oil terminal and the Ruchi oil depot caught fire; one person was killed and five wounded, the regional governor said1.

Diplomacy runs alongside. Zelenskyy said US-Ukraine intelligence sharing had resumed after being “blocked for some period of time”, and that he had spoken with the envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner about next steps1. Trump, defending his sanctions waiver, wrote that he did not want to drive Russia “away from the bargaining table” and that “great progress is being made”1,2.

The desk’s view: a ceasefire without a verification mechanism is a podium. It becomes a print when someone neutral starts counting. What would make this one real is dull to the point of tedium: an agreed list of covered facilities, a start time, a reporting channel and a third party that logs incidents and publishes them. None of that has been made public.

Until it is, read both numbers, 76 and 1,015, for what they are. They are not evidence that a ceasefire failed. They are evidence that one has not yet been built. Ceasefires that hold are almost always the boring kind, with an annex.

What to watch

  • Whether any text of the ‘energy ceasefire’ is published: covered sites, start date, who verifies.
  • Outage reports from the four Ukrainian regions whose energy facilities were struck overnight into Sunday.
  • The follow-up to the 9 October US–Ukraine meeting in Miami that Zelenskyy discussed with the US envoys on Sunday.
Desk comment

A ceasefire is a ledger. Until someone neutral keeps it, every side's count is a press release.
