The founder is not in the room, and that is the point

SpaceX and Tesla have each set up a committee of independent directors to look at a merger. Elon Musk backs the idea but, as a conflicted party, cannot take part. Corporate law's oldest safeguard just got its biggest test.

Tech & Compute Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Wednesday, 21 October 2026, 08:30 CEST· 2 min read·2 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On SpaceX’s third-quarter earnings call on Tuesday, Elon Musk said the boards of SpaceX and Tesla had each formed special committees of independent directors to review a possible merger, at the request of shareholders1. CNBC TV18 called it a preliminary step towards what would be the largest corporate tie-up ever2.

Why he is not in it. “Because I’m a conflicted party, I can’t actually be in the discussions of the special committees,” Musk said, calling it “an odd kabuki dance where two groups of people are talking, and I can’t be in the conversation”1. He is chief executive and the largest individual shareholder of both companies1.

Who could be. Tesla’s October 19 proxy filing lists seven independent directors under Nasdaq standards, including chair Robyn Denholm1. SpaceX lists six1. One name, Ira Ehrenpreis, appears on both lists1. That overlap is worth watching: an independent director who sits on both sides of a deal is the sort of detail a committee is supposed to deal with in writing.

What is not decided. Musk said he supports the idea but called a merger far from certain: “At the end of the day, the shareholders have to be supportive of this”1. At SpaceX he holds more than 50% of the voting power1. At Tesla, he noted, a deal would need the votes of 50% of all shares outstanding, not just of those who vote1.

The constructive reading. Special committees are slow and procedural, and they are often mocked. They exist so that the person with the most to gain on both sides does not set the price. Tuesday’s call showed the rule working as intended: the most powerful person in the story described himself as being outside the room.

What to watch

  • The names of the committee members; Musk said only that they are independent directors1.
  • Any terms; Musk said the committees have not agreed any1.
  • Tesla's own results on Wednesday, and whether its board says anything about the process.
Desk comment

A good governance rule is one that a powerful man has to describe as a kabuki dance.
