A circuit breaker is a pause, not a verdict

Seoul halted trading on Thursday and bounced on Friday. The halt did its job: it bought minutes. Different people used them.

Psychology & Behaviour Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Friday, 16 October 2026, 15:28 CEST· 2 min read·5 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On Thursday Seoul’s Kospi fell more than 8% intraday, triggering a brief trading halt, and closed down 7.6% at 5,554.221. On Friday it rose 2.05% to 5,668.05, ending a seven-day losing streak2.

The halt is the interesting institution. A circuit breaker does not judge whether prices are right. It interrupts the sequence: the screen goes still, and the people watching it get time they did not ask for. Institutions beat influencers partly because they build pauses into the rules, so nobody has to find the willpower live.

The minutes were used differently. On Friday foreign investors were net buyers of 343.5 billion won of Korean stocks, while local retail and institutional investors were net sellers of a combined 1.17 trillion won2. Same prices, same news, opposite decisions. In New York the VIX, the market’s price for near-term fear, fell to 16.46 and unwound most of Wednesday’s spike3.

Households are running the same experiment more slowly. Michigan’s preliminary October survey put views of current conditions at 44.7, from 50.9 in September4, which PNC notes is an all-time low5. The expectations index rose, to 47.3 from 46.34. People feel worse about today and slightly better about later. That is not a contradiction. It is what a pause looks like from the inside.

Optimism is a risk limit, not a mood. The practical version: decide in advance what would make you act, and let the halt, the weekend or the night do the rest. This is a description of behaviour, not advice on any security.

What to watch

  • Who buys the rebound. In Seoul on Friday it was foreign investors, not local ones2.
  • The VIX against its Wednesday spike: on Thursday it had unwound most of it3.
  • Michigan's final October reading. Current conditions are the mood; expectations are the plan4.
Desk comment

A halt buys minutes. What you do with them is the trade.
