MONEY · DESK NOTE

The committee is behind the print, on purpose

Euro-area inflation is 3.8%. The deposit rate is 2.5%. That gap is not negligence. It is a bet that wages stay calm.

Money, FX & Committees Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Friday, 16 October 2026, 14:39 CEST· 2 min read·1 chart · 4 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

Eurostat confirmed this morning that euro-area inflation was 3.8% in September, up from 3.2% in August1. Energy prices were 18.9% higher than a year earlier. Strip out energy, food, alcohol and tobacco and the rate was 2.5%; services ran at 3.2%1.

The ECB’s deposit rate is 2.50%, after increases that took effect in June and September2. Against headline inflation, that is a real rate of about minus 1.3 points. If you think the committee is asleep, read what it says it is doing.

“We do not react to energy prices, we react if we see risks of higher energy prices becoming embedded in inflation,” Christine Lagarde told the European Parliament’s economic committee on 28 September3. Her evidence that it is not yet embedded: compensation per employee grew 3.3% in the second quarter, down from 3.6% in the first, and most measures of longer-term inflation expectations stand at around 2%3. And a quieter argument: long-term interest rates “have risen notably”, which she said will slow growth and reduce pass-through by more than the September projections assumed3. The bond market is doing some of the committee’s work for it.

That is a coherent position, and also a bet. It holds as long as two dull prints stay dull: wages and core. Core edged up from 2.4% to 2.5% in September1. Not a break; a lean. The next decision is on 29 October4. The desk will be reading the wage line before the press conference, not after.

What to watch

  • The Governing Council decision and press conference on 29 October4.
  • Wage data before the press conference, not after: the ECB's own test is whether energy reaches pay.
  • Core inflation. A move from 2.5% is a lean; a run of them is a break.
Desk comment

A committee that waits is not a committee that sleeps. Read the wage line.
