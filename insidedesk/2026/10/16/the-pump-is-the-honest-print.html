ORDINARY · DESK NOTE

The pump is the honest print

Inflation is 3.8% on average. Nobody lives on the average. Which pipe comes into your house decides your year.

Ordinary & Inherited Life Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Friday, 16 October 2026, 17:30 CEST· 2 min read·1 chart · 2 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

Averages are for committees. Households live inside categories, and Eurostat’s September detail shows how unevenly this shock lands1.

Liquid fuels and fuels for transport (petrol, diesel, heating oil) cost 33.8% more across the euro area than a year earlier. In Germany the same category was up 37.3%, from 27.5% in August1. Electricity, gas, solid fuels and heat, by contrast, were up 6.9% for the euro area. Everything outside energy, food, alcohol and tobacco: 2.5%1. German headline inflation on the harmonised measure was 3.3%, up from 2.9%1.

So two neighbours on the same street can be living in different inflation rates. The one who commutes by diesel and heats with oil sits near the top line of that chart. The one on a fixed electricity tariff who takes the train sits near the bottom. The headline 3.8% describes neither of them precisely.

The constructive part is that this is legible. Fuel is the part of the bill that reacts fastest to the screen in today’s lead piece: up first, and eventually down first. Grid tariffs and gas contracts move more slowly, in both directions. If you are deciding when to fill a heating-oil tank, which tariff to sign, or how to argue for a travel allowance, you need your category, not the average. That is information, not advice. Your contract and your local prices are the binding numbers.

What to watch

  • Pump and heating-oil prices in the European Commission's Weekly Oil Bulletin2.
  • The heating season. Liquid fuels hit the oil-heated house first.
  • October's HICP detail: fuel reacts to the screen fastest, in both directions.
