MARKETS · DESK NOTE

The average stock had a better day than the index

Thursday's headline said plus 0.33%. The equal-weighted index said plus 1.36%. The difference is where the money went.

Markets & Macro Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Friday, 16 October 2026, 15:28 CEST· 2 min read·1 chart · 4 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On Wednesday the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.88% and the S&P 500 1.11%, after US consumer prices for September came in hotter than expected1. On Thursday the S&P 500 rose 0.33% to 7,744.062. That is the number on the evening news. It is not the number that describes the day.

The equal-weighted S&P 500 rose 1.36% and the Russell 2000 of smaller companies 1.38%2. Healthcare gained 1.86% and financials 1.38%, while Nvidia fell 1.84% and the semiconductor ETF 1.04%2. The Dow, which owns less of the AI trade, rose 1.08%3. Money did not leave the market. It left the most crowded room in it.

The reason has a number. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.00% on 15 September, its first close at that level this year, and at 5.31% on 5 October4. A builder that borrows to put up data centres pays for that number before it sells a single hour of compute. On Thursday, after producer prices rose 6.3% on the year, the highest since December 2022, yields actually fell, briefly below 5.04%2. Rates gave the market a day off, not a verdict.

The desk reading: a rotation is not a crash, and a cap-weighted index is a weighting decision, not a census. If your pension or savings plan tracks the headline index, you own the crowded room in its full size. That is a fact about your portfolio, not a recommendation to change it.

What to watch

  • The FOMC decision on 28 October. Futures-implied odds of a hike fell to about 15% on Thursday2.
  • The 10-year yield against 5%. It is the hurdle every debt-financed build now has to clear4.
  • Breadth: the equal-weighted index against the headline. When they part, read the wider one.
Desk comment

The index is a weighting decision. The market is everyone else.
