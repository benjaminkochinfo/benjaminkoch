SECURITY · DESK NOTE

Shabwa: the deadliest day of the escalation

Three ballistic missiles hit a training centre for Yemen's internal-security recruits. The local authority's toll rose from 53 to 73 overnight, with 14 missing.

Security & Order Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Tuesday, 20 October 2026, 17:30 CEST· 2 min read·2 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On Monday the Houthis fired three ballistic missiles at al-Alam camp near Ataq, in Yemen’s Shabwa province, according to the Saudi-backed Yemeni armed forces1. The camp was a training centre for recruits to the internal security forces and police1.

Shabwa’s media office first reported 53 dead and 200 injured. On Tuesday it said an investigative committee had told the governor the toll was 73, with 14 people missing1. Hospitals in the province were overwhelmed and moved the most seriously wounded to Hadramout and Aden1. The BBC called it the deadliest attack reported since the escalation began1.

Both sides’ words. The head of the Presidential Leadership Council, Rashad al-Alimi, called it a “criminal attack”1. The Houthis’ military spokesman said the missiles targeted “Saudi enemy” forces1. Houthi-run media reported a Saudi air strike in Saada on Sunday that killed at least 10 people1.

The ledger. The government’s health ministry said last week that at least 1,282 people had been killed across Yemen since 6 August; at least 19 people have been killed in Houthi attacks on Saudi airports since the start of October1. Separately, the Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi energy sites, which Reuters could not verify2.

This desk cannot add to what the authorities and correspondents have counted. It can insist that the count comes before the commentary.

What to watch

  • Whether Saudi authorities comment on the strike or on the Houthi claims against Yanbu and Shaybah1,2.
  • The fighting around Taiz, Marib and al-Jawf, and on the Red Sea coast near Bab al-Mandab1.
