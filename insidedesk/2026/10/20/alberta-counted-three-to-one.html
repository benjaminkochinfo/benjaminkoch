GEO-ECON · DESK NOTE

Alberta counted, and it came out three to one

After months of petitions, court rulings and foreign-influence warnings, 75.8% of Alberta's voters chose to remain in Canada. The ballot box did what the speeches could not: it settled the question for now.

Geo-economics Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Tuesday, 20 October 2026, 17:30 CEST· 2 min read·3 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

On Monday Albertans answered a question built as a choice between two options: remain a province of Canada, or have the provincial government begin the legal process for a binding referendum on separation1. With about 73% of the count reported, Remain had 1,315,426 votes (75.8%) and the referendum option 420,038 (24.2%)1.

The machinery before the vote. The path to the ballot ran through institutions at every step. A separatist petition was struck down by the Court of King’s Bench in May over the duty to consult Indigenous peoples; a pro-Canada petition was verified with more than 400,000 signatures1. The final polls had Remain between about 66% and 74%1. The result landed at the top of that range.

The noise around it. A Globe and Mail investigation reported that operatives tied to the US Republican Party had helped the secession campaign, and University of Regina researchers attributed most suspected foreign influence activity to Russian sources, with a rising US share1. None of that moved the count far from where the pollsters had it.

What the market did. The Canadian dollar rose 0.4% to C$1.405 per US dollar on Tuesday, which Reuters linked to the result2.

The constructive point is simple. A contested constitutional question was taken out of rallies and put into a count, run by an elections office, with numbers anyone can check3. It does not end the argument. It ends this round of it.

What to watch

  • Elections Alberta's official count; the figures here are the reported count with about 73% reporting1,3.
  • Results of the other nine referendum questions, still being tallied1.
  • Canada's foreign-influence commissioner, whose office contacted a separatist group before the vote1.
Desk comment

A referendum is a very large, very boring receipt. That is its virtue.
