Parliament booked its questions

After the speeches, the calendar: a Senate mission awaiting its rapporteurs, an Assembly inquiry proposed this morning, unions at Matignon at 10:30 the day after.

Politics & Institutions Dr. Benjamin Koch / The Desk· Tuesday, 20 October 2026, 08:30 CEST· 2 min read·5 sources

ShareXLinkedInWhatsAppTelegramEmail

Sunday was a speech and Monday was a curfew map. Tuesday is the day the institutions book their questions — in writing, with dates.

The Senate’s law committee decided on Monday to open a cross-party fact-finding mission on the events of 17 and 18 October1. Its own press release does not mince words: the policing plan “a rapidement été débordé par les événements”, and the mission will establish “la chaîne des événements”, judge the policing set-up and the penal response, and determine administrative, institutional, legal and political responsibilities1. Every Senate group sits on it; it may ask for the powers of a commission of inquiry, and its rapporteurs are to be named “mercredi prochain”1.

At the Assembly, Yaël Braun-Pivet proposes this morning, at the Conférence des présidents, a commission of inquiry into the disorder, the methods of the violent groups and the public-order strategy, including “le dimensionnement et la doctrine” of policing2. Socialist, LFI and Ensemble deputies backed the idea on LCP on Monday evening; the RN said it would decide its position on Tuesday morning3. One dull constraint matters: with the presidential election ahead, parliamentary work ends on 28 February, so any inquiry may report early next year rather than run its usual six months3.

The government’s own table is set for Wednesday. Matignon has invited the CFDT, CGT, FO, CFE-CGC and CFTC for 10:30, with the prime minister, the interior minister and the labour minister, according to the invitation letter seen by franceinfo4. Separately, the Senate’s long-running mission on the future of public order — launched in January — was due to close its work on Monday and present its report to the press on Wednesday5. Coincidence of calendar, says Public Sénat. Useful coincidence, says this desk: a report written before the weekend is a baseline nobody can accuse of hindsight.

You do not have to pick a side in the argument to notice what changed overnight. Questions now have owners, rooms and dates. That is how a country that lost control of some streets for a weekend starts taking back the paperwork.

What to watch

  • Tuesday's Conférence des présidents at the Assembly: is the inquiry created, and with what remit and end date?
  • The Senate rapporteurs (due 'mercredi prochain') and Wednesday's report from the existing public-order mission.
  • Wednesday 10:30 at Matignon: does the union meeting end with dated commitments or a communiqué?
Desk comment

Anger fills a weekend. A committee with a deadline fills a file.
