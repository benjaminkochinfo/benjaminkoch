CLIMATE · LEAD

The night came in under the forecast

Météo-France warned on Monday that Gard and Hérault could go red. By 04:06 on Tuesday nine départements were orange and none red. A warning that overshoots is not a failed warning; it is a cheap one.

On Monday morning Météo-France wrote that if its scenario of very intense, possibly stationary storms was confirmed, "a move to red vigilance would then be expected" for the Hérault and the Gard. The Hérault prefecture passed on the same message: a risk of the warning being raised to red from the middle of the day. The agency's episode note put the expected totals on the eastern Hérault and the Gard at 200–250 mm, locally 300 mm.

At 04:06 on Tuesday the bulletin read differently. Nine départements are orange and none is red. The Gard is orange for rain-flood, storms and floods; the Hérault is now orange only for floods, with its rain-flood and storm risks down to yellow. The Aveyron and the Lozère are orange for floods. The new orange cells lie to the east: Ardèche, Bouches-du-Rhône, Haute-Corse, Var and Vaucluse. Twelve more départements are yellow.

Before you close the tab

One thing you can do this week

This week, before any journey in rough weather, open your national weather service's warning map once and note the colour where you are going, not just where you are. Ten seconds. If it is orange, think about bridges and underpasses on the way rather than the sky above you.

A habit, not advice. Nothing here tells you what to buy, sign or switch.

What went right

Germany's grid ran mostly on renewables this year. Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts reports 215 TWh of renewable public net generation in the first three quarters of 2026 against 120 TWh from fossil sources, covering 62.1% of grid load 1 — up from 35.2% in the same months of 2015 2. Solar panels alone fed in more than 76 TWh, 21% more than a year earlier 3. That is not a pledge. It is metered electricity.

The day in one sentence

Southern France was warned of worse rain than came, and the closed bridges were the point; Paris read its curfew orders, oil waited near $110, and Parliament booked its questions.

In euros at home

At 05:12 on Tuesday, the national average for a litre of Super E10 at open German stations was €2.089, from the official price-reporting data; Hessen averaged €2.139 and Baden-Württemberg €2.049 4. Desk arithmetic: that 9-cent gap comes to €4.50 on a 50-litre tank. The ADAC motoring club says prices are lowest shortly before 12:00 and rise sharply at noon 5, so the hour you fill up can matter about as much as where you live.

Desk arithmetic on the cited averages. This is an early-morning snapshot; prices change through the day. Nothing here is advice.

Small words

Vigilance orange

Météo-France's warning colours run from yellow to orange to red. Orange means dangerous weather is forecast: keep informed and follow the safety advice from the authorities. Red is kept for phenomena of exceptional intensity 6. On Tuesday morning nine départements were orange and none red 6. The colour is a timetable for behaviour, not a forecast of disaster.
