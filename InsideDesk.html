HEALTH · LEAD

An outbreak is beaten one house visit at a time

Ebola reached an eighth Congolese province on Sunday. The headline counts provinces. The response counts tests, contacts and quiet health zones, and those are the numbers that end outbreaks.

Congo's health ministry said on Sunday that the Ebola outbreak had reached Nord-Ubangi, an eighth province, with two confirmed cases, one of them fatal, both men in their twenties. Authorities have recorded at least 9,400 cases and more than 4,500 deaths since the outbreak was declared in mid-May, the deadliest in the country's history. It is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment. Reuters, citing the latest situation report, put the total at 9,415 cases and 4,536 deaths in the 24 hours to 16 October, with 67 new confirmed cases that day, 44 of them in Ituri.

That is the headline, and it is grim. It is also the least useful number in the story. A province is a line on a map. An outbreak is beaten, or not, in smaller units: a sample in a laboratory, a contact visited at home, a burial done safely, a health zone that goes six weeks without a case.

Before you close the tab

One thing you can do this week

This week, when a number reaches you through a picture, a burning street, a red screen, a map with one more province shaded, ask one question before you form a view: out of how many? Write the denominator next to the number. It takes ten seconds, and it changes most stories.

A habit, not advice. Nothing here tells you what to buy, sign or switch.

What went right

Congo's Ebola response reported 38 patients discharged as recovered on 14 October alone, each after two negative control tests, bringing recoveries since the outbreak began to 2,486 1. The same day its laboratories analysed 506 samples, 28% more than the day before, while the share testing positive fell to 12.8% 1. Twelve health zones have now gone at least 42 days without a new confirmed case 1. Every one of those numbers was produced by someone with a swab, a vehicle and a clipboard.

The day in one sentence

Ebola is spreading across a wider map but shrinking in some places, France answered a night of riots with a promise of meetings and teachers, and oil got a little cheaper because Washington chose not to use a law it had just signed.

In euros at home

The comparison portal tanke-günstig put the national average for a 2,000-litre heating-oil order at €177.59 per 100 litres late on Sunday 2. That order therefore costs about €3,551.80. The spread between Länder, from €171.90 in Saxony-Anhalt to €183.91 in Hesse 2, is €12.01 per 100 litres, or about €240 on the same order. The portal says heating oil has fallen by almost ten cents a litre since the start of October 2: on 2,000 litres that is worth up to about €200.

Desk arithmetic on the portal's averages. Your dealer's quote for your postcode is the binding number.

Small words

Contact follow-up rate

In an outbreak, everyone who was close to a confirmed case is a contact, and must be checked every day for symptoms until the incubation period has passed. The follow-up rate is the share of those contacts actually seen that day. Congo's response reported 78.0% on 14 October, against a required 95% 1. The gap is the people whose chain nobody checked that day, which is why the response publishes it.
