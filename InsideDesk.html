The barrel and the screen

The futures screen says the oil shock is cooling. The cargo market says it is still here. Only one of them has to be delivered by Tuesday.

There are two oil prices this week, and they are not having the same week.

The first lives on the screen. Brent futures for December delivery settled on Thursday at $105.92 a barrel, fourteen cents lower on the day, and were trading at $105.14 late on Friday morning, European time. That is the number on the evening news, the one that moves in cents and gets a verb: eased, edged, steadied.

Before you close the tab

One thing you can do this week

Once a month, write three numbers on one line: what you paid per litre of fuel, per unit of heating, and per kWh of electricity. Ten minutes, no app. Over a winter it turns this edition's headlines into your own line on the chart, and your own line is the one you can act on.

A habit, not advice. Nothing here tells you what to buy, sign or switch.

What went right

The sun paid part of the bill. Between 1 March and 15 October, EU solar helped avoid more than €40 billion of gas imports, by the industry body SolarPower Europe's own estimate 1. In the week of 5 October, Germany, France and Spain each set records for solar output on an October day, Germany with 297 GWh on 5 October, and windier days pulled weekly power prices down in most European markets even as gas rose 2.

The day in one sentence

Energy is still dear because of the war, the chip shares had a scare rather than a collapse, and the sun quietly paid part of Europe's bill.

In euros at home

Eurostat's September figures: liquid fuels and fuels for transport cost 33.8% more across the euro area than a year earlier, 37.3% more in Germany; electricity, gas and heat cost 6.9% more 3. So fuel that cost a household €100 in September 2025 cost about €134 in September 2026, or about €137 in Germany. A €100 electricity, gas and heat outlay became about €107.

Desk arithmetic on Eurostat's annual rates. Your contract and your local prices are the binding numbers.

Small words

Equal-weighted index

A normal stock index gives big companies big weights, so a few giants can move the whole number. An equal-weighted index gives every company the same share. On Thursday the normal S&P 500 rose 0.33% and its equal-weighted version 1.36% 4: the typical company had a better day than the giants.
